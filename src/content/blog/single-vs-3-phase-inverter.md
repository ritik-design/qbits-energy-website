The answer (single-phase or 3-phase) determines which inverter you can legally install, how large your solar system can be, and whether you qualify for net metering at all. Getting this wrong means your DISCOM will refuse to commission the system, your subsidy claim will fail, and you may need to replace expensive equipment before the first unit of solar power reaches your meter.

## What Single-Phase and 3-Phase Actually Mean

Alternating current (AC) electricity alternates direction at a fixed frequency, 50 Hz in India, meaning 50 complete cycles per second. A single-phase supply delivers one alternating voltage wave at 230 V between the live wire and neutral. A 3-phase supply delivers three such waves, each offset by 120 degrees from the next, resulting in three live conductors at 230 V each to neutral and 415 V between any two live wires.

The practical consequence of this difference is enormous for solar installations.

A single-phase inverter converts DC from the solar panels into a single AC voltage wave that synchronises with the single live wire in your building. It feeds into the standard 230 V wiring that powers your lights, fans, refrigerators, televisions, and air conditioners. Almost every domestic appliance in India runs on single-phase 230 V supply.

A 3-phase inverter converts DC into three simultaneous AC voltage waves, one for each phase. It synchronises with all three live conductors and distributes solar generation evenly across them. This matters when your loads are 3-phase loads, industrial motors, large central air conditioning systems, commercial lifts, Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs), or arc welders.

A key point that causes confusion: the number of solar panels or the peak generation capacity does not determine whether you need single-phase or 3-phase. Your existing electricity connection determines this. You cannot install a 3-phase inverter if your meter supplies only one live wire, no matter how many panels you have.

| Feature | Single-Phase | 3-Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply voltage | 230 V (L to N) | 415 V (L to L) / 230 V (L to N) |
| Conductors at meter | 2 (L + N) | 4 (L1, L2, L3 + N) |
| Typical solar capacity | Up to 10 kW | 10 kW to 10 MW+ |
| Suited for | Homes, small shops | Factories, hospitals, large commercial |
| Inverter phase | Must match connection | Must match connection |
| DISCOM net-metering limit | 5–10 kW (state-dependent) | Above 10 kW (most states) |

## How to Identify Your Connection Type

The correct way to identify your connection type requires looking at the physical wiring and your meter, not your electricity bill. Bills sometimes carry ambiguous load-sanctioned figures that do not directly indicate phase type.

Here are three reliable methods, in order of accuracy:

**Method 1, Count the wires at your main switchboard.** Switch off the main breaker and locate the point where the utility supply enters your building. Count the insulated wires coming in. Two wires (one red or brown live, one black or blue neutral) indicate single-phase. Four wires (three coloured lives (red, yellow, blue) plus one black or grey neutral) indicate 3-phase. If you are unsure, ask a licensed electrician to verify; do not touch live conductors yourself.

**Method 2, Measure the incoming voltage.** A single-phase connection measures approximately 230 V between the live wire and neutral. On a 3-phase connection, measuring between any two live wires reads approximately 415 V, while measuring from any live wire to neutral still reads 230 V. An electrician with a multimeter or clamp meter can confirm this in 60 seconds.

**Method 3, Check your meter and DISCOM sanction letter.** Your DISCOM sanction letter specifies the sanctioned load and the supply type. A sanctioned load below 5 kW is almost always single-phase in Indian residential practice. A sanctioned load above 7.5 kW is typically 3-phase. Smart meters issued under the national Advanced Metering Infrastructure rollout also carry a phase indicator on the display screen.

> **~95%.** The proportion of Indian residential electricity connections that are single-phase, according to the [Central Electricity Authority](https://cea.nic.in/) distribution reports. Only large homes with sanctioned loads above 7 kW, factories, and commercial establishments hold 3-phase residential connections. *Source, CEA Annual Report on Growth of Electricity Sector in India, 2025.*

## The 4-Question Phase Test: Know Your Connection Before You Buy

This is the named framework for this decision. Run through these four questions in sequence before shortlisting any solar inverter. No guessing, no assumptions, answer each question based on verified facts from your meter or DISCOM sanction letter.

### The 4-Question Phase Test

1. **How many wires enter your main switchboard?** Two wires means single-phase. Four wires means 3-phase. If the answer is four, proceed to Question 2 for 3-phase options. If two, proceed to Question 2 for single-phase sizing.

2. **What is your DISCOM-sanctioned load?** Find this on your sanction letter or on the meter itself. If it is 5 kW or below, your DISCOM will almost certainly approve single-phase solar up to the same capacity. If it is above 7 kW, you likely have or need a 3-phase connection to install the corresponding solar capacity.

3. **What is your highest-power load?** List every piece of equipment that runs simultaneously at peak. If any single appliance is 3-phase (marked 415 V on the nameplate, or "3-phase" in the specification), you need a 3-phase inverter, and you need to verify your supply connection is 3-phase.

4. **What solar capacity do you need?** Divide your monthly electricity units by 120 (a reliable thumb rule for Indian conditions: 1 kW of panels generates approximately 120 units per month on average). If the resulting system size is below 10 kW, single-phase is suitable in most states. Above 10 kW, you will need a 3-phase connection and a 3-phase inverter regardless of your current supply type.

A homeowner with a ₹8,000–₹12,000 monthly electricity bill consuming roughly 1,000–1,500 units typically needs a 8–12 kW system. If their current connection is single-phase, the 4-Question Phase Test surfaces the need for a DISCOM upgrade before they purchase anything, saving months of commissioning delays.

## DISCOM Capacity Limits for Single-Phase Solar in India

The most misunderstood aspect of this decision is the regulatory limit, not the technical one. Single-phase inverters are manufactured in capacities up to 20 kW by several brands. But your DISCOM may only sanction 5 kW of solar on a single-phase connection. Installing a 10 kW inverter without prior DISCOM approval creates a system that cannot be net-metered, cannot receive PM Surya Ghar subsidy, and may face disconnection.

The [Central Electricity Authority Technical Standards for Connectivity of the Distributed Generation Resources Regulations](https://cea.nic.in/) establish a national framework, but each state DISCOM implements its own limits based on feeder capacity, transformer loading, and local grid conditions. As of June 2026, the landscape is broadly as follows:

- **Most states:** Single-phase net metering permitted up to 10 kW. States including Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu generally allow up to 10 kW on a single-phase connection for residential customers under the PM Surya Ghar scheme framework.

- **Congested urban feeders:** Some DISCOMs in Delhi (BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna, Tata Power Delhi) restrict single-phase solar to 5 kW on specific feeders where transformer utilisation is already high.

- **Rural connections:** Single-phase sanctioned loads in rural areas are often below 3 kW, which means the allowed solar capacity is also below 3 kW. Larger systems in rural areas frequently require 3-phase upgrades before solar can be commissioned.

- **Commercial single-phase:** LT commercial connections (shops, small offices) on single-phase are typically capped at 5 kW solar by most DISCOMs even if the sanctioned load is higher.

The correct process: before purchasing any inverter, submit a solar net-metering application to your DISCOM and obtain their technical feasibility report. This document confirms the allowed solar capacity and the required connection type.

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy available under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW residential system, disbursed only after DISCOM commissioning verification. Systems installed on connections that do not match DISCOM sanction lose this subsidy entirely. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

Consulting the [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) guidelines for your specific state before finalising your inverter selection is not optional, it determines whether your system earns you money or becomes a stranded asset.

## When 3-Phase Is Mandatory: Industrial and Commercial Loads

For homeowners in standard 2–4 BHK homes, single-phase solar almost always suffices. The 3-phase inverter requirement is triggered by specific load types or by capacity thresholds, not by a desire for better performance.

### VFD-Driven Motors and Industrial Pumps

Variable Frequency Drives control the speed of 3-phase induction motors by varying the frequency and voltage of the supply. Submersible pumps above 3 HP, industrial blowers, compressors, and textile machinery are common examples. These loads require 3-phase supply, a single-phase inverter cannot power them. Trying to run a 3-phase motor on single-phase supply causes overheating and winding failure within hours.

### Large Central Air Conditioning Systems

Split ACs and window units are single-phase. But central chiller-based air conditioning systems used in large commercial buildings, hotels, and hospitals are 3-phase. If your building uses centralised air conditioning with a chilled water plant or a VRF system above 10 tonnes, you almost certainly have a 3-phase connection and need a 3-phase inverter.

### Commercial and Industrial Net Metering

The [Central Electricity Authority](https://cea.nic.in/) technical standards establish that solar systems above 10 kW connected to the LT distribution network must use 3-phase inverters to avoid phase imbalance on the feeder. DISCOMs will not approve net metering for a large single-phase solar system regardless of the technical configuration. For any commercial or industrial installation above 10 kW, a 3-phase inverter and a 3-phase connection are mandatory.

### Harmonics and Load Balancing

[Harmonics](/glossary/harmonics/) are distortions in the electrical waveform caused by non-linear loads (VFDs, rectifiers, LED drivers). A 3-phase inverter with active harmonic mitigation helps reduce the total harmonic distortion (THD) injected into the grid, a requirement under [IEC 61727](https://www.iec.ch/homepage) and the CEA Technical Standards. Single-phase inverters also comply with THD limits but operate on one phase only, so any harmonic issue affects a single feeder rather than being distributed across three.

## Can You Upgrade from Single-Phase to 3-Phase?

This is one of the most common questions from homeowners whose solar system outgrows their connection. The answer is yes, but it requires formal action through your DISCOM, not just an inverter swap.

The process involves four steps:

1. **Application to DISCOM.** Submit a request for enhancement of sanctioned load and conversion of supply type from single-phase to 3-phase at your local DISCOM office or online portal. You will need proof of ownership or tenancy, a copy of your electricity bill, and a load schedule listing all connected equipment.

2. **Technical feasibility check.** The DISCOM engineer will inspect the nearest distribution transformer and feeder to confirm there is spare capacity for your upgraded connection. In densely built urban areas, this step can take 30–90 days.

3. **Connection upgrade and new meter.** If feasibility is confirmed, a 3-phase service connection is drawn from the nearest distribution point, and a 3-phase bidirectional smart meter is installed. You pay the applicable charges, typically ₹8,000–₹25,000 depending on the state and distance to the nearest 3-phase feeder.

4. **Fresh solar sanction.** With the new 3-phase connection in place, you submit a fresh solar net-metering application for the increased capacity. The inverter selection, string sizing, and panel layout must all be revised for the 3-phase configuration.

Skipping any step (for instance, buying a 3-phase inverter and connecting it to a single-phase meter) results in a system that does not start, an invoice for a commissioning call, and possible equipment damage. The upgrade process is straightforward when followed in sequence. A typical single-to-3-phase conversion including DISCOM charges and new meter installation adds ₹15,000–₹40,000 to the project cost but enables systems up to 100 kW.

## Power Factor, Load Balancing, and Neutral Current Explained

Technical readers and EPC installers frequently ask about three concepts that behave differently across single-phase and 3-phase systems.

### Power Factor

[Power factor](/glossary/power-factor/) is the ratio of real power (kW) consumed by a load to the apparent power (kVA) drawn from the supply. A power factor of 1.0 means all the current drawn is doing useful work. Motor loads typically have power factors of 0.7–0.9; they draw more current than their useful power alone would require.

Single-phase inverters export power at unity power factor (1.0) from the solar side. But if your single-phase building also has inductive loads (motors, old transformers), the net power factor seen by your DISCOM meter may be less than 1.0. Some commercial DISCOMs charge a penalty when the average power factor falls below 0.9.

3-phase inverters typically offer reactive power support, the inverter can inject or absorb reactive power to correct the site power factor automatically, reducing the risk of power factor penalties. This is a specific advantage of 3-phase systems for commercial and industrial customers, not relevant for standard residential use.

### Load Balancing

A 3-phase building has three live conductors. Ideally, loads should be evenly distributed across all three. When one phase carries significantly more load than the others, the excess current flows back through the neutral conductor, heating it and wasting energy. A 3-phase solar inverter with active load balancing monitors all three phases and adjusts its output accordingly, reducing neutral current and improving feeder efficiency.

In a single-phase installation, load balancing is irrelevant, there is only one live conductor. All loads and all solar generation share that single wire.

### Neutral Current

In a balanced 3-phase system, the three phase currents cancel each other in the neutral wire, reducing neutral current close to zero. This extends the life of the neutral conductor and the distribution transformer. Single-phase installations always carry full load current in the neutral, which is acceptable for residential loads but becomes a design constraint for higher-capacity systems.

A useful rule of thumb: if your total connected load exceeds 20 kW, the neutral current from a single-phase installation becomes a meaningful engineering concern. At that scale, a 3-phase connection and 3-phase inverter are the correct technical solution regardless of DISCOM limits.

## Common Mistakes When Choosing Between Single-Phase and 3-Phase

The errors below appear repeatedly in solar projects across India. Each one is avoidable with the 4-Question Phase Test.

- **Buying based on panel count, not connection type.** The number of panels does not determine phase requirement. Twelve 500 Wp panels (6 kW) can connect to either a single-phase or 3-phase inverter, what matters is your electricity connection.

- **Assuming 3-phase is always better.** A 3-phase inverter connected to a single-phase supply will not work. "Bigger" is not "better" when it does not match your infrastructure.

- **Ignoring DISCOM limits when sizing single-phase systems.** Ordering a 10 kW single-phase inverter before confirming DISCOM approval creates a commissioning problem. The inverter arrives, the panels go on the roof, and the DISCOM says the feeder is already loaded and limits you to 5 kW. The remaining capacity sits idle.

- **Skipping the DISCOM upgrade for 3-phase.** Purchasing a 3-phase inverter before the DISCOM has issued a new 3-phase sanction leaves the equipment in a warehouse. DISCOM upgrades take 30–90 days; order the inverter after the sanction letter arrives.

- **Misidentifying 3-phase loads.** Some homeowners see a large motor-driven well pump and assume 3-phase is needed. Check the nameplate: many residential submersible pumps up to 1.5 HP are single-phase. A 3-phase pump will be clearly marked "3-phase 415 V" on the motor plate.

- **Choosing a [string inverter](/glossary/string-inverter/) without confirming phase compatibility.** String inverters for residential use are typically single-phase. Specifying a string inverter for a commercial 3-phase installation requires checking the datasheet carefully.
