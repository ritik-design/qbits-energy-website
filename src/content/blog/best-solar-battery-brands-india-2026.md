From the manufacturer documents compared here, Pylontech US5000 has the most complete datasheet for a roughly 5 kWh lithium quote. ## How the shortlist was chosen

We checked six brands that Indian homeowners are likely to meet: Pylontech, Luminous, Livguard, Loom Solar, Exide and Amaron. Product pages and datasheets were read between 26 September and 14 October 2026. Each figure below is as printed by the manufacturer on that date, and manufacturers revise these documents, so check the revision date before you buy.

The method has five rules:

1. **Compare within a battery class.** A 12 V, 150 Ah tubular battery is not compared with a rack-mounted lithium module as if they were interchangeable.
2. **Use exact-product documents.** Brand reputation, marketplace reviews and unsourced dealer tables do not tell you whether a product fits.
3. **Mark missing fields as unknown.** We do not estimate voltage, usable energy, current limits, warranty coverage or service reach. Where a brand's own site blocked us or did not publish a field, the review says so.
4. **Do not score reliability.** No comparable independent field-failure data exists for these products, so there are no scores or rankings here.
5. **Make compatibility a separate gate.** Matching voltage or communication-port labels do not mean two devices can operate together.

This approach favours transparent documentation. It does not mean the first product in a table will perform better at your home.

## How India's heat affects batteries

Datasheet cycle ratings are set at a stated temperature, usually 25 °C. Pylontech prints its cycle life and design life "at 25 °C". Indian rooms, battery cupboards and covered terraces often run warmer than that during summer, and the effect is not small.

- **Sealed lead-acid:** Battery University gives a guideline that each 8 °C rise cuts the life of a [sealed lead-acid battery](https://www.batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-806a-how-heat-and-loading-affect-battery-life) in half. A battery rated for 10 years at 25 °C would last about 5 years at 33 °C. Flooded tubular batteries cope with heat better, but they lose water faster, which is why Luminous and Livguard print top-up intervals.
- **Cycle life at temperature:** Battery University's [discharge temperature article](https://www.batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-502-discharging-at-high-and-low-temperatures/) says cycle life falls by about 20 percent at 30 °C and 40 percent at 40 °C, relative to a moderate room temperature.
- **Lithium:** Battery University says [environmental conditions, and especially a full battery kept hot](https://www.batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-808-how-to-prolong-lithium-based-batteries/), drive lithium-ion ageing as much as cycling does. LFP tolerates heat better than other lithium chemistries, but a cool, ventilated indoor location still helps.

In practice: keep the battery out of direct sun and away from the inverter's exhaust air, leave the clearances the maker prints, and ask for the manufacturer's operating temperature range for charge and discharge, not just the cycle count.

## Battery sizing: how much capacity do you need?

Choose the chemistry and brand after you know the energy you need. The full method is in the [hybrid battery-sizing guide](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/). In short:

- Load energy (Wh) = running load (W) x backup hours.
- Usable battery energy needed = load energy / inverter efficiency.
- Nominal battery energy = usable energy / depth of discharge.
- Battery Ah = nominal Wh / system voltage.

Efficiency of 0.95 and the depth of discharge are assumptions for illustration. Use the values your datasheets print.

| Case | Load energy | Usable energy at 0.95 efficiency | Nominal energy | Ah at 48 V |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 W for 4 h, lithium at 80% DoD | 2,000 Wh | 2,105 Wh | 2,632 Wh | 54.8 Ah |
| 1,000 W for 6 h, lithium at 80% DoD | 6,000 Wh | 6,316 Wh | 7,895 Wh | 164.5 Ah |

For the second case, the Pylontech US5000 prints 4.56 kWh usable per module, so 6.316 / 4.56 is 1.39 and you need two modules. A single 48 V string of four 12 V, 150 Ah tubular batteries holds 7.2 kWh nominal, or 5.76 kWh at the 80% DoD the Luminous and Livguard pages rate their cycle life at, which is below 6.316 kWh, so that case needs a second string.

Check that the battery's own charge and discharge current limits agree with the inverter. Qbits QBH datasheets print a maximum charge and discharge current of 75 to 120 A for the 3 to 6 kW single-phase family, 175 to 190 A for 7 to 8 kW, and 120 to 250 A for the three-phase family, by model. A battery rated for 70 A maximum charge, like the Loom Solar page lists, needs the inverter's charge current limit set to the battery's limit. For pairing on a QBH inverter, ask Qbits to confirm the exact model, as the datasheets do not print a communication protocol or approved battery list. Datasheets are on the [Qbits datasheet page](/download-datasheets/), and the [QBH 3 to 6 kW model page](/our-products/QBH-3-6KS48P/) lists the single-phase family.

## Lithium vs VRLA: 10-year cost of ownership

Price per kWh is the wrong comparison, because the two chemistries are usable to different depths and last for different numbers of cycles. Use this formula:

**10-year cost per usable kWh = (units bought over 10 years x unit price) / usable kWh per unit**

where **units bought = round up of (cycles used in 10 years / rated cycles)**, and cycles used in 10 years is about 3,650 at one full cycle a day.

This is an illustration using two listed prices from 14 October 2026. It excludes discounting, price changes, labour, the charger or inverter differences, and capacity fade. It also assumes the datasheet cycle rating holds at your room temperature, which the heat section above shows is optimistic.

| Item | Luminous LPTT 12150H (tubular) | Loom Solar CAML-10051 (lithium) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listed price | Rs 17,999 incl. taxes | Rs 1,08,000, tax treatment not stated |
| Nominal energy | 12 V x 150 Ah = 1.8 kWh | 4.92 kWh (technical table) |
| Assumed depth of discharge | 80% (as the cycle rating is printed) | 80% (our assumption; the page prints none) |
| Usable energy | 1.8 x 0.80 = 1.44 kWh | 4.92 x 0.80 = 3.94 kWh |
| Rated cycles | Up to 1,500 | Not printed; assume 3,650 or more |
| Units bought in 10 years | 3,650 / 1,500 = 2.43, so 3 | 1 |
| 10-year spend | 3 x 17,999 = Rs 53,997 | Rs 1,08,000 |
| 10-year cost per usable kWh | 53,997 / 1.44 = about Rs 37,500 | 1,08,000 / 3.936 = about Rs 27,400 |

On these inputs, the lithium unit costs less per usable kWh over 10 years only if its datasheet confirms at least 3,650 cycles at the depth you will use. If the real rating were 2,000 cycles you would buy it twice. Change the inputs to your own quotes. If outages are rare, cycles used in 10 years can be far below 3,650 and the tubular option may need fewer replacements. As cycling approaches daily, the lithium option gains.

## What we did not use to choose a winner

### Price

No price ranking is published. A displayed unit price can exclude the number of batteries required, rack or cabinet, cables, protection, communications hardware, installation, transport and tax treatment. Compare written quotes only after the battery architecture and scope match.

### Warranty and service

A headline warranty duration does not reveal the remedy, capacity threshold, registration deadline, transport responsibility, labour coverage, pro-rata treatment or exclusions. Request the current warranty document for the exact model and confirm the claim route in writing.

### Cycle life and expected life

Cycle claims are not comparable unless depth of discharge, temperature, charge and discharge rate, end-of-life capacity and test method are aligned.

### Safety certification

A standard number on a product page is not enough to establish that the unit offered to you is covered by a current certificate. Ask for the certificate, model schedule, issuing body and validity details for the exact SKU.

## Compatibility is the purchase gate

For lithium, check nominal and operating voltage, charge and discharge current, CAN or RS485 protocol, cable pinout, firmware, approved module count and inverter settings. A shared connector name does not guarantee protocol support.

For tubular lead-acid batteries, check the charging profile, bank voltage, current limit, low-voltage cutoff, equalisation requirements, ventilation and maintenance instructions. As one example, Exide prints a bulk setpoint of 2.60 V per cell for its flooded tubular range. A 48 V bank has 24 cells, so that is 24 x 2.60 = 62.4 V, while Qbits QBH datasheets print a maximum charging voltage of 60 V or below, configurable. Ask the seller and Qbits to confirm which bulk voltage the bank will use. The [battery connection guide](/blog/inverter-battery-connection-diagram/) explains why the power path and communications path need separate checks.

Do not ask only, "Does this battery work with a hybrid inverter?" Ask the seller to confirm the exact inverter model, firmware, exact battery SKU, module count, cable or pinout and required settings in one written response.
