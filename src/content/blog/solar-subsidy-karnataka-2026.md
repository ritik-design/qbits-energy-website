## What PM Surya Ghar Provides in Karnataka: The Central Subsidy

The Pradhan Mantri Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is administered nationally through [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and disbursed as a direct bank transfer after installation and commissioning. The subsidy structure is nationally standardised:

| System Size | Rate | Total Central Subsidy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | ₹30,000/kW | ₹30,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹30,000/kW | ₹60,000 |
| 3 kW | ₹30,000 (first 2 kW) + ₹18,000 (third kW) | ₹78,000 |
| 4 kW+ | Capped at 3 kW equivalent | ₹78,000 maximum |

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW residential system, paid directly to the buyer's bank account after commissioning. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

Eligibility requires ALMM-listed panels, a BIS-certified inverter, and installation by a DISCOM-empanelled vendor. ## Karnataka State Solar Policy: What KREDL Administers

Karnataka does not offer a dedicated state-level cash top-up for residential rooftop solar in 2026 comparable to Gujarat's Surya Gujarat or Maharashtra's Mukhyamantri Saur Krishi Vahini equivalents. The state's solar policy framework, administered by [KREDL](https://kredl.kar.gov.in/) (Karnataka Renewable Energy Development Ltd), is oriented primarily toward utility-scale and commercial-industrial solar rather than residential incentives.

What the Karnataka state framework does provide for residential applicants:

- **Empanelled vendor lists**: KREDL maintains state-level empanelment of solar vendors for state-sponsored programmes; however, PM Surya Ghar uses DISCOM empanelment.
- **KERC net metering regulations**: Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission sets the export tariff and banking mechanism that governs residential surplus generation.
- **HESCOM, MESCOM, CESC, GESCOM facilitation**: KREDL coordinates with all five DISCOMs on implementation of national programmes and sets compliance standards.

The practical implication for Karnataka homeowners: your subsidy income is the central PM Surya Ghar amount. The state does not currently add additional cash. The economic case still works well, primarily because Karnataka's solar resource is genuinely strong.

## The 5-Pillar Karnataka Subsidy Stack Framework

Navigating the Karnataka solar subsidy process requires managing five distinct elements in sequence. Each pillar must be completed correctly to ensure the central subsidy is received without delay.

### The 5-Pillar Karnataka Subsidy Stack

1. **National portal registration**: Register at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) with your DISCOM consumer number, Aadhaar details, and bank information before any installation begins. Registration after installation creates verification complications and often delays subsidy disbursement.

2. **DISCOM technical feasibility**: Submit to your DISCOM (BESCOM for Bangalore, or one of the four regional DISCOMs) for technical feasibility approval. BESCOM accepts online submissions through its consumer portal; other DISCOMs may require in-person submission at divisional offices.

3. **ALMM-compliant installation**: Only panels and inverters on the current MNRE ALMM list qualify. Verify specific model numbers on the [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/), the list updates quarterly. The [ALMM guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains which phase applies to your application.

4. **Net meter commissioning**: After installation, the DISCOM replaces your existing meter with a bidirectional smart meter. BESCOM's queue for this step typically runs 30 to 45 days post-inspection.

5. **Subsidy disbursement**: After commissioning is confirmed on the PM Surya Ghar portal, the central subsidy transfers to your Aadhaar-linked bank account within 30 days. Karnataka does not currently have a parallel state disbursement mechanism for a separate top-up.

## Karnataka's Five DISCOMs: Which One Covers Your Area?

Karnataka's electricity distribution is divided among five companies, each with distinct geographic coverage and varying levels of digital infrastructure for solar applications.

### BESCOM: Bangalore and Surrounding Districts

BESCOM (Bangalore Electricity Supply Company Limited) covers Bangalore Urban, Bangalore Rural, Tumkur, Kolar, Chikkaballapur, and Ramanagara. It is Karnataka's largest DISCOM by consumer base and has the most developed digital portal for solar applications. BESCOM's online application system allows submission, document upload, status tracking, and communication without requiring office visits for standard residential cases. Portal: [bescom.karnataka.gov.in](https://bescom.karnataka.gov.in/). Average timeline for straightforward residential applications: 45 to 75 days end-to-end.

### HESCOM: Northern Karnataka

HESCOM (Hubballi–Dharwad Electricity Supply Company) covers Hubballi–Dharwad, Gadag, Haveri, Uttara Kannada, Belagavi, and Bagalkot. Northern Karnataka has higher solar irradiance than Bangalore but HESCOM's application processing is less digitised. Expect 60 to 90 days for net metering completion. HESCOM divisional offices in Hubli and Belagavi are the primary contact points.

### MESCOM: Coastal Karnataka

MESCOM (Mangaluru Electricity Supply Company) covers Mangaluru, Udupi, Chikkamagaluru, Shivamogga, Kodagu, and Hassan. Coastal Karnataka's higher humidity and moderate irradiance (4.8 to 5.2 kWh/m²/day) make IP66 weather-rated equipment particularly important here. MESCOM processing: 60 to 80 days typical.

### CESC: Mysuru Region

CESC (Chamundeshwari Electricity Supply Corporation) covers Mysuru, Mandya, Chamarajanagar, and Kodagu. Mysuru city has a growing residential solar market. CESC portal: [cescmysore.karnataka.gov.in](https://cescmysore.karnataka.gov.in/). Timeline: 55 to 75 days.

### GESCOM: Hyderabad Karnataka

GESCOM (Gulbarga Electricity Supply Company) covers Kalaburagi, Bidar, Yadgir, Koppal, Ballari, and Raichur. This region has the highest solar irradiance in Karnataka (up to 6.0 kWh/m²/day) but lower urban density. GESCOM processing timelines can extend to 90 days in rural subdivisions.

## BESCOM Net Metering: The Online Process Step by Step

BESCOM has invested significantly in its consumer digital infrastructure. The solar net metering application is largely paperless for standard residential cases. Here is the actual sequence:

- **Step 1: Portal login**: Access BESCOM's consumer services portal or use the PM Surya Ghar national portal, which routes BESCOM consumers to the BESCOM backend. Log in with your consumer number.
- **Step 2: Solar application submission**: Fill the net metering application form online. Specify proposed system capacity, inverter type, and installer details. Upload scanned documents (bill, Aadhaar, PAN, property proof, rooftop photographs).
- **Step 3: Feasibility assessment**: BESCOM's divisional office reviews the application. For standard cases (1–3 kW, existing meter board adequate), approval is granted online with a digital feasibility consent letter. Complex cases (larger systems, older infrastructure) require a site visit.
- **Step 4: Installation and inspection request**: After feasibility approval, the empanelled installer completes the installation. The consumer then submits an inspection request through the BESCOM portal, uploading post-installation photographs and the installer's completion certificate.
- **Step 5: BESCOM inspection**: A BESCOM technician visits within 7 to 15 days of inspection request to physically verify the installation matches the approved design.
- **Step 6: Meter change**: Following a successful inspection, BESCOM orders the bidirectional meter change. This is the longest step, 30 to 45 days in most Bangalore divisions.
- **Step 7: Commissioning and subsidy trigger**: Once the bidirectional meter is active, BESCOM updates the PM Surya Ghar portal. The central subsidy disbursal begins within 30 days.

The BESCOM process is faster than most Indian DISCOMs but still requires active tracking by the consumer. Setting calendar reminders every 10 days to check portal status and follow up with the divisional office directly reduces delays meaningfully.

## Karnataka Net Metering Economics: What KERC Rules Mean for Your Bill

Karnataka's net metering framework under KERC (Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission) governs how surplus generation is credited and how annual settlements work.

**How Karnataka net metering billing works**:

Under the KERC net metering regulation, monthly surplus generation (units where your solar system generates more than your consumption) is credited as a carry-forward balance. At the end of the financial year (typically March 31st), any remaining banked units are settled at the applicable export tariff, not at the import tariff.

This distinction matters significantly for economics:
- Import tariff (what you pay per unit drawn from the grid): ₹5.00 to ₹8.00 per unit depending on consumption slab
- Export tariff (what the DISCOM pays for surplus): approximately ₹3.00 to ₹4.00 per unit under applicable KERC order

A 3 kW system in Bangalore generating 4,800 kWh annually with 80% self-consumption gets credited at ₹6.50/unit (average) for 3,840 kWh (₹24,960) and at ₹3.50/unit for the exported 960 kWh (₹3,360), a total of ₹28,320 annual value.

At 100% self-consumption (unlikely but illustrative): ₹4,800 × ₹6.50 = ₹31,200. The difference underscores why right-sizing to your actual consumption matters more than maximising panel count.

## Documents Required for Karnataka Solar Subsidy: State-Specific Checklist

- **Electricity bill**: Recent BESCOM/HESCOM/MESCOM/CESC/GESCOM bill (within 2 months); shows consumer number and division
- **Aadhaar card**: Linked to OTP-enabled mobile for PM Surya Ghar portal authentication
- **PAN card**: Required for subsidy disbursement
- **Bank account proof**: Passbook or cancelled cheque; account must be Aadhaar-linked for direct transfer
- **Property ownership**: Khata certificate from BBMP/Panchayat, sale deed, or property tax receipt
- **For apartments, Association NOC**: Apartment owners association or RWA letter permitting rooftop installation, specifying allocated rooftop area
- **Rooftop photographs**: Minimum four views showing shadow-free area and existing electrical board
- **Installer's technical feasibility form**: Signed by DISCOM-empanelled vendor; includes single-line diagram of proposed system
- **ALMM compliance certificates**: Separate certificates for panels and inverter confirming current ALMM listing

For BESCOM applicants, all documents can be uploaded digitally through the BESCOM consumer portal. For HESCOM, MESCOM, CESC, and GESCOM, call the divisional office in advance to confirm whether physical submission is still required.

## Common Mistakes Karnataka Solar Applicants Make

- **Not verifying vendor DISCOM empanelment**: Karnataka has many solar installers, but only DISCOM-empanelled vendors can process PM Surya Ghar applications. Ask the vendor to show their empanelment certificate for the specific DISCOM (BESCOM, HESCOM, etc.) before paying any advance.
- **Installing before DISCOM feasibility approval**: Even if the installer assures you approval is "just a formality," install only after the written feasibility consent letter arrives. Pre-installation claims are frequently not honoured if the feasibility is later denied or modified.
- **Choosing a 5 kW system when the bill supports only 3 kW**: The PM Surya Ghar subsidy is capped at 3 kW regardless of system size. For a Bangalore home consuming 450–600 units per month, a 3 kW system covers most of the bill without over-generating and exporting at the lower tariff.
- **Skipping ALMM verification**: The ALMM list changes quarterly. A model proposed by an installer today may have been delisted three months ago. Check the [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) directly for the exact panel and inverter models in your quote.
- **Underestimating the monsoon impact on payback**: Bangalore's June–September monsoon reduces daily generation by 40 to 50 percent compared to peak months. Payback calculations should use annual generation figures, not summer peak figures, to avoid overestimating economics.
