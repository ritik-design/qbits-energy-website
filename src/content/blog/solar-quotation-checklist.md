## The 12-Point Quote Audit Framework

This named framework is the structured method for evaluating any residential solar proposal received from an Indian installer or EPC company. Apply it to every quotation before signing. Each point has a minimum threshold — anything below that threshold is a negotiation item or a reason to walk away.

### The 12-Point Quote Audit Framework

1. **Inverter warranty depth** — Minimum 10 years; 12 years is the new benchmark for established Indian brands. Confirm whether it is full-replacement or prorated.
2. **Panel ALMM status** — Both solar panels and inverter must appear on the current [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) for PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility.
3. **Panel wattage and tier** — Nameplate Wp should be verified against a Tier-1 or BIS-certified panel brand; reject generic or unbranded panels.
4. **Mounting structure specification** — Material (HDG steel or anodised aluminium), gauge (≥ 2 mm steel / ≥ 2.5 mm aluminium), and wind-zone rating.
5. **Subsidy and installer empanelment** — Confirm empanelment on the PM Surya Ghar portal for the correct state-DISCOM combination.
6. **Net metering facilitation** — End-to-end filing, bidirectional meter cost, and DISCOM liaison must be explicitly included.
7. **DC and AC cable specifications** — Rated voltage, conductor cross-section, and BIS mark for all cable types.
8. **MPPT count vs roof orientation** — Each distinct orientation requires its own MPPT input; verify the inverter spec matches your roof layout.
9. **Monitoring system and duration** — Daily generation data, fault alerts, and subscription period (should cover full warranty term).
10. **After-sales SLA in writing** — Response time, on-site visit window, and replacement TAT — all signed as part of the agreement.
11. **Payment milestone structure** — Payments should track physical milestones (delivery, installation, commissioning, net metering approval) — not calendar dates.
12. **Commissioning documentation handover** — Single-line diagram, test report, warranty certificates, and DISCOM registration number — all in the buyer's name.

The following sections expand on each cluster of items with the exact questions to ask and the thresholds that separate a professional proposal from a speculative one.

## Item 1 — Inverter Warranty: The Single Most Critical Line Item

Inverter replacement is the largest unbudgeted expense in a residential solar system's 25-year life. The inverter is the only active electronic component in the system; panels are passive and typically last 25–30 years. The inverter, if under-specified, may need replacement at year 7 or year 10 — precisely when the system should be generating its best returns.

Verify these four dimensions of the warranty clause in the quotation document:

- **Warranty duration** — 10 years is the floor; 12 years is the standard that engineering-first brands now offer. A 5-year warranty on an inverter that costs ₹20,000–₹60,000 to replace is a hidden liability clause.
- **Full-replacement vs prorated** — A prorated warranty pays 50 percent of replacement cost at year 8. A full-replacement warranty covers 100 percent of parts and labour at any point within the warranty period. Ask explicitly which type applies.
- **On-site service or depot service** — Does the warranty require you to ship the inverter to a service centre, or does the brand send a technician to your premises? On-site service eliminates the logistics risk that frequently voids consumer warranties in practice.
- **Firmware and software coverage** — AI-monitored inverters push firmware updates over Wi-Fi or 4G. Confirm that firmware updates are included and do not carry a separate subscription charge.

The [solar inverter warranty guide](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty/) has a full comparison of warranty structures across Indian and imported brands. For reference: Qbits offers a 12-year full-replacement warranty with on-site service through its authorised partner network — the longest among Indian-manufactured inverter brands at their price point.

> **12 years.** That is the warranty horizon that separates Indian inverter brands that have committed to after-sales infrastructure from those that have not. A brand offering 5 years on a residential inverter is implicitly telling you that it does not expect to be servicing that unit beyond year 5. *Source — [MNRE Empanelled Vendor Data](https://mnre.gov.in/), 2026.*

## Item 2 — ALMM Compliance: The Subsidy Gate

The [Approved List of Models and Manufacturers](/glossary/almm-list/) (ALMM) is maintained by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. For PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, both the solar panels and the inverter must be ALMM-listed at the time of installation. If either component is unlisted, the DISCOM rejects the subsidy application.

The financial stakes are significant:

- **Up to ₹78,000** central subsidy for a 3 kW residential system, paid directly to the bank account.
- **Up to ₹94,500** for a 2 kW system in high-priority states under the enhanced subsidy slabs.
- **State-level top-ups** in Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu add a further ₹10,000–₹30,000.

Ask every installer to provide the ALMM certificate number for both the panel model and the inverter model. Cross-check on the MNRE ALMM portal — the list is updated quarterly and models do get removed when manufacturers fail annual audits. An inverter that was ALMM-listed 18 months ago may no longer qualify.

The [ALMM Phase III guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains the compliance tiers, what Phase III expansion means for residential buyers, and which inverter brands maintain consistent listing status across audit cycles.

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW residential system, paid directly to the buyer's bank account after DISCOM verification. *Source — [PM Surya Ghar Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

Verifying ALMM status takes under 5 minutes on the MNRE portal. No other single check in this solar quotation checklist protects more money per unit of effort.

## Item 3 — Panel Specifications: Wattage, Tier, and Degradation Guarantee

The panel line items in a quotation often read "330 Wp mono-PERC" or "400 Wp bifacial" without naming the manufacturer. That is a deliberate vagueness. The wattage printed on a generic panel may not be independently tested, and the degradation rate — which determines how much power the system produces in year 20 — is often unverified.

Check these five panel-specific items in any quotation:

- **Manufacturer name and model number** — Verify the brand appears on either the ALMM list or Tier-1 bankability lists published by BloombergNEF or BNEF-aligned research firms.
- **Nameplate wattage and tolerance** — A panel rated "330 Wp ±5%" can legally deliver 313.5 Wp. Ask for positive-tolerance-only panels (0/+5%) to ensure you get at least the rated output.
- **First-year degradation guarantee** — Industry standard is ≤ 2% in year one. Cheap panels may degrade 3–4% in the first year, equivalent to losing one full panel's output in a 10-panel array.
- **Annual degradation guarantee post year one** — The standard is ≤ 0.7% per year. At 25 years, this delivers ≥ 80% of nameplate output. Unbranded panels often guarantee only ≥ 70% at year 25.
- **BIS certification (IS 14286)** — Mandatory for panels sold in India. Ask for the BIS licence number or the BIS test report reference. [IEC 61215](https://www.iec.ch/dyn/www/f?p=103:38:0::::FSP_ORG_ID,FSP_APEX_PAGE,FSP_PROJECT_ID:1297,23,103397) and IEC 61730 are the international equivalents that reputable panel manufacturers also carry.

A 10-panel array of 330 Wp panels from a brand with 1% extra annual degradation will produce roughly 6–8 fewer units per day by year 15 compared with a Tier-1 equivalent. Over that period, at ₹8 per unit, that gap costs the homeowner approximately ₹1.5–2 Lakh in lost savings — far more than the premium paid for a quality panel brand. The [International Energy Agency's 2024 solar PV market report](https://www.iea.org/reports/solar-pv) confirms that module quality variance remains one of the top long-term yield risk factors in residential solar globally.

## Item 4 — Mounting Structure: The Most Overlooked Component

The mounting structure is the component that holds every other component in place for 25 years through monsoons, wind loads, thermal cycling, and UV exposure. It is also the component most frequently under-specified in entry-level quotations.

| Structure Type | Material | Typical Life | Risk Profile | Suitable for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hot-dip galvanised steel | MS + zinc coating ≥ 275 g/m² | 25+ years | Low | All zones, coastal, cyclone-prone |
| Anodised aluminium | 6063-T5 alloy | 20–25 years | Low | Standard residential, inland |
| Painted mild steel | MS + powder coat | 8–12 years | High | Avoid in coastal / humid zones |
| Galvanised iron (GI) | Thin zinc plating | 10–15 years | Medium | Inland, low-humidity only |

Ask the installer for the structure's material certification — a HDG structure should come with a mill certificate showing zinc coating weight. Structures sold without certification are almost always mild steel with a thin paint finish that begins to rust after the first monsoon season.

Wind-zone compliance matters more than most buyers realise. A structure designed for Wind Zone II (basic wind speed 39 m/s) should not be installed in a Zone IV coastal district where the basic wind speed reaches 50 m/s per IS 875 Part 3. A mis-rated structure risks physical failure during a cyclone — with panels, wiring, and all.

## Item 5 — Subsidy Eligibility and Installer Empanelment

Subsidy claims under PM Surya Ghar are processed through a chain: the homeowner applies, the DISCOM verifies, the empanelled vendor executes the installation, and the DISCOM approves the claim before releasing funds to the buyer's bank account. If any link in this chain is broken — particularly if the installer is not empanelled for your DISCOM — the subsidy does not reach you.

Verification steps:

1. **Confirm installer empanelment** on the PM Surya Ghar portal ([pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/)) under the vendor registration section, filtering by state and DISCOM.
2. **Confirm DISCOM area** — your DISCOM is determined by your meter number's issuing authority, not by geography alone. In Maharashtra, for example, MSEDCL and Tata Power are separate DISCOMs serving adjacent areas.
3. **Confirm system size eligibility** — PM Surya Ghar covers 1–10 kW for residential consumers. Commercial or agricultural connections are not covered under the standard residential slab.
4. **Confirm ownership** — the solar system must be installed on a property owned or permanently occupied by the subsidy applicant. Rented premises or shared ownership may complicate the claim process.

The [PM Surya Ghar subsidy amounts guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/) breaks down the current slab structure by system size and state-level add-ons that can increase total benefits above ₹1 Lakh for certain configurations.

## Item 6 — Net Metering: From Facility to Line Item

[Net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) is the mechanism by which a grid-connected solar system's excess generation is exported to the DISCOM and credited against the consumer's bill. Without net metering registration, excess solar power is exported for free — an ongoing financial loss that starts on day one of the system's life.

A professional quotation includes net metering facilitation as an explicit deliverable, not an optional add-on. The end-to-end process involves:

- Filing the net metering application with the DISCOM
- Passing the DISCOM's technical inspection (which includes verifying the inverter's anti-islanding compliance)
- Arranging the bidirectional energy meter (some DISCOMs supply this; others require the consumer to procure a BIS-approved unit)
- Obtaining the DISCOM's interconnection agreement
- Activating the net-export credit on the consumer's account

The [net metering India complete guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) details the state-by-state process, typical timelines (4–12 weeks depending on DISCOM), and the checklist of documents required. Ask your installer specifically: does the quotation include all of the above steps, or does it stop at physical installation?

| Net Metering Item | Should Be Included | Often Excluded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DISCOM application filing | ✓ Standard in professional quotes | ✗ In budget quotes |
| Bidirectional meter cost | ✓ Should be itemised | ✗ Often a surprise charge |
| DISCOM inspection support | ✓ Installer should escort | ✗ Often left to the owner |
| Interconnection agreement | ✓ Installer obtains | ✗ Often undefined |
| Net export credit activation | ✓ Confirm with DISCOM | ✗ Frequently incomplete |

If the quotation is silent on net metering, treat that silence as a flag. Either the installer does not provide this service, or the cost is hidden and will surface later.

## Item 7 — DC and AC Cable Specifications

Cable quality is invisible to the eye and irrelevant until it fails. Thermal degradation, moisture ingress, and UV breakdown of undersized or uncertified cables cause resistance losses that subtract 2–5 percent from annual generation — and in failure scenarios, create fire risks.

Verify the following in the quotation's bill of materials:

- **DC cables** — Minimum 4 mm² cross-section for strings under 20 metres, 6 mm² for longer runs. Rated for 1,000 V DC (or 1,500 V DC for higher-voltage string configurations). Must carry IS 7098 BIS mark for solar-specific cables.
- **AC cables** — Rated for the inverter's full rated output current with a 10 percent safety margin. IS 694 BIS mark for AC cables. Should be XLPE-insulated for outdoor or conduit runs in high-temperature environments.
- **MC4 connectors** — Must be UV-resistant, IP67-rated, and compatible with the panel junction box connector type. Mismatched MC4 brands (e.g., Amphenol panels with Stäubli connectors) can cause arcing and connector failure under thermal cycling.
- **Conduit and trunking** — All roof-level cable runs should be in UV-resistant HDPE conduit or galvanised steel trunking. Exposed cables without conduit degrade within three to five monsoon seasons.
- **Cable trays and labels** — Professional installations label every cable with its string number and polarity at both ends. This makes future fault diagnosis and inverter replacement straightforward.

Cheap cables are the easiest place for an installer to reduce their bill of materials cost invisibly. A properly specified 3 kWp system uses approximately 80–120 metres of DC cable and 20–30 metres of AC cable. Downgrading from 4 mm² to 2.5 mm² cable on a 3 kWp system saves the installer roughly ₹1,500 but increases I²R losses by approximately 1.5 percent annually for the system's life. The [Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)](https://www.bis.gov.in/) IS 7098 standard mandates the construction, insulation class, and voltage rating for PV cables used in Indian solar installations.

## Item 8 — MPPT Count and Roof Orientation Compatibility

The [MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) (Maximum Power Point Tracker) is the circuit inside the inverter that continuously adjusts its operating point to extract maximum power from the solar string. Each MPPT input operates independently — strings on different orientations or with different shading profiles must be on separate MPPTs to avoid one string dragging down the other's performance.

The quotation should specify how many MPPT inputs the inverter carries and how the installer plans to assign your roof's strings to those inputs.

- **Single south-facing roof, no shading** — One MPPT is sufficient. A dual-MPPT inverter used here offers no yield benefit but does offer future expansion flexibility.
- **East-west roof split** — Two MPPTs are the minimum. East and west strings peak at different times of day; sharing an MPPT cuts total daily yield by 8–15 percent.
- **Mixed orientations (e.g., south + east + west terrace)** — Three or more MPPTs. Ask the installer to provide a shade analysis report showing the string layout and expected yield impact.
- **Partially shaded rooftop** — Consider whether a multi-MPPT inverter or module-level power electronics (microinverters or DC optimisers) are appropriate. The [string vs microinverter guide](/blog/string-vs-microinverter/) compares the economics of both approaches.

A mismatch between MPPT count and roof orientation is a design error, not a specification preference. If the installer is assigning two differently oriented strings to one MPPT input, ask them to justify the yield impact in writing before accepting. Research published by [NREL on residential PV system performance](https://www.nrel.gov/solar/) confirms that orientation and shading mismatches are among the top five causes of underperformance in systems below 10 kW.

## Item 9 — Monitoring System: Eyes on the Investment

A solar system without monitoring is a black box. Generation data from the DISCOM bill arrives monthly — too slow to catch inverter faults, clipping events, or degraded strings. By the time a monthly bill reveals underperformance, a system may have lost 30–45 days of generation that it will never recover.

Verify the monitoring deliverable in the quotation:

- **Monitoring platform** — App-based, web portal, or WhatsApp-based. WhatsApp monitoring is particularly practical for homeowners who prefer not to manage another app. The [WhatsApp solar monitoring guide](/blog/whatsapp-solar-monitoring/) explains how inverter-native WhatsApp alerts work and what data they surface.
- **Data granularity** — Minimum daily generation totals. Better systems provide 15-minute interval data, allowing precise identification of which hour a fault or shading event occurred.
- **Fault alerts** — Real-time push notifications for inverter faults, grid disconnections, and zero-generation events. Without alerts, a fault discovered on a Saturday may run through the weekend unresolved.
- **Subscription term** — Monitoring subscriptions should cover the full warranty period at no additional charge. If the monitoring subscription expires after 1 year and requires renewal at ₹2,000–₹5,000 per year, factor that cost into the total system TCO.
- **Remote diagnostics** — Whether the inverter brand's service team can remotely read fault logs and performance data without requiring an on-site visit. This capability dramatically reduces diagnostic turnaround time.

The [solar inverter monitoring systems in India guide](/blog/solar-inverter-monitoring-systems-in-india/) covers the full spectrum of monitoring options available for residential systems, including a comparison of app quality across the major inverter brands.

## Item 10 — After-Sales SLA: The Commitment Behind the Warranty

A warranty document and a service-level agreement (SLA) are not the same thing. The warranty defines what is covered; the SLA defines how fast it gets fixed. In Indian residential solar, the SLA is frequently absent from quotations — and its absence is a reliable indicator of whether the installer has a functioning service network.

Ask for the following SLA commitments in writing, as part of the quotation or the accompanying agreement:

- **Ticket acknowledgement** — Within 24 hours of a fault report via phone, WhatsApp, or app.
- **First on-site visit** — Within 48–72 hours for standard faults; within 24 hours for complete system shutdowns affecting the household's power supply.
- **Board-level replacement or inverter swap** — Within 72 hours of fault diagnosis. This is the RMA turnaround time that distinguishes brands with warehouse inventory from those that order parts on demand.
- **Escalation path** — A named escalation contact (regional service manager or national helpline) for cases where the local partner fails to meet the SLA.
- **Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) terms** — Whether the installer offers a post-warranty AMC, what it covers (cleaning, electrical inspection, inverter health check), and the pricing.

Qbits' own service model targets a sub-72-hour RMA resolution through its authorised service partner network — a commitment that is documented and tracked at the brand level, not just promised verbally by the local installer.

**Pros and Cons: Installer SLA vs Brand-Direct SLA**

| | Installer SLA | Brand-Direct SLA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accountability** | ✓ Single point of contact | ✗ May require escalation to brand |
| **Coverage depth** | ✗ Dependent on installer capacity | ✓ Supported by brand's service infrastructure |
| **Tier-2/3 city coverage** | ✗ Often weak in smaller towns | ✓ Better with brands that have wide partner networks |
| **Documentation** | ✗ Frequently verbal only | ✓ Written, tracked by brand |
| **Post-installer-exit risk** | ✗ High if installer folds | ✓ Brand warranty survives installer closure |

For homeowners outside metro areas, the brand's own service network is more reliable than the installer's verbal promise. Ask both questions: what does the brand guarantee, and what does the installer commit to on top of that?

## Item 11 — Payment Milestone Structure

Solar quotations frequently propose a payment schedule that works in the installer's favour — large advance payments before any equipment delivery, with the final milestone at installation completion rather than at net metering approval or system commissioning. This structure leaves the homeowner with no negotiating position after the hardware is bolted to the roof.

A payment schedule that protects the buyer looks like this:

| Milestone | Suggested Payment Tranche |
| --- | --- |
| Agreement signing + equipment order | 30% (advance) |
| Equipment delivery to site (verified) | 30% (on delivery) |
| Physical installation complete | 20% (on completion) |
| System commissioning and testing | 15% (on commissioning) |
| Net metering approval received | 5% (final) |

The 5% held back until net metering approval is a small amount but a powerful incentive. Installers who exclude net metering from their scope suddenly find a reason to include it when the final tranche depends on it. The commissioning milestone should be accompanied by a formal handover packet (see Item 12 below).

Reject any quotation that requests more than 50 percent before equipment delivery or that collapses the commissioning and net metering milestones into a single "installation complete" payment.

## Item 12 — Commissioning Documentation Handover

The final line item in the 12-Point Quote Audit Framework is often the most telling indicator of an installer's professionalism: the commissioning documentation handover. A properly commissioned residential solar system comes with a defined set of documents that the buyer needs to (a) register the system's warranty, (b) file the net metering application, and (c) troubleshoot or service the system in future years.

The commissioning packet should include, at minimum:

- **Single-line diagram (SLD)** — The electrical layout of the system, showing panel strings, combiner box (if present), DC isolator, inverter, AC isolator, and grid connection point. Essential for any future service work.
- **String test report** — Open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current of each string, measured at commissioning and compared against the calculated values from the panel specifications. Deviations indicate connection faults.
- **Inverter commissioning report** — Date of first energisation, firmware version, MPPT configuration, and initial generation reading.
- **Warranty certificates** — Separate documents for the inverter (from the inverter brand) and for the panels (from the panel manufacturer). Both must be in the buyer's name, not the installer's.
- **DISCOM interconnection agreement** — The signed agreement between the consumer and the DISCOM permitting net export.
- **DISCOM consumer number and net meter serial number** — Needed for future billing queries and generation disputes.

Installers who cannot or will not provide the SLD and string test report have almost certainly not conducted proper commissioning tests. A system that goes live without a string test may have a miswired string or a faulty panel junction box that reduces output by 10–30 percent from day one — and that defect will not be identified until a detailed audit years later.

## Common Mistakes Homeowners Make With Solar Quotations

The 12-Point Quote Audit Framework catches specification errors, but there are several procedural mistakes that cost homeowners equally:

- **Accepting verbal commitments** — Warranty duration, ALMM status, and SLA response times are meaningless unless they appear in the signed quotation or agreement. Installers who resist putting commitments in writing are signalling that they do not intend to honour them.
- **Comparing only the headline price per kW** — A ₹55,000/kW quotation with a 12-year inverter warranty, Tier-1 panels, and full net metering support may be cheaper over 10 years than a ₹48,000/kW quote with 5-year warranty and excluded net metering.
- **Ignoring the inverter brand in favour of the panel brand** — Many homeowners recognise panel brands (Adani, Waaree, Tata) but are unfamiliar with inverter brands. The [how to choose a solar inverter for home](/blog/how-to-choose-solar-inverter-for-home-india/) guide explains why inverter selection is at least as important as panel selection for long-term system performance.
- **Skipping the site survey step** — A quotation prepared without a physical site survey (or at minimum a satellite roof measurement) cannot accurately specify the number of panels, cable lengths, or structure type. Always ask whether the installer visited or measured the site before sending numbers.
- **Not verifying the installer's track record** — Ask for a list of completed residential installations in your city or district from the past 12 months. A legitimate installer will provide references. Check whether those installations have active net metering — it is a reliable proxy for full-service delivery.
