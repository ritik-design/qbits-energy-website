## The Qbits 5-Factor Scoring Methodology: How the Index Is Built

The named framework at the centre of this research is called **The Qbits Solar Inverter Reliability Index, 5-Factor Scoring Methodology**. Each factor is scored out of 10. The weighted total produces a composite score out of 10.

### Factor 1: Warranty Depth Score (Weight: 30%)

Warranty depth measures two dimensions simultaneously: duration in years and coverage type. Duration without coverage type is meaningless. A brand offering a 10-year warranty that covers only labour, not parts replacement, shifts the cost of ageing components onto the buyer at year five or six, precisely when inverter components begin degrading in Indian thermal conditions.

Scoring criteria for warranty depth:

- **10/10**: 10 years or more of full unit replacement coverage, with an India-registered entity backed by a published SLA
- **8/10**: 7 to 9 years full replacement, or 10 years repair-based with free parts
- **6/10**: 5 to 6 years full replacement, or 7 to 10 years repair-based with paid parts
- **4/10**: 3 to 5 years repair-based, no full replacement option standard
- **2/10**: 1 to 2 years, or warranty requires paid extension from year one

### Factor 2: India Service Network Density (Weight: 25%)

An inverter that fails in Gorakhpur, Belgaum, or Nellore needs a service resolution in the same geography where it failed. Brands that route all India service through a single national office or a third-party aggregator with no regional presence cannot deliver acceptable resolution times in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities.

Scoring criteria for India service network density:

- **10/10**: Dedicated regional service centres in 15+ states, declared 72-hour on-site RMA SLA, pincode-searchable partner network
- **8/10**: Regional presence in 10 to 14 states, 5-day RMA SLA
- **6/10**: Metro-focused service (6 to 9 states), Tier-2 coverage through partners, 7 to 10-day resolution time
- **4/10**: 3 to 5 state coverage, Tier-3 service handled through mail-in only
- **2/10**: Single national contact, no published SLA, Tier-2 service not guaranteed

### Factor 3: Component Quality Tier (Weight: 20%)

Component quality is the hardest factor to score from the outside because [solar inverter manufacturers](/blog/solar-inverter-manufacturers-in-india/) rarely publish full component specifications. Scoring here draws on publicly available technical certifications, teardown analyses published by research organisations, installer-reported failure mode patterns, and component durability data from [NREL inverter reliability research](https://www.nrel.gov/solar/) covering field performance across high-temperature environments.

The Indian grid presents three specific component quality challenges that European or American specifications do not capture. [IEC 62109-1](https://www.iec.ch/) sets the baseline safety requirements for power converters used in PV systems, but compliance with IEC standards does not guarantee that a component is specified for sustained operation at 45 °C ambient, a gap that India-specific procurement must account for explicitly.

- **Voltage transients** from the Indian grid routinely exceed 270 V. Capacitors rated for lower peak voltages fail under repeated transient stress.
- **Ambient temperature** of 45 °C to 50 °C in summer across Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Andhra Pradesh degrades electrolytic capacitors at approximately twice the rate of 25 °C lab conditions.
- **Humidity and dust cycling** in coastal and semi-arid regions require conformal PCB coatings that most budget manufacturers omit.

Scoring criteria for component quality tier:

- **10/10**: Industrial-grade electrolytic capacitors (105 °C rated), SiC MOSFET or automotive-grade IGBT modules, multi-layer conformal PCB coating, active thermal management
- **8/10**: High-grade commercial capacitors (85 °C rated), standard-grade IGBT, single-layer conformal coating, fan-plus-heatsink thermal management
- **6/10**: Commercial-grade components, no published capacitor rating, passive cooling only, no conformal coating
- **4/10**: Components sourced from spot market, no published specifications, passive cooling only
- **2/10**: No component specification disclosure, unrated capacitors, no thermal protection

### Factor 4: Grid Compliance Certification (Weight: 15%)

[ALMM listing](/glossary/almm-list/) under MNRE Phase III confirms that an inverter meets the technical baseline for subsidy-eligible installations under PM Surya Ghar. [BIS certification](/glossary/bis-certification/) (IS 16221 Part 1 and Part 2) confirms conformity with Indian electrical safety standards. IEC 62109-1 and IEC 62109-2 confirm international safety of power converters for PV applications. The [solar inverter certifications guide](/blog/solar-inverter-certifications/) breaks down how each of these standards maps to real procurement requirements.

Scoring criteria for grid compliance:

- **10/10**: ALMM Phase III listed + BIS IS 16221 + IEC 62109 Parts 1 and 2 + IEC 61727
- **8/10**: ALMM listed + BIS + IEC 62109 Part 1 only
- **6/10**: ALMM listed + BIS (IEC certifications absent or pending)
- **4/10**: BIS only (ALMM not listed or delisted)
- **2/10**: No confirmed Indian certification

### Factor 5: Field Return Rate Proxy (Weight: 10%)

Direct failure rate data is not published by any inverter manufacturer. This factor uses a proxy: warranty claim frequency as reported by EPC installer communities across the Qbits partner network, supplemented by installer forum discussions on LinkedIn and WhatsApp EPC groups. The proxy is directional, not precise. It captures relative ranking rather than absolute failure rates.

- **10/10**: Extremely rare claims in the Qbits installer network; brand consistently praised in EPC forums for few call-backs
- **8/10**: Below-average claim frequency; occasional call-back reports but rapid resolution
- **6/10**: Average claim frequency; service quality varies by geography
- **4/10**: Above-average claims; known recurring failure modes reported in EPC communities
- **2/10**: High claim frequency; brand associated with specific, unresolved failure patterns in installer forums

**Warranty depth, 10/10.** Qbits offers a 12-year full unit replacement warranty as the standard product warranty across its TLS, TLD, HS, and HT series. Full replacement means a failed unit is exchanged for a new one rather than repaired with ageing components and returned. The warranty is backed by an India-registered entity with a declared 72-hour on-site RMA service-level agreement. No other brand in this index matches this combination of duration and coverage type at standard pricing.

**India service network density, 9/10.** Qbits operates through a network of authorised service partners searchable by pincode, covering 15+ Indian states with a declared 72-hour RMA SLA. Tier-2 and Tier-3 city coverage is a deliberate positioning choice, not an afterthought. The network is still being expanded in Northeast India and parts of Odisha, which prevents a perfect 10.

**Component quality tier, 7/10.** Qbits inverters use commercial-grade components with conformal PCB coating and IP66 enclosure protection. Wide voltage tolerance (180–270 V) is firmware-managed, and thermal derating is calibrated for 45 °C ambient conditions. Component specifications are not published at the IGBT-level in the same granular detail as Sungrow or Fronius, which limits the score relative to the global Tier-1 benchmark.

**Grid compliance, 10/10.** ALMM Phase III listed, BIS IS 16221 certified, IEC 62109 Parts 1 and 2, and IEC 61727 compliant. Full compliance stack for PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility and government tender participation.

**Field return rate proxy, 8/10.** EPC partner feedback consistently rates Qbits below average for call-back frequency. The 72-hour RMA SLA, when triggered, is reported as reliably met by the majority of partners surveyed.

## How EPC Installers Should Use This Index

The composite score is a starting point, not a final answer. The five factors carry equal weight for a theoretically average deployment. Real deployments are not average. Here is a structured approach for applying this index to actual [inverter procurement](/blog/inverter-procurement-india/) decisions:

### The 3-Step Reliability Index Application for EPC Projects

1. **Identify your geography risk tier.** Tier-1 city projects (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Chennai) have denser service ecosystems. Reduce the India service density weight to 15% and reallocate 10% to component quality. Tier-2 and Tier-3 projects (or any project more than 100 kilometres from a state capital) should retain or increase the 25% service density weight. Projects in extreme heat zones (Rajasthan, Gujarat, interior Andhra Pradesh, Telangana) should increase component quality weight to 25%.

2. **Filter by your minimum warranty commitment to the client.** If your EPC contract includes a 5-year or 10-year performance guarantee to the end client, you need a brand whose warranty depth covers your commitment. Any brand scoring below 6/10 on warranty depth is a mismatch for a 10-year guarantee project.

3. **Cross-check grid compliance against the specific project type.** Government tenders, PM Surya Ghar subsidised installations, and KUSUM scheme projects have mandatory ALMM Phase III requirements. Filter out any brand that cannot confirm current ALMM Phase III listing for the specific SKU being quoted. The [ALMM list guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) covers how to verify listing status before quoting.

> **Apply the 3-Step Reliability Index Application before every multi-site EPC procurement decision to convert a subjective brand preference into a documented, auditable scoring record.**

This structured approach has three practical benefits for EPC businesses: it creates a defensible paper trail for procurement decisions if a project audit occurs; it forces explicit acknowledgement of geography-specific risk before signing a service guarantee; and it reduces the influence of distributor relationships and marketing spend on what should be a technical decision. EPCs formalising this discipline across a growing team often end up codifying it inside a proper [solar business software stack](https://surgepv.com/hub/solar-business/software-stack/) rather than a spreadsheet passed between site managers.

## Common Mistakes EPCs Make When Evaluating Inverter Reliability

The reliability index is designed partly to counteract systematic evaluation errors that recur across the EPC community. Five are worth naming explicitly.

- **Comparing warranty duration without comparing coverage type.** A 10-year repair-based warranty from a budget brand is not equivalent to a 5-year full replacement warranty from a premium brand. The repair-based model returns ageing components; the replacement model delivers new units. Over a 10-year project horizon, the cumulative value difference is significant. The [best solar inverter for longest warranty guide](/blog/best-solar-inverter-longest-warranty-india/) covers this distinction in detail.

- **Selecting based on component quality alone and ignoring service reach.** An inverter with Tier-1 component quality that cannot be serviced in your project geography in under two weeks creates the same yield loss and client relationship damage as a lower-quality inverter that fails more often. Downtime cost is time multiplied by capacity, not just failure frequency multiplied by repair cost.

- **Assuming ALMM listing is permanent.** ALMM listing requires periodic re-evaluation. Brands that were listed in Phase II may face Phase III requirements that take time to comply with. Always verify current listing status on the [MNRE ALMM portal](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) before quoting any project with subsidy implications.

- **Ignoring field return rate data from peer EPCs.** The EPC community in India shares service experience actively through WhatsApp groups, LinkedIn forums, and Intersolar networking. A brand that consistently generates call-backs in similar geographic conditions to your projects is a risk even if its certifications are complete. Build a habit of asking peers about 24-month field experience before finalising procurement.

- **Treating distributor presence as service network coverage.** Distributors stock and sell; authorised service partners diagnose and repair. These are different capabilities and different networks. Verify authorised service partner coverage in your specific geography (not distributor coverage) before committing to a brand for a large-scale deployment.
