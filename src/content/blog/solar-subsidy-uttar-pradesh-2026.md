## What Is the Solar Subsidy in UP - Central and State Schemes Explained

Uttar Pradesh homeowners in 2026 can access two distinct solar subsidy programmes simultaneously. Understanding both from the outset prevents the most common mistake: completing only one application and missing the other.

**The Central Scheme - PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana**

Launched in February 2024 by the Government of India and administered through [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), PM Surya Ghar is the largest residential rooftop solar subsidy in Indian history. It is available to any residential electricity consumer in India - including in UP - whose property is grid-connected and whose proposed solar system uses ALMM-listed equipment installed by a DISCOM-empanelled vendor. The subsidy is disbursed as a direct bank transfer after the DISCOM completes commissioning inspection. For a comprehensive national overview of the scheme including eligibility, documentation, and the five-step application process, the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) is the authoritative reference.

**The State Scheme - UPNEDA Rooftop Solar Programme**

[UPNEDA](https://upneda.org.in/) (UP New and Renewable Energy Development Agency) is the state nodal agency for renewable energy in Uttar Pradesh, operating under the UP government's energy department. UPNEDA administers the state-level incentive component on top of the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy. The exact per-kW state top-up amount is updated annually; verify current-year rates on the UPNEDA portal or at your district UPNEDA office, as amounts can change mid-year and vary by consumer category.

The two schemes run through slightly different channels - national portal for the central subsidy, UPNEDA/DISCOM channel for the state component - but a competent empanelled installer handles the coordination. Your job is to confirm both applications have been filed and both portal statuses are being tracked independently.

## UP's Four DISCOMs - Who You Apply To

Uttar Pradesh is served by four distribution companies, each with distinct geographic jurisdiction. Your electricity bill identifies your DISCOM, and that DISCOM owns your subsidy application, net metering approval, and smart meter installation. Processing culture, empanelled vendor lists, and timelines differ across the four.

Understanding your [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) before beginning the application process prevents the common mistake of filing with the wrong entity.

### PVVNL - Pashchimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited

**Coverage:** Meerut, Agra, Mathura, Aligarh, Saharanpur, Muzaffarnagar, Bareilly, and western UP districts

PVVNL serves the densely populated western UP region, including the economically active Meerut-Agra belt. This zone sees significant rooftop solar demand due to high residential consumption and proximity to Delhi's solar awareness culture. Agra, with its high solar irradiance (5.0–5.3 peak sun hours annually), is among the most financially attractive PVVNL locations for rooftop solar. Processing timelines for PM Surya Ghar under PVVNL average 60 to 75 days in urban centres, extending to 90 days in smaller western UP towns.

- **Key PVVNL note:** The Agra and Meerut divisions have dedicated solar processing desks due to application volume
- **Documentation:** Property ownership via sale deed or property tax receipt; rural applicants may need khasra-khatauni land records

### DVVNL - Dakshinanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited

**Coverage:** Allahabad (Prayagraj), Jhansi, Mirzapur, Chitrakoot, Banda, and southern UP districts

DVVNL covers the historically underserved southern UP belt, where grid reliability is among the weakest in the state. Prayagraj city has better infrastructure than rural DVVNL areas, where 6–8 hours of daily outages are common in summer. This grid context makes hybrid systems with battery backup the rational default for DVVNL applicants rather than the exception. Net metering timelines under DVVNL average 75 to 100 days, partly due to infrastructure for bidirectional meter installation in rural feeders.

- **Hybrid preference:** DVVNL rural consumers should strongly favour hybrid inverters; the grid cannot be relied upon during peak solar generation hours
- **Prayagraj city applicants:** Urban DVVNL zones have better processing infrastructure; expect 55 to 70 days

### MVVNL - Madhyanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited

**Coverage:** Lucknow, Kanpur, Unnao, Sitapur, Hardoi, Rae Bareli, Lakhimpur Kheri, and central UP districts

MVVNL is UP's largest DISCOM by consumer count and handles the state capital Lucknow and the industrial hub Kanpur. This concentration of urban consumers has made MVVNL's rooftop solar processing more institutionally mature than other UP DISCOMs. Lucknow applicants can expect 50 to 70 days for commissioning. Kanpur, with its heavy commercial and industrial load on the grid, can see urban residential applications processed in 45 to 65 days. The Lucknow smart city initiative has accelerated smart meter rollout in the district, marginally reducing net metering installation timelines.

- **Lucknow advantage:** Highest concentration of UPNEDA-registered empanelled vendors in the state; best vendor selection
- **Kanpur note:** Industrial grid load means voltage fluctuation is common - inverters with wide voltage tolerance (180–270 V) are important

### PUVVNL - Purvanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited

**Coverage:** Varanasi, Gorakhpur, Azamgarh, Mau, Ghazipur, Deoria, Ballia, Jaunpur, and eastern UP districts

PUVVNL serves eastern UP, which has some of the state's weakest grid infrastructure and longest average outage durations - 5 to 8 hours per day in many districts. This makes hybrid systems with battery storage not a premium option but an operational necessity for homeowners who want usable electricity during daylight outage hours. Varanasi city has better urban grid quality than surrounding districts. Processing timelines for PM Surya Ghar under PUVVNL are among the longest in the state - plan for 80 to 120 days in semi-urban and rural areas.

- **Critical PUVVNL consideration:** Do not install an on-grid-only system in rural PUVVNL areas; frequent outages render on-grid systems unproductive for large portions of the day
- **Varanasi city:** More manageable timelines of 60 to 80 days; larger installer network available

## Net Metering in UP - UPERC Rates, Process, and What You Actually Earn

Once your rooftop solar system is commissioned in UP, the financial relationship with your DISCOM is governed by UPERC (UP Electricity Regulatory Commission) [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) regulations. UP has a functioning net metering framework, though the process timelines are longer than in Gujarat or Maharashtra and the buyback rates are lower than the import tariff - which has direct implications for how you should size and operate your system.

**How UP net metering works:**

- Your existing meter is replaced with a bidirectional smart meter, supplied by the DISCOM under the PM Surya Ghar scheme at no cost to you
- Surplus solar generation beyond your instantaneous consumption is exported to the grid
- The UPERC-set [feed-in tariff](/glossary/feed-in-tariff/) applies to exported units
- Surplus units are credited to your account and can offset future bills
- At the end of the annual settlement period, remaining credited units are settled in cash at the prevailing buyback rate

## On-Grid vs Hybrid Inverter in UP - The Honest Assessment

The on-grid versus hybrid inverter decision is more consequential in Uttar Pradesh than in any other major Indian state. UP's grid reality - 4 to 8 hours of daily outages across large rural and semi-urban areas - fundamentally changes the economics and practical utility of each system type. This section covers the decision honestly, without defaulting to either option as universally correct.

**Why on-grid systems underperform in UP:**

An [on-grid inverter](/glossary/on-grid-inverter/) shuts down automatically when the grid goes offline. This is a regulatory safety requirement - the anti-islanding function prevents back-feeding electricity into a line that grid workers may be servicing. In a state like Gujarat with 1–2 hours of annual outage, this is an inconvenience. In rural PUVVNL or DVVNL areas with 5–8 hours of daily summer outages, this means your solar panels are producing power during peak sun hours but your inverter is silenced - you receive no benefit from your investment during those hours.

**Why hybrid systems make sense in UP despite higher cost:**

A [hybrid inverter](/glossary/hybrid-inverter/) with a battery bank operates independently of the grid. During a grid outage, the system isolates from the grid (the safety requirement is met), continues drawing from solar panels, charges the battery, and powers your home. When the grid returns, it reconnects and any surplus generation is exported for net metering credit. In UP's outage environment, a hybrid system can deliver 90–95% of its theoretical annual generation to your home, while an on-grid system might deliver 60–70% of theoretical output in a rural PUVVNL location with frequent daytime cuts.

## How to Apply for UP Solar Subsidy - Step-by-Step Process

The UP solar subsidy application involves two parallel tracks - the national PM Surya Ghar portal and the UPNEDA/DISCOM state scheme - but an experienced empanelled installer handles the majority of coordination. Understanding each step allows you to verify progress rather than discovering a procedural gap weeks after installation.

**Before you apply - prerequisites:**

- **Identify your DISCOM** - Check your electricity bill for DISCOM name (PVVNL/DVVNL/MVVNL/PUVVNL)
- **Check your sanctioned load** - A 3 kW solar system requires a minimum 5 kVA sanctioned load; hybrid with battery may need 7.5 kVA; apply for load enhancement in parallel if required
- **Confirm property ownership** - Sale deed, property tax receipt, or khasra-khatauni (for rural UP land); apartment owners need RWA no-objection certificate
- **Gather documents** - Electricity bill, Aadhaar, PAN, bank passbook, property proof, roof photographs

**The six-step UP application process:**

1. **Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in** with your consumer number, Aadhaar, and mobile number. Complete e-KYC. Select "Uttar Pradesh" and your DISCOM. This creates your national scheme application and generates an application reference number.

2. **Register simultaneously on the UPNEDA portal** at [upneda.org.in](https://upneda.org.in/) for the state incentive component. The two registrations can and should run in parallel from day one - do not wait for central scheme approval before filing the state application.

3. **Obtain DISCOM technical feasibility approval.** Your DISCOM reviews your connection details and confirms grid capacity at your meter point. MNRE mandates a 15-day response window; UP DISCOMs typically respond in 10–25 days in urban areas and up to 35 days in rural zones.

4. **Select a UPNEDA-registered and DISCOM-empanelled vendor.** Your installer must appear on both the national PM Surya Ghar vendor list and the UPNEDA state vendor registry. Verify on the respective portals - not on vendor marketing materials. Confirm the specific inverter model appears on the [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/); a non-ALMM-listed inverter means the entire subsidy claim is rejected at the verification stage.

5. **Install, commission, and notify your DISCOM for inspection.** The DISCOM engineer visits to verify ALMM equipment, earthing, safety compliance, and arrange bidirectional smart meter installation. Allow 15–30 days from notification to inspection in UP, depending on DISCOM and urban/rural location.

6. **Upload commissioning documents to both portals.** Upload the DISCOM commissioning certificate, vendor invoice, system photographs, and bank details to pmsuryaghar.gov.in for the central subsidy. Do the same on the UPNEDA portal for the state component. Both disbursements are direct bank transfers - expect 45–90 days for each from document upload.

## Common Mistakes That Delay or Deny UP Solar Subsidy

UP's subsidy process has improved significantly with PM Surya Ghar, but procedural errors remain the leading cause of application delays and rejections. These are the mistakes reported most frequently by UP DISCOM officers and UPNEDA administrators.

- **Installing before DISCOM feasibility approval** - All four UP DISCOMs require written feasibility approval before physical installation begins. Installing first and applying after is grounds for commissioning refusal; the DISCOM will not inspect an installation that pre-dates the approval.
- **Not verifying ALMM listing before installation** - Verify the exact inverter model number against the MNRE ALMM list before equipment is delivered to site. Verbal confirmation from the vendor is not sufficient. A non-ALMM inverter means the full subsidy - central and state - is rejected regardless of all other documentation being correct.
- **Choosing a vendor on only one empanelment list** - To claim both the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy and the UPNEDA state top-up, your vendor must appear on both portals. Some vendors are listed on one but not the other. Ask to see current portal screenshots of both registrations.
- **Bank account details not matching Aadhaar** - The subsidy disbursement bank account must exactly match the account linked to the Aadhaar used for e-KYC. Name discrepancies - even initials vs full name - cause transfer rejections and add 30–60 days to disbursement timelines.
- **Missing the UPNEDA state application** - The central PM Surya Ghar application does not automatically register you for the UPNEDA state incentive. Many homeowners complete the national portal registration and miss the state application entirely. The state top-up cannot be claimed retrospectively after commissioning.
- **Insufficient sanctioned load for the proposed system size** - A 3 kW hybrid system on a connection with 3 kVA sanctioned load will fail feasibility. Apply for load enhancement at the same time as the solar application; UP DISCOMs process both concurrently.
- **Not tracking both portal statuses weekly** - Each portal has an independent status tracker. Both portals occasionally request re-upload of documents without sending an active notification. Checking weekly prevents silent stalling.

## UP-Specific Details That Change the Solar Maths

Several features of Uttar Pradesh's solar and grid environment are either not prominently covered on subsidy portals or require local knowledge that first-time applicants miss. These are worth understanding before finalising your system design.

**The generator displacement calculation is a first-class financial input.** In most states, financial models for rooftop solar focus on electricity bill reduction and net metering export credits. In UP, the generator operating cost is often as large as the electricity bill itself. A household running a 2 kVA generator for 5 hours per day at ₹95 per litre and 0.7 litres per hour is spending approximately ₹99,000 per year on diesel. A properly sized hybrid solar system with a 5–10 kWh battery bank eliminates 70–90% of this expenditure. Including this in your financial model is not optional - it is the dominant variable in many UP households.

**Voltage fluctuation in UP requires inverter tolerance.** UP's grid is notorious not just for outages but for persistent voltage fluctuation - it is common to see grid voltage swing from 180 V to 260 V across the day, particularly on rural feeders and in older urban substations. A standard inverter specified for 200–240 V input will trip frequently under these conditions, reducing effective generation even during grid-available hours. Inverters with a wide input voltage range of 180–270 V are not a premium feature in UP - they are a practical necessity.

**Peak sun hours in UP vary significantly by region.** Northern UP districts near the Himalayan foothills (Saharanpur, Lakhimpur Kheri, Pilibhit) average 4.5–4.8 peak sun hours annually. Central UP around Lucknow and Kanpur averages 4.8–5.1 peak sun hours. Southern UP districts like Jhansi and Banda - which receive drier, clearer skies - average 5.1–5.5 peak sun hours. A 3 kW system in Jhansi (DVVNL) will generate approximately 12–15% more electricity annually than the same system in Saharanpur (PVVNL). Confirm local irradiance data from your installer before accepting a quoted system size.

**The UPERC banking year matters for cash flow.** UP's net metering settlement typically resets annually. Systems commissioned between October and March - when solar generation is lower - begin banking in a seasonally weak period before the next settlement. Commissioning between April and September maximises the banking window and first-year cash flow.

**Fixed charges persist regardless of solar output.** UP DISCOMs levy a minimum monthly fixed charge on every residential connection regardless of consumption. For a standard LMV-1 residential connection in Lucknow, this runs ₹50–₹120 per month depending on sanctioned load. A "zero bill" expectation is not achievable - the minimum bill is the fixed charge component. This is worth explaining to family members who are expecting to eliminate the electricity bill entirely.
