Most proposal tools handle both as flat assumptions, which produces quotes that are wrong for nearly every household and wrong in a direction nobody can predict. ## The Grant Taper Changes Where The Marginal Kilowatt Stops Paying

The SEAI structure is worth spelling out in full, because the shape is unusual.

| System size | Grant | Grant per kWp | Marginal grant on the last kWp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kWp | EUR 700 | EUR 700 | EUR 700 |
| 2 kWp | EUR 1,400 | EUR 700 | EUR 700 |
| 3 kWp | EUR 1,600 | EUR 533 | EUR 200 |
| 4 kWp | EUR 1,800 | EUR 450 | EUR 200 |
| 6 kWp | EUR 1,800 | EUR 300 | Nil |

The right-hand column is the one that drives the recommendation. Going from 2 kWp to 3 kWp draws EUR 200 of support against a full kilowatt of cost. Going from 4 kWp to 6 kWp draws nothing.

> **What most Irish quotes get wrong.** They present the grant as a percentage discount on the total. On a 6 kWp system the EUR 1,800 maximum is a much smaller proportion of cost than on a 3 kWp system, so a percentage framing flatters the small system and understates how much of a larger one the customer is funding themselves.

This does not mean small systems are the right answer. Above the grant ceiling the marginal kilowatt has to justify itself on energy value alone, and on an Irish roof with good orientation it frequently still does, particularly for a household with high daytime consumption or an electric vehicle. The point is that the argument changes at 4 kWp, and the proposal should make that visible rather than hiding it inside an averaged discount.

## Supplier Export Rates Are The Largest Unmodelled Variable

Clean Export Guarantee rates are set by suppliers, and the standard range spans roughly 15.2 to 32.0 cents per kWh. Pinergy at 25.0 cents sits near the high end of standard offerings.

A spread of more than two to one on export value is not a detail. On a system exporting several thousand kilowatt-hours a year, moving between the ends of that range changes annual revenue by hundreds of euro and shifts payback by years.

Three consequences for the document:

**Ask which supplier the customer is with, before quoting.** It is a one-line question that determines a headline number. Quoting a national average guarantees being wrong for almost everyone.

**Say what rate you assumed.** If the customer later switches supplier the payback changes, and a proposal that named its assumption survives that conversation. One that buried an average does not.

**Treat switching as a recommendation, not an aside.** For a household on a low export rate, moving supplier can be worth more than any hardware upgrade on the table. Saying so is unusual, credible, and costs nothing.

## The EUR 400 Exemption And Its Quiet Sizing Effect

The first EUR 400 of annual export income is exempt from income tax through the end of 2028. For a typical residential system that covers a good share of export earnings, and it is worth stating plainly because customers frequently assume export income is either fully taxed or not taxed at all.

There is a second-order effect worth understanding even if it does not always belong in the customer document. Export income above EUR 400 is taxable, so the after-tax value of the marginal exported kilowatt-hour falls once a system crosses that threshold. Combined with the grant ceiling at 4 kWp, Irish residential economics have two separate points where the marginal kilowatt gets less attractive. Neither is a reason to undersize, but both belong in the model.

## Connection Pathway Decides The Programme

NC6 and NC7 are not two versions of the same form.

| | NC6 | NC7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Notification | Application |
| Single-phase | Below 6 kW at 25A | 6 to 50 kW |
| Three-phase | Below 11 kW at 16A per phase | 11 to 50 kW |
| Timing | File at least 20 working days before installation | Approval required, not automatic |
| Risk to programme | Low | Real |

The threshold matters commercially because a proposal that promises an installation date has implicitly promised a connection pathway. Below the NC6 limits that is a reasonable promise. Above it, approval is not guaranteed and the timeline is not yours to set. A proposal for a 7 kW single-phase system that quotes the same lead time as a 4 kW one is making a commitment it cannot underwrite.

## What Else Belongs In The Document

**Planning status, checked rather than assumed.** S.I. 493/2022 exempts most rooftop installations within limits, including 15 cm maximum projection on pitched roofs and 50 cm on flat, and ground-mount to 25 square metres. Protected Structures and Architectural Conservation Areas need full permission, and that changes both cost and programme materially.

**EN 50549 and RECI registration.** Naming the grid protection standard and confirming the contractor is RECI-registered addresses the two questions a technically literate Irish customer asks.

**A realistic diffuse-corrected yield.** Irish generation modelling that uses direct-beam shading assumptions overstates shading losses and understates output. Our [solar shading analysis software Ireland](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software-ireland/) guide covers why.
