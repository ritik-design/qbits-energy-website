## What is a solar mounting structure

A solar mounting structure is the mechanical framework that supports PV modules at the designed tilt angle and azimuth, transferring wind uplift, snow and gravity loads from the modules to the building roof or the ground. The structure also provides the bonding path for module frames to earth.

Indian solar mounting structures are most commonly fabricated from hot-dip galvanised iron (GI) with zinc coating between 80 and 300 g/m² depending on environment. Aluminium structures appear in premium rooftop installations where weight matters. Stainless steel is used in highly corrosive coastal environments.

Mounting design must account for site-specific wind loads (per IS 875 Part 3), snow loads (relevant for Kashmir, Himachal, Uttarakhand), seismic considerations and corrosion environment. Properly designed mounting lasts 20 to 25 years with minimal maintenance.

## Why mounting structure matters

Structural integrity. The mounting bears 25 years of weather including cyclones, dust storms and heat.

Tilt and orientation. Mounting sets the geometric angles that drive yield.

Earthing path. Structure provides bonding for module frames.

Maintenance access. Walkways and serviceability are designed into the mounting.

Project economics. Mounting accounts for 8 to 15 percent of solar plant capex.

Regulatory compliance. CEIG approval and CEA safety regulations apply.

## Common mounting problems

Corrosion in coastal areas with light galvanising.

Loose hardware after thermal cycling.

Roof leakage from improper waterproofing of penetrations.

Inadequate wind load design failing in cyclones.

Ballast moving on flat roofs.

Bird nesting under modules.

Earthing continuity lost over years.

## Best practices

Specify galvanising mass per environment (IS 2062 grade and IS 4759 specifications).

Use IS 875 for wind load calculations.

Engineer-sealed structural calculations for roof load.

Stainless steel hardware (not plain steel) for fasteners.

Anti-theft hardware in unattended sites.

Annual visual inspection and torque check.

Document mounting structure on the SLD.

## Common mistakes

Light galvanising in coastal locations.

Using plain steel hardware instead of stainless.

Inadequate roof waterproofing at penetration points.

Skipping structural engineering for old or sheet metal roofs.

Ballast undersized for cyclone wind loads.

Missing earthing bond on every metallic element.

## Key takeaways

Mounting structures hold solar modules at design tilt and orientation, transferring loads to the supporting building or ground. Indian mounting uses hot-dip GI with zinc coating sized for local environment. Wind load design per IS 875, structural compliance per IS 800, and earthing continuity are the key design criteria. Trackers add yield but increase capex and O&M. Mounting accounts for 8 to 15 percent of total solar capex.
