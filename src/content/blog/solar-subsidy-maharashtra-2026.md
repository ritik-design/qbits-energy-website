## Solar Subsidy in Maharashtra: One Central Scheme, One Narrow State Scheme

Understanding which scheme applies to your household is the starting point for any application.

The **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** is the central government scheme administered by MNRE through the [PM Surya Ghar national portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/). It provides ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kilowatt, capping at ₹78,000 for a 3 kW or larger residential system. We could not open the national portal from our checking environment (it returned a 403 error), so the slabs were cross-checked against MSEDCL's own i-SMART page, which states the same amounts.

> [!FORMULA]
> Central subsidy = (₹30,000 × first 2 kW) + (₹18,000 × third kW), capped at ₹78,000 from 3 kW upward

The subsidy is credited to the applicant's Aadhaar-linked bank account after the DISCOM completes commissioning.

The **SMART scheme** (Swayampurna Maharashtra Residential Rooftop) is the state-backed layer. It was approved in October 2025 and is implemented by MSEDCL. As reported at launch, it targets households using under 100 units a month: BPL households get a 35% state share, SC/ST households 30% and other economically weaker households 20% of installation cost, funded with ₹330 crore for FY 2025-26 and ₹325 crore for FY 2026-27, first come first served, and expected to run to March 2027. MSEDCL's i-SMART page lists "PMSGMBY + SMART Solar Scheme" among its connection streams but does not publish the terms, so confirm eligibility and remaining funds there. A household using 300 units a month does not qualify.

The **Maharashtra Energy Development Agency (MEDA)** is the state nodal agency for renewable energy. It coordinates PM Surya Ghar at state level, and its site is [mahaurja.com](https://www.mahaurja.com/meda/en). Some online pages describe a separate MEDA per-kW incentive that stacks on the central amount. We could not find an official source for that, so this guide does not count on it.

## How the Benefits Stack in Maharashtra

Every Maharashtra homeowner who installs rooftop solar in 2026 taps into two layers, and some low-use households into a third.

### How the Benefits Stack

1. **Central Subsidy (Layer 1)**: PM Surya Ghar provides up to ₹78,000 directly to your bank account after DISCOM commissioning. This is a one-time capital reduction. On an assumed 3 kW system costing ₹1,50,000, it cuts your net investment to ₹72,000.

2. **Net Metering Savings (Layer 2)**: Under MERC's rooftop regulations, units your system exports are netted against units you import. Because Maharashtra's upper slabs are expensive, every unit you avoid buying in those slabs is worth more than a unit in the lowest slab. This is a recurring monthly bill reduction, not a one-off payment.

3. **SMART Scheme (Layer 3, eligible low-use homes only)**: A state share for households under 100 units a month who meet the category conditions. It is not a general top-up.

Layers 1 and 3 lower the payback by reducing the capital you need, which means Layer 2 starts producing net savings earlier. Homeowners who want to [model these savings](https://surgepv.com/generation-financial-tool/) themselves before committing to a system size can do so with a dedicated financial modelling tool.

## Last Date and Scheme Validity

MNRE's guidelines run PM Surya Ghar through FY 2026-27, and the national portal shows the scheme closing on 31 March 2027. As of mid-October 2026, reports say the Centre is considering an extension and a successor phase, but nothing has been notified, so treat 31 March 2027 as the working deadline. The subsidy is released after commissioning, so a system installed late in the window but commissioned after the date is at risk. The SMART scheme is separately reported to run to March 2027 on first-come-first-served funds.

## Maharashtra's DISCOMs and Their Net Metering Processes

One of the most common mistakes Maharashtra solar applicants make is treating MSEDCL as the only distribution company in the state. Several DISCOMs serve distinct geographies, and each has its own portal and process. Your electricity bill header tells you which DISCOM you are on, and the PM Surya Ghar portal asks you to select it.

### MSEDCL (Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company Limited)

MSEDCL is Maharashtra's largest [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/), serving most of the state including Pune, Nashik, Nagpur, Aurangabad, Solapur, Amravati and Kolhapur, plus rural Maharashtra. Its [i-SMART portal](https://portal.mahadiscom.in/ismart/index.php) integrates the PM Surya Ghar application and the SMART scheme, and lists basic per-kW prices for empanelled vendors (₹41,400 per kW excluding GST for 1 to 3 kW systems when we checked on 14 October 2026). Treat that as a benchmark, not a quote.

We have not found an MSEDCL-published end-to-end timeline, so none is quoted. Use the portal's status screens and ask your vendor which stage you are at.

### BEST (Brihanmumbai Electric Supply and Transport)

BEST supplies electricity to the island city of Mumbai (south Mumbai). BEST customers apply for net metering through BEST's own consumer channels, with their own inspection team.

### Tata Power and Adani Electricity (Mumbai suburbs)

Tata Power Distribution and Adani Electricity Mumbai serve parts of the Mumbai suburbs. Which one serves a given address depends on the supply area, so read the bill rather than assuming from the locality. Customers following the PM Surya Ghar route should check that the installer is registered both on the national portal and with the DISCOM's own vendor process, where one applies.

### Torrent Power (Thane belt)

Torrent Power serves parts of the Thane belt, including Bhiwandi, and appears in the PM Surya Ghar DISCOM list for Maharashtra. If your bill carries Torrent's name, select it on the portal.

## Step-by-Step: How to Apply for Solar Subsidy in Maharashtra

The application process has a specific sequence. Installing before the DISCOM's approval is the commonest way to put the central subsidy at risk.

> [!WARNING] Installing first can cost you the central subsidy
> The scheme expects you to register and obtain your DISCOM's approval before installation. A system installed first can be ineligible for the central subsidy, however good the equipment, so confirm the current sequence on i-SMART or your DISCOM's portal before you sign a contract.

**Step 1, Register on the PM Surya Ghar National Portal**

Visit [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and register using your mobile number, state (Maharashtra), and DISCOM name. Enter your electricity consumer number from your bill. Complete Aadhaar-linked e-KYC. You will receive a registration reference number. MSEDCL consumers can start from the i-SMART portal, which integrates the same application.

**Step 2, Obtain DISCOM Approval**

Submit the application through your DISCOM portal (linked to the national portal for MSEDCL). The DISCOM assesses your sanctioned load, transformer capacity in your area and technical suitability for grid-connected solar. Do not begin physical installation until approval arrives.

**Step 3, Appoint an Empanelled Installer and Select ALMM Equipment**

Choose an installer who is empanelled on the national portal and with your DISCOM, and ask for the module ALMM listing in writing before you sign.

**Step 4, Installation and Electrical Completion**

After installation, obtain whatever electrical completion or inspection certificate your DISCOM and capacity require. Ask your vendor which certificate applies to your system size.

**Step 5, DISCOM Commissioning Inspection and Net Metering Activation**

The DISCOM schedules an on-site inspection, verifies the installed system against the approved specifications, installs a bidirectional meter and uploads the commissioning report to the PM Surya Ghar portal.

**Step 6, Subsidy Disbursement**

After commissioning verification, the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ₹78,000) is transferred to your bank account. We have not found an official transfer deadline, so none is quoted. If you qualify for the SMART scheme, its state share is handled through MSEDCL.

- **Central PM Surya Ghar:** up to ₹78,000 (3 kW), via national portal direct benefit transfer
- **SMART state share:** eligible low-use homes only, via MSEDCL
- **Net metering:** ongoing monthly bill credits, via DISCOM billing

## MSEDCL Net Metering: Setting Realistic Expectations

For most Maharashtra homeowners, MSEDCL is the relevant DISCOM. The process works best for applicants who prepare documentation carefully and follow up.

The most common causes of delay or rejection in net metering applications are:

- **Mismatched technical documents**: the single-line diagram must match the installed configuration. A change of inverter model or panel brand after approval can require a fresh submission.

- **Sanctioned load mismatch**: net-metered capacity is tied to your sanctioned load or contract demand. If the proposed system exceeds it, apply for a load enhancement first.

> [!TIP] Read your sanctioned load before you size
> Look for "sanctioned load" or "contracted demand" on the bill before fixing system capacity. A system larger than that figure can get the application rejected, and load enhancement has to come first.

- **Missing electrical certification**: depending on capacity, an electrical certificate may be required before commissioning. Ask your vendor which one applies and when.

> [!IMPORTANT] What MERC's March 2026 order means for a home system
> MERC's post-remand order of 25 March 2026 (Case No. 75 of 2025) exempts residential rooftop net-metering consumers from time-of-day slot-wise banking, and exempts rooftop systems up to 10 kW from the grid support charge. For systems above 10 kW the order computes a grid support charge of ₹1.96 per unit (low tension, FY 2026-27), payable once state rooftop capacity reaches 5,000 MW, which MSEDCL reported as passed in February 2026. A 3 kW to 5 kW home system falls under the exemption.

Once the bidirectional meter is installed, net metering works through the DISCOM billing system. You receive a statement showing units imported, units exported and net payable. Surplus is carried forward and settled under the MERC regulations, which do not pay at the retail slab rate, so keep your system sized to your own use.

## KUSUM Scheme in Maharashtra: Agricultural Solar Briefly Explained

Maharashtra has a large agricultural economy, and the PM Surya Ghar scheme (which targets residential rooftops) is entirely separate from the KUSUM (Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan) scheme for farmers.

The [KUSUM](/glossary/kusum/) scheme's Component B covers standalone solar-powered agricultural pump sets. Under the national guidelines the funding pattern is:

- **30% central government subsidy** (MNRE)
- **30% state subsidy**
- **40% farmer contribution** (own funds or an agricultural loan)

Maharashtra's farmer-facing solar pump scheme is run through MSEDCL, and its terms can differ from the national pattern, so confirm the current terms on MSEDCL's scheme page before you budget. KUSUM is not relevant to residential urban homeowners; it is for agricultural connections with pump loads.

For urban and peri-urban homeowners on MSEDCL residential connections, the PM Surya Ghar route described throughout this guide is the applicable pathway.

## Common Mistakes Maharashtra Solar Applicants Make

Installers and consumers who have navigated the Maharashtra solar subsidy process report a consistent set of avoidable errors. Knowing them before you apply saves time.

- **Installing before DISCOM approval**: the central subsidy can be refused if the system is installed outside the approved sequence, regardless of equipment quality.

- **Counting on a state top-up you do not qualify for**: the only state share we could confirm is the SMART scheme for homes under 100 units a month. If you use more, budget on the central subsidy alone.
- **Choosing a non-empanelled installer**: MSEDCL maintains its own empanelled vendor list, which can differ from the national portal's. Confirm your installer is on both.
- **Ignoring the sanctioned load**: if your proposed system capacity exceeds your sanctioned load, the application can be rejected. Check your bill for "sanctioned load" or "contracted demand" before sizing the system.
- **Not linking Aadhaar to the bank account**: PM Surya Ghar subsidy is paid by direct benefit transfer to the Aadhaar-linked bank account, and the transfer can fail if the account is not seeded.
