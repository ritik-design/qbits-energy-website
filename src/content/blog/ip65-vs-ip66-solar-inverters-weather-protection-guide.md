**IP65 and IP66 are enclosure ingress-protection codes, not complete outdoor-suitability verdicts.** Both use the first digit 6 for dust-tight protection. Their second digits refer to different water-jet test categories. The project must still check heat, flooding, condensation, corrosion, cable entries, mounting and the exact manufacturer's permitted conditions.

This guide uses the IEC 60529 coding framework and avoids converting one laboratory rating into an unsupported lifespan, failure-rate or cost claim.

## What do the two digits in an IP rating mean?

**The first IP digit describes protection against access and solid foreign objects; the second describes protection against water under defined test categories.** In IP65 and IP66, the first digit is the same. The water digit differs, so the applicable test definition and product evidence must be checked.

| Code element | IP65 | IP66 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First digit | 6, dust-tight category | 6, dust-tight category |
| Second digit | 5, water-jet category | 6, powerful-water-jet category |
| What it establishes | Tested ingress protection under the standard conditions | Tested ingress protection under the standard conditions |
| What it does not establish | Universal outdoor placement or service life | Universal outdoor placement or service life |

The [IEC publication page for IEC 60529](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/2452) identifies the standard covering enclosure protection degrees. Use the edition and evidence applicable to the supplied product.

## Does IP66 make an inverter suitable for every outdoor location?

**No. An IP code addresses defined ingress tests, while outdoor suitability also depends on the installation manual and site exposure.** A model may have restrictions for direct sunlight, mounting orientation, clearance, altitude, temperature, flooding, corrosive atmosphere or cable-entry arrangement even when its enclosure carries an IP66 rating.

Before selecting a location, record:

- Expected rain, spray and washdown exposure.
- Flood level, standing water and drainage.
- Direct sun and local temperature conditions.
- Dust type and cleaning method.
- Salt, ammonia, chemicals or other corrosive agents.
- Condensation and daily temperature cycling.
- Mounting surface, orientation and clearances.
- Cable, conduit, gland and connector arrangement.
- Service access and safe isolation.

Compare every item with the exact model manual. Do not treat a blog recommendation as permission to ignore a prohibited location.

## What does an IP rating not cover?

**An IP rating does not automatically establish corrosion resistance, salt-mist durability, UV ageing, chemical resistance, impact strength, thermal performance, condensation control or flood survival.** Some of these properties have separate test methods or manufacturer qualifications. Ask for the evidence relevant to the project's actual exposure.

In particular:

| Risk | Why IP65 or IP66 alone is insufficient |
| --- | --- |
| Coastal salt | Salt corrosion is not the same question as water ingress |
| Flooding or immersion | Water-jet categories do not establish an immersion rating |
| Direct sun and heat | Ingress protection does not state the full-output temperature curve |
| Condensation | Moisture can form internally without an external jet penetrating the enclosure |
| Chemicals or animal environments | Material compatibility and corrosion need separate evidence |
| Impact | Mechanical impact resistance uses other evidence or coding |
| Cable installation | The tested enclosure can be compromised by unsuitable entries or workmanship |

The [temperature-derating guide](/blog/solar-inverter-summer-derating-india/) explains why an IP rating is not a thermal-performance claim.

## How should coastal and corrosive sites be evaluated?

**For a coastal or chemically aggressive site, review materials and corrosion evidence in addition to ingress protection.** Request enclosure, heatsink, fastener and connector materials; coating information; salt-mist or corrosion-test evidence where relevant; location restrictions; cleaning instructions and warranty exclusions. Document the exposure assumptions.

Distance from the coast is not a complete exposure model. Wind, shelter, industrial pollution and maintenance can change conditions. Record the site basis and have the product owner confirm suitability in writing for the proposed location.

Do not claim that IP66 seals out corrosive gas or guarantees a corrosion-free life. Those outcomes require different evidence.

## How can installation compromise the tested rating?

**The enclosure rating can depend on covers, seals, glands, connectors and unused entries being installed exactly as specified.** Drilling an unapproved hole, using the wrong cable diameter, leaving a gland loose or omitting a blanking plug can create an ingress path not represented by the original test.

The installer should verify:

1. Permitted mounting orientation and surface.
2. Required glands, connectors, seals and blanking parts.
3. Cable diameters and tightening method from the manual.
4. Condition of gaskets and covers before closure.
5. Drain or vent features left in their intended state.
6. Conduit arrangements that do not direct or trap water.
7. Required clearances and access.
8. Photographic record before and after closure.

Do not add a canopy, cabinet or sealant that obstructs cooling or violates the manual. Weather protection and thermal performance must be reviewed together.

## How should product evidence be verified?

**Match the IP declaration or report to the exact model and enclosure revision being supplied.** Check the standard edition, report scope, issuing laboratory, tested accessories and configuration. A family brochure or website icon may not establish that every variant shares the same tested enclosure.

Use this evidence register:

| Evidence | Verification question |
| --- | --- |
| Exact datasheet | Does it name the quoted model and current revision? |
| Declaration or test report | Does the model and enclosure match? |
| Laboratory information | Is the issuer and scope identifiable? |
| Installation manual | Which glands, connectors and mounting conditions preserve the rating? |
| Warranty policy | Which environmental and installation exclusions apply? |
| Delivered-unit inspection | Do the label, housing and accessories match the approved submittal? |

## How should an EPC choose between IP65 and IP66?

**Define the exposure and mandatory project requirements first, then compare only models permitted for that location.** A higher water-jet category can be relevant where the specified exposure demands it, but it cannot compensate for unsupported corrosion, flooding or thermal conditions.

Avoid a universal “IP66 minimum for India” rule. The correct specification comes from the surveyed exposure, tender or owner requirements and documented product suitability.

## What should be checked at commissioning and maintenance?

**Commissioning should record the delivered enclosure, cable entries, seals, mounting and environment against the approved submittal.** Maintenance should follow the manufacturer's inspection method and intervals, especially after building work, cable changes, severe weather or evidence of damage. Preserve that evidence with the handover pack.

Keep photos of labels, entries and closed covers. Record any replaced gasket, gland, connector or cover with the approved part. Do not pressure-test an installed inverter or open it while energised to prove the rating.
