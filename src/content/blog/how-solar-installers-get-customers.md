**A small solar installer gets customers by running two or three channels well and measuring each one by orders, not by enquiries.** Referrals, a properly set up Google Business Profile and a few local partners usually cost the least. Paid ads and the PM Surya Ghar vendor listing can bring volume, but they only pay if you answer fast and qualify hard. Whatever you pick, a lead is a person who asked to hear from you, and the law expects you to be able to show that.

This guide is for an installer with one to twenty staff. It compares channels by effort, cash and speed, shows how to measure them with a worked example, and gives you a tracking sheet and a weekly routine. It ends with the rules of the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) and the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, read from the primary text.

The guide is general information, current to **30 October 2026**, and not legal advice. It promises no leads or income and says nothing about any brand's partner programme, including Qbits.

> **Short version**
> - Judge every channel by cost per order and cost per qualified lead. Cost per enquiry is a vanity number.
> - Start with channels you control: your customers, your Google profile and your local partners. Add ads only when you can reply within minutes.
> - The PM Surya Ghar portal lets a consumer choose among registered vendors. It helps you be found, but it does not supply customers.
> - Record consent for every lead. Keep a sheet with source, first-response time, quote date and outcome.
> - TRAI regulates promotional SMS and calls, and the DPDP Act regulates personal data. Both are covered below, including what we could not verify.

## What counts as a lead for a solar installer?

A solar lead is best counted as a seven-step funnel, with each step recorded separately:

1. **Enquiry.** Someone filled a form, called, messaged or was introduced.
2. **Qualified.** They own or can authorise work on the property, have a usable roof, a bill that justifies solar, and a city you serve.
3. **Site visit** done.
4. **Quotation** sent.
5. **Order** received.
6. **Commissioned** and paid.
7. **Review or referral** received.

Most small installers track only steps 1 and 5, and the gap between them hides which channel wastes your time. A channel that sends 60 enquiries and two orders is a very different business from one that sends 10 enquiries and two orders.

We could not find a dated, named primary source that publishes typical cost per lead or conversion rates for Indian solar installers. Many blogs quote them, and we do not repeat figures we cannot trace. Our [guide to installer unit economics](/blog/unit-economics-indian-solar-installer/) covers margin and after-sales cost. The worked example below shows you how to compute your own channel costs.

## Google Business Profile and local search

Most local searches for a solar installer begin on Google Maps or a "near me" search. A Google Business Profile is the free listing that appears there. Follow its rules closely, because a suspended profile is worse than none. For a deeper walkthrough of profile and catalogue setup, see our guide to [Google Business Profile and WhatsApp catalogues for solar dealers](/blog/google-business-profile-whatsapp-catalogue-solar-dealers/).

Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), which we read on 30 October 2026, say a profile must be for your "actual, real-world location". They also say:

- A service-area business, one that travels to customers, "should hide your business address from customers".
- A business that rents a mailing address but does not operate from it is not eligible for a profile at that location.
- A virtual office is not allowed unless it is staffed during business hours.
- Your name "should reflect your business's real-world name". Marketing taglines and business hours are not allowed in it, and unnecessary information could lead to suspension.

For an installer working from a small office or from home, this usually means three things. Use your real trading name. List a staffed office only if customers can actually visit it. Otherwise set a service area and hide the address. Add the phone number, a WhatsApp link, photos of your own completed work with the customer's permission, and a short description of what you install. Create one page on your website for each city you serve, with the same name, address and phone as the profile.

## Referrals from customers

A satisfied customer's neighbour already trusts the recommendation, so a referral costs mostly good work and a habit of asking. Build the ask into the job, not into a campaign:

- **At handover,** give the customer a one-page card with your phone number and a line asking them to introduce one neighbour or relative who has a similar roof.
- **After the first net-metering bill,** when the customer sees savings on paper, send a short message asking for a review and one introduction.
- **If you pay a referral reward,** pay it after the new job is commissioned and paid for. Put the amount and the rule in writing. Ask your accountant how to record and tax such payments. We have not verified the treatment.

Keep the referral reward separate from the review request. Google's review policy, covered below, prohibits incentives for reviews. It says nothing we read about a thank-you for introducing a new customer, but check the current text before you combine the two.

## How do solar installers win customers through housing societies?

A residents' welfare association (RWA) is a ready audience of owners, useful for two different jobs: common-area solar for lifts, pumps and lights, and solar for individual flat owners. Our guide to [solar for apartment complexes and RWAs](/blog/solar-for-apartment-complex-rwa-india/) explains the two models and the approvals each one needs.

A workable approach:

1. Write to the secretary or managing committee and ask for a ten-minute slot at the next general meeting, or a stall at a society event.
2. Ask who can approve a roof or common-area installation before you spend time. In a building with shared terrace space, individual owners may not be able to decide alone.
3. Bring a one-page summary, not a brochure, and state what you will and will not promise.
4. Collect interest with a short form that states what you will use the contact details for. See the data section below.
5. Follow up with each interested household individually. The meeting creates names, not orders.

## Can electricians, architects and loan agents send you solar customers?

Yes, because these people meet your customers before you do. A local electrician is asked about the electricity bill. An architect or builder knows who is renovating a terrace. A chartered accountant or tax adviser meets business owners who care about depreciation and power cost. A loan agent of a bank or non-banking finance company (NBFC), often called a direct selling agent (DSA), meets people who already want finance. Our guide to [offering solar financing as an installer](/blog/installers-offer-solar-financing-customers/) covers that route.

What makes a partner worth keeping:

- **A clear, written deal.** State the commission, the event that triggers it (order received, or commissioned and paid), the date by which you pay, and how disputes are settled.
- **Quick feedback.** Tell each partner what happened to every enquiry within a few days. A partner who hears nothing stops sending enquiries.
- **Credit where it is due.** Do not try to take the customer away from the partner who introduced them.

Two cautions apply. First, some professions limit the referral arrangements their members may accept, so ask the adviser or lender what is allowed before you propose a payment. We did not check the rules of any professional body, or the Reserve Bank's rules on lending agents. Second, a partner's channel is only as good as your response time. If you take three days to call back, that partner will find someone else.

## Does a PM Surya Ghar vendor listing bring solar customers?

It makes you findable, but it does not assign customers. The national portal at pmsuryaghar.gov.in returned an access error to our tools, so we read the scheme text instead. The source is MNRE's Office Memorandum of 7 June 2024, "Operational Guidelines for Implementation of PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana for the component CFA to Residential Consumers" ([copy hosted by JAKEDA](https://jakeda.jk.gov.in/pdf/Guidelines.pdf)). We re-read it on 31 October 2026. MNRE amended the guidelines on 7 July 2025, and MNRE's own notice page for the amendment did not open for us. We did read a searchable copy of the amended guidelines hosted by a trade magazine. It carries no memorandum number or date. Its vendor-choice, vendor-profile, vendor-rating and five-year maintenance clauses read as in the 7 June 2024 text below, so confirm the current wording on the live portal.

The 7 June 2024 text sets out how a consumer and a vendor meet:

- A consumer applies on the National Portal with a valid consumer account number. The portal gives an application ID.
- The consumer "shall then choose a vendor who is registered on the National Portal" and is free to agree rates with any such vendor. The text says there is no tendering or rate discovery by DISCOMs to empanel vendors.
- The portal is to include a mechanism for vendor comparison.
- Registered vendors can create profiles with the systems and prices they offer and key contacts. Consumers can raise enquiries through the portal, and the text says it is expected that vendors respond "in a timely and reasonable manner".
- The profile also shows a vendor rating and consumer feedback. The rating depends on scale, equipment quality, workmanship and service, and the method is "as prescribed by MNRE". We have not read that method.
- Registered vendors owe free repair and maintenance for five years from commissioning.

What this means for lead generation:

1. **The listing makes you findable by consumers who have already chosen the scheme.** That is a motivated audience.
2. **It does not assign customers to you.** The text describes a consumer choosing, and we found no provision that routes enquiries to a vendor or promises volume.
3. **Response time is the part you control.** Enquiries arrive through the portal, and the guideline expects a timely reply.
4. **Price in the five-year maintenance duty.** It is a cost that follows every job.
5. **Ratings are public and take completed jobs to build.** A newcomer should plan for a slow start.

Registration has its own requirements, including the PBG. Our [guide to PM Surya Ghar vendor registration](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vendor-registration-installers/) covers the state and national routes.

## Can a DISCOM send you solar customers?

A DISCOM (power distribution company) is not a lead source under the PM Surya Ghar text. Under the 7 June 2024 guidelines above, DISCOMs approve applications, inspect systems and process net metering, and they do not empanel vendors through tenders for this scheme. We found no provision under which a DISCOM passes consumers to a named installer. State schemes can work differently, so check your own state's scheme document.

Two rules follow. Do not build a business on an informal tip from a DISCOM employee, because it can vanish with a transfer. And never pay or promise anything to a DISCOM employee for a referral. Treat the DISCOM as the body whose approval timeline you manage.

## How should an installer use WhatsApp Business to win customers?

Use the free WhatsApp Business app to follow up with people who contacted you first and to send service messages to existing customers. It offers a business profile, a product catalogue, quick replies and labels.

We did not open WhatsApp's business messaging policy, so check its current terms before you send anything in bulk. We also did not verify how TRAI's rules, covered below, treat WhatsApp. Use consent under the DPDP Act as your baseline here too: message people who asked you to, about what they asked, and stop when they say stop.

## Do paid ads bring solar installers good leads?

Google search ads and Meta lead forms give you enquiries within days, which no other channel here does. They also produce the widest gap between enquiries and orders. These are the problems to test for in your own sheet, not claims about the market:

- **Wrong place.** The enquiry is from a town or a pin code you do not serve.
- **Not the decision-maker.** A tenant, or a relative asking for a friend.
- **No usable roof.** Shaded, small, or a shared terrace that the person cannot authorise.
- **Subsidy shoppers.** The person wants a subsidy amount and nothing else.
- **Price shoppers.** The person is collecting quotes with no date to buy.
- **Fake or mistyped numbers.** Common on instant forms where a tap submits the details.
- **Slow reply.** A person who filled a form on Tuesday and heard from you on Friday has usually talked to someone else.

Three controls help. Ask three qualifying questions on the form (own or rent, monthly bill band, city), call within minutes during working hours, and set a daily spend you can afford to lose. Meta and Google change their ad products often, so we describe no screen or setting here.

## A worked funnel: how to compute what a channel really costs

**All numbers in this section are assumed for illustration. They are not market data, and not a prediction for your business.**

Assume two channels over one month.

| Funnel step | Paid ads (assumed) | Customer referrals (assumed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend | Rs 30,000 | Rs 2,000 reward per commissioned order |
| Enquiries | 60 | 10 |
| Qualified | 24 | 7 |
| Site visits | 12 | 5 |
| Quotations | 9 | 4 |
| Orders | 2 | 2 |
| **Cash per enquiry** | Rs 500 | Rs 400 (Rs 4,000 / 10) |
| **Cash per qualified lead** | Rs 1,250 (Rs 30,000 / 24) | Rs 571 (Rs 4,000 / 7) |
| **Cash per site visit** | Rs 2,500 (Rs 30,000 / 12) | Rs 800 (Rs 4,000 / 5) |
| **Cash per order** | Rs 15,000 (Rs 30,000 / 2) | Rs 2,000 (Rs 4,000 / 2) |
| Staff hours on visits, at 3 hours each | 36 | 15 |

Ads look fine at Rs 500 an enquiry. At Rs 15,000 an order and 36 hours of visits, they are the expensive way to win the same two jobs. Referrals win on cost, but they sent only 10 enquiries and cannot be scaled by spending more.

Now put the cash cost against what a job earns. Assume your gross margin on an order is Rs 25,000, before staff time, after-sales cost and overheads:

- Ads: Rs 25,000 less Rs 15,000 leaves Rs 10,000 per order, before 18 hours of visit time per order is counted.
- Referrals: Rs 25,000 less Rs 2,000 leaves Rs 23,000.

To see whether ads can work for you, find the improvement that matters most. Raise the qualified share of enquiries from 24 of 60 to 36 of 60, with every later rate unchanged. You then get 18 visits, about 13 quotations and about three orders for the same Rs 30,000. Cash per order falls to about Rs 10,000 (Rs 30,000 / 3). A better form and a faster call can move the result more than a bigger budget.

Repeat this method every month. Compute the four cash lines for every channel and set your own gross margin against the cash per order. Cut any channel whose cost per order stays above what you can afford after a full quarter.

It is not an appointment, and this guide describes no terms, so build your lead plan from the channels you control. For the structure choices behind a dealership or franchise, read [solar franchise vs dealership](/blog/solar-franchise-vs-dealership/).

## How do you turn a solar enquiry into an order?

Most orders are won or lost between the first call and the quotation.

**Qualify before you drive.** A five-minute call should confirm who owns the property, the roof type and free area, the last few electricity bills, the DISCOM and phase of the connection, who decides and when they plan to buy. Book a visit only when the answers fit.

**On the visit, record** shadow sources through the day, roof condition and structure, the positions of the meter and main panel, earthing, the sanctioned load, and where the inverter will sit. Photograph each one. A careful customer will check these points, so your quotation should already answer them.

**Send an itemised written quotation.** List the system size, panel and inverter make and model, structure, cables and protection. State what the price includes (design, net-metering paperwork, commissioning) and excludes, the payment stages, the delivery timeline and how long the quote is valid. Never state a subsidy amount you cannot confirm for the customer's case, and never promise generation or payback figures that you cannot document.

**Set a rule for quote speed** and record it in the sheet. A quotation that arrives two weeks after the visit is usually too late to matter.

## How often should you follow up a solar quotation?

Most orders come after more than one contact. The cadence below is a suggestion, not a measured optimum, so adjust it from your own sheet.

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Within minutes to an hour, in working hours | First call. Qualify and offer a visit slot. |
| Same day | A short written message with your name, what you will do next and when. |
| Day after the visit | Send the quotation, with a call to walk through it. |
| Three days after the quotation | A call to answer questions. |
| Ten days after the quotation | One message with a useful update, such as a change in the customer's DISCOM process. |
| Three weeks after the quotation | A final call to ask for a decision or a reason. Record the reason. |
| After that | Stop, unless the person agreed to hear from you later. |

If the person says no or asks you to stop, stop and mark the sheet. The data rules below make that an obligation as well as a courtesy.

## How do you get reviews and case-study permission after an install?

**Reviews.** Google's [policy on fake engagement and rating manipulation](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) says merchants must not offer incentives, "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services", in exchange for posting any review, and must not "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". The practical rules follow:

- Ask every customer, not only the happy ones.
- Do not give a discount, a gift or a free service for a review.
- Ask after commissioning and after the first bill that shows savings, and send a direct link to your profile.
- Reply to every review in a calm, factual tone, including the unfair ones.

**Case studies.** A photo of a customer's roof, a name, a locality, a bill image and monthly generation figures all belong to the customer. Ask for written permission for each item you plan to publish, say where it will appear, and say they can withdraw it. Do not publish a result you cannot back with the customer's own meter or bill data, and do not describe any job as typical.

## Which TRAI and DPDP rules apply to solar lead generation?

This section records the rules we read and the points we could not verify.

### Promotional SMS and calls: the TRAI rules

The Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018, are the TRAI rules on commercial SMS and voice calls. We read the [Gazette text of 19 July 2018](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/RegulationUcc19072018.pdf) and the [consolidated copy on trai.gov.in](https://trai.gov.in/node/3199) (file dated May 2026). The consolidated copy carries its own disclaimer that the Gazette text prevails. We did not compare it with each amendment. We re-read both on 31 October 2026, and the points in the table below match the text.

| Point | What the text says | What it means for an installer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered senders only | Regulation 3: commercial communication on an access provider's network takes place "only using registered headers or the number resources allotted to the Senders from special series". A sender not registered with an access provider for this purpose may not make commercial communication at all, and its telecom resources may be suspended or disconnected. | Do not send promotional SMS from a personal SIM or an unregistered header. Use a registered route, ask your telecom provider or SMS vendor to show the registration, and keep it. |
| Who is the sender | The definition of "Sender" includes the person who caused a message to be sent or authorised it, and the entity dealing with the goods or services being promoted. | Hiring an agency does not by itself move your responsibility to the agency. |
| Promotional messages | The definition says the sender must give an opt-out in the same message, and that such messages go only to subscribers who have not blocked their preference or have given consent. Mixed promotional and transactional content counts as promotional. | Add a clear opt-out to every promotional message and do not slip an offer into a service message. |
| Consent | "Consent" is voluntary permission to receive commercial communication "related to specific purpose, product or service". Inferred consent is permission reasonably inferred from conduct or the relationship, and it "shall not extend beyond duration / discharge of the contract". | A form that clearly asks to be contacted about solar is better evidence than a bought list. A past customer is not open to promotions forever. |
| Explicit consent | The consolidated copy defines "explicit consent" as consent "verified directly from the Recipient in a robust and verifiable manner" and recorded by a Consent Registrar. It says promotional messages and calls sent with such explicit consent reach the recipient irrespective of preferences registered. Its access-provider duties describe recording this consent after the customer sees the consent template, agrees to the purpose and sender, and is authenticated by OTP. | A ticked box on your own form may not be the recorded explicit consent the regulations describe. Ask your telecom provider how consent is recorded for your sender. |
| Transactional and service messages | A transactional message answers a customer-initiated transaction within thirty minutes, such as an OTP. A service message informs a customer about a product or service they bought, such as warranty or safety information. Neither is promotional. | Commissioning confirmations and service reminders to existing customers are in a different class from offers. |
| Auto-dialers | Regulation 4: a sender must notify its originating access provider in advance about an auto-dialer or robo-call and its objective. | If you use an auto-dialer, tell your provider first. |
| Penalties | For promotional voice calls made on number series meant for transactional or service calls, the text provides suspension of the sender's outgoing services after complaints from five or more unique recipients in ten days, a fifteen-day bar for a first violation, and disconnection for one year for a second. | The consequences are real and can stop all your outgoing calls, not only the offending number. |

**Not verified:** whether a one-line web form counts as enough consent for you to phone the person from an ordinary number, how the rules treat a call made at the person's own request, how the Do Not Disturb (DND) preference interacts with a lead who just enquired but has no recorded explicit consent, and how TRAI treats WhatsApp. Ask your telecom provider and a lawyer. Do not assume the answer.

### Personal data of leads: the DPDP Act

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, applies to personal data that you collect digitally, or collect on paper and later digitise, as section 3 says. A lead's name, phone number, address and bill are all personal data. We re-read the Act's [text on meity.gov.in](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) on 31 October 2026.

| Section | What it says | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 and 6(1) | Processing is allowed for a lawful purpose with the person's consent, or for certain legitimate uses. Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", for a specified purpose, and limited to the data necessary. | Use an unticked consent box or a clear opt-in message. Ask only for what you need. Do not bundle "contact me about solar" with "share my number with partners". |
| 5 | Every consent request must be accompanied or preceded by a notice saying what data, for what purpose, how the person can exercise their rights and how to complain to the Data Protection Board. | Put a short plain notice next to every form and on the ad landing page. |
| 6(3) and 6(4) | The request must be in clear, plain language, with an English or Eighth Schedule language option and a contact for questions. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it. | Give a phone number or email for requests. Make "stop" work with one message. |
| 6(6) and 8(7) | After withdrawal, the data fiduciary must cease processing and have its processors cease, and must erase data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is no longer served, unless the law requires retention. | Delete or anonymise lost leads on a schedule you set. Tell your processors, such as your CRM or SMS vendor. |
| 6(10) | If a question arises, the data fiduciary must prove that notice was given and consent obtained. | Record in the sheet when, how and in what words each lead agreed. |
| 7(a) | A legitimate use covers processing for the specified purpose for which the person "has voluntarily provided" the data and has not indicated that they do not consent. | A person who asks you for a quote has given data for that purpose. Using it for something else needs fresh consent. |
| 8(5) | Reasonable security safeguards to prevent a data breach. | Do not keep lead data in loose WhatsApp groups or shared phones. Use a protected sheet or CRM with named users. |
| Schedule | The penalty for failing security safeguards may extend to Rs 250 crore. | Treat security as a core duty even for a small business. |

On timing, the Press Information Bureau's [explainer of 17 November 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf) says the Government notified the DPDP Rules, 2025, on 14 November 2025 with an eighteen-month period for phased compliance. The Gazette text of the Rules that we opened on 31 October 2026 (G.S.R. 846(E)) is dated 13 November 2025. Its rule 1(4) brings rules 3 and 5 to 16 into force eighteen months after publication, and rule 4 (Consent Managers) one year after. The commencement notice for the Act itself (G.S.R. 843(E), also dated 13 November 2025) puts sections 3 to 5, section 6(1) to (8) and (10), and sections 7 to 17 on the same eighteen-month footing. We read that notice as a Gazette copy hosted on a non-government site. Counting from the Gazette date, the duties in the table start about 13 May 2027, so confirm the exact day. Build the habit now: a sheet that records consent from today is much cheaper than one rebuilt later.

Two more points come from the texts. Section 17(1) lists the processing it exempts, such as enforcing a legal claim or processing by a court, and none of the listed cases is an installer's enquiry list. Section 17(3) lets the Government notify classes of data fiduciaries, including startups, as exempt from section 5, section 8(3) and (7), and sections 10 and 11, and we found no such notification in the Rules. Rule 3 says a notice must be understandable on its own, give an itemised description of the data and the specific purpose, and give the means to withdraw consent, use rights and complain to the Board. The fixed erasure periods in rule 8 and the Third Schedule apply only to e-commerce entities with at least two crore registered users, online gaming intermediaries with at least fifty lakh and social media intermediaries with at least two crore, and installers are not named.

**Not verified:** whether a notification under section 17(3) exists outside the Rules that applies to an installer, and how long you may keep lead data when no fixed period applies. Ask a lawyer.

## What is a weekly routine for getting solar customers?

This routine takes about three hours a week, plus a monthly review.

| When | Task | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every working day | Reply to every new enquiry within the hour and update the sheet. Call everyone whose follow-up is due today. | 30 to 45 minutes |
| Monday | Count last week's enquiries, qualified leads, visits, quotations and orders by channel. | 30 minutes |
| Tuesday or Wednesday | Contact two partners with feedback on what happened to their enquiries, and ask one for an introduction. | 30 minutes |
