### Option 1: 1 kW On-Grid

**Suits:** a 1 BHK without AC, consumption below about 150 units a month, very limited roof space. It covers only part of a typical 1 BHK bill if consumption is higher. Check your own bills.

### Option 2: 2 kW On-Grid

**Suits:** a home with one AC or 200 to 250 units a month of consumption, using the generation above. It cannot back up the home during power cuts, and it cannot run two ACs at once without grid supply.

This is also the capacity where many residential turnkey EPC installers concentrate their standard packages, since it balances subsidy efficiency with load coverage for small homes.

**Suits:** a larger home with higher consumption. At the benchmark, 3 kW is ₹67,000 net, and the extra ₹27,000 over 2 kW buys 50% more generation, so if your consumption supports it, 3 kW has a similar payback. Match the size to your bills, not to the budget alone.

## The ₹1 Lakh Solar Budget Checklist

Every solar buyer on a tight budget should verify these five items. Missing any one of them can turn a "cheap" deal into an expensive mistake:

### The ₹1 Lakh Solar Budget Checklist

> [!CHECKLIST] The ₹1 lakh solar budget checklist
> - **ALMM compliance**: Verify the exact proposed module and cell models against the current MNRE ALMM orders. ALMM covers modules and cells, not inverters.
> - **[BIS-certified](/glossary/bis-certification/) panels**: Ask for the BIS certificate number for the modules and verify it on the BIS portal; check the standard that applies to the module technology.
> - **Full BOS scope**: Confirm the quote includes mounting structure, DC cables, AC cables, surge protection devices (SPDs), earthing system, and AC distribution board wiring. A quote that omits these items can look cheap and require extras.
> - **GST invoice**: Insist on a GST-compliant tax invoice with the installer's GSTIN. PM Surya Ghar subsidy disbursal requires a valid tax invoice. Cash payments without invoicing are a subsidy red flag.
> - **[Empanelled installer](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/)**: Verify that the installer is empanelled on the PM Surya Ghar National Portal. Only empanelled installers can process subsidy applications. Check at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}.

For the full vetting process behind the empanelled-installer item, the [8-step guide to finding a reliable solar installer](/blog/find-reliable-solar-installer/) covers reference checks, DISCOM track record, and quotation red flags in detail.

> [!EXAMPLE] Payback at the ₹1 lakh budget level (assumed inputs)
> **2 kW system, benchmark net cost ₹40,000, at ₹7/unit:**
> - Annual generation: 2 × 1,500 = 3,000 units

## Common Mistakes in the Under-₹1 Lakh Segment

- **Choosing gross price over net price**: The correct comparison is net cost after subsidy. A system that is not ALMM-listed forfeits the subsidy, so a lower gross price can mean a higher effective cost.
- **Buying from non-empanelled installers**: Many local electricians offer "solar installations" outside the PM Surya Ghar framework. These cannot process the subsidy and the work quality is unverified.
- **Choosing a 1 kW system to stay "cheap" when 2 kW is the right fit**: If your consumption needs more than 1 kW generates, a 1 kW system covers only part of it; compare the payback of each size from your own bills.
- **Overbuying**: Buyers with low consumption who are pushed to buy a larger system are overbuying. Right-sizing matters both ways. The [1 kW solar system price guide](/blog/1kw-solar-system-price-india/) helps identify when 1 kW is genuinely sufficient.
