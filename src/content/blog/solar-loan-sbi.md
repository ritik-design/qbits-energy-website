## What Is the SBI Surya Shakti Solar Finance Scheme

SBI launched Surya Shakti Solar Finance as a dedicated product for residential and small commercial solar installations. Unlike a generic personal loan that happens to be used for solar, this product recognises solar systems as productive assets, equipment that generates income in the form of electricity bill savings and net metering credits.

The product covers the complete solar system cost: photovoltaic panels, the solar inverter, mounting structure, DC wiring, AC distribution box, and installation charges. It does not cover batteries in a standalone configuration, though hybrid inverter installations where the solar system is the primary cost may be funded case-by-case.

Key parameters of the scheme are summarised below:

| Parameter | SBI Surya Shakti Solar Finance |
| --- | --- |
| Interest rate | 9–11% per annum (MCLR-linked) |
| Maximum tenure | 7 years (84 EMIs) |
| Loan-to-value | Up to 90% of project cost |
| Collateral | Property documents (for amounts above ₹1.5 Lakh) |
| Processing fee | 0.5–1% of loan amount (plus GST) |
| Prepayment penalty | Nil for floating-rate loans |
| Minimum credit score | CIBIL 700+ |
| Eligibility | Salaried or self-employed; property owner |

The MCLR linkage means the interest rate adjusts periodically (typically quarterly or annually) based on RBI policy signals. During a rate-cut cycle, borrowers on floating-rate loans benefit without needing to renegotiate. Always confirm the current MCLR-linked rate at your branch before signing, as the 9–11% band is the prevailing range as of June 2026.

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW residential system, disbursed directly to the homeowner's bank account after DISCOM verification of the completed installation. *Source - [PM Surya Ghar Portal, MNRE](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

When you apply this subsidy against a 7-year SBI loan on a 3 kW system, the net loan principal drops meaningfully, which is why registering on the PM Surya Ghar portal before approaching SBI is the recommended first step, not an afterthought.

## SBI Solar Loan Eligibility: Who Qualifies

SBI evaluates solar loan applications against four primary criteria. Meeting all four without exception is the fastest path to approval.

### Property Ownership

The applicant must be the legal owner of the property where the solar system will be installed. A registered sale deed, property tax receipt in the applicant's name, or an allotment letter from a housing authority is acceptable. Tenants and renters do not qualify for the SBI Surya Shakti product unless they obtain a notarised no-objection certificate from the property owner and the owner co-signs the loan.

### Income Profile

Both salaried and self-employed applicants are eligible. Salaried individuals must show a minimum monthly income that supports the EMI without breaching the bank's debt-to-income threshold (typically 40–50% of net take-home pay). Self-employed individuals (traders, professionals, and business owners) are assessed on their average net income across the last two years of Income Tax Returns.

### Credit Score

A CIBIL score of 700 or above is the practical threshold. A score of 750+ qualifies for rates at the lower end of the 9–11% band. Applicants below 700 may be asked for a co-applicant or additional security. If you have not checked your CIBIL score recently, do so via the [CIBIL website](https://www.cibil.com/), the free annual check costs nothing and prevents unpleasant surprises at the branch counter.

### Existing SBI Relationship (Preferred, Not Mandatory)

SBI does not legally require applicants to hold an existing account. In practice, existing SBI salary or savings account holders move through verification faster because the bank already holds KYC and income-flow data. If you do not bank with SBI, opening a basic savings account 30–60 days before applying and maintaining regular salary credits significantly improves processing speed.

## The SBI Solar Loan Checklist: 7 Documents Before You Walk In

This is the named framework for this guide. Every rejected or delayed SBI solar loan application we have tracked comes down to missing one of these seven documents. Carry all seven on your first branch visit and you eliminate the most common reason for a return trip.

### The 7-Document Framework

1. **Aadhaar card**: original plus one self-attested photocopy. The Aadhaar number is linked to your DISCOM electricity connection and serves as the primary KYC document for PM Surya Ghar registration.

2. **PAN card**: mandatory for any loan above ₹50,000 under income tax rules. Self-attested photocopy alongside the original for verification.

3. **Income proof**: salaried applicants: last three months salary slips plus Form 16 from the most recent financial year. Self-employed applicants: last two years of Income Tax Returns with computation sheets and CA-certified profit-and-loss statements.

4. **Electricity bill**: the last three consecutive electricity bills for the property where the solar system will be installed. The DISCOM account number on the bill must match the name on the property documents.

5. **Property documents**: registered sale deed, index-II extract, or housing board allotment letter confirming ownership of the installation site.

6. **DISCOM sanction letter**: the net metering or grid-connection approval issued by your local electricity distribution company after you complete the PM Surya Ghar portal registration. This is the document that most delays applications because applicants arrive at the bank before the DISCOM process is complete. Initiate the DISCOM application first.

7. **Vendor quotation / pro forma invoice**: a detailed quotation from an ALMM-empanelled solar installer listing the system size (kW), panel specifications, inverter brand and model, and itemised costs. SBI requires this to assess the technical viability and cost reasonableness of the proposed installation.

Carry all seven. The branch will retain photocopies and return originals after verification.

## How to Apply for an SBI Solar Loan: Step-by-Step Process

The application journey has five distinct stages. Understanding what happens at each stage helps you set realistic timelines.

- **Step 1: Register on PM Surya Ghar portal**: Visit [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and complete the homeowner registration. Select your state, DISCOM, and electricity consumer number. The portal guides you through vendor selection from the empanelled list and generates the application number that your DISCOM uses to issue the sanction letter. Read the [PM Surya Ghar application guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/) for a detailed walkthrough of this stage.

- **Step 2: Obtain DISCOM sanction letter**: After portal registration, your DISCOM inspects the connection feasibility and issues a technical sanction for net metering. This takes 7–15 working days in most states. Do not visit the bank before you have this letter in hand.

- **Step 3: Get a vendor quotation**: Obtain a [formal, itemised quotation](https://quickestimate.co/features/quotation-system/) from an ALMM-empanelled installer. The quotation must include the system size, component specifications, and a line-by-line cost breakdown. Confirm that the inverter is ALMM-listed, this is mandatory for PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility. Learn about [ALMM-listed products](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) to understand what qualifies.

- **Step 4: Visit SBI branch and submit the application**: Carry all seven documents from the checklist above. Visit the branch nearest to the installation property. The loan officer will conduct a preliminary assessment, collect documents, and initiate the credit appraisal. An existing SBI account holder can also start the preliminary application online via YONO and visit the branch only for document submission.

- **Step 5: Verification, sanction, and disbursement**: SBI conducts a property valuation (if collateral is required) and credit verification. The typical approval timeline is 10–21 working days. Once sanctioned, the loan amount is disbursed directly to the vendor's account upon production of the first invoice or milestone payment confirmation. Post-installation, you initiate the PM Surya Ghar subsidy claim, and the subsidy amount (up to [₹78,000 for 3 kW](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/)) is credited to your bank account to apply against the loan principal.

## SBI vs IREDA vs Private Banks vs NBFCs: Which Lender Is Right for You

Not every homeowner should default to SBI. Here is an honest comparison of the four lender categories available for residential solar loans in India.

### SBI (State Bank of India)

**Strengths:** Widest branch network in India (22,000+ branches), government-backed credibility, MCLR-linked rates that benefit from RBI rate cuts, direct tie-up with PM Surya Ghar scheme, and the ability to link the loan to your existing salary or savings account for auto-debit EMI.

**Weaknesses:** More paperwork than private banks, slower turnaround in rural and semi-urban branches, requirement for physical branch visit, and occasional unfamiliarity with solar-specific documents at non-metro branches.

**Best for:** Homeowners with an existing SBI relationship, stable salaried income, and no urgent timeline pressure.

### IREDA (Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency)

[IREDA](https://www.ireda.in/) is a government institution dedicated to renewable energy financing. Its interest rates, typically 8–9.5% for well-rated borrowers, are lower than SBI's standard retail offering. However, IREDA primarily serves commercial and industrial borrowers, larger EPCs, and state nodal agencies. Individual homeowners applying for a 3–5 kW residential system will find the process less straightforward. IREDA has a small number of regional offices and does not have the branch density of a commercial bank.

**Best for:** Homeowners willing to invest time in the application process and seeking the lowest possible interest rate; works better for 10 kW+ systems where the rate difference materially affects total interest cost.

### Private Banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak)

Private banks typically process solar loans faster than SBI, approvals in 7–10 working days are common. They also offer online application portals, video-based KYC for existing customers, and dedicated relationship managers for high-income applicants. The trade-off is interest rate: private bank rates for personal or home improvement loans typically range from 10.5–14% per annum, noticeably higher than SBI's MCLR-linked offering.

**Best for:** Homeowners who need fast disbursement, have a strong existing relationship with a private bank, and are comfortable paying a higher rate for convenience.

### NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies)

NBFCs such as Tata Capital, Mahindra Finance, and specialised solar NBFCs offer more flexible underwriting, they may accept applicants with non-standard income profiles, shorter credit histories, or informal sector employment. Rates range from 12–18% per annum. Some NBFCs have tie-ups with specific solar installers and offer point-of-sale financing at the installer's office.

**Best for:** Self-employed individuals with limited formal income documentation, or homeowners in areas without convenient SBI branch access.

## PM Surya Ghar + SBI Solar Loan: How They Work Together

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is not a standalone subsidy, it is a policy framework that coordinates the DISCOM, the empanelled installer, the bank, and the homeowner into a single workflow. SBI is one of the banks formally integrated into this framework, which has a practical implication: SBI branch staff who handle PM Surya Ghar cases are more familiar with solar loan documentation than branches that have not processed such applications before.

The combined PM Surya Ghar + SBI solar loan journey works as follows:

1. Register on the PM Surya Ghar portal and select your installer.
2. Obtain the DISCOM technical sanction letter.
3. Apply for the SBI solar loan with the DISCOM letter and vendor quotation.
4. Loan is sanctioned; funds are disbursed to the installer.
5. System is installed and commissioned.
6. DISCOM inspects and verifies the installation.
7. PM Surya Ghar subsidy is credited to your account (2–4 weeks post-verification).
8. Apply the subsidy credit to reduce the loan outstanding principal.

This sequence is important: the subsidy arrives after installation, not before. You need the full loan amount upfront. Do not plan to use the subsidy as a down payment for the loan, plan to use it as a principal prepayment after it arrives.

For a deeper understanding of the [PM Surya Ghar scheme eligibility criteria](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-eligibility/) and how the subsidy amount is calculated, those posts cover each aspect in detail.

## Common Mistakes Homeowners Make With SBI Solar Loan Applications

These are the five errors that most consistently result in delayed approvals or outright rejections:

- **Visiting the branch before getting the DISCOM sanction letter**: The DISCOM letter is a mandatory document, not optional. Loan officers cannot proceed with appraisal without it. The letter takes 7–15 days. Apply to the DISCOM first, then visit the bank.

- **Submitting a quotation from a non-ALMM-empanelled installer**: SBI requires the solar system to be installed by an empanelled vendor. A quotation from an unregistered installer will be rejected. Verify your installer's status on the [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) before accepting any quotation.

- **Using the PM Surya Ghar subsidy as the loan down payment**: The subsidy is disbursed post-installation. Plan your finances assuming you need to fund the full project cost and treat the subsidy as a future prepayment windfall.

- **Applying with a CIBIL score below 700 without a co-applicant**: A low score does not automatically disqualify you, but going in alone with a score below 700 almost always results in a higher interest rate or a rejection. Add a co-applicant (typically a spouse with stable income) to strengthen the application materially.

- **Choosing an undersized system to minimise the EMI**: A 2 kW system on a household with a ₹5,000–₹8,000 monthly bill will not cover the load. The EMI savings calculation only works when the system size matches the actual consumption. Use the [solar inverter sizing guide](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) to determine the right system size before approaching any lender.

## How to Maximise Returns From an SBI Solar Loan

Getting the loan approved is step one. Getting the full financial value out of the installation over 7 years requires a few additional steps that most homeowners skip.

- **Apply the PM Surya Ghar subsidy as a principal prepayment**: When the ₹78,000 (or applicable amount) arrives, prepay the loan principal immediately. On a 10% loan, this saves ₹5,460–₹8,190 in total interest depending on how early in the tenure the prepayment lands.

- **Register for net metering as soon as the system is commissioned**: Net metering credits reduce your electricity bill further, improving the bill saving assumption used in the EMI offset calculation. The [net metering application process in India](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) requires a separate application to the DISCOM post-installation.

- **Track generation via monitoring**: Monthly generation data is the evidence base for calculating actual bill savings vs EMI. Qbits AI-powered WhatsApp monitoring delivers this data passively, you do not need to log into an app to check performance.

- **Understand payback period vs loan tenure**: The [solar inverter payback period](/blog/solar-inverter-payback-period-in-india/) for a 3–5 kW system in India typically ranges from 4–6 years at current electricity tariffs. This means the system pays for itself before the 7-year loan ends, creating a period of pure surplus generation during the final 1–3 years of the loan tenure.

- **Claim accelerated depreciation if self-employed**: Self-employed professionals and business owners who install solar at a property used partly for business can claim 40% [accelerated depreciation](/glossary/accelerated-depreciation/) in the first year under the Income Tax Act, significantly reducing the effective cost of the installation. Consult a chartered accountant to confirm eligibility.

The intersection of a subsidised government scheme, a low-interest SBI loan, net metering income, and accelerated depreciation is where the financial case for residential solar becomes genuinely compelling, not just in theory, but on an actual monthly cash flow basis.
