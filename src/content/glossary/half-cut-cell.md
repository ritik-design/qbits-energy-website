## What is a half-cut cell

A half-cut cell module is a photovoltaic module whose cells have been laser-cut in half along their midline, doubling the cell count and halving the current flowing through each cell. The reduced per-cell current cuts I²R resistive losses in the interconnect ribbons and the junction box, raising module efficiency by 1 to 3 percent.

The architecture also enables dual-string interconnection inside the module: the top half forms one string and the bottom half forms another. When partial shading affects the lower row, only that string is curtailed, while the upper string continues at full output. Conventional full-cell modules with linear interconnection lose entire strings under any panel-edge shading.

Half-cut cell modules have become the default in Indian and global crystalline silicon solar from 2020 onward. Most ALMM-listed modules in 2024 to 2026 are half-cut, with full-cell modules persisting only in legacy spare supply.

## Half-cut explained simply

Take a standard solar cell. Slice it in half with a laser. You now have two cells where you had one, each producing half the current but the same voltage. Wire them together to make the same panel, and the lower current means less heating and less power loss.

Bonus: split the panel into top half and bottom half, with each half wired separately. If a shadow falls on the bottom row, only the bottom half loses output. The top keeps producing.

## Why half-cut matters

Efficiency gain. 1 to 3 percent module efficiency uplift over equivalent full-cell.

Lower hot-spot temperature. Lower current means lower local heating under partial shading.

Better partial shade response. Dual-string architecture isolates shaded sections.

Standard at module level. Almost all new module manufacturing uses half-cut.

Minimal cost premium. Manufacturing additional cost is small, easily recovered in yield gain.

## How half-cut cells work

1. Full silicon cell is laser-cut along the midline.
2. Two half-cells produced, each half the area of the original.
3. Half-cells are interconnected in series.
4. The top half of the module and the bottom half are wired as separate strings.
5. Each half-string has its own bypass diode in the junction box.
6. Under uniform illumination, both halves contribute equal power.
7. Under partial shading, only the affected half is curtailed.

The voltage per cell is unchanged (defined by silicon physics, not area). The current per cell is halved. Module voltage is unchanged. Module current is halved (split between top and bottom strings).

## Technical deep dive

### Loss reduction

I²R losses in the interconnect ribbons:
P_loss = I² × R

Halving I quarters the loss. Across a 144 half-cell module, the ribbon and busbar resistance losses drop by 60 to 75 percent.

### Junction box architecture

| Diode configuration | Notes |
|---|---|
| Three diodes | Standard, one per half-string of three sub-strings |
| Six diodes | Premium, six sub-strings, finer shade response |

### Module form factor

A 144 half-cell module is roughly the same physical size as a 72 full-cell module. Wattage class typically 540 to 600 W for utility-grade bifacial half-cut TOPCon.

### Combination with cell technology

| Cell tech | Half-cut form |
|---|---|
| PERC | Standard 144 half-cell PERC mono |
| TOPCon | 144 half-cell TOPCon mono |
| HJT | 132 or 144 half-cell HJT bifacial |
| Shingled | Further sub-division |

### Manufacturing

1. Standard cell production through doping and metallisation.
2. Laser cutting of finished cells.
3. Edge passivation (some processes) to prevent recombination at cut edges.
4. Standard stringing, lamination and framing.

## Advantages

Higher module efficiency.

Better partial shade tolerance.

Lower hot-spot temperatures.

Lower operational cell temperatures (slightly).

Minimal cost increase over full-cell.

Compatible with all major cell technologies.

## Limitations

Slightly more complex manufacturing process.

More cell edges potentially susceptible to micro-cracks.

Negligible drawback in practice.

## Common problems

Cut-edge micro-crack initiation in older or poorly-processed half-cells.

Shorter expected reliability versus full-cell in theory; mitigated by modern process control.

Bypass diode failure in junction box, same as full-cell modules.

## Best practices

Specify half-cut as default for new installations.

Choose ALMM-listed manufacturers with track record.

Verify junction box quality and diode redundancy.

Use proper handling to avoid edge stress.

Document module electrical parameters at commissioning.

## Common mistakes

Choosing full-cell modules to save a small upfront cost without considering lifecycle yield.

Ignoring junction box diode configuration when selecting half-cut modules.

Mishandling at site, breaking cut-edge cells.

Mixing half-cut and full-cell in same string. (Same string voltage but different optimal MPP behaviour.)

Half-cut cell modules are the default for Indian and global solar module manufacturing from 2020 onward. Minimal cost premium and broad ALMM availability make it the rational choice for any new installation.
