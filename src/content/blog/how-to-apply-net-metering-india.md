Every year, thousands of Indian households install rooftop solar and then discover that getting net metering approved takes far longer than the installation itself. The solar panels go up in two days; the bidirectional meter from the DISCOM sometimes takes three months. The frustration is real, and it is almost entirely avoidable, if you know the process before you begin.

Net metering in India is not a single national process. It runs through your state DISCOM, under state SERC regulations, with state-specific document requirements and portal URLs. A homeowner in Bengaluru navigates a different set of steps than a homeowner in Jaipur. What is universal is the underlying six-stage sequence, and knowing that sequence, plus the complete document checklist, is what separates applications that sail through from applications that bounce back twice before clearing.

> **Direct answer.** To apply for net metering in India, follow six steps: (1) verify DISCOM eligibility and system size limits, (2) install solar with an empanelled vendor using ALMM-listed equipment, (3) submit the application to your DISCOM with all required documents, bill, ownership proof, commissioning certificate, inverter datasheet, and single-line diagram, (4) pass the DISCOM technical inspection, (5) get the bidirectional meter installed, and (6) sign the net metering agreement and activate. Total timeline: 30–90 days depending on state.

The [net metering in India complete guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) covers the billing mechanics and carry-forward rules in depth. This post focuses specifically on the application process, the steps, the documents, and the state-by-state differences that determine how quickly your [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) connection actually goes live.

## What You Need to Know Before Applying for Net Metering

Before submitting a single document, three eligibility questions must be answered. Getting these wrong wastes weeks.

### Question 1: Does your DISCOM serve your area with net metering?

All DISCOMs in India are legally required to offer net metering under the Electricity Act 2003 and the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy's Net Metering Guidelines. The [MNRE Net Metering Guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/) mandate that every state's SERC frame net metering regulations, and all major states have done so. The practical question is not whether net metering is available, it is whether the specific feeder serving your premises has capacity to absorb additional solar generation. In congested urban feeders, DISCOMs sometimes defer new net metering connections until infrastructure upgrades are complete.

Check your DISCOM website or call the consumer helpline to confirm your feeder area's current net metering status before spending on installation.

### Question 2: What system size are you eligible for?

Most State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs) cap the net metering system size at the **sanctioned load** of your electricity connection. A home with a 5 kW sanctioned load can install up to 5 kW under net metering. The upper ceiling for residential net metering in most states is 500 kW, though this is rarely relevant for homes. Some states have different rules:

- **Maharashtra**: net metering permitted up to sanctioned load, maximum 1 MW for LT consumers.
- **Gujarat**: net metering up to sanctioned load; gross metering mandated above 10 kW in some circles.
- **Kerala**: net metering up to sanctioned load; no mandatory switchover to gross metering.
- **Karnataka**: net metering up to sanctioned load, capped at 500 kW.
- **Tamil Nadu**: net metering up to sanctioned load, with export units credited at Average Pooled Purchase Cost.

Confirm your sanctioned load from your electricity bill before finalising system size. If your bill shows 3 kW sanctioned load and you install a 5 kW system without upgrading the connection, the DISCOM will refuse net metering for the excess capacity.

### Question 3: Are you eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy alongside net metering?

If this is a new residential installation and you have not previously received a central government rooftop solar subsidy, you are likely eligible to combine the [PM Surya Ghar](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/) scheme with net metering. The two are processed together via the PM Surya Ghar portal for scheme beneficiaries, net metering activation is part of the DISCOM inspection and commissioning step. Read the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) to understand the subsidy structure before applying.

## The Universal 6-Step Net Metering Application Process

This six-step process applies across all Indian states, regardless of which DISCOM or which portal you are using. State-specific variations in portal URLs, required documents, and timelines are covered in the next section.

### Step 1: Verify DISCOM eligibility and confirm system size limits

Call or visit your DISCOM consumer service centre, or check the state DISCOM portal. Confirm that your feeder area has net metering capacity, identify your sanctioned load, and determine the maximum system size you can install under net metering without needing a load enhancement. Note the DISCOM's current processing timelines, ask directly, or check recent consumer reviews on state solar energy forums.

At this stage, also verify whether your DISCOM mandates a feasibility application **before** installation or accepts the application **after** installation. Most DISCOMs now process the net metering application post-installation (after the system is commissioned), but states using the PM Surya Ghar portal route require DISCOM feasibility approval before the installation begins.

### Step 2: Get the solar system installed by an empanelled vendor using ALMM-listed equipment

The installation must be completed by a vendor empanelled under MNRE or your state DISCOM scheme. For PM Surya Ghar-linked net metering, the installer must be on the [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in) empanelled vendor list. For standalone net metering without PM Surya Ghar, a licensed electrical contractor with a valid CEI or CEIG certificate is typically accepted.

The inverter and solar panels must meet the technical requirements your DISCOM will check at inspection:

- **Inverter**: ALMM-listed, BIS-certified, IEC 62116 anti-islanding compliant. Confirm the specific model appears on the current [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/).
- **Panels**: ALMM-listed under the module category.
- **Earthing**: Double earthing for the mounting structure and the inverter body.
- **AC and DC isolators**: Properly rated and labelled.
- **Surge protection devices (SPDs)**: On both the DC string and the AC output side.

The installer should provide you with the commissioning certificate, the [single-line diagram](/glossary/single-line-diagram/), and the inverter datasheet before you submit the net metering application. Confirm these three documents are ready before the installer leaves the site.

### Step 3: Submit the net metering application to your DISCOM with all required documents

This is the formal application step. In most states, this is done via the DISCOM's online portal or via the PM Surya Ghar portal. The application form asks for:

- Electricity consumer number (from your bill)
- System capacity in kW
- Installer name and empanelment number
- Equipment details (inverter model, panel model, total Wp)

Along with the form, upload or submit all documents from the Net Metering Application Checklist (see the following section). Submit only after all documents are ready, partial submissions are a leading cause of resubmission loops.

### Step 4: DISCOM technical inspection

After the application is submitted and found complete, the DISCOM assigns a technical inspection date. A DISCOM engineer visits the site and inspects:

- Panel mounting orientation, tilt, and shading assessment
- DC wiring, string configuration, and connector quality
- Inverter installation and earthing
- AC wiring, isolator ratings, and surge protection
- Consistency between the installed system and the submitted single-line diagram

If the inspection passes, the DISCOM proceeds to meter replacement. If it fails, the DISCOM issues a deficiency notice listing specific items to be corrected. Once corrected, you request a re-inspection.

### Step 5: Bidirectional meter installation

After successful inspection, the [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) replaces your existing single-direction meter with a [bidirectional meter](/glossary/bidirectional-meter/), also called a net meter or smart meter. This meter records both import (units drawn from the grid) and export (units pushed to the grid from your solar system). The meter installation is done at the DISCOM's cost in most states, though some DISCOMs charge a meter installation fee of ₹2,000 to ₹8,000 depending on phase and size.

Once the bidirectional meter is live, your solar system can legally export to the grid. Units exported accumulate as export credits against your monthly import.

### Step 6: Net metering agreement signing and activation

The final step is signing the net metering agreement with your DISCOM. This document formalises the terms: the export credit rate, the settlement period (monthly or annual), the carry-forward rules for excess credits, and the conditions for disconnection. Sign the agreement, retain a copy with the DISCOM seal, and note the agreement number for future reference.

From this point, your net metering connection is live. Your next electricity bill will reflect both import and export readings, and you pay only for the net difference.

> **30–90 days.** The typical end-to-end timeline for net metering approval in India, from application submission to bidirectional meter activation. Gujarat and Karnataka process faster at 30–45 days; Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu average 60–90 days. *Source - [MNRE Net Metering Guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/), updated 2025.*

## The Net Metering Application Checklist: 12 Items Before You Submit

This framework captures every document and verification item that a successful net metering application requires. Work through all 12 items before clicking submit on any portal.

### The Net Metering Application Checklist

1. **Latest electricity bill**: Must show the consumer number, sanctioned load, and DISCOM name clearly. Use a bill from the last three months. Ensure the consumer number in the form exactly matches the bill, including any leading zeros.

2. **Property ownership proof**: Sale deed, registered gift deed, property card, or municipal extract confirming ownership. If the property is in a relative's name, a registered NOC is required. For rented property, the landlord's ownership document plus a signed tenant NOC is needed.

3. **Aadhaar card copy**: Self-attested photocopy or PDF. For PM Surya Ghar-linked applications, the mobile number registered on the portal must be Aadhaar-linked for OTP authentication.

4. **Commissioning certificate**: Issued by the empanelled installer on their company letterhead after the system is fully installed and functional. This document confirms the installation date, system capacity, equipment details, and the installer's empanelment number.

5. **Inverter datasheet**: The manufacturer's official technical datasheet for the specific inverter model installed. Should clearly show the model number (matching the ALMM-listed designation), rated output power, voltage range, efficiency rating, and IEC 62116 compliance notation. Cross-check the model number against the current [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/).

6. **Single-line diagram (SLD)**: The electrical schematic showing the solar system layout: panel strings, DC combiner (if used), DC isolator, inverter, AC isolator, energy meter, and grid connection point. The SLD must reflect the actual as-installed configuration, any deviation during DISCOM inspection triggers rejection.

7. **Installer empanelment certificate**: Confirms the vendor is officially empanelled under PM Surya Ghar or the relevant state scheme. Download from the pmsuryaghar.gov.in portal or the state DISCOM portal.

8. **Passport photograph**: Recent passport-size photograph of the applicant. Required by most DISCOMs for the net metering agreement.

9. **Cancelled cheque or bank passbook front page**: Required for PM Surya Ghar subsidy transfer via NACH. The name on the bank account must match the electricity consumer name or the applicant's Aadhaar name.

10. **Completed net metering application form**: Download from your DISCOM portal, fill in all fields (do not leave optional fields blank, some DISCOMs reject forms with blank fields), and sign where required.

11. **Load calculation sheet (where required)**: Some DISCOMs (particularly in Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan) require a load calculation showing that the proposed system capacity does not exceed the sanctioned load. Your installer should prepare this document.

12. **Surge protection device (SPD) test certificate (where required)**: Karnataka's BESCOM and some Gujarat DISCOMs require a factory test certificate for the SPD installed on the AC and DC sides. Confirm with your DISCOM whether this is mandatory in your area.

Keep digital copies of all 12 items in PDF format, each under 2 MB. Compress large PDFs using a standard PDF compressor before upload. Label files clearly (for example: "1\_Electricity\_Bill.pdf", "5\_Inverter\_Datasheet.pdf") to avoid confusion during multi-document upload sessions.

Each state has its own portal, processing norms, and pain points. The table below gives the practical overview for the seven most active net metering states in India as of 2026.

### Maharashtra (MSEDCL)

MSEDCL processes net metering applications through its online portal and, for PM Surya Ghar beneficiaries, through the central portal. The main challenge in Maharashtra is inspection capacity: the state has one of the highest rooftop solar application volumes in India, and DISCOM inspection queues in Pune, Nashik, and suburban Mumbai regularly stretch to 30 to 45 days after application submission. Applicants should submit the complete document set immediately after installation, do not wait for the installer to schedule the inspection separately. The DISCOM inspection is triggered by the application, not by the installer's separate request.

Maharashtra allows export credits at the retail tariff rate, which makes net metering financially attractive for consumers paying higher urban tariff slabs.

### Gujarat (DGVCL, PGVCL, MGVCL, UGVCL)

Gujarat is split across four DISCOMs, each with its own portal. The Gujarat Energy Development Agency ([GEDA](https://geda.gujarat.gov.in)) coordinates the state's solar programme and provides guidance on DISCOM-specific processes. Processing times in Gujarat are among the best in India (30 to 45 days) thanks to the state's long history of rooftop solar promotion. The Saurashtra region (PGVCL territory) is noted for particularly smooth processing.

The main complexity in Gujarat is identifying which DISCOM serves your specific address. Confirm your DISCOM from your electricity bill header, it will clearly state DGVCL, PGVCL, MGVCL, or UGVCL.

### Karnataka (BESCOM)

BESCOM serves Bengaluru and eight surrounding districts. Karnataka has a well-established online application process and one of the clearer inspection checklists among Indian DISCOMs. The most common rejection at BESCOM inspection is the single-line diagram mismatch, where the as-installed system differs from the submitted SLD due to last-minute changes in panel count or inverter placement. Ensure the installer finalises the SLD only after installation is complete and has the DISCOM-prescribed format.

### Kerala (KSEB)

KSEB processes net metering through an online portal with a generally consumer-friendly approach. Kerala stands out for permitting net metering up to the sanctioned load with no mandatory switchover to gross metering above a certain threshold, making it one of the most favourable states for residential net metering financially. Delays in Kerala are typically at the bidirectional meter procurement and installation stage rather than at the inspection stage.

### Tamil Nadu (TANGEDCO)

TANGEDCO's net metering process remains partially offline in 2026. The application form is available online, but many documents must be physically submitted to the local Assistant Engineer or Assistant Divisional Engineer office. Export credits are at the Average Pooled Purchase Cost (APPC) rate, which is lower than the retail tariff, typically ₹2.50 to ₹4.00 per unit, rather than the retail rate. This reduces the financial benefit compared to states that credit at the retail tariff. Processing times average 60 to 90 days, with feeder capacity rejections particularly common in congested Chennai feeders.

### Rajasthan (JVVNL and AVVNL)

Rajasthan has two major DISCOMs for the purposes of residential net metering: Jaipur Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited (JVVNL) covering Jaipur zone, and Ajmer Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited (AVVNL) covering Ajmer, Jodhpur, and other areas. Both DISCOMs have high application volumes, and inspection queues in Jaipur and Jodhpur regularly stretch beyond 45 days. The load calculation sheet is frequently rejected by JVVNL for format non-compliance, ask your installer to prepare this document using the JVVNL-prescribed template, which is downloadable from [jvvnl.in](https://www.jvvnl.in).

### Delhi (BSES and Tata Power)

Delhi is served by three DISCOMs: BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna, and Tata Power Delhi Distribution Limited. Each has its own online portal and its own document checklist. Confirm which DISCOM serves your specific address, the DISCOM name appears on your electricity bill. Delhi has an active rooftop solar programme and generally processes net metering applications faster than the national average, with Tata Power DDL noted for particularly transparent portal tracking.

### What to do if your DISCOM is delaying

Delays beyond 90 days without explanation warrant action. Follow these steps:

- **Week 6**: Submit a written follow-up to the DISCOM's executive engineer or nodal officer for rooftop solar. Use the DISCOM's grievance portal where available.
- **Week 8**: File a formal consumer complaint with your DISCOM's consumer grievance forum. Most DISCOMs have a time-bound grievance resolution mechanism under SERC orders.
- **Week 10**: Escalate to your State Electricity Regulatory Commission (SERC). SERCs have consumer cells that can direct DISCOMs to process applications within stipulated timeframes. SERC orders carry legal weight.
- **Week 12+**: If SERC intervention does not produce results within 30 days, a consumer dispute can be filed with the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.

Keep records of every submission, acknowledgement number, and written communication. Without records, grievance processes are significantly harder to pursue.

## Common Rejection Reasons and How to Avoid Them

Applications fail at three checkpoints: document review, technical inspection, and system capacity verification. The [solar inverter commissioning guide for India](/blog/solar-inverter-commissioning-in-india/) is an essential companion read for understanding what the DISCOM inspector checks, know this before your installation is finalised.

### At document review

- **Consumer number mismatch**: The consumer number in the application form must match the bill character-for-character, including leading zeros and any suffix codes. Copy it directly from the bill PDF rather than typing it from memory.
- **Document quality failure**: Blurry scans, shadow-obscured text, or files exceeding the upload size limit cause automatic rejection. Use a flatbed scanner at 300 dpi or a scanning app (such as Adobe Scan or Microsoft Lens) in bright, even lighting.
- **Single-line diagram format**: Many DISCOMs require the SLD in a specific format with prescribed symbols and labels. Download the prescribed SLD template from your DISCOM portal and ask your installer to complete it in that format.
- **Commissioning certificate missing required fields**: Some DISCOMs require the commissioning certificate to state specific items: system capacity in kW and kWp, inverter model and serial number, panel model and count, installation date, and the installer's CEIG licence number. Confirm with the DISCOM what fields are mandatory before accepting the certificate from your installer.

### At technical inspection

- **Non-ALMM equipment**: This is the most serious rejection. If the inverter or panels are not on the current ALMM list, the DISCOM will refuse net metering connection until the equipment is replaced. Verify ALMM listing before the equipment is procured, not after installation.
- **SLD mismatch**: The as-installed system must match the submitted SLD exactly. Any deviation (a different panel count, a relocated isolator, a changed string configuration) requires a revised SLD and re-inspection. Finalize the SLD only after installation is complete.
- **Earthing deficiency**: DISCOM inspectors test earthing continuity. The mounting structure must have independent earthing, the inverter body must have its own earth connection, and the earthing pit must meet the IS 3043 standard for earth resistance. Ask your installer to conduct a continuity check before the inspection.
- **Absent or incorrect labelling**: Several state DISCOMs, including BESCOM and MSEDCL, now check that all isolators, the inverter, and the AC main board carry safety labels with voltage and current ratings. Ensure labelling is complete before scheduling inspection.

### At capacity verification

- **System exceeds sanctioned load**: If the installed system capacity is larger than the sanctioned load on the connection, the DISCOM will either reject net metering for the excess capacity or ask for a load enhancement application to be filed first. Confirm sanctioned load before finalising system size.
- **Feeder capacity constraints**: If the local feeder is saturated with solar generation, the DISCOM may defer the net metering connection. Applying for a smaller system size or requesting a specific feeder capacity report can resolve this. Some DISCOMs have a waitlist mechanism for feeder capacity.

For a comprehensive look at the compliance requirements that govern DISCOM inspections, the guide on [solar inverter regulations in India 2026](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) covers BIS, IEC, and ALMM requirements in detail.

## What Happens After Net Metering Is Activated

Once the bidirectional meter is live and the net metering agreement is signed, your monthly electricity billing changes. The DISCOM reads both your import register (units drawn from the grid) and export register (units pushed to the grid from your solar system) at each billing cycle. Net units = imports minus exports. You pay only for net units at your applicable tariff slab.

Several states carry forward unused export credits across billing cycles:

- **Gujarat**: annual settlement, with export credits carried forward for 12 months.
- **Karnataka**: monthly settlement with annual true-up for excess credits.
- **Kerala**: monthly settlement; excess credits carried forward to the next month.
- **Maharashtra**: monthly settlement; excess export in a month is carried forward with limits depending on the regulatory order in effect.

Check your state's current SERC order on carry-forward rules, these are updated periodically. Your DISCOM consumer service centre or the state SERC website publishes the current tariff order.

For households on the PM Surya Ghar scheme, net metering activation also triggers the subsidy processing sequence. Once the DISCOM marks the net meter as live on the backend, the subsidy amount (up to ₹78,000 for a 3 kW system) is credited to the bank account via NACH within 15 to 30 days.

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW or larger residential system, directly linked to successful net metering commissioning and DISCOM verification. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*
