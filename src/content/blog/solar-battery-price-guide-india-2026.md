When your solar installer hands you a quotation with a "150Ah battery" line next to another line for a lithium pack, you face a comparison that is genuinely difficult without a common framework. These are not just different prices, they are different chemistries, different lifespans, different backup capabilities. EPCs preparing these quotes with a [cost estimation tool](https://quickestimate.co/pricing/) should present both options in usable-kWh terms, not raw sticker price, to avoid this exact confusion.

This guide builds that framework. It provides complete price tables by chemistry, capacity, and brand, and includes backup time calculations for typical Indian household loads so you can compare options on equal footing.

> **Direct answer.** Battery prices cannot be summarised in one range. Dated listings on 14 October 2026 include 150 Ah tubular VRLA at ₹16,760 (Amaron AM150TT42) and ₹17,999 (Luminous LPTT 12150H), and lithium at ₹1,08,000 (Loom Solar CAML-10051, 5.12 kWh by title) and ₹1,23,000 (Deye SE-F5 PLUS, 5.12 kWh). Use the **Total Ownership Cost per kWh Delivered** framework to compare packs: battery cost ÷ (usable kWh × cycle life at your operating temperature).

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - VRLA batteries are cheaper per pack, but have a shorter cycle life and a 50% depth-of-discharge limit; dated listings on 14 October 2026 were ₹16,760 and ₹17,999 for 150 Ah tubular models.
> - LiFePO4 lithium packs cost more per pack (₹1,08,000 and ₹1,23,000 for 5.12 kWh listings) but support deeper discharge and more cycles; check each maker's printed cycle life.
> - On a cost-per-kWh-delivered basis, the assumed-cycle example in this guide gives about ₹6.6 per kWh for lithium against about ₹57 for tubular VRLA; the cycle counts are assumptions, so recompute with printed figures.
> - Lead-acid cycle life falls as ambient temperature rises, which favours lithium in hot states; check the maker's temperature derating.
> - A total-cost comparison can favour lithium despite the higher upfront cost, because VRLA needs replacing more often; work it through on your own quotes.
> - Any lithium battery in a grid-connected system should carry [IEC 62619](/glossary/iec-62619/) or BIS certification to avoid fire risk and warranty voids.

**VRLA limitations in Indian conditions:**
- **Cycle life:** shorter than lithium; check the maker's printed cycle life at your depth of discharge. At one cycle a day in a power-cut zone, cycle life is used up quickly.
- **Heat sensitivity:** lead-acid cycle life falls as ambient temperature rises, so check the maker's temperature derating and keep batteries in a cool room.
- **Self-discharge:** VRLA loses capacity while idle, so top up batteries that sit unused for months.
- **Maintenance:** Sealed VRLA is maintenance-free in theory, but periodic equalising charges and capacity checks every 12–18 months are recommended.

## The Total Ownership Cost per kWh Delivered Framework

Price-per-unit (₹/Ah or ₹/kWh upfront) is the wrong metric for battery comparison. The right metric is **total cost per kWh of electricity delivered over the battery's life**:

> [!FORMULA]
> Cost per delivered kWh = Battery cost ÷ (Usable capacity × Cycle life at operating temperature)

## Backup Time Calculations for Indian Homes

### Critical load definition (minimum comfort during power cut)
- Refrigerator (200L): 160 W
- Ceiling fans × 3: 225 W
- LED lights × 6: 54 W
- Phone charging: 25 W
- **Total critical load: ~464 W (call it 500 W)**

### Extended comfort load (AC + critical)
- 1-ton inverter AC (steady state): 900 W
- Critical load above: 500 W
- **Total extended load: ~1,400 W**

## State-Specific Buying Considerations

- **Rajasthan, Gujarat (extreme heat):** LiFePO4 strongly preferred over VRLA due to heat tolerance. VRLA cycle life falls with heat, so install batteries indoors in a cool room.
- **UP, Bihar, Jharkhand (high outage frequency):** A higher daily cycle count means VRLA is used up faster. LiFePO4 is more economical long-term. A 4–6kWh bank covers most daily outage patterns.
- **Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu (2–4 hours outage):** Either chemistry works. LiFePO4 is the better long-term choice; VRLA is acceptable for budget-constrained buyers.
- **Kerala, northeast (lower irradiance, moderate outage):** System generation is lower; battery sizing remains the same, but payback may extend slightly.

## Common Mistakes When Buying Solar Batteries

- **Comparing Ah ratings across chemistries**: A 150Ah VRLA battery at 12V has 1.8kWh total energy with 0.9kWh usable at 50% DoD. A 2.4kWh lithium battery would have 1.92kWh usable at 80% DoD. Comparing "150Ah vs 2.4kWh" at face value is meaningless, always compute usable kWh.
- **Ignoring inverter compatibility**: A lithium battery requires a hybrid inverter with lithium support and BMS communication. Using a lithium battery with a basic inverter/charger designed for VRLA can damage the battery or the inverter.

> [!WARNING] Match the charger to the chemistry
> A VRLA-profile charger on a lithium battery can damage the battery or the inverter. Before you buy, confirm the inverter's lithium support, BMS protocol and the battery's IEC 62619 or BIS certificate in writing.
- **Undersizing for the actual outage pattern**: If your area has 4 hours of outage per day, you need significantly more storage than for a 1-hour occasional outage. Use the backup time table above to size correctly.
- **Not accounting for temperature derating**: Battery Ah specifications are given at 25°C.  In hot rooms, check the battery datasheet's temperature curve and add a design margin. 
- **Using unrecognised brands without IEC 62619 certification**: Any lithium battery installed in a grid-connected solar system in India must comply with [IEC 62619](https://www.iec.ch/) safety standards. Non-certified batteries pose fire risk and may void the inverter warranty. Always ask for the IEC certification certificate before purchase.
- **Ignoring DISCOM export limits when sizing storage**: Excess battery storage capacity beyond what the inverter can charge and discharge within daily generation cycles adds cost without adding value. Consult the [net metering India guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) to understand how storage interacts with export credits. 
- **Buying imported brands without verifying local service**: For VRLA, the service network of Exide and Amaron is unmatched. For lithium, verify the distributor's authorisation and service process before purchasing. See the [Exide vs Amaron solar battery comparison](/blog/exide-vs-amaron-solar-battery-india/) for a full breakdown of brand service quality, or purchase through a verified equipment retailer that stands behind the certification. [MNRE's rooftop solar guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} require all storage systems to meet minimum safety certifications, confirm your chosen battery has BIS or IEC 62619 certification before purchase.
