## What Does a 1kW Solar System Actually Include?

Understanding each component prevents you from being undersold or overcharged when you get multiple quotations.

### Solar Panels

A 1kW system uses 2 panels at 535 Wp each, or 3 panels at 335 Wp each. Modern residential installations have moved toward 530–575 Wp monocrystalline PERC or TOPCon panels because fewer panels mean fewer mounting holes, less shading loss, and a tidier install. Higher-wattage panels also tend to have better temperature coefficients, which matters for Indian rooftops that reach 55–65 °C in summer.

For a 1kW system, the total panel area is approximately 3.5 to 4 square metres. The panels must carry at least a 10-year product warranty and a 25-year linear performance warranty guaranteeing at least 80% of rated power at end of life.

### On-Grid Solar Inverter

The inverter is the brain of the system, it converts the DC power from the panels into 230V AC that your home appliances use. For a 1kW on-grid system, you need a 1 kW or 1.5 kW string inverter. The inverter must be:

- **[BIS certified](/glossary/bis-certification/)** under IS 16221 for domestic grid connectivity
- **ALMM-listed** if you want the PM Surya Ghar subsidy to be processed
- Equipped with [anti-islanding protection](/glossary/anti-islanding/) per IEC 62116
- Rated for India's grid voltage range (180–270V), this is non-negotiable given the voltage fluctuations in Tier-2/3 towns

The [on-grid inverter](/on-grid-inverter/) specification most often quoted for a 1kW system is a single-[MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) unit with peak efficiency of 97–98% and a self-consumption below 5W at night.

### Balance of System (BOS)

BOS covers everything that connects the panels to the inverter to the grid:

- **[DC cabling](/glossary/dc-cable/)**: 4mm² to 6mm² UV-resistant cables from panels to inverter
- **[AC cabling](/glossary/ac-cable/)**: from inverter to distribution board
- **[MC4 connectors](/glossary/mc4-connector/)**: panel-to-cable joints (always use genuine MC4, counterfeit MC4s are a fire risk)
- **DC isolator and AC MCB**: protection switches
- **[Surge protection device (SPD)](/glossary/spd/)**: DC-side and AC-side protection from lightning and grid transients
- **[Earthing](/glossary/earthing/) and bonding**: all metallic parts to earth as per CEA standards

BOS is often where budget quotations cut corners. Undersized cables overheat. Cheap SPDs fail to protect. Ask your installer for the cable cross-section in every quotation.

### Mounting Structure

For a flat concrete roof, a GI or aluminium ballasted frame is standard. Flush-mounted structures for sloped Mangalore tiles exist but require additional waterproofing work. A 1kW system on a flat roof typically uses 2 rows of single-panel mounting frames, occupying roughly 4 square metres including spacing.

### Installation and Commissioning

Installation includes physical mounting, electrical wiring, [commissioning](/glossary/commissioning/) (powering up and verifying the inverter reads panels correctly), and filing the [net-metering](/glossary/net-metering/) application with your DISCOM. Net-metering approval timelines vary from 15 days (Gujarat, Karnataka) to 60–90 days (UP, Bihar, some parts of Maharashtra). The installation cost also covers the cost of the net-meter ([bidirectional meter](/glossary/bidirectional-meter/)) that the DISCOM installs, this is typically paid to the DISCOM directly and ranges from ₹2,500 to ₹5,000.

## The 3-Question 1kW System Suitability Test

### The 3-Question 1kW System Suitability Test

This framework helps you decide in under 3 minutes whether a 1kW system is the right size for your home:

1. **What is your average monthly bill?** If your bill is below ₹2,500, a 1kW system will cover roughly 50–70% of your consumption and deliver a solid payback. If your bill is above ₹5,000, a 1kW system will be undersized and you should look at [2kW](/blog/2kw-solar-system-price-india/) or [3kW](/blog/3kw-solar-system-price-india/) systems.

2. **Do you run an air conditioner?** A 1-ton AC draws 800–900W. A 1kW system cannot run it on solar alone, especially in morning or evening when sun is low. If you run an AC regularly, add at least 1kW per AC to your system size.

3. **What is your available shadow-free roof area?** A 1kW system needs 6–8 sq metres of clean, south-facing, unshaded roof. Water tanks, TV antennas, and overhead cables cause shading that cuts output disproportionately (a 20% shading on one panel can cut system output by 30–40% without bypass diodes). If your roof area is severely limited, a 1kW system remains viable but ensure your installer checks for partial shading before finalising.

If all three answers check out, a 1kW on-grid system is a strong first step into solar for your home.

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy: Step-by-Step Process for 1kW

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy is available to all residential consumers with a valid electricity connection in India. Here is the process for a 1kW system:

- **Step 1**: Register on the [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) with your electricity consumer number, Aadhaar, and mobile number.
- **Step 2**: Select an empanelled vendor from your DISCOM's list. Only empanelled vendors can install subsidy-eligible systems, you can also cross-check installer credentials using a directory of local solar installers before committing.
- **Step 3**: The vendor submits the application and technical documents to the DISCOM for feasibility approval.
- **Step 4**: After feasibility is approved (7–30 days), the vendor installs the system.
- **Step 5**: The DISCOM inspector visits to verify the installation and activate the net meter.
- **Step 6**: You submit your bank account details on the portal. The ₹30,000 subsidy is transferred within 30 days of verification.

The subsidy is applicable only on on-grid (grid-tied) systems. Off-grid and hybrid systems do not qualify unless the hybrid system is specifically configured for net metering under the scheme. See the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) for detailed documentation requirements.

## Common Mistakes When Buying a 1kW Solar System

Homeowners who rush the purchase often end up with a system that underperforms or is difficult to service. Here are the most common mistakes to avoid:

- **Choosing on price alone**: A ₹45,000 quotation that uses unbranded panels and a non-BIS inverter will not qualify for the subsidy and may void your home insurance. The cheapest quote is rarely the best value. India's residential solar market grew 72% in 2024 according to [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/), meaning vendor quality varies widely across the market.
- **Skipping the ALMM check**: The inverter and panels must be on the MNRE ALMM list for subsidy eligibility. Ask your vendor to show the ALMM listing certificate before signing the contract. See the [ALMM list guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) for details.
- **Ignoring shading**: Even partial shading from a water tank or building shadow can reduce a 1kW system's output by 15–30%. Ask the installer to provide a shade analysis using a Sun Surveyor tool or equivalent.
- **Not reading the warranty card**: Some inverter warranties are "brand warranty" but backed by a company with no Indian service network. Verify that the warranty is backed by a legal Indian entity with a physical service centre.
- **Accepting a verbal net-metering guarantee**: Net-metering approval is the DISCOM's decision. Ensure your installer handles the application and gives you a written timeline commitment with a refund clause if approval is denied.
