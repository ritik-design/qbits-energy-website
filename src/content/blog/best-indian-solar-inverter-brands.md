An Indian name on an inverter box does not answer the question buyers think it answers. It may identify the brand, the seller, the importer, the assembler or the legal manufacturer. Those can be five different parties.

This guide looks at eight Indian-market solar inverter routes: Havells, Luminous, Microtek, Polycab, Qbits, Su-Kam, UTL and Waaree. For each, it records the company behind the brand, what its own pages say about product range and manufacturing, and what you still have to ask the seller. It also gives an inverter company name list you can scan quickly, and a method to check BIS registration before you buy.

Qbits publishes this article and sells solar inverters. It is included under the same rules as every other brand, listed in alphabetical order and not ranked first. No brand here receives a score.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - An Indian company, an Indian brand and a Made in India product are not the same claim.
> - Havells states that every component in its GREEHA Solar kit is manufactured in-house by Havells India Limited. Do not apply that claim to every Havells inverter without checking the quoted product.
> - Luminous product pages name factory sites in Himachal Pradesh. Microtek mentions a plant in Baddi. UTL's datasheet lists plants in Parwanoo, Greater Noida and Bawal. A plant list for a company does not say where your exact model was built.
> - Waaree's brochure describes inverters made in India, while its single-phase 3 to 6 kW datasheet table lists the country of manufacture as China. That is why a per-model check matters.
> - Su-Kam publishes solar hybrid UPS products on its site and no grid-tie inverter we could find.
> - For any brand, BIS registration for the exact model is the document that matters, and the buyer can check it.

**Short answer.** Start with the operating type your home needs, then shortlist brands. For each finalist, match the model code across the nameplate, datasheet, invoice, compliance record and warranty. That process tells you more than an Indian flag on a brochure.

## Why Indian solar inverter brands matter in 2026

Buyers ask about Indian brands for four practical reasons. They want a company that can be served legal notice and is registered here. They hope for spares and service close by. They want firmware and documents that suit Indian grid codes and DISCOM forms. And some want local manufacturing for supply-chain reasons.

None of these is guaranteed by nationality. A foreign company can have an Indian subsidiary, a service desk and a plant. An Indian company can import a finished inverter and put its brand on it. What helps a buyer is evidence for the exact unit: who is named on the nameplate, who issues the invoice and warranty, which standard marks apply, and who will answer a service call at your PIN code.

Two policy facts are worth knowing, because dealers often blur them.

**Inverters now sit under a BIS order.** The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy's Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025 brought PV inverters, among other products, under compulsory BIS registration, so notified inverters must carry the BIS Standard Mark under licence. BIS's list of notified products names IS 16221 (Part 2) for the safety of PV inverters and IS 16169 for islanding prevention in utility-interconnected inverters. See the [BIS notified-products annexure](https://www.bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/temp_2131689472.pdf) and BIS's [changeover guideline for IS 16169](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/changeover_guidelines_IS_16169_V2_final.pdf), read October 2026. Transition dates and capacity thresholds changed after the order, so confirm the current position for your inverter size.

**PLI covers modules, not inverters.** MNRE's [Production Linked Incentive scheme page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/production-linked-incentive-pli/) is titled the National Programme on High Efficiency Solar PV Modules. It does not mention inverters. Do not accept "PLI-backed inverter" as a selling point.

## What makes a brand genuinely Indian, and what does not

These phrases answer different questions:

Table: Origin and ownership terms, what each tells you and what it does not

| Term | What it tells you | What it does not tell you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indian brand | The brand is positioned for or associated with India | Who built the quoted unit |
| India-registered seller | The invoice may come from an Indian legal entity | Indian ownership or Indian manufacture |
| Legal manufacturer | The entity accepts manufacturing responsibility on the product record | Where every component came from |
| Manufacturer or importer | The entity named for sale and compliance in India | Whether it designed the inverter |
| Assembled in India | Final assembly took place in India | Domestic component depth or brand ownership |
| Country of origin | The declared origin of the exact supplied unit | Reliability, service quality or suitability |

A brand may be honest in calling itself Indian while selling a model made elsewhere. A global company may manufacture a model here. Neither arrangement is automatically good or bad.

The problem starts when a seller turns one true statement into four unsupported ones. A company having Indian factories does not mean your inverter came from one. An Indian office does not mean Indian manufacture. A domestic-sounding model name tells you nothing at all.

## Havells: the clearest product-bound manufacturing statement

Havells' official [solar solutions page](https://havells.com/business/discover-solar-solutions) names Havells India Limited as the manufacturer or importer. The same page says every component in its GREEHA Solar kit is manufactured in-house by Havells India Limited. The Enviro GTi grid-tie range is documented in a separate brochure, for example the single-phase Enviro GTi 5000 D-NG (5 kW, two MPPTs, 80 to 550 V, IP65).

The GREEHA statement is stronger than a general "we have factories" claim because it is tied to a named kit. It still needs a boundary. The wording should not be transferred to a different Havells inverter, an older product or a dealer-created bundle.

Havells also presents the inverter inside a broader system route, so a homeowner can evaluate a packaged proposal rather than an isolated appliance.

**Conditional verdict:** compare Havells when the quote is for a named GREEHA Solar configuration and the model documents match the equipment delivered. If the dealer substitutes a different inverter, restart the origin check for that model.

## Luminous: an Indian operating company with a broad backup catalogue

The official Luminous site identifies the organisation as [Luminous Power Technologies Pvt. Ltd.](https://www.luminousindia.com/about-us) and reports eight manufacturing units. Its [solar inverter catalogue](https://www.luminousindia.com/solar-inverters/all-products) includes multiple household solar and backup products, including the 5 kW NXI T150 grid-tie model. Its product pages name Baddi and Gagret in Himachal Pradesh as manufacturing sites.

The useful strength is choice for homes where outages matter. The risk is comparing unlike products. A solar UPS or off-grid inverter is not automatically a grid-tied inverter that exports energy through a bidirectional meter.

The eight-unit statement is company-wide. It does not say which factory or country of origin applies to every catalogue item. That remains an exact-model question.

**Conditional verdict:** shortlist Luminous for a backup-led home after the seller writes down the operating type, battery requirement, output rating and grid-export function. Ask for the exact unit's manufacturer and origin rather than inferring them from the company profile.

## What most "Made in India" lists get wrong

They score the strength of a claim instead of checking its scope.

A company says it has four factories. The list changes that into "this inverter is manufactured in India". A catalogue says "manufacturer or importer". The list quietly drops "or importer". A brand displays a certification logo. The list applies it to every SKU.

Each edit makes the answer sound cleaner and the evidence worse.

There is also no basis for assuming that local origin means higher reliability. Reliability needs comparable failure data, defined operating conditions and enough time in the field. None of these brands publishes a common dataset that supports such a ranking. See the [reliability and service guide](/blog/most-reliable-solar-inverter-india/).

Treat manufacturing location as one procurement field. It can affect supply-chain preference, documentation and accountability. It cannot replace electrical fit, warranty terms or local service verification.

## How to check BIS registration before you buy

BIS registration is the Indian compliance document for the exact model, so check it yourself.

<!-- steps -->
1. **Get the model code and the R-number.** Ask for the BIS registration number (the "R-" number) and the licensee named for the exact model on the quote.
2. **Match the standard.** For a grid-connected inverter, the record should map to IS 16221 (Part 2) for safety and IS 16169 for islanding prevention, per BIS's notified list. Brochures often print the standard mark for some families and only "IEC certified" for others, so ask for the record of the exact variant on your quote.
3. **Look it up.** BIS's [Manak Online registration report](https://www.manakonline.in/MANAK/ApplicationLicenceRelatedrpt) has a field for the registration number and a search by Indian Standard. The BIS Care mobile app, listed on BIS's [apps page](https://www.bis.gov.in/bis-apps/), is another route.
4. **Match the entity.** The licensee, the model and the nameplate should agree. A certificate for one family does not cover a different variant.
5. **Keep the evidence** with the invoice and warranty.

If the seller cannot give a number or the record does not match, treat the compliance claim as unverified and escalate before paying.

## ALMM does not answer the inverter-origin question

MNRE's [Approved List of Models and Manufacturers page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/) publishes List-I for solar PV modules and List-II for solar PV cells. It is not a universal solar inverter list. Some vendor blogs claim List-II covers inverters, and that contradicts the MNRE page.

> [!WARNING] "ALMM-listed inverter" is not a valid claim
> An installer saying "the inverter is ALMM listed" should be able to identify the exact order and entry. Do not accept ALMM language as a substitute for the inverter's applicable compliance documents or your DISCOM's interconnection requirements.

The same caution applies to subsidy eligibility. A brand's nationality does not create approval. Scheme rules, the registered-vendor route, module and cell requirements, technical feasibility and DISCOM verification all matter, and they change by date and jurisdiction. See the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/).

## Choose the use case before the nationality

The practical shortlist is conditional:

- **On-grid home without backup:** start with exact grid-tied or on-grid models from Havells, Microtek, Polycab, Qbits, UTL or Waaree, then compare input limits, phase and written service responsibility. Availability still needs confirmation.
- **Home where battery backup is the main requirement:** start with the relevant Luminous, Qbits, Su-Kam or UTL hybrid, solar UPS or PCU category. Confirm what powers loads during a cut and which battery is supported.
- **Buyer prioritising a product-bound Indian manufacturing statement:** the Havells GREEHA Solar kit has the clearest official statement here. Verify the delivered configuration is the named kit.
- **Buyer prioritising transparent model documents:** use datasheet libraries, then close the warranty, legal-manufacturer, origin and service gaps in writing.
- **Buyer unsure about on-grid versus hybrid:** pause the brand comparison. A brand cannot fix the wrong system architecture. See the [on-grid versus hybrid guide](/blog/on-grid-vs-hybrid/).

## Common mistakes when choosing an Indian brand

- **Believing a factory count applies to the exact model.** Ask for the nameplate and declaration.
- **Treating a BIS logo as a certificate.** Get the R-number and look it up.
- **Using ALMM as an inverter test.** It covers modules and cells.
- **Comparing a hybrid UPS with a grid-tie inverter.** They answer different needs.
- **Trusting a dealer's "Indian brand" label for an unbranded import.**
- **Skipping the service check.** Confirm the party for your PIN code before you pay.
