## Reading Exide's and Amaron's Warranty

Exide's [warranty terms and conditions](https://www.exidecare.com/warranty_terms_and_conditions/batteries) say the warranty period, made up of free replacement and pro-rata cover, is as printed on the battery label and runs from the date of sale to the end consumer as per the invoice. For example, Exide's own Inva Tubular page lists a 150 Ah IT500 at "48+18" months, meaning 48 months of free replacement plus 18 months pro-rata; that is an inverter battery rather than a solar one, and it shows how the periods are written.

Reseller listings are less consistent. One listing shows an Exide 150 Ah solar tubular at 36 months and another at 60 months, and listings show an Amaron solar 150 Ah tubular at 60 months on some sites and 48 months on another. Treat any such listing as a lead, not a term, and ask for the warranty card or the label of the exact unit.

> [!FORMULA]
> Pro-rata credit = (Months remaining ÷ Total warranty months) × Purchase price

> [!EXAMPLE] Pro-rata credit on a battery failing late in its warranty
> On a hypothetical battery with a 60-month warranty and a ₹15,000 purchase price, a failure at month 48 leaves 12 months, so a pro-rata formula of the type above would credit (12 ÷ 60) × ₹15,000 = ₹3,000. Check each warranty's own pro-rata formula, because makers define it differently.

## Cycle Life and Heat

Both brands print rated cycles at stated conditions on the datasheet, commonly a depth of discharge and a reference temperature. Compare like with like: the same depth of discharge and temperature, and the exact model's own figures. For lead-acid, Battery University's rule of thumb is that every 8°C above 25°C cuts life in half, so a battery in a hot enclosure runs well below its rated life whatever the brand. ## Common Mistakes When Buying Exide or Amaron Solar Batteries

- **Buying the wrong product class for solar**: not every lead-acid battery is built for repeated deep cycling; confirm the exact model is rated for the cycling you plan and read its rated cycles at your depth of discharge.
- **Not setting the inverter to the lead-acid charging profile**: using a lithium or gel charging profile on a flooded or VRLA lead-acid battery can overcharge or undercharge it. Configure the inverter to the battery maker's profile and record it at commissioning.
- **[Deep discharging](/glossary/deep-discharge/) beyond the rated depth**: lead-acid warranties and cycle ratings are tied to a stated depth of discharge. Set the inverter's low-voltage cut-off to the maker's instructions to protect both the battery and any warranty claim.
- **Not checking registration requirements**: some warranties require registration or the original invoice. Check the warranty card for conditions before installation.
- **Skipping the safety and certification documents**: ask the seller for the battery's applicable BIS or test documents for the exact model, and confirm with your DISCOM or empanelled vendor whether the scheme sets any requirement for batteries (PM Surya Ghar central assistance is calculated on solar capacity, and a battery is a separate item).
