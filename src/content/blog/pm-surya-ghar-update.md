## PM Surya Ghar Scheme Progress: Where Things Stand in Mid-2026

The flagship rooftop solar scheme launched under the full name PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana targets 1 crore household installations with a total government outlay of ₹75,021 crore. The [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) reports real-time installation counts, and MNRE announced on 4 August 2026 that more than 50.6 lakh households had installed rooftop solar under the scheme, with a record 5.06 lakh installations in July 2026 and ₹28,024 crore of subsidy released through direct benefit transfer ([DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/08/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-rooftop-solar-installations/), 4 August 2026).

Further applications sit at various approval stages. Check the portal for the live count.

> **₹75,021 crore.** The total approved outlay for PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, covering central subsidies for 1 crore residential households across India. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Scheme Document](https://mnre.gov.in/), 2024.*

MNRE's current ALMM module and cell lists give empanelled vendors a clear equipment specification to follow, which is the main compliance point for homeowners.



The government is separately reported to be evaluating a follow-up expansion, described as [PM Surya Ghar 2.0](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-battery-storage-update/), that would add battery storage subsidies and a shared rooftop model, though this remains an unnotified proposal as of mid-2026.

## What the PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Structure Looks Like in 2026

The central government subsidy amounts have not changed since the scheme launched. This is important because social media posts frequently circulate false claims that the subsidy has been reduced or that a new, higher amount has been approved.

The verified 2026 subsidy structure from [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/) is:

> [!FORMULA] Central subsidy
> Central subsidy = ₹30,000 × first 2 kW + ₹18,000 × the third kW, capped at ₹78,000

Table: PM Surya Ghar central subsidy by system size, 2026

| System Size | Rate per kW | Total Central Subsidy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | ₹30,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹30,000/kW | ₹60,000 |
| 3 kW | ₹30,000 for first 2 kW + ₹18,000 for 3rd kW | ₹78,000 |
| 4 kW and above | Same calculation as 3 kW | ₹78,000 (cap) |

The ₹78,000 cap applies regardless of system size. A 5 kW system earns the same central subsidy as a 3 kW system. The subsidy is paid as a direct bank transfer (DBT) to the homeowner's Aadhaar-linked account after commissioning verification, not as a discount on the installer's invoice.

Three things define eligibility in 2026:

- **Residential category**: the electricity connection must be domestic, not commercial. Consumers on commercial tariffs are not eligible for the residential subsidy, even if the property doubles as a home office.
- **Owned or NOC-covered property**: the applicant must own the roof or hold an RWA no-objection certificate. Tenants are excluded.
- **DISCOM coverage**: the scheme now covers virtually all major DISCOMs across India, though remote or newly formed DISCOMs in some states are still in the onboarding queue.

## What Matters in 2026: The Four Key Points

Four points matter most for homeowners in 2026. Understanding each one prevents the avoidable errors that lead to subsidy rejections.

### ALMM and inverter compliance are separate checks

> [!IMPORTANT] Two separate checks
> MNRE's current ALMM page publishes PV module and cell lists, not an inverter list. Verify the exact proposed module and cell models against the current orders, and check the inverter's BIS registration, certificates and DISCOM requirements separately.

### Portal and DISCOM Stages

The national portal carries the whole application from registration to subsidy credit confirmation, and DISCOMs run the feasibility, inspection and net-metering stages. The details differ by state, so confirm each stage with your DISCOM.

The scheme guidelines and state regulations set the time DISCOMs have for feasibility and meter installation, and actual times differ widely between DISCOMs. Ask your DISCOM for its current figure and keep the portal's last-updated date as evidence if you need to escalate.

### State Top-Up Subsidies

Some states add their own top-up on the central ₹78,000. Amounts, eligibility and whether a scheme is currently open differ by state and change by order, so this guide quotes no state figures.

Always verify the current state top-up directly with your state's nodal agency or DISCOM before signing a contract. Read the [solar subsidy Gujarat guide](/blog/surya-gujarat-scheme-guide/) and [solar subsidy Maharashtra guide](/blog/solar-subsidy-maharashtra-2026/) for state-specific details.

### Empanelled Vendor Requirements

DISCOMs and the portal set the empanelment requirements for vendors, which typically cover:

- Proof of sourcing only ALMM-listed modules (and cells where required) for all PM Surya Ghar jobs.
- Registration and licensing requirements set by the portal and the DISCOM.
- Any guarantee or documentation the state's guidelines require.

The practical benefit for homeowners is that a vendor listed on the PM Surya Ghar portal has met the portal's empanelment requirements, which is a baseline, not a guarantee of quality. Read the [empanelled vendor guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) to understand how to verify a vendor's current standing before you pay. ## The Five Gates Behind Most Rejections

Most subsidy rejections in 2026 trace back to one of five gates in the application process. Clearing all five before installation starts eliminates the vast majority of rejection risk.

<!-- steps -->
1. **Gate 1, Aadhaar-DISCOM name match**: Confirm that the name on your electricity bill exactly matches the name on your Aadhaar card. A single-letter mismatch (e.g., "Suresh Kumar" vs "S. Kumar") triggers an automatic Aadhaar DBT verification failure. Correct this with your DISCOM before registering on the portal.

2. **Gate 2, Feasibility letter before any vendor contract**: Wait for the DISCOM feasibility letter, which states the approved system size, before you sign a contract with any vendor.

3. **Gate 3, Feasibility letter size match**: Install the exact system size approved in the DISCOM feasibility letter. If the letter says 3 kW and the installer fits 3.5 kW citing "better value", the commissioning inspection will fail. Any size change requires a fresh feasibility application.

4. **Gate 4, Net-metering activation before subsidy claim**: The net-metering application must be submitted through the official channel and the bidirectional meter installed before the subsidy claim is processed. Filing the claim while the net-metering application is still pending is among the top three rejection reasons in 2026.

5. **Gate 5, Commissioning report and inspection sequence**: The DISCOM inspection must happen first. Only after the inspector signs off can the empanelled vendor upload the commissioning report. Uploading the report before the inspection is complete voids that report submission.

This framework applies in every state. The specific documents differ slightly (some DISCOMs require an electrical safety certificate from a licenced wireman; some states require a building completion certificate and CEIG-approved electrical drawings before final sign-off), but all five gates exist across every DISCOM in the country.

## Application Process: Step-by-Step for 2026

The core application flow has not changed structurally, and the steps below are the process as it works in 2026.

### Step 1: Register on the PM Surya Ghar Portal

Visit [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and create an account using your state, DISCOM, electricity consumer number, and mobile number. The portal will send an OTP to the mobile number linked to your Aadhaar. This is also where the Aadhaar name match at Gate 1 becomes visible, if the portal cannot verify your Aadhaar linkage, resolve it with UIDAI before proceeding.

### Step 2: Submit Application and Receive Feasibility Letter

Once registered, submit your rooftop solar application including roof area details and desired system size. The DISCOM assesses technical feasibility (shadow-free roof area, transformer capacity, existing load). A feasibility approval letter specifies the approved system size. 

> [!WARNING] No contract before the feasibility letter
> Do not sign any vendor contract before receiving this letter.

### Step 3: Choose an Empanelled Vendor and Sign the Contract

Pick a vendor listed on the PM Surya Ghar portal, since listed vendors have met the empanelment requirements described above, and check the vendor's current standing before you pay. Sign the contract only after the feasibility letter is in hand, and make sure the contracted system size matches the size the letter approves.

### Step 4: Installation, Inspection, and Net-Metering

Your empanelled vendor installs the system. They submit a request for DISCOM commissioning inspection through the portal. The DISCOM inspector visits, verifies the installation, and signs off. The net-metering application is processed and a bidirectional meter is installed around the same time.

### Step 5: Upload Commissioning Report and Claim Subsidy

After successful inspection, the vendor uploads the commissioning report with the site photographs, meter serial numbers, and equipment serial numbers. You upload your bank details and confirm them via Aadhaar OTP. MNRE targets 30 days for subsidy credit after this step, and you can [track your application through each stage](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-status-check/) on the portal dashboard.

## Common Rejection Reasons in 2026: and How to Avoid Each

Rejection data from empanelled vendors and MNRE circulars point to five recurring failure points. These are avoidable with the right preparation.

- **Non-ALMM module**: Modules must remain on Phase I/II ALMM lists throughout the project. Some low-cost module brands have been suspended from the list; always check before purchase.
- **Aadhaar-DISCOM name mismatch**: Rejection happens at the DBT step, not the application step, so it can cost weeks of delay. Resolve before registering.
- **System size deviation**: Installing 20% more capacity than the approved feasibility letter specified will trigger a site re-inspection and re-approval cycle that can take 30+ days.
- **Net-metering not activated before claim**: The bidirectional meter must be installed and the meter number registered before the subsidy claim is processed. Filing early without this causes automatic return.
- **Commissioning report before inspection**: Some vendors, trying to be efficient, upload the report before the DISCOM inspector visits. The portal now flags this sequence error and voids the submission.

## On-Grid vs Hybrid: Which System Type to Choose Under PM Surya Ghar

The subsidy applies to both on-grid and hybrid inverter configurations, though the economic case for each differs significantly.

Table: On-grid compared with hybrid systems under PM Surya Ghar, by subsidy, cost, backup and payback

| | On-Grid System | Hybrid System |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Central subsidy** | Full ₹78,000 available | Full ₹78,000 available (on solar portion) |
| **Battery cost** | Not applicable | ₹40,000–₹1,20,000 additional (not subsidised centrally) |
| **Power during outages** | None - must be grid-connected | Yes - battery backup |
| **Payback period** | 4–6 years typical | 6–9 years typical |
| **Best for** | Stable grid areas (Gujarat, Maharashtra coastal) | Frequent outage zones (UP, Bihar, parts of MP) |

For most urban and semi-urban homeowners in areas with fewer than 2 hours of daily outages, an on-grid system maximises the financial return on the subsidy. For homes in rural Uttar Pradesh or Bihar where outages run 4–8 hours daily, the hybrid route makes more sense despite the additional battery cost.
