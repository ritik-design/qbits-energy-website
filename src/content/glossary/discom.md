## What is a DISCOM

A DISCOM is the electricity distribution company that delivers power to retail customers within a defined service area, manages the local distribution network, bills customers and administers regulatory obligations like net metering. India has roughly 70 to 75 DISCOMs across states and union territories, most owned by state governments and a handful by private operators.

DISCOMs are the operational interface between customers and the electricity system. Generators sell into the grid through transmission utilities. Transmission utilities deliver to substations. DISCOMs take it from there: 11 kV and 33 kV distribution feeders, low-tension service connections, customer meters and bills.

For solar customers, the DISCOM is the single most important counterparty. Net metering applications, smart-meter installation, commissioning, billing, subsidy disbursement and ongoing dispute resolution all route through the DISCOM.

## DISCOM explained simply

The DISCOM is the company on your electricity bill. They own the poles and wires from the substation to your meter. They read your meter, send you the bill, and handle complaints when the power goes out. If you install rooftop solar, you also work with the DISCOM to approve net metering and install the bidirectional meter.

## Why DISCOMs matter

Gatekeepers of grid connection. No grid-tied solar runs without DISCOM commissioning.

Net metering administration. Application, approval, meter installation and billing all sit with the DISCOM.

PM Surya Ghar implementation. Subsidy disbursement waits for DISCOM commissioning data.

Technical feasibility. The DISCOM determines whether the feeder can absorb requested solar capacity.

Customer relationship. DISCOMs handle all customer-side billing, complaints and connections.

## How DISCOMs operate

1. Purchase bulk power through PPAs, exchange markets and short-term tenders.
2. Receive power from transmission utilities at 33 kV substations.
3. Distribute through 11 kV feeders to local transformers.
4. Step down to LT and supply customers through service connections.
5. Read meters monthly, calculate bills and collect payment.
6. Maintain network infrastructure and respond to outages.

## Advantages of DISCOM coordination

Single counterparty for the entire solar net metering lifecycle.

Standardised processes under state SERC regulations.

Increasingly digitised application and approval portals.

Smart meter rollout under RDSS accelerating coordination.

## Limitations

Variable implementation capacity across DISCOMs.

Subsidy disbursement delays where commissioning lag is high.

Technical feasibility studies can stall in feeders with high penetration.

Net metering caps and gross metering threshold differences across states.

Customer service quality variation.

## Common DISCOM problems for solar customers

Smart meter delivery delay.

Net metering application rejection due to documentation gaps.

Technical feasibility study refusal due to feeder loading.

Commissioning visit postponements.

Subsidy data upload delays affecting disbursement.

Reactive power penalty disputes.

Bidirectional meter incorrectly configured at installation.

## Best practices

Use the DISCOM's online portal where available.

Engage with the local DISCOM office at the application stage.

Document all submissions with date-stamped receipts.

Escalate to nodal officers when timelines slip.

Use CGRF when nodal officers do not respond.

Track PM Surya Ghar portal status weekly.

Audit the first three bills carefully.

## Indian market context

The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) is a ₹3 lakh crore central programme aimed at reducing AT&C losses to 12 to 15 percent by 2026 through smart metering, infrastructure upgrades and operational reforms.

DISCOM finances have improved post-Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana (UDAY) 2015 and subsequent reforms, but several state DISCOMs remain financially stressed.

Private DISCOMs outperform state DISCOMs on most operational metrics but cover only a small share of national consumption.

PM Surya Ghar success depends materially on DISCOM execution capacity.

## Common mistakes

Treating all DISCOMs as identical. Each has its own portal, forms and bottlenecks.

Skipping the technical feasibility step.

Engaging non-empanelled vendors expecting subsidy.

Not retaining receipts and seal numbers at meter installation.

Letting net metering application sit in queue without follow-up.

## Key takeaways

DISCOMs are the operational gatekeepers for grid-connected solar in India. Net metering, smart meters, PM Surya Ghar disbursement and ongoing billing all flow through the local DISCOM. State and DISCOM-specific variation is the norm. Customer outcomes depend heavily on DISCOM capacity, smart meter availability and process discipline.
