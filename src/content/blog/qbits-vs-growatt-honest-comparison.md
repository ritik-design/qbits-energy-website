## The 6-Point Comparison Matrix: How It Works

Most inverter comparisons score brands on specifications alone, peak efficiency, DC input range, MPPT count. Specifications matter, but they do not determine the buyer's 12-year experience with the product. The **6-Point Comparison Matrix** scores each brand on the six dimensions that define real-world satisfaction over the life of an Indian rooftop solar system.

### The 6 Axes

1. **Warranty Depth**: What is covered, for how long, and who actually backs the claim? Scored on term length, repair-vs-replacement distinction, and enforceability in India.
2. **After-Sales Service**: How quickly does the brand resolve a fault? Scored on documented RMA SLA, geographic service reach, and India-based logistics.
3. **ALMM Compliance**: Is the brand Phase III listed, and is that listing maintained across current models? Scored on listing status, model-level coverage, and track record of maintaining compliance.
4. **Monitoring Capability**: How does the homeowner see system performance and receive alerts? Scored on platform depth, ease of adoption in India, and fault notification speed.
5. **Price and Value**: Upfront cost and total cost of ownership over 12 years, inclusive of likely replacement scenarios.
6. **Hybrid Options**: Is a battery-ready upgrade path available, and how capable is the hybrid product line?

Each axis is scored 1–10. Maximum total: 60. The scores are explained below, axis by axis.

## Common Mistakes Indian Buyers Make in This Decision

Before reaching a final verdict, three decision mistakes are worth flagging, they appear frequently in online forums and WhatsApp groups and lead to buyers regretting their choice.

**Mistake 1: Comparing peak efficiency figures without checking temperature derating.** Many inverter spec sheets report peak efficiency at 25 °C. Indian rooftops operate at 45 °C and above in summer. Ask for the derating curve at 45 °C ambient before comparing efficiency figures between brands. An inverter rated 98% peak efficiency that derades to 92% at 45 °C performs less well in practice than one rated 97.5% that holds 97% at operating temperature.

**Mistake 2: Assuming ALMM listing by brand name.** Both Qbits and Growatt are brands with ALMM-listed models. Neither brand's entire product line is automatically listed. Verify the exact model number against the current live list at the [MNRE portal](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) before placing an order. See our [complete ALMM Phase III guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) for how to check correctly.

**Mistake 3: Treating warranty years as equivalent regardless of what they cover.** Growatt's 5-year warranty and Qbits's 12-year warranty sound like a simple duration comparison. They are not: one covers repair of a unit, the other covers replacement of a unit. A 5-year repair warranty on a board that has been repaired once will fail again faster than a new unit. Read the warranty terms, not just the number of years.

**Mistake 4: Not accounting for [MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) configuration.** Both brands offer single and dual-MPPT residential inverters. If your roof has panels on two orientations, or one string has shading, dual-MPPT is required. Buying a [single-MPPT unit for a dual-orientation roof](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/) costs you yield every day of the system's operating life, a far larger financial impact than a price difference between brands.
