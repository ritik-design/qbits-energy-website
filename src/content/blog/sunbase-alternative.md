Searching for a Sunbase alternative usually means one of two very different things, and getting the difference right saves you a wasted procurement cycle. Sunbase is a modular CRM, field sales, and operations platform for solar, roofing, and construction contractors. Plenty of people arrive at that search because they need a different CRM. Plenty more arrive because they expected a design and proposal engine and found a customer management system instead. Those two readers need opposite shortlists. This guide separates the two paths, sets out what to evaluate in each, and compares six realistic options with honest notes on where each one fits.

> **TL;DR**
> - Sunbase is a solar CRM and field operations platform, not an engineering design tool, and it does not claim to be one.
> - Half the people searching for an alternative actually need a design and proposal platform, not a different CRM.
> - SurgePV covers design plus proposals from one project record at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year, with multi-market compliance built in.

> - The workable stack for most volume installers is two connected layers, not one product stretched across both.

If you need engineering design and defensible proposals, SurgePV at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year is the strongest option. If you need a CRM and field operations system, compare against other CRMs, connected to a design tool by API. Aurora, HelioScope, and OpenSolar cover specific design cases. ## Why People Look for a Sunbase Alternative

Four patterns come up repeatedly, and only one of them is a complaint about the product.

**The category mismatch.** This is the biggest single driver, and it is a buying error rather than a product failure. A team signs up expecting module-level layout, shading analysis, and a production number a lender will accept. They find system sizing inputs, a quote builder, and remote rooftop assessment visuals designed to support a sales conversation. Sunbase's own material says it integrates with separate solar design tools. The disappointment is real, but the honest response is to buy the missing layer rather than blame the CRM.

**Pricing predictability.** Sunbase publishes a starting price of $59 per user per month and no fixed tiers. Everything above the entry configuration is quoted per business against seat count, industry workflow, module selection, and implementation. That model works fine for buyers who can run a procurement cycle. It is genuinely awkward for a small installer trying to get a budget signed off, or for anyone building a three-year cost model before a demo.

**Multi-trade breadth.** Serving solar, roofing, and construction from one platform is a deliberate strategy and it wins the contractor who does two of the three. For a solar-only shop, it means solar-specific workflow depth shares a roadmap with two other industries. That is a legitimate reason to look at a solar-native tool, without implying Sunbase is doing anything wrong.

**Market fit.** The field sales motion Sunbase is built around, territory mapping, route optimisation, rep tracking, door-to-door canvassing, is a North American residential pattern. An Indian EPC dealing with subsidy portals and DISCOM approvals, or a European installer selling through referrals and lead marketplaces, is buying a lot of machinery for a motion they do not run.

## The Two Paths: Which One Are You On

Before comparing anything, answer one question in writing.

> **What most buyers get wrong.** They shortlist products before deciding which problem costs them more money. Weak design output and a leaking pipeline are two different failures with two different buyers inside the company. Solve the expensive one properly and integrate the other, instead of buying one tool that half-solves both.

**Path A: you need a different CRM.** Your leads leak, your reps are unmanaged, your contracts live in email, and your installs are tracked on a whiteboard. Your alternative is another CRM or operations platform, and design belongs in a separate tool regardless of which one you pick.

**Path B: you need the design and proposal platform you thought Sunbase was.** Your proposals get challenged, your production numbers are estimates, your C&I clients want a real yield report, and your team is rebuilding layouts in three different tools. Your alternative is a design platform, and your CRM question is unrelated.

Most growing installers eventually need both. The mistake is buying them as one line item.

## What to Evaluate in a Sunbase Alternative

Whichever path you are on, the same seven checks separate a good decision from an expensive one.

1. **Layer clarity.** Write down which of design, proposals, CRM, and field operations the product owns. If a vendor claims all four, ask which one they were built for and check the depth of the other three.
2. **Pricing transparency.** Published per-seat pricing lets you model three years in a spreadsheet. Quote-based modular pricing does not, and that is a procurement cost even when the final number is fair.
3. **Engineering defensibility.** Ask whether the production number survives a challenge. Module-level shading, documented loss assumptions, and hourly simulation are the difference between an estimate and a report.
4. **Market coverage.** Electrical codes, tariff structures, and subsidy schemes vary by country. A platform tuned for one market means manual rework in every other one.
5. **One record, or two.** If the design and the proposal come from separate systems, every revision is entered twice. That is where proposals and work orders start disagreeing.
6. **API quality between layers.** Whatever you buy, it has to hand system size, production, and the finished proposal to the other half of your stack without retyping.
7. **Real project, real time.** Run one live job end to end during the trial. Scripted demos hide the steps that actually consume your afternoon.

## The Six Alternatives Compared

### 1. SurgePV, the design and proposal replacement

**Best for:** teams who wanted engineering depth and customer-facing proposals from one platform, in any market.

SurgePV runs $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year depending on team size. One licence covers array layout, 8,760-hour module-level shading, yield simulation, financial modelling, single-line diagrams, BOQ, and white-label proposals, all generated from the same project record. When a customer asks for a revision, you change the design and the proposal follows, because there is only one record to change.

The multi-market compliance library is the part that matters outside North America. NEC, IEC, AS/NZS, and IS code handling plus tariff and incentive structures for multiple countries mean the financial model is produced inside the platform rather than rebuilt in a spreadsheet. For a team selling in more than one market, that is usually the deciding factor.

**Where it does not replace Sunbase:** it is not a CRM. It does not do territory mapping, canvassing routes, rep tracking, or inventory. It hands its output to whichever CRM you run, by API.

**Weaknesses:** the community is smaller than the long-established US design tools, and some niche local permitting quirks are still maturing.

### 2. A general CRM connected by API

**Best for:** teams on Path A who want CRM strength and pricing they can predict, and who already have or are buying a design tool.

If what you actually need is a customer database, a pipeline, and automated follow-ups, a mainstream CRM does that job with published pricing, a large integration ecosystem, and no requirement to also buy proposal and inventory modules. You then connect it to your design platform so finished designs and proposals attach to the deal record.

**Trade-off:** you lose the solar-specific and field-specific workflow that is Sunbase's real strength. Territory mapping, canvassing routes, and rep location tracking are not standard CRM features, and rebuilding them with custom objects and automations is a project, not a configuration. If door-to-door is your growth engine, this path costs more than it saves.

### 3. Solar Ladder, for India-focused operations

**Best for:** Indian EPCs and installers whose workflow is built around subsidy schemes, portal submissions, and DISCOM approvals rather than door-to-door canvassing.

Solar Ladder is built for the Indian solar installer, which shows in the way the operational steps map to how projects actually move here. For a team whose bottleneck is paperwork sequencing and site execution rather than territory management, a locally designed platform removes more friction than a North American field sales product. Our [Solar Ladder alternative comparison](/blog/solar-ladder-alternative/) goes deeper on where it fits and where it does not.

**Trade-off:** the scope is regional, so it is not the answer for a company selling across several countries. Like Sunbase, it sits on the operations side rather than the engineering side, so the design question remains open.

### 4. Aurora Solar, for US residential design

**Best for:** teams on Path B whose business is exclusively United States residential and who want the most established design product in that market.

Aurora is strong on residential design accuracy in the US, with deep NEC handling and a large installed base. If your entire pipeline is US rooftop residential and you are already trained on the interface, it is a serious option.

**Trade-off:** pricing sits at the higher end of this list, capability is spread across tiers and add-ons, and coverage outside the United States is worth verifying for your specific market before you commit. It is also not positioned as a CRM, so it answers only half the question a Sunbase leaver is asking. The [Aurora Solar alternative comparison](/blog/aurora-solar-alternative/) covers the tier structure in detail.

### 5. HelioScope, for commercial and industrial engineering

**Best for:** engineering teams whose output is simulation and layout for C&I and ground-mount projects, with sales handled elsewhere.

HelioScope has a long-standing reputation among engineers for commercial layout flexibility and mature hourly simulation. If your work is C&I and your proposals are produced by a separate team or tool, it does the engineering job well.

**Trade-off:** it is not positioned as a proposal tool, so the customer-facing document is still your problem. The interface is engineer-first, which non-technical staff tend to find slower. And it is not a CRM either. See the [HelioScope alternative comparison](/blog/helioscope-alternative/) for the fuller picture.

### 6. OpenSolar, as the free starting point

**Best for:** small residential installers with simple roofs who need to keep fixed software cost at zero while they build volume.

OpenSolar offers a free entry tier for design and proposals, funded through partner arrangements and paid add-ons. For a one-to-three person residential outfit doing straightforward gable roofs, it is a genuinely sensible place to start, and starting free beats not starting.

**Trade-off:** the free model comes with add-on costs that stack as you grow, and the simulation depth thins out quickly once you move into commercial work or complex shading. It is a design and proposal tool, not an operations platform. The [OpenSolar alternative comparison](/blog/opensolar-alternative/) covers the point at which teams outgrow it.

## Who Should Choose What

- **Choose SurgePV** if you expected Sunbase to design systems and produce defensible proposals, if you sell in more than one market, or if you want design and proposals from one record instead of two tools that disagree with each other. It is the strongest option on Path B and the one most former Sunbase evaluators were actually looking for.
- **Stay with Sunbase** if your growth engine is door-to-door canvassing, you need territory mapping and rep tracking, you work across solar and roofing or construction, and you are willing to run a procurement cycle to get an itemised quote. Buy a design platform beside it.

## Verdict

The most useful thing this comparison can tell you is that "Sunbase alternative" is two searches wearing one label. Sunbase is a competent CRM and field operations platform, strongest at organising door-to-door sales and keeping jobs moving after signature, and it never advertised an engineering engine. If that is the job you need done, compare it against other CRMs and pick on workflow fit, pricing transparency, and market suitability.

If you arrived here because the design and proposal output fell short of what you expected, you were never really comparing CRMs. You need a design platform, and SurgePV at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year is the option we recommend, because design, shading, simulation, financial modelling, and branded proposals come from one project record with compliance for multiple markets built in rather than bolted on.
