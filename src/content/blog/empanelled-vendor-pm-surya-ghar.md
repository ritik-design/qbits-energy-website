You have spent weeks researching rooftop solar. You have compared system sizes, read through subsidy slabs, and you are now ready to hire someone. Then a neighbour tells you: "Make sure the vendor is empanelled, or you lose the subsidy." Suddenly the homework is not done yet.

For most homeowners, "empanelled vendor" is a term they encounter late in the process, after they have already received two or three quotes from solar companies they found on Google or WhatsApp. This guide explains exactly what empanelment means, why it is the single most consequential vendor qualification under PM Surya Ghar, and how to verify any installer before you sign anything.

> **Direct answer.** An empanelled vendor solar installer for PM Surya Ghar is a company formally registered with your state DISCOM, vetted for financial and technical capacity, bound by pricing norms, and authorised to submit commissioning reports that trigger government subsidy. Using a non-empanelled vendor disqualifies your ₹78,000 subsidy claim regardless of how good the installation looks. Verify the vendor's registration number on [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) before paying any advance.

Understanding this rule in full means understanding the broader PM Surya Ghar structure, how [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) registration works, what the [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) requires of equipment, and what your rights are if something goes wrong after installation.

## What Empanelment Actually Means: Beyond the Buzzword

"Empanelled" is a regulatory term that many solar marketing brochures mention without explaining. In plain terms, empanelment is the process by which a solar installation company earns the right to work under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana scheme in a given state.

When a solar company applies for DISCOM empanelment, the electricity utility evaluates three broad criteria before approving them:

- **Technical capacity**: the company must demonstrate that it has trained installation engineers (typically holding electrical licence or MNRE-recognised training certificates), safety equipment, and prior installation experience of a minimum number of kilowatts in the state.
- **Financial standing**: the company must not be blacklisted, must have valid GST registration, and in many states must submit a performance bank guarantee ranging from ₹1 Lakh to ₹5 Lakh depending on the volume of work they wish to undertake.
- **Agreement to scheme norms**: this is the most important element. The company signs a formal agreement with the DISCOM committing to: use only ALMM-listed equipment, charge consumers no more than the state benchmark cost per kW, follow the portal-based application workflow, and submit accurate commissioning documentation.

Only after clearing these three gates does a company receive an official registration number in the state's empanelled vendor list. That number is the mechanism through which every subsequent subsidy application flows.

If a company is not in that list (even if they are technically brilliant, use excellent panels, and have been in business for fifteen years) they cannot submit a valid commissioning report on the PM Surya Ghar portal, and your application simply cannot be processed to the disbursement stage.

## Why the Government Made Empanelment Mandatory

PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana aims to deploy rooftop solar across one crore (10 million) households by March 2027, according to the [MNRE scheme documentation](https://mnre.gov.in/). At that scale, the government cannot inspect every installation before releasing funds. Empanelment is the quality-control mechanism that solves this problem.

Before this structure existed, India's earlier rooftop solar subsidy programmes under the Grid Connected Rooftop Solar Phase II suffered from three documented failure modes:

- **Equipment substitution**: installers quoted ALMM-compliant panels and inverters at the time of proposal, then sourced cheaper, non-listed substitutes during installation. Homeowners rarely knew the difference.
- **Price gouging**: with no benchmark enforcement, some installers charged 30–50% above market rate to subsidised consumers, knowing the government payment would de-risk the deal.
- **Documentation fraud**: false commissioning reports and inflated capacity claims were submitted to DISCOMs, triggering subsidy payments for systems that were never installed or were installed at lower capacity.

The empanelment framework addresses all three. By tying the vendor's registration number to every application on the portal, the DISCOM can audit the vendor's track record, pull up all installations they have filed, and cancel their registration if patterns of non-compliance emerge.

> **₹75,000 crore.** The total allocation for PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana across its programme period, the largest single residential solar commitment in Indian government history. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Scheme Notification](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2024.*

For homeowners, the empanelment rule is ultimately consumer protection. The grievance mechanism, the pricing cap, the ALMM requirement, none of these apply to a transaction with a non-empanelled vendor. The moment you work outside the scheme's structure, you are on your own.

## How to Verify an Empanelled Solar Vendor: Step by Step

The PM Surya Ghar portal at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) is the primary source of truth. Below is the verification workflow in sequence.

### Step 1: Log into the Portal

Go to [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and select "Rooftop Solar." If you have not registered yet, you will need your DISCOM consumer number and Aadhaar to create an account. The full application walkthrough is covered in the [PM Surya Ghar apply online guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/).

### Step 2: Navigate to the Vendor List

Once logged in, select your state and then your DISCOM from the dropdown menus. Look for the "Empanelled Vendor List" or "Registered Vendor List" section, the exact label varies slightly by DISCOM interface. The list shows vendor names, registration numbers, coverage districts, and often a contact number.

### Step 3: Search by District

Filter by your district. In densely populated states like Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, you will see dozens of empanelled vendors. In smaller states or rural districts, the list may be shorter, but it will still show you who is genuinely authorised.

### Step 4: Cross-Check the Registration Number

When a vendor gives you a quote, ask them directly for their DISCOM empanelment registration number. Then go back to the portal list and confirm that number appears, that it matches the company name, and that the registration validity date has not expired. Some empanelments are time-limited and must be renewed annually.

### Step 5: Verify Their ALMM Equipment

Ask the vendor which specific inverter model and solar panel brand they plan to install. Look up both on the [MNRE ALMM list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/). The ALMM list is a live document that is updated quarterly, confirm you are checking the current version, not a printed screenshot from six months ago.

## The 5-Point Vendor Verification Checklist: Before You Sign Anything

This checklist is the named framework for every homeowner going through the PM Surya Ghar process. Run through all five points before paying any advance or signing a supply agreement.

### The 5-Point Vendor Verification Checklist

1. **DISCOM registration number confirmed on the portal**: log into pmsuryaghar.gov.in, find the vendor list for your state and district, and visually confirm the vendor's name and registration number match what they have given you. A vendor who hesitates to share their registration number or gives a number that does not appear on the portal is not empanelled.

2. **ALMM certificate for the inverter model**: request the ALMM certificate document number and cross-check it against the live MNRE list. The inverter brand name alone is not sufficient, the specific model number must be listed. Many brands have some models listed and others not.

3. **ALMM certificate for the solar panel brand and model**: solar panels fall under a separate ALMM category from inverters. Verify both independently. Some vendors use a listed inverter with unlisted panels, which fails inspection just as definitively.

4. **Warranty terms in writing, before you pay**: get a written warranty document specifying: manufacturer warranty duration for each component (panels: 25-year performance, 10-year product; inverter: minimum 5 years; mounting structure: 10 years), and the vendor's own workmanship warranty (minimum 1 year). Verbal warranty promises are unenforceable.

5. **RMA process and subsidy processing timeline stated in writing**: ask: "If the inverter fails in year two, what is the exact replacement process and how long will it take?" A reliable vendor will have a clear answer, a registration number with the manufacturer, a documented escalation path, and a stated turnaround time. Also ask them to estimate the subsidy disbursement timeline based on your DISCOM's recent track record.

Any vendor who cannot answer points 1, 2, and 3 with documentary evidence has disqualified themselves. Points 4 and 5 separate a good vendor from a great one.

## Red Flags That Signal a Fake or Unregistered Vendor

Beyond the verification checklist, certain behaviours during the sales conversation are reliable warning signals. These patterns appear consistently in homeowner complaints filed on the PM Surya Ghar portal's grievance system.

- **No physical address, only a mobile number**: empanelled vendors must have a registered business address submitted to the DISCOM. A vendor who operates only through WhatsApp or gives a residential address instead of a business premises warrants investigation before proceeding.
- **"We will handle the subsidy paperwork for you, just pay us the full amount now"**: subsidy is paid directly into the homeowner's bank account by the government, not through the vendor. Any vendor who asks you to pay them the subsidy amount upfront or who says the subsidy will be added to their payment is misrepresenting how the scheme works.
- **Vague or oral warranty terms**: legitimate equipment manufacturers provide written warranty documents. If a vendor cannot produce a warranty card or certificate for the inverter model they are proposing, the equipment is likely grey-market or unregistered.
- **Pressure to decide immediately**: the PM Surya Ghar scheme is not a limited-time offer with the scheme closing next week. Vendors who manufacture urgency around subsidy windows are using a sales tactic, not conveying regulatory fact. You have time to verify.
- **Quoted price significantly below benchmark**: while bargaining on price is normal, a quote that comes in 30–40% below the state benchmark cost per kW should raise questions about what corners are being cut. This often signals unlisted equipment or unregistered labour.
- **No ALMM proof available**: this is the single most important red flag. A vendor who cannot produce ALMM documentation for their proposed equipment, or who says "ALMM does not matter for your application," is simply wrong about scheme rules and the subsidy will be at risk.

## What Empanelment Means for Equipment: The ALMM Connection

Empanelment and ALMM compliance are two separate but interlocking requirements under PM Surya Ghar. A vendor can be empanelled and still lose your subsidy by installing non-ALMM equipment. Understanding the distinction matters.

The [ALMM list (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers)](/glossary/almm-list/) is maintained by MNRE and covers two separate categories: solar photovoltaic modules and solar inverters. Each category has its own approval process, testing standards, and list. A component must appear in the correct category to satisfy scheme requirements.

For solar panels, the test standards include IEC 61215 and IEC 61730, internationally recognised quality benchmarks. For inverters, the BIS certification under IS 16221 / IEC 62109 is the primary gate, along with grid connectivity standards required under [Central Electricity Authority](https://cea.nic.in/) regulations.

| Component | ALMM Category | Key Standard | Test Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solar PV modules | Module ALMM | IEC 61215, IEC 61730 | Annual renewal |
| Solar inverters | Inverter ALMM | BIS IS 16221, IEC 62109 | Annual renewal |
| Battery (hybrid only) | Not ALMM-mandated | IEC 62619 recommended | N/A |

An empanelled vendor is contractually required to use only ALMM-listed equipment. But the verification is ultimately your responsibility, the DISCOM inspection at commissioning will check model numbers, and if anything is unlisted, the application stops there. Do not assume the vendor has done this check; do it yourself using the 5-Point Checklist above.

## Questions to Ask Any Vendor Before You Sign

Most homeowners approach the vendor conversation as a price negotiation. The more important conversation is a qualification interview. These are the seven questions that reveal everything you need to know:

- **"What is your DISCOM empanelment registration number for my district, and can I look it up on the portal right now?"**: a confident, immediate answer is a positive signal.
- **"Which specific inverter model are you proposing, and can you show me its ALMM certificate document number?"**: the answer must name both the brand and the exact SKU.
- **"Which solar panel brand and model, and is it on the current ALMM module list?"**: same specificity required.
- **"What is the manufacturer's warranty on the inverter, and how do I register a warranty claim if it fails?"**: the answer should include the manufacturer's name, warranty duration, and a contact number or portal for claims.
- **"What is the per-kW cost you are charging, and how does it compare to the state benchmark?"**: a legitimate vendor will answer this without hesitation and will not charge above the benchmark.
- **"How long does subsidy disbursement typically take in my DISCOM?"**: a vendor with real experience in your area will have a concrete answer based on recent cases.
- **"If there is a problem after installation (panels not generating expected yield, inverter tripping) what is your workmanship warranty and process?"**: the answer reveals their after-sales commitment.

## What Happens If an Empanelled Vendor Does a Poor Job

The empanelment structure does not just protect you before installation, it gives you a formal recourse channel if things go wrong after installation. This is one of the most underappreciated advantages of working strictly within the scheme.

### The Grievance Mechanism on the PM Surya Ghar Portal

After logging into [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), registered applicants can navigate to the grievance filing section. A complaint requires:

- Your application reference number
- The vendor's registration number
- A description of the problem (poor generation, incomplete installation, documentation not submitted, equipment substitution)
- Supporting evidence where available (photographs, WhatsApp messages, invoices showing different equipment than contracted)

The complaint is routed to the state DISCOM's nodal officer for PM Surya Ghar. The DISCOM is required to acknowledge the grievance within 7 days and resolve it within 30 days. For unresolved complaints, escalation is possible to the state electricity regulatory commission.

### What Enforcement Looks Like

When complaints pile up against a specific vendor, the DISCOM can:

- Issue a show-cause notice requiring the vendor to respond within a stated period
- Suspend the vendor's empanelment, barring them from taking new applications
- Cancel the empanelment permanently, removing them from the portal list
- Refer the matter to state consumer protection authorities if fraud is suspected

This enforcement chain is what makes empanelment meaningful, it is not just a piece of paper. Vendors with a pattern of complaints lose their registration and their ability to work under the scheme. That accountability loop does not exist at all when you hire outside the official channel.

> **30 days.** The maximum time the PM Surya Ghar portal's grievance mechanism allows for DISCOM response to a consumer complaint filed against an empanelled vendor. *Source - [PM Surya Ghar Citizen Charter](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

## Subsidy Eligibility at Risk: The Most Expensive Mistake in Solar

Every year, a significant number of PM Surya Ghar applicants fail at the commissioning inspection stage, not because their roof was unsuitable or their paperwork was wrong, but because their vendor was not properly empanelled or because the installed equipment was not ALMM-listed. The subsidy is forfeited in its entirety.

The financial consequence is stark. For a 3 kW system, losing the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy means forfeiting ₹78,000 that the government was prepared to transfer directly to your bank account. In states with additional state subsidies (Gujarat, Maharashtra, UP, Tamil Nadu) the total loss including state top-ups can exceed ₹1 Lakh.

There is no appeals process for a commissioning failure due to non-ALMM equipment. The applicant must either have the non-compliant equipment replaced (at their cost, since the vendor is responsible but often unwilling) or start a fresh application with a different empanelled vendor.
