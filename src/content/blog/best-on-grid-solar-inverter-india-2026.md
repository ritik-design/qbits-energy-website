## Who Should Choose an On-Grid Solar Inverter

Before reviewing individual products, it is worth confirming that on-grid is the right topology for your situation. Choosing the wrong inverter type wastes money at the start and frustrates you every time the grid trips.

On-grid solar is the correct choice when:

- **You live in a metro or industrial zone** — cities such as Bangalore, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Ahmedabad, and Surat typically have grid availability above 22 hours per day, which is the threshold where on-grid payback beats hybrid payback.
- **Your primary goal is subsidised electricity, not backup power** — the PM Surya Ghar scheme is designed for on-grid systems. Hybrid systems can qualify in some configurations, but the core subsidy structure rewards grid-connected net metering.
- **Your monthly bill exceeds ₹5,000** — at this consumption level, the economics of on-grid strongly favour payback within 4 to 6 years, compared to 7 to 10 years for hybrid systems with battery storage.
- **Your DISCOM actively supports net metering** — states including Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Rajasthan, and Telangana have functional net metering frameworks that credit surplus units against your future bills.
- **You are maximising the PM Surya Ghar subsidy** — the central government currently offers ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 per kW for the third kW, capped at ₹78,000 for 3 kW, paid directly to your bank account. Most PM Surya Ghar installs use on-grid inverters.

On-grid is not the right choice if your area experiences more than four hours of daily outages, if your DISCOM has not enabled net metering in your area, or if you run critical equipment — medical devices, industrial machinery — that cannot tolerate even momentary interruptions. ## The On-Grid Inverter Selection Scorecard — 5 Must-Check Specs

Most homeowners walk into an inverter purchase comparing only price and brand name. That approach is how you end up with a cheaper unit that fails at month 18 and carries a 5-year warranty from a company with no service centre within 200 kilometres of your roof. The On-Grid Inverter Selection Scorecard gives you five objective criteria that predict long-term performance better than any brochure.

1. **ALMM Phase III listing** — mandatory for PM Surya Ghar subsidy; verify on the MNRE portal the day you finalise.
2. **Anti-islanding compliance** — BIS IS 16169 and IEC 62116 certificates must be on the datasheet, not just mentioned.
3. **Input voltage range 180–270 V** — the true Indian-grid tolerance, not the European 220–240 V band.
4. **Efficiency at 45 °C** — ask for the derating curve, not just the 25 °C peak figure.
5. **Warranty depth and serviceability** — full replacement, India-registered entity, sub-72-hour RMA SLA.

Apply these as pass/fail gates before comparing price. The detailed criteria for each follow.

### 1. ALMM Phase III Listing

The [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers), maintained by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, is the single most important filter for any homeowner pursuing the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Without ALMM Phase III listing, your DISCOM will not approve net metering under the scheme, and the subsidy will not reach your bank account. Score: a listed model earns full marks; an unlisted model is automatically disqualified for subsidy-linked projects.

Check the current ALMM list directly at the [MNRE portal](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) before finalising any purchase. The list is updated quarterly, and models that were listed six months ago may have expired certification if manufacturers missed renewal deadlines.

### 2. Anti-Islanding Compliance (BIS IS 16169 + IEC 62116)

[Anti-islanding](/glossary/anti-islanding/) protection is not optional — it is a legal requirement. BIS IS 16169 governs grid-interactive inverters in India and mandates that the inverter cease power export within two seconds of detecting a grid outage. This is tested per IEC 62116 methodology. An inverter without certified anti-islanding creates an electrocution hazard for utility lineworkers and will be flagged during DISCOM commissioning inspection. Look for explicit mention of BIS IS 16169 and IEC 62116 in the product datasheet.

### 3. Wide Input Voltage Range (180–270 V AC)

Indian grid voltage is nominally 230 V per [BIS standards for LT supply](https://www.bis.gov.in/), but real-world supply in many Tier-2 and Tier-3 areas swings between 180 V and 270 V throughout the day. An inverter tuned only for the European 220–240 V band will trip frequently under Indian conditions, reducing your generation hours and increasing thermal stress on components. Look for an inverter with a documented AC output voltage range of at least 180–270 V and ask the dealer whether the unit's firmware has been calibrated for Indian grid parameters.

### 4. Peak Efficiency at 45 °C, Not Just 25 °C

Every inverter datasheet lists peak efficiency — but the fine print matters. Rated efficiency is measured at 25 °C ambient in a laboratory. On an Indian rooftop in May, the ambient temperature around the inverter can reach 45 °C to 50 °C, and actual efficiency drops by 2–5 percentage points depending on the inverter's thermal management design. Always ask: what is the manufacturer's rated efficiency at 45 °C operating temperature? Brands that publish this figure honestly are the ones engineering for India; brands that only publish the 25 °C figure are optimising for marketing.

### 5. Warranty Depth and Serviceability

A 5-year warranty is the industry minimum. A 10-year warranty is adequate. A 12-year full replacement warranty — where the manufacturer replaces the entire unit, not just a prorated component — is the benchmark that signals genuine engineering confidence. Pair warranty length with an honest assessment of the service network: can the brand replace a faulty unit within 72 hours in your pincode? "12 years" from a company with no India-registered service entity is not a warranty — it is a marketing claim.

Apply these five criteria as pass/fail gates. Any product that fails two or more of them is off the list, regardless of how attractive the price looks at the time of purchase. The cheapest on-grid inverter that fails after year three — with a disputed warranty claim and a six-month wait for a replacement board — costs far more than the premium model you should have bought in the first place.

## Why Anti-Islanding Matters for Grid Safety and DISCOM Approval

Anti-islanding is one of the most misunderstood features of on-grid solar inverters. Many homeowners assume it is a technicality that matters only to engineers. In practice, it determines whether your DISCOM will approve your commissioning and whether your system can legally export power to the grid.

When the utility grid trips — due to a fault, maintenance work, or a local transformer issue — the inverter must detect the outage and stop exporting power within two seconds. This is not arbitrary: if an inverter continues to feed power into an apparently "dead" section of the grid while lineworkers are working on it, the consequences can be fatal.

The Indian testing standard for anti-islanding is BIS IS 16169, which is harmonised with [IEC 62116](https://www.iec.ch/) — the international standard for utility-interconnected photovoltaic inverter testing. Type-testing under this standard simulates a range of grid-loss scenarios and verifies that the inverter's anti-islanding algorithm reliably trips within the required window. A BIS IS 16169 certificate number should appear on the product's compliance documentation.

From the homeowner's perspective, the practical consequence of an inverter without certified anti-islanding is straightforward: the DISCOM inspection team will reject your installation during commissioning, you will not receive net-metering approval, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy disbursement will be blocked.

Every inverter reviewed in this guide carries certified anti-islanding protection per BIS IS 16169 and IEC 62116. That is a minimum requirement for inclusion in this list.

## Five Mistakes That Derail On-Grid Inverter Purchases

These are the most common errors homeowners and their installers make when buying on-grid inverters. Each one is avoidable with 30 minutes of pre-purchase verification.

- **Choosing on ALMM status from six months ago** — ALMM listing is renewed periodically. A model that was listed when you started researching may have had its certification lapse by the time you sign the purchase order. Check the live MNRE portal on the day you finalise. See the [ALMM Phase III guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) for renewal timelines.

- **Ignoring the input voltage specification** — dealers often quote only the "nominal" 230 V input without disclosing the minimum input voltage at which the inverter will operate without tripping. In areas where grid voltage drops to 190 V on summer evenings, an inverter rated for 200 V minimum will trip repeatedly, reducing your generation hours and adding stress to internal components.

- **Oversizing based on sanctioned load, not actual consumption** — your DISCOM typically allows you to install up to your sanctioned connected load. But if your actual daily consumption is much lower, an oversized system generates surplus units that the DISCOM may not credit at a favourable rate under your state's net-metering tariff policy. Size to your actual consumption plus a 10–15% growth buffer. The [solar inverter sizing guide](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) covers the methodology.

- **Skipping the anti-islanding certificate check** — a BIS type-test certificate for IS 16169 is a formal document with a registration number. Ask your dealer for a copy. If they cannot produce it, the inverter has not been tested to Indian standards, regardless of what the datasheet says.

- **Treating warranty length as equivalent across brands** — a 12-year warranty from a brand with an India-registered entity and a pincode-searchable service network is worth the premium over a 10-year warranty from a brand whose nearest service centre is a three-day courier away. Before signing, call the service helpline and ask: "If my unit fails in [your city] next Tuesday, what is the replacement timeline?" The answer tells you more than any brochure.
