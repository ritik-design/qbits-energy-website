## Why Installers Look for a Scanifly Alternative

Scanifly carved out a strong niche by leading with drone-based 3D capture. The output is high fidelity, the as-built models are excellent, and installers who already run a drone fleet got a clear workflow. Most installers shopping for an alternative are not unhappy with Scanifly's accuracy. They are unhappy with the operational overhead, the volume-linked economics, and the fact that Scanifly is scoped to one part of the job.

### Drone dependency

The Scanifly workflow is built around drone capture, which requires a pilot, FAA Part 107 compliance in the US (or local equivalents), insurance, weather windows, and travel, on top of the [site survey](/glossary/site-survey/) work most teams already do. For a 100-system-per-year installer, that is real money and real calendar friction. Teams that try to scale find drone ops becomes a bottleneck.

### Per-project pricing

Scanifly does not publish list prices, so pricing is quote-based and reported figures vary. Buyers commonly describe volume-linked, per-project arrangements. That structure is fine at low volume and gets harder to justify at high volume, because the bill tracks project count rather than headcount. Installers crossing 100 projects a year should price their own quote against a flat per-seat licence for the rest of the [solar design platform](https://surgepv.com/solar-designing) before assuming either is cheaper.

### Measurement-focused scope

Scanifly is positioned around survey and measurement. Once the 3D model exists, the design, 8,760-hour shading, financial model, BOQ, single-line diagram, AutoCAD export, and proposal generally happen in other tools. Each handoff is a copy-paste step.

### Site-visit cost for residential

For residential rooftops, satellite imagery is now sharp enough that in our own testing AI-driven 3D modelling gets close to drone-grade accuracy on most homes. Paying a drone fee plus a pilot's time for a 6 kW residential system rarely pencils out. Our full [satellite roof measurement breakdown](/blog/satellite-roof-measurement-solar/) covers exactly where the accuracy holds up and where it does not.
