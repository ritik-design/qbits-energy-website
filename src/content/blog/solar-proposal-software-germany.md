The Solarspitzengesetz removed EEG payment entitlement during negative wholesale price intervals for systems commissioned after 25 February 2025. Negative price hours are no longer rare in a grid with this much installed PV, and they cluster in exactly the sunny midday periods when the array exports most. So the old calculation, exported kWh multiplied by the EEG rate, now overstates near-term feed-in income. Add the 60% feed-in cap for systems without smart meter control, and a German proposal built on a simple export assumption can be materially wrong. ## What A German Solar Proposal Has To Get Right

**The right EEG rate for the right commissioning window.** Rates step down on a published schedule, and the rate locked at commissioning applies for the full 20 year term. Quoting the current figure on a project that will energise after the next step-down overstates two decades of income. The proposal should state the assumed commissioning window explicitly.

**Volleinspeisung versus Überschusseinspeisung.** At 12.34 ct/kWh full feed-in against 7.78 ct/kWh partial, the full feed-in rate looks better in isolation. It is usually not, because self-consumed energy avoids a retail import price several times higher than either rate. The correct answer depends on the household load profile, which requires an hourly model rather than an annual total.

**The Solarspitzengesetz negative-price rule.** For systems commissioned after 25 February 2025, no EEG payment accrues during negative wholesale price intervals, though the time is added to the end of the term. Near-term cashflow drops while total term extends. Customers evaluating payback care about the former.

**VDE-AR-N 4105 compliance.** Protection relay settings, anti-islanding, and inverter power quality requirements all sit here, with the 2026-03 revision simplifying balcony system rules. These are connection prerequisites, not optional checks. SurgePV's [Europe compliance library](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/europe) covers the German requirements alongside the EU-level rules.

**The 60% feed-in cap.** Without a smart meter and control device, feed-in is limited to 60% of installed capacity. Systems of 7 kWp and above need smart meter control for full feed-in. This is a BOQ item with a direct revenue consequence.

## The Negative-Price Problem Most Tools Ignore

> **What most German proposals get wrong.** They compute feed-in income as exported kWh multiplied by the EEG rate. Since February 2025 that formula has been structurally optimistic, because the hours with the most export are increasingly the hours with negative wholesale prices and therefore no EEG payment.

The mechanism is worth understanding because it changes what a good design looks like. Negative prices occur when generation exceeds demand, which in Germany means sunny, windy, low-demand periods. Those are precisely the hours when a south-facing array is at maximum output. So the correlation is unhelpful: the array exports most exactly when the export is worth least.

Three design responses follow, and a proposal that models the effect can argue for all three. East-west array orientation flattens the generation curve and shifts output away from the midday peak. Battery storage converts midday export into evening self-consumption. Load shifting, particularly heat pumps and EV charging, does the same thing without hardware.

None of those arguments can be made from an annual kWh total. They need an hourly model, ideally one that can overlay a price curve. ## Worked Example: 9.6 kWp Near Stuttgart, South Versus East-West

An illustrative calculation showing why orientation now interacts with the tariff rule. Inputs: 9.6 kWp, 1,020 kWh per kWp specific yield, 9,792 kWh generation, 4,200 kWh household consumption, import at 32 ct per kWh, partial feed-in at 7.78 ct per kWh.

| Line | South-facing | East-west split |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual generation | 9,792 kWh | 9,204 kWh |
| Self-consumed | 2,350 kWh | 3,020 kWh |
| Exported | 7,442 kWh | 6,184 kWh |
| Export in negative-price hours (uncompensated) | 1,190 kWh | 680 kWh |
| Compensated export | 6,252 kWh | 5,504 kWh |
| Value of avoided import at 32 ct | EUR 752 | EUR 966 |
| Feed-in income at 7.78 ct | EUR 486 | EUR 428 |
| Total year one benefit | EUR 1,238 | EUR 1,394 |

The east-west array generates 6% less electricity and returns 13% more money in year one. The flatter curve raises self-consumption and reduces exposure to the uncompensated midday hours. A tool that ranks designs on annual kWh recommends the south array. A tool that models hours and tariff rules recommends the east-west one, and it is right.
