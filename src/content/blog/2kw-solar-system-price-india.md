## What a 2kW Solar System Can Actually Power

This is where most homeowners get surprised, in the right or wrong direction. The rating of the solar system (2kW) is the *capacity* of the inverter and panels. It does not mean you can run exactly 2kW of appliances at all times. Here is the realistic picture:

Table: Daily consumption of a typical 2 BHK household with one 1-ton AC

| Load | Wattage | Hours/Day | Units/Day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-ton AC (5-star BEE) | 750 W | 6 h | 4.5 units |
| Refrigerator (250L) | 150 W | 8 h (compressor cycle) | 1.2 units |
| LED lighting (8 bulbs) | 80 W | 5 h | 0.4 units |
| Ceiling fans (4) | 120 W | 8 h | 1.0 units |
| Washing machine | 500 W | 1 h | 0.5 units |
| Miscellaneous | 100 W | 3 h | 0.3 units |
| **Total household consumption** | | | **~7.9 units/day** |

A 2kW system generating 8 units/day in a city like Bengaluru or Hyderabad can effectively offset nearly all of this household's solar-hours consumption. During peak sunshine (10 AM to 3 PM), the system produces its maximum output. Morning and evening loads (when the sun is low) still draw from the grid.

## The 4-Factor 2kW System Sizing Test

Use this framework before committing to a 2kW system to verify it is the right size for your home:

### The 4-Factor 2kW System Sizing Test

1. **Monthly bill check**: Your monthly bill should be ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 for a 2kW system to be the appropriate size. Below ₹3,000, a [1kW system](/blog/1kw-solar-system-price-india/) is sufficient. Above ₹8,000, consider a 3kW system.

2. **AC count**: A 2kW system comfortably serves one 1-ton AC plus household base load. If you run two ACs simultaneously during peak summer, you need at least 3kW. Each additional 1-ton AC adds approximately 1kW to your recommended system size. The [3kW vs 5kW vs 10kW capacity guide](/blog/3kw-vs-5kw-vs-10kw-solar-inverters/) breaks down generation and cost at each step above 2kW if your load keeps growing.

3. **Roof orientation**: A south-facing roof with zero shading between 9 AM and 4 PM is ideal. An east-west orientation can reduce annual output compared with a south-facing roof (see our [east-west roof design guide](/blog/east-west-roof-solar-design/)), so you may need to upsize to match the same annual generation. Use the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/) to verify your configuration.

4. **Future load growth**: If you plan to buy an EV in the next 3–5 years or add a second AC, sizing up to 3kW now adds ₹45,000 at the MNRE benchmark (₹1,45,000 against ₹1,00,000) and ₹18,000 of extra central subsidy, so about ₹27,000 more net, which can be cheaper than upgrading the system later.

> [!TIP] Count your ACs before you pick the size
> One 1-ton AC fits a 2kW system. Each extra 1-ton AC adds about 1kW to the recommended size, and an east-west roof may need more capacity for the same annual generation.

## Component Deep Dive: What to Verify in Your Quotation

The total system cost matters less than whether each component is specified correctly. Here is what to check:

### Solar Panels for a 2kW System

A 2kW system uses 4 panels at 530–540 Wp each. Panels must be monocrystalline PERC or TOPCon technology for Indian rooftop conditions, polycrystalline panels are increasingly obsolete and offer lower temperature coefficient performance on 55–65 °C summer rooftops.

- **Product warranty**: minimum 10 years (covers manufacturing defects)
- **Performance warranty**: minimum 25 years, guaranteeing at least 80% of rated power at end of life
- **ALMM listing**: mandatory for PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility. Ask for the ALMM certificate number.

### Inverter for a 2kW System

A 2kW [on-grid inverter](/on-grid-inverter/) is a single-[MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) string inverter with a nominal output of 2kW and a maximum DC input capacity of 2.4–3.2 kW (to allow [DC oversizing](/glossary/dc-oversizing/)). Key specifications to verify:

- **[BIS certification](/glossary/bis-certification/)** under IS 16221, non-negotiable for grid connectivity
- **Voltage range**: the allowed AC grid range depends on the model and on your DISCOM's connection conditions, so read it from the exact model's datasheet
- **[Peak efficiency](/glossary/inverter-efficiency/)**: 97% or above minimises conversion losses
- **Monitoring**: Wi-Fi or 4G monitoring to track daily generation via app

See the full [on-grid inverter buying guide](/blog/how-to-choose-solar-inverter-for-home-india/) for specification details, and see our [QB 3.0KTLS single-phase on-grid inverter](/our-products/QB-1.5-4.0KTLS/) for a single-phase example.

### Balance of System (BOS)

- **Cable cross-section**: DC cables should be at least 4mm² (6mm² preferred) for runs longer than 10 metres
- **[SPD](/glossary/spd/) rating**: at least 600V DC for the panel string side; 20kA minimum discharge capacity
- **[MCB](/glossary/mcb/) and [RCCB](/glossary/rccb/)**: properly rated for the system's maximum current; [isolator](/glossary/isolator/) on DC side mandatory per CEA norms
- **[Mounting structure](/glossary/mounting-structure/)**: hot-dipped GI or marine-grade aluminium; wind load rated for your local wind zone per IS 875

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Process for a 2kW System

The subsidy process for 2kW follows the same steps as 1kW but with a larger credit amount. The key documents required are:

- **Electricity consumer account number** (must be residential category)
- **Aadhaar card** linked to the account
- **Bank account details** for direct credit
- **Roof ownership proof** (if registered in a different name from the electricity account)

After installation and DISCOM inspection, the ₹60,000 subsidy is credited to your bank account; the time taken varies by DISCOM and by portal processing, so ask your installer and your DISCOM for their current timeline and check the status on the portal. Refer to the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) and the [eligibility page](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-eligibility/) for the full document list.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid with a 2kW Solar System

The most common errors when purchasing a 2kW system are avoidable with a little preparation:

- **Not accounting for cable length**: Long roof-to-inverter DC cable runs increase voltage drop and heat. Runs longer than 15 metres should use 6mm² cable. Ask your installer to specify cable length and cross-section in writing.
- **Undersizing the BOS for export**: If your DISCOM allows net metering and your evening consumption is low, your 2kW system may export up to 8 units/day. Ensure your isolator and MCB ratings account for this bidirectional current.
- **Skipping monitoring**: Without app-based monitoring, you have no visibility into whether your system is performing as expected. A faulty connection or inverter fault can go undetected for weeks, costing 50–100 units of lost generation.

> [!WARNING] A silent fault is lost money
> A 2kW system generates about 8 units a day, so a fault nobody sees for a week or two costs the 50 to 100 units this guide counts as lost generation.
