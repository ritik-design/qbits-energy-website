## Who Lends to Businesses for Solar in 2026

Four lender categories serve the business solar market, and each plays a different role. Knowing which one fits your profile saves weeks of rejected applications.

1. **Public and private sector banks.** SBI, HDFC Bank, PNB, Bank of Baroda, and Axis all run SME green or solar-specific products. Typical terms: 10 to 12.5 percent interest, 5 to 7 year tenure, 70 to 75 percent of project cost, minimum ticket around ₹25 lakh. Banks are the most accessible route if you already bank with them, but usually the most expensive. Our bank-specific guides cover the consumer side in detail: [SBI solar loan](/blog/solar-loan-sbi/) and [HDFC solar loan](/blog/hdfc-solar-loan-india-2026/).
2. **Development finance institutions.** SIDBI funds MSME energy efficiency and rooftop solar directly through schemes such as 4E. IREDA, the government's dedicated renewable energy lender, finances C&I solar at concessional rates. These are the cheapest rupees in the market, but the paperwork is heavier and timelines longer.
3. **NBFCs and green financiers.** Ecofy, Tata Capital, and similar lenders offer fast, lightly documented rooftop solar loans. Ecofy's MSME rooftop product spans ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 crore with tenures of 6 to 60 months. That range comes from [Ecofy's rooftop solar loan page](https://www.ecofy.co.in/rooftop-solar), 2025. Rates range from 8 to 24 percent depending on creditworthiness, so this route rewards strong balance sheets and punishes weak ones.
4. **EPC-arranged financing.** Many installers have tie-ups with banks or NBFCs and push your file through a known desk. Convenient, but always compare the quoted rate against a direct application.

Eligibility baseline across all four is similar. Lenders want Udyam Registration and at least two years of operating history. They also want a bureau score above roughly 700 for promoters and debt-service coverage of 1.5 times or better. Startups under two years old usually get routed to NBFCs or asked for a co-applicant.

One more naming note, because it confuses borrowers. A business solar loan is not a home improvement loan with a green label. It is asset-backed project finance assessed on plant cash flows. Lenders who understand solar will ask for your electricity bills before your balance sheet, because the avoided tariff is the real repayment source.

## CGTMSE: The Collateral-Free Route Most MSMEs Miss

**CGTMSE** (Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises) is a Government of India guarantee scheme operated with SIDBI. When a member bank lends to an eligible MSE under CGTMSE, the trust guarantees up to 75 percent of the loan amount. The guarantee ceiling is ₹5 crore since April 2023, according to [HDFC Bank's CGTMSE scheme page](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/msme-banking/msme-government-schemes/credit-guarantee-fund-trust-for-micro-and-small-enterprises-cgtmse), 2026.

Three things matter for a solar borrower:

- **No collateral or third-party guarantee.** The bank's risk is covered by the trust, so it can drop the demand for property security. This is the single biggest benefit for MSMEs that rent their premises or have already mortgaged their factory.
- **A guarantee fee applies.** The trust charges an annual guarantee fee on the covered amount, which the bank passes to you. Even with the fee, total cost usually stays below a collateralised loan at a higher rate.
- **You must ask for it.** Banks do not always offer CGTMSE cover proactively. Tell the credit officer explicitly that you want the solar loan booked under CGTMSE. Also confirm your Udyam Registration is current, since eligibility is checked against it.

The practical ceiling effect is large. A micro or small enterprise can realistically raise a collateral-free solar loan of ₹1 to ₹5 crore through a PSU bank. That covers rooftop plants up to roughly 1 MW at 2026 prices.

## SIDBI and IREDA: Cheapest Money, Heaviest Paperwork

SIDBI's 4E (End to End Energy Efficiency) scheme finances MSME energy-saving and renewable projects at concessional rates around 7 to 8 percent. It can fund up to 100 percent of eligible project cost, according to [SIDBI's scheme summaries](https://www.sidbi.in/), 2026. Eligibility hinges on Udyam Registration, a satisfactory credit history, and a project that passes a technical energy assessment. Expect 4 to 8 weeks from application to sanction.

IREDA is the better fit for larger C&I projects. It lends at roughly 8.5 percent for commercial and industrial solar, according to [IREDA](https://www.ireda.in/), 2026. Commercial banks quote 10.5 to 11.5 percent, so the saving runs 150 to 300 basis points. On a ₹2.5 crore loan, that spread is worth ₹35 to ₹75 lakh over the tenure. IREDA typically wants 20 to 30 percent promoter equity, a bankable detailed project report, and security over project assets. Timelines run 6 to 12 weeks.

Our rule of thumb from structuring C&I deals: below ₹50 lakh, the paperwork cost of DFI money often outweighs the rate saving. A bank or NBFC is the pragmatic route there. Above ₹1 crore, always run the IREDA or SIDBI process in parallel with your bank. ## Tax Interplay: Depreciation and Interest Deduction

The tax treatment is where business solar diverges completely from residential. A homeowner gets a subsidy; a business gets the Income Tax Act.

- **Accelerated depreciation.** Solar plant and machinery sits in the 40 percent written-down-value block. Eligible businesses can also claim 40 percent additional depreciation in the first year under Section 32. So up to 80 percent of plant cost can be depreciated in year one. Our [solar depreciation guide](/blog/solar-inverter-depreciation-in-india/) summarises the Income Tax Act provisions. At a 25 percent corporate tax rate, the year-one depreciation on a ₹42 lakh plant is worth roughly ₹8.4 lakh in tax saved.
- **Interest deduction.** Loan interest is a business expense, deductible in full. In year one of the example above, interest is about ₹3.2 lakh, saving another ₹80,000 in tax.
- **GST input credit.** Businesses can claim input tax credit on the GST paid on plant and installation where the output supply is taxable. That effectively cuts net capex by the GST amount.

Stack these on the cash-flow table and the picture changes. First-year tax savings of ₹9 lakh or more sit against ₹10.5 lakh of equity. For a profitable company, the owner's actual out-of-pocket is nearly recovered within the first year. This is why we tell MSME clients the loan-versus-cash question is often the wrong one; the real question is loan versus RESCO.

The exception: your entity may have no taxable profits, as with a new unit or a loss-making year. In that case these benefits are worth nothing this year, and the ownership case weakens sharply.

## Loan vs RESCO: The Decision Framework

**RESCO** (also called OPEX or PPA solar) means a developer installs, owns, and maintains the plant on your roof. You buy the power at an agreed tariff, typically ₹3.5 to ₹5 per unit with an annual escalator. Our [RESCO model guide](/blog/solar-resco-model-india-guide/) and [CAPEX vs OPEX comparison](/blog/solar-capex-vs-opex-india-guide/) go deep on the structure. Here is the decision in four questions.

1. **Do you have taxable profits this year?** Yes favours the loan. The depreciation and interest deductions are only valuable against real tax liability. No favours RESCO.
2. **Can you commit the roof for 15 to 25 years?** RESCO contracts are long and include lock-ins and buyout clauses. If your lease is short or relocation is possible, a loan on a plant you own and can move or sell is safer.
3. **Is capital better used elsewhere?** If your business earns 20 percent on working capital, handing ₹10.5 lakh of equity to a solar plant may still win. Financed solar returns its savings steadily and is cash-positive from month one. Run the numbers, not the intuition.
4. **What is the all-in cost of power?** Owned and financed solar lands near ₹2.5 to ₹3.5 per unit levelised over 25 years. RESCO starts around ₹3.5 to ₹5 and escalates. Ownership wins on cost; RESCO wins on effort, since the developer handles O&M and performance risk.

Honest tradeoff: RESCO is not a bad product. For a profitable services firm in a rented office with a 9-year lease, RESCO is often the only sensible route. For a manufacturing MSME with owned premises and steady profits, borrowing and owning is almost always worth several lakh rupees more over the plant's life.

## Five Mistakes MSMEs Make With Business Solar Loans

1. **Borrowing short against a long asset.** A 3-year loan on a 25-year plant makes EMIs exceed savings. Match tenure to at least 7 years where possible, or use IREDA's 10 to 15 year terms.
2. **Ignoring CGTMSE.** Pledging property when a government guarantee would do. Always ask for the guarantee cover first.
3. **Quoting the wrong tariff.** Using the headline slab rate instead of the blended landed cost from actual bills. That overstates savings and can flip the cash-flow sign.
4. **Forgetting the O&M and insurance lines.** Budget 1 to 1.5 percent of plant cost per year for operations, maintenance, and insurance. Lenders increasingly ask for an O&M contract before sanction.
5. **Buying the cheapest inverter to cut project cost.** Lenders and DISCOMs both care about plant quality. A failed inverter in year six wipes out a year of savings. The [inverter financing options guide](/blog/inverter-financing-options-in-india/) covers how equipment choice interacts with loan sizing. Specify ALMM-listed, long-warranty equipment in the DPR; it strengthens the loan file and the plant.

## How to Apply: Documents and Timeline

A clean file sanctions in 2 to 4 weeks at a bank or NBFC, and 4 to 12 weeks at SIDBI or IREDA. Prepare these before you approach any lender:

- Udyam Registration certificate and promoter KYC.
- Three years of financials, ITRs, GST returns, and 12 months of bank statements.
- Last 12 electricity bills showing sanctioned load and tariff.
- Roof ownership proof or a registered lease with 10-plus years remaining.
- A detailed project report from your EPC: itemised cost, generation estimate, equipment datasheets, and payback analysis.
- The EPC's credentials, since lenders check installer track record for larger tickets.

Two process tips from deals we have seen sanctioned faster. First, submit the DPR with a generation estimate from a recognised tool and P50 and P90 scenarios; credit officers trust conservative numbers more than optimistic ones. Second, apply in the first half of the financial year. Bank SME targets reset in April, and files move measurably faster between April and September than in the March rush.
