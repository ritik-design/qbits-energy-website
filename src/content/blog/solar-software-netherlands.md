Dutch solar software has a deadline. The salderingsregeling ends on 1 January 2027, with termination approved by the Senate in December 2024, and from that date suppliers set export compensation, expected in the region of 5 to 10 cents per kWh against a government floor. Annual netting, the mechanism nearly every Dutch quoting tool is built around, simply stops applying. Residential payback projections move from roughly 7 to 9 years to roughly 12 to 17. Any system being sold today has most of its life on the far side of that change, and most proposals do not say so. This guide covers what the Dutch stack has to do now.

> - Salderingsregeling ends 1 January 2027; the Senate approved termination in December 2024.

> **The unusual thing about the Dutch situation.** Four of the six categories are unaffected by the biggest regulatory change in the market's history. The disruption is concentrated entirely in design and proposals, and specifically in whether those tools can model a world without annual netting. That makes the software decision unusually focused: you are not rebuilding a stack, you are replacing one capability that most incumbents do not have.

## What Is Genuinely Netherlands-Specific Right Now

**The termination date sits inside the payback period.** This is the crux. A system installed in 2026 with a projected payback beyond 2027 spends most of its economic life under rules that do not exist yet in most quoting tools. Presenting a single payback figure calculated on saldering is not optimistic, it is describing a scheme that ends partway through.

**Terugleverkosten already bite.** Several supplier tariffs apply charges to customers who feed back, and under those tariffs export is already negative-value. That matters for two reasons. It means some customers should be advised to raise self-consumption today rather than in 2027, and it means the "export is worthless" conversation is not hypothetical, which makes it easier to have.

**The expected 5 to 10 cent band with a floor.** Suppliers will set the rate, so it will vary, and the government floor prevents negative compensation. For modelling this means a range rather than a number, and a proposal should show sensitivity across it rather than picking one.

**Hourly self-consumption becomes the only meaningful metric.** Annual generation and annual consumption can match perfectly while the household exports every midday and imports every evening. Under saldering that was fine. After 2027 it is the difference between a good system and a disappointing one.

## Where Dutch Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Underbought: hourly self-consumption modelling.** The defining gap. Most Dutch quoting has never needed it, because annual netting made timing irrelevant. Tools bought on that basis are about to be structurally inadequate.

**Underbought: battery dispatch simulation.** The natural response to the change is storage, and selling storage credibly requires showing what it shifts rather than asserting a benefit.

**Underbought: two-period proposal modelling.** Very few tools can show the customer a saldering period and a post-saldering period in one document, and that is now the honest shape of a Dutch proposal.

**Overbought: nothing much, in fairness.** Dutch teams tend to run lean stacks. The problem here is not overspend, it is that the core tool is about to stop describing reality.
