**There is no universal minimum investment for a solar dealership in India.** The correct number is the highest cash shortfall in your own launch plan, using the actual programme fee, stock commitment, setup costs, payment terms, customer collection timing, and contingency.

The current public Qbits partner page does not publish a minimum dealer investment. It asks applicants about working capital, loans, cash-credit limits, other capital, and infrastructure. Those questions are inputs for review, not a published price or approval threshold.

## What do current solar programmes actually publish?

Published amounts cannot be compared until you identify what each number buys. A registration payment, a demo purchase, opening stock, and total working capital are four different things.

The [Loom Solar dealer page](https://www.loomsolar.com/products/dealer-registration) and [Loom Solar distributor page](https://www.loomsolar.com/products/distributor-registration) were live and purchasable when checked on 26 September 2026. Their public product data dates the original listings to 26 October 2018, so applicants should reconfirm the full current terms before paying. These examples expose the central problem with search results about dealership cost. A small headline amount may be a registration item. Another amount may include stock. A third may describe an installer, franchise, or distributor rather than a dealer. None can safely fill the blank in a different manufacturer's proposal.

## Calculate required cash, not a headline investment

Build the budget from cash movements. Use this structure:

> **Required launch cash = programme commitments + setup cash + opening stock cash + peak operating cash gap + contingency**

Each term needs a written source.

| Budget block | Include | Evidence to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programme commitments | Registration fee, security, deposit, training charge, demo requirement, licence or onboarding payment | Dated programme sheet, invoice, refund terms |
| Setup cash | Premises deposit, fit-out, signage, computer, billing system, insurance, tools, safe storage | Supplier quotations and lease terms |
| Opening stock cash | Exact models, quantities, taxes, freight, transit insurance, unloading, demo units, service spares | Approved opening order and delivered-cost sheet |
| Operating cash gap | Payroll, rent, utilities, travel, local marketing, customer credit, tax timing, warranty logistics | Month-by-month cash-flow plan |
| Contingency | Cost increases, delayed collections, slow stock, damaged goods, delayed credit notes | A documented downside scenario chosen by the business |

Do not add every line from a generic dealership article. If you already have suitable premises, record the incremental cost rather than pretending to rent a second shop. If the role is resale only, do not budget an installation team that the agreement does not require. If service tools or spares are compulsory, do not hide them inside contingency.

The figure that matters is not total annual spending. It is the lowest point in the cumulative cash balance before customer collections, supplier credit, and approved refunds arrive.

After the launch budget is set, use the [working-capital and stock-finance guide](/blog/working-capital-solar-dealers/) to model repeated replenishment, customer collections, facility headroom, and a slow-stock case.

## Build the cash-timing sheet before choosing a number

A dealership can appear affordable on opening day and still run out of cash during replenishment. Put each expected movement on a timeline.

1. Enter only cash that is available on the calculation date.
2. Add each payment on its contractual due date.
3. Add customer collections on a conservative expected date, not the invoice date.
4. Add supplier credit only after it is approved in writing.
5. Keep GST, freight, deposits, and credit notes as separate lines.
6. Find the largest cumulative negative balance.
7. Add the contingency approved by the business owner and accountant.

## What most dealership cost estimates get wrong

The most common error is treating one visible payment as the whole investment. The Loom Solar example shows why that fails: the current dealer page lists a ₹5,000 registration product and separately discusses ₹25,000 to ₹1,00,000 of demo product.

Other errors can distort the result just as much:

- using a distributor stock requirement for a dealer application;
- counting a requested loan as approved cash;
- counting one cash-credit facility twice;
- valuing stock at the expected selling price instead of delivered purchase cost;
- assuming credit, stock return, buyback, exclusivity, or lead allocation before approval;
- omitting tax and freight timing because they may later be recoverable;
- subtracting expected profit from the cash requirement before a sale occurs; and
- committing to premises, hiring, or inventory before appointment terms are final.

A low entry payment can sit beside a large cash cycle. A high stock commitment may also include saleable inventory rather than a fee. Label each amount by purpose, timing, ownership, and recoverability before comparing programmes.

## Which Qbits financial fields should you prepare?

As of 26 September 2026, the Qbits partner form asks for annual turnover, total available working capital in lakhs, total bank-loan amount, cash-credit limits, other capital, and the hypothecated bank. It also asks about office type, warehouse availability and size, team capacity, and target monthly sales.

The form does not explain a scoring formula or publish a required minimum. Prepare the figures from current records and use one reporting date. If a term such as `working capital available` is unclear for your business structure, confirm the basis with your accountant and ask Qbits what it expects.

The [field-by-field Qbits partner application guide](/blog/qbits-partner-application-guide/) explains how to keep these answers consistent. Use the [dealer application process](/blog/how-to-become-a-solar-inverter-partner-in-india/) separately for eligibility, documents, and submission steps.

## Ask these questions before spending

If the application progresses, request a dated written answer for every cash commitment:

- Is there a registration, onboarding, training, demo, or security payment?
- Which payment is refundable, under what conditions, and by what process?
- Is opening stock compulsory? Which models, quantities, and delivery dates apply?
- Are taxes, freight, transit damage, unloading, and insurance included?
- Is any supplier credit approved? What are its limit, security, due date, and suspension rules?
- Who owns demo units and slow-moving stock?
- What happens when a model changes or the relationship ends?
- Which tools, spares, software accounts, signage, and premises are compulsory?
- Which service, return, and warranty logistics costs fall to the dealer?
- What happens to payments if the application is declined or onboarding does not finish?

Carry the answers into the [solar dealer agreement checklist](/blog/solar-dealer-agreement-checklist/). A verbal estimate should remain an estimate until the authorised proposal, schedules, invoices, and refund terms agree.

## Decide what you can commit without assuming future sales

Run at least three cases: the planned case, slower sales with delayed collections, and an exit case with unsold stock. The decision is viable only if the business can fund the downside without relying on an unpublished margin, an unapproved credit line, or a stock-return right that is not written.

Qbits does not publish a dealer investment, margin, earnings, credit, territory, or sales promise. Do not enter a competitor's number into the Qbits form. Report your actual current position, then calculate any proposed commitment after you receive the relevant written terms.

When your records and role are ready, complete the [official Qbits partner application](/become-our-partner/) and select `Dealer`. Treat submission as the start of review, not permission to spend or represent the business as an authorised Qbits dealer.
