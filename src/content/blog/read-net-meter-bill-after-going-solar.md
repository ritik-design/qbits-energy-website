A net-metered bill is your old bill with a few new lines. It shows the units you took from the grid (import), the units your solar sent back (export), and the difference (net units). You pay for the net units at your normal slab rates. You also pay the fixed charge, the fuel surcharge, electricity duty and taxes. If you sent back more than you took, the extra units are either carried forward or bought by the DISCOM. Your state decides which.

That is why the bill rarely falls to zero. Fixed charges, duty and taxes stay. Solar stops at night. And in some states, the surplus from a sunny month cannot pay for a cloudy one.

> **What this is built on**
>
> - **National rule:** the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Amendment Rules, 2021 (G.S.R. 448(E), 28 June 2021), read in the gazette text.
> - **Four states, read on 30 October 2026:** Gujarat (GERC Third Amendment Regulations 2022, Notification 2 of 2022, dated 31 May 2022, and the GERC tariff schedule effective 1 April 2026), Maharashtra (MERC Regulations 2019 and the Commission's statements of reasons for the 2023 and 2024 amendments), Karnataka (KERC Distributed Solar PV Regulations 2026, dated 19 August 2026) and Rajasthan (RERC Regulations 2021).
> - **Limits:** we did not open every later amendment. The Gujarat tariff schedule came from an archived copy of the GERC file, because the regulator's own site would not open securely from our network. The Rajasthan regulation copy we read has a blank notification date. Your DISCOM's current order decides your bill, so use this post to know what to ask, not to calculate a refund.

## What does each line on a net meter bill mean?

A net meter bill carries the same items whatever the DISCOM calls them. Maharashtra's regulation says a bill must show five things for each billing period: generation recorded, units consumed, net units billed, banked energy and the money adjusted for it. Karnataka's 2026 regulation and Gujarat's amendment list similar items.

| Line | What it means | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Billing period and reading dates | The days the bill covers, from one meter reading to the next | Matches your own note of the date the meter was read. A 33-day period raises units and can push you into a higher slab |
| Import (units supplied) | Units the DISCOM supplied to you, mostly at night and on cloudy days | It should be higher than your old daytime usage, and similar to the evening and night use you expect |
| Export (units injected) | Units your solar sent to the grid after your own use | Compare with the export figure on your inverter app. It will not match to the unit, but a big gap is a flag |
| Net units billed | Import minus export, if import is higher | This is the number the slab rates apply to |
| Net exported or credit balance | Export minus import, if export is higher. Either carried forward as units or bought as money, per state rule | Opening balance plus this month should equal closing balance |
| Generation meter reading | Total solar made, if your DISCOM fits a separate generation meter | Some states list it only for the DISCOM's own record. It is not the same as export |
| Fixed or demand charge | A monthly sum set by your connected load or sanctioned load | It is payable even with zero net units |
| Energy charge | Net units multiplied by slab rates from the tariff order | Check the slab bands against the order for your category |
| Fuel surcharge (FPPAS, FAC or similar) | A pass-through for changes in power purchase cost, set by a formula | Gujarat's schedule says it follows a formula the Commission approves. How it is charged on net units depends on your DISCOM, so ask |
| Electricity duty and taxes | A state levy, not part of the tariff | Gujarat's tariff schedule says tariffs are exclusive of duty and tax, so they are added on top |
| Meter rent or meter charge | A line some DISCOMs charge, depending on meter type | We did not find a rate in the documents we read. Ask your DISCOM if you see one |
| Arrears and late payment charge | Unpaid amounts and the charge for paying after the due date | Gujarat's schedule says 15% a year for non-agricultural consumers, from the due date |

The most common misreading is the "net" line. A net figure is not your use. It is what is left after your solar covered some of your use. Your own consumption is import plus the solar you used in the house. The meter does not record that second part.

## What do the national rules say about net metering bills?

The national rule gives three definitions and leaves the billing detail to each state. The Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, 2020 were amended on 28 June 2021. Rule 11 now says the arrangements for net metering, gross metering, net billing and net feed-in "shall be in accordance with the regulations made by the State Commission". A prosumer's solar energy, it adds, "shall be adjusted against energy consumed and bill amount as per regulations made by the Commission". A prosumer is a consumer who also generates power.

The three definitions matter for reading your bill:

| Arrangement | Rule text, in short |
| --- | --- |
| Net metering | Exported units are deducted from imported units (kWh) and the net is billed, credited or carried over at the retail tariff, on one bidirectional meter |
| Net billing or net feed-in | Imports are valued at the retail tariff and exports at a feed-in tariff set by the Commission. The money value of exports is deducted from the money value of imports |
| Gross metering | All solar generated is accounted at a feed-in tariff, and all you consume is billed at the retail tariff |

The rules also let a DISCOM fit a separate meter for gross solar generation, for its own renewable purchase obligation. That is why some homes have a second small meter that does not feed the bill. Our [gross versus net metering guide](/blog/gross-metering-vs-net-metering/) explains the money difference between the arrangements. Read the approval letter you got when the system was commissioned, because it names which one you are on.

## How do four states settle your surplus units?

"Settlement" is what happens to the units you export beyond what you import. Each state answers it differently, and the answer decides whether a sunny month can pay for a cloudy one. None of these is a national rule.

| State | Settlement basis | What happens to surplus | Source and date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gujarat (residential, net metering) | Billing cycle | Surplus after set-off is bought by the DISCOM. For self-owned systems and Surya Gujarat consumers the rate is Rs 2.25 a unit for the first 5 years from commissioning | GERC Third Amendment 2022, Regulation 9.1.3, dated 31 May 2022 |
| Maharashtra (net metering) | Carried forward within the year 1 April to 31 March | Surplus carries to the next billing period as credited units. At year end, leftover units are bought at the Generic Tariff the Commission approves, and the money comes as bill credit | MERC Regulations 2019, Regulation 11.4, notified 30 December 2019 |
| Karnataka (net metering) | Monthly | Excess energy is bought by the DISCOM at the tariff set by the Commission and settled every month | KERC DSPV Regulations 2026, Regulations 5.1.4 and 5.1.7, dated 19 August 2026 |
| Rajasthan (domestic, net metering) | Billing cycle, with year-end true-up | Domestic surplus is bought at a rate based on large solar bids of the last financial year, and the money is credited in the next bill. Leftover credited units at 31 March are bought at the same rate in April. For non-domestic consumers, net surplus at the end of the billing period lapses | RERC Regulations 2021 (issued 8 April 2021 per RERC's order of 7 February 2024), Regulation 12.6 |

Three points from these texts change what you see on the bill.

**Maharashtra is the only one of the four that carries surplus forward as units through the year.** Those units appear as an opening and closing balance. MERC's 2023 statement of reasons adds a rule for credit that keeps growing for three financial years in a row. Half is paid in cash and half is credited to the bill. We read that in the statement of reasons, not in the notified text.

**Monthly settlement means no banking.** In Gujarat and Karnataka, a surplus in April is paid out at the set rate in that month. It cannot lower your July bill. A house that exports a lot in summer and imports a lot in the monsoon feels this directly.

**Time-of-day tariffs change how units are matched.** If your category is on a time-of-day tariff, Maharashtra and Karnataka both match units block by block. Your consumption in a time block is first set off against your generation in the same block. Any extra generation is treated as off-peak. For categories not on time-of-day tariffs, MERC's 2024 statement of reasons says energy is settled over the whole month, whatever the time of injection or drawal. Gujarat's schedule separately gives a Rs 0.60 a unit discount for consumption from 11 am to 5 pm for homes with a smart meter. Your bill will say which of these applies to you.

Your state may be none of the four. Most others settle under one of these patterns, but read your own DISCOM's order. Our [net metering approval timeline](/blog/net-meter-approval-timeline/) names more state frameworks.

## Worked example: what a Gujarat home pays in three months

This example uses real slab rates, so you can check the arithmetic. The rates are the GERC tariff schedule effective 1 April 2026 for DGVCL, MGVCL, PGVCL and UGVCL, Rate RGP (residential, post-paid):

- Fixed charge: Rs 25 a month for a connected load above 2 kW and up to 4 kW.
- Energy: first 50 units Rs 3.05, next 50 Rs 3.50, next 150 Rs 4.15, above 250 Rs 5.20.
- Minimum bill: the fixed charge.
- Surplus units: bought at Rs 2.25 a unit (self-owned system, first 5 years, GERC Regulation 9.1.3).

The units below are **illustrative**, not any home's meter. They assume the slabs apply to net billed units, which is how we read "net billed electricity for payment" in Regulation 9(b). The text does not spell out the slab basis, so confirm it on your own bill.

| Month (illustrative) | Import | Export | Net | Energy charge | Fixed | Before FPPAS, duty and tax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bright month | 260 | 180 | 80 units billed | 50 x 3.05 + 30 x 3.50 = Rs 257.50 | Rs 25 | **Rs 282.50** |
| Surplus month | 150 | 210 | 60 units exported | Nil | Rs 25 | **Rs 25.00** payable, and 60 units bought at Rs 2.25 = Rs 135.00 |
| Cloudy month | 330 | 60 | 270 units billed | 50 x 3.05 + 50 x 3.50 + 150 x 4.15 + 20 x 5.20 = Rs 1,054.00 | Rs 25 | **Rs 1,079.00** |

Three things stand out.

- The cloudy month costs almost four times the bright month. The net units, not the season, drive it, and 270 units reach the top slab.
- The surplus month leaves a Rs 25 bill, which is the minimum bill. The Rs 135 for exported units is a separate amount. The clause does not say whether it shows as a bill credit or is paid out, so check how your DISCOM treats it.
- FPPAS, electricity duty and tax are on top of every figure. The tariff schedule says its rates exclude duty and tax, and says FPPAS follows a formula the Commission approves. Neither percentage is in the schedule, so read both from your bill.

## Worked example: why one house gets different monsoon bills in different states

Take one house with a 4 kW array. In a summer month it imports 200 units and exports 300 (surplus 100). In a monsoon month it imports 350 and exports 100 (shortfall 250). These units are illustrative.

| Rule | Summer month | Monsoon month, net units billed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly settlement (Gujarat residential, Karnataka) | 0 billed, 100 surplus units bought at the set rate | 250 |
| Carry forward to 31 March (Maharashtra) | 0 billed, 100 units banked | 150 (250 less 100 banked) |
| Billing-cycle set-off with lapse (Rajasthan, non-domestic) | 0 billed, the 100 surplus units lapse | 250 |

The panels, the weather and the meter are identical. The settlement rule alone moves the monsoon bill by 100 units. This is the first thing to learn before you size a system above your own use. If your state buys surplus at a low rate, a bigger array makes few rupees. Our guide on [how many panels fit a 5 kW inverter](/blog/how-many-solar-panels-for-five-kw-inverter/) shows how array size changes daily units.

A second Rajasthan detail can surprise a household. The RERC text says the net imported energy of a net-metered consumer "shall be billed according to the highest slab corresponding to the total consumption from all sources". As we read it, if your total use (grid plus solar) puts you in a high slab, your net units are billed at that high slab's rate.

Here is an illustrative case with invented slabs: Rs 5 up to 150 units, Rs 6.50 for 151 to 300, and Rs 8 above 300. Total use is 420 units, solar covers 250, and net import is 170 units. On plain slabs that is 150 x 5 + 20 x 6.50 = Rs 880. At the highest-slab rate it is 170 x 8 = Rs 1,360. We did not check whether a later RERC amendment changed this, so ask your DISCOM how it bills net units.

If you want to see the inverter side of this, the numbers on your inverter app will not equal your meter. [This guide to monitoring systems](/blog/solar-inverter-monitoring-systems-in-india/) explains what the app measures and what the DISCOM meter measures.

If you have not bought a system yet, find your state's settlement rule before you pick a size. Then look at the home inverter families Qbits lists on the [residential solar page](/residential-solution/). The surplus rate in your state, not the biggest array your roof allows, should set the size.

## Why is my solar bill not zero?

A solar bill stays above zero for six ordinary reasons, and none is a fault.

1. **Fixed charge.** Gujarat's minimum bill is the fixed charge. Karnataka's regulation says that whatever excess energy you have, you still pay fixed or demand charges and government levies.
2. **Duty and taxes.** They sit outside the tariff in Gujarat's schedule, so they are added to what remains.
3. **Fuel surcharge.** It follows a formula and can still apply to the units you are billed for.
4. **Units cannot pay fees.** Karnataka says exported units are used only to offset consumption in kWh. They cannot offset other fees and charges.
5. **Night and cloudy-day import.** On-grid solar makes nothing at night.
6. **Low surplus rate.** Where surplus is bought, the rate can be below the retail rate, so a summer surplus may not cover a full monsoon bill.

A bill near the fixed charge in a bright month is the normal good result. Do not read a small positive bill as a sign of an error.

## How do you check a net meter bill for errors?

Most errors show up in the first two bills. Check these with the bill in one hand and the approval letter in the other.

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Export shows 0 while your inverter app shows export | Meter not yet changed to a bidirectional meter, export register not read, or wrong meter code | Photograph the meter display. Ask the DISCOM to confirm the export register is billed |
| Import and export shown as one net figure | Bill format shows only net units | Ask for separate readings. Maharashtra's regulation lists net units and banked units as separate items |
| Billing period much longer or shorter than usual | Late or estimated reading | Compare dates with your photos. Estimated reads can move you into a higher slab |
| Credit balance does not carry into the next bill | Bill not showing opening and closing balances | In a state that carries units forward, raise it in writing with both bills attached |
| Billed at gross-metering rates or no solar adjustment | Wrong category or wrong arrangement in the DISCOM's billing system | Quote the approval letter and the arrangement it names |
| Slab looks too high for the units billed | Slabs applied to total use rather than net units, or the Rajasthan highest-slab rule | Ask in writing which units the slab rate is applied to |
| Surplus units not paid or credited | Wrong rate or wrong start date for the 5-year or agreement period | Ask for the rate, the start date and where the money appears |
| Meter reads fine but bill jumps after an old meter was removed | Final reading of the old meter not carried across | Ask for the final reading of the old meter and the first reading of the new one |

If the DISCOM does not fix it, use the grievance route the Electricity Act sets up. Go to the DISCOM's consumer grievance forum first, then the electricity ombudsman. MERC's regulation points a billing dispute to that route. The [bidirectional meter glossary page](/glossary/bidirectional-meter/) and the [smart meter page](/glossary/smart-meter/) explain the two devices you may have.

## Your bill checklist

Print this and tick it on each of the first three bills.

- [ ] The billing period dates match the dates on your meter photos.
- [ ] Import and export are shown separately, and export is not zero.
- [ ] Net units equal import minus export, or the credit balance is shown.
- [ ] Opening plus this month's change equals the closing balance, if your state banks units.
- [ ] The slab bands and fixed charge match the current tariff order for your category.
- [ ] You know which units the slab rate is applied to.
- [ ] Surplus units show a rate and a rupee amount, and you know where that credit appears.
- [ ] FPPAS or the fuel charge, electricity duty and any meter charge are listed on their own lines.
- [ ] Your approval letter names the arrangement (net metering, net billing or gross metering) and the bill follows it.

## What to ask your installer and your DISCOM

Ask the installer, in writing, before you sign:

1. Which arrangement does the quote assume: net metering, net billing or gross metering?
2. Does the quote's savings figure use the surplus rate, or does it assume every exported unit is worth the retail rate?
3. Who applies for the bidirectional meter, and who is the DISCOM contact for the first bill?
4. Is my system size counted as panel kWp or inverter kW in the approval? Our [net metering application guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) covers the paperwork.

Ask the DISCOM:

1. Is settlement monthly, per billing cycle, or annual from 1 April? Where do carried-forward units show?
2. At what rate is surplus bought, from what date, and for how long?
3. Are slab rates applied to net units or to total use?
4. Is my category on a time-of-day tariff, and is my meter smart?
