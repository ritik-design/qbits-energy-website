## What is polycrystalline

Polycrystalline solar, also called multi-crystalline silicon (mc-Si), refers to PV modules whose cells are cut from silicon wafers cast from molten silicon that solidifies as multiple grains. The wafers contain many small crystals separated by grain boundaries. Cells are processed through diffusion, metallisation and interconnection similar to monocrystalline.

For two decades polycrystalline was the cost-effective workhorse of global solar deployment. Lower manufacturing energy, simpler casting process and easier ingot growth gave poly a structural cost advantage. From roughly 2020 onward, monocrystalline manufacturing scaled to a point where mono cost per watt fell below poly, eliminating poly's commercial advantage. By 2026, poly is a legacy technology with limited new deployment.

In India, polycrystalline dominated rooftop and utility solar from 2010 to 2020. Tata Power Solar, Vikram Solar, Waaree and several others sold gigawatts of poly modules during this period. From 2022 onward, ALMM listings shifted heavily toward mono, and most new Indian installations specify mono.

## Polycrystalline explained simply

Think of mono silicon as a single ice crystal grown slowly under controlled conditions, and poly silicon as ice cubes frozen quickly into a tray with many grains. The poly version is faster and cheaper to make but the grain boundaries inside reduce efficiency. A poly cell converts about 17 to 19 percent of sunlight to electricity, against 20 to 23 percent for modern mono. Most Indian buyers now know to ask whether a quote is mono or poly; see [common solar inverter buying mistakes](/blog/solar-inverter-buying-mistakes/) for why the same scrutiny needs to extend to the inverter, not just the panel.

## Why polycrystalline still matters

Legacy installation base. Hundreds of GW of installed poly worldwide, including roughly 30 GW in India.

Replacement supply. Existing poly arrays need spare modules with matching electrical characteristics.

Specific budget markets. Some cost-sensitive small applications still source poly.

Historical context. Understanding poly informs the choice for new installations.

## How polycrystalline works

1. Molten silicon poured into a casting mould.
2. Slow cooling creates many small crystal grains.
3. Ingot sliced into wafers.
4. Wafers processed through doping, anti-reflective coating, metallisation.
5. Cells interconnected with ribbons or busbars.
6. Encapsulated between glass and back sheet.

The fundamental physics is identical to monocrystalline. The difference is the silicon's crystal structure: many small grains in poly, one large grain in mono. Grain boundaries trap electrons and reduce open-circuit voltage.

## Technical deep dive

### Cell efficiency progression

| Year | Standard poly cell |
|---|---|
| 2010 | 15.5% |
| 2015 | 17.5% |
| 2018 | 18.5% |
| 2020 | 19.5% (PERC poly) |
| 2022 | 20.0% (PERC poly, plateau) |

PERC technology added efficiency to poly but the gap to mono never closed.

### Module efficiency

| Year | Standard poly module |
|---|---|
| 2010 | 13 to 14% |
| 2015 | 15 to 16% |
| 2020 | 17 to 18% |
| 2025 | 17 to 19% |

### Temperature coefficient

| Parameter | Poly | Mono PERC |
|---|---|---|
| Voc coefficient (%/°C) | -0.30 to -0.32 | -0.27 to -0.30 |
| Pmax coefficient (%/°C) | -0.39 to -0.41 | -0.34 to -0.37 |

Poly is slightly worse at high temperatures, which matters in Indian summers.

### Degradation

| Mechanism | Poly |
|---|---|
| LID first month | 1 to 2.5% |
| Annual degradation | 0.6 to 0.8%/year |
| 25-year retention | 80 to 83% |

## Real-world applications

Legacy rooftop systems installed 2010 to 2020.

Spare module supply for existing poly arrays.

Budget off-grid systems in some markets.

Educational and demonstration installations.

## Advantages of polycrystalline

Lower upfront cost (historically).

Wide manufacturing footprint built up over two decades.

Lower manufacturing carbon footprint than early mono.

Familiar to installers.

Compatible with same balance-of-system components as mono.

## Limitations

Lower efficiency. Less power per square metre.

Slower technology progression. No clear path to TOPCon or HJT-like jumps.

Slightly higher temperature coefficient.

Slightly worse degradation profile.

ALMM listings increasingly dominated by mono.

Bifacial development limited.

For a cost breakdown that puts budget polycrystalline modules side by side with mid-range PERC and premium TOPCon panels, see [10kW solar system price in India](/blog/10kw-solar-system-price-india/).

## Common problems

PID susceptibility. Older poly modules without PID-resistant materials suffer significant degradation in 5 to 10 years.

Snail trails and back sheet issues similar to mono.

Hot spots from cracked cells.

Limited replacement availability for older specific wattages.

String mismatch when mixing replacement modules with original array.

Soiling losses similar to mono, 5 to 25 percent depending on cleaning schedule.

## Best practices

Verify ALMM listing and BIS certification.

Match replacement modules to original Voc, Imp and Vmp specifications.

Use PID-resistant modules in systems with grounded inverter.

Document module serial numbers and electrical parameters.

Plan annual cleaning.

## Indian market context

Polycrystalline dominated Indian solar from 2010 to 2020 through the National Solar Mission (Phase I, II, III).

ALMM List I now contains predominantly mono modules. Poly listings are limited and shrinking.

Most Indian module manufacturers (Waaree, Adani Green Solar, Tata Power Solar, Vikram Solar, Goldi Solar, Premier Energies) have shifted production capacity to mono PERC and TOPCon.

PM Surya Ghar accepts ALMM-listed poly modules, but supply availability is limited. Most installers default to mono.

Legacy poly systems installed before 2020 continue to operate. Operations and maintenance for these systems remains an active business.

## Common mistakes

Buying poly to save money. Mono price gap has closed; no significant saving available.

Mixing mono and poly in one string. 5 to 15 percent string mismatch loss.

Trusting older poly modules without PID test certification.

Ignoring temperature coefficient in tropical Indian conditions.

Choosing poly for ALMM-eligible projects when better mono alternatives are listed.

## Key takeaways

Polycrystalline is a legacy crystalline silicon technology that dominated the early decade of solar PV. Modern manufacturing scale has shifted the cost advantage to mono. Poly continues to serve legacy installations and replacement supply but has lost the new-deployment market. For any new Indian solar project, mono is the obvious choice.
