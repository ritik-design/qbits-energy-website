## What Actually Moves the Price: Feature-Level Cost Drivers

Two 5 kW hybrids with a ₹40,000 price gap are not the same machine at different margins. Five engineering differences account for nearly all of the spread.

**Battery architecture.** Low-voltage (48 V) battery ports, used by Deye and the Qbits HS-5K, accept a wide range of lead-acid and generic LFP batteries. High-voltage platforms (80 to 460 V), used by Sungrow, charge and discharge more efficiently. The catch is lock-in: they work only with the brand's own battery modules. High-voltage hardware adds ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 to the inverter price, and more to the battery later.

**MPPT count.** Dual-MPPT units track two panel strings independently. They recover 8 to 15% more generation on east-west or partially shaded 3BHK roofs. Genuine dual MPPT adds ₹5,000 to ₹10,000. Most 3BHK terraces need it.

**Switchover speed.** Budget hybrids transfer to battery in 10 to 20 milliseconds. That suits lights and fans, but it can reboot a desktop or reset a router. UPS-class transfer under 10 milliseconds costs more in relay and control hardware.

**Warranty depth.** A 5-year warranty is the segment floor. Sungrow offers 10 years on the SH5.0RS, double the budget norm, according to [Energy Bee's 2026 Sungrow pricing review](https://energybee.co.za/products/inverters/sungrow-5kw-hybrid-inverter), 2026. Qbits prices a 12-year full replacement warranty into the HS-5K. A longer warranty means the manufacturer carries the ₹68,000 to ₹85,000 replacement risk for more years. That risk is priced into the sticker.

**Certification.** ALMM listing, BIS certification, and IP66 ingress protection require testing investment. The Qbits HS-5K carries all three. Ultra-cheap imports skip them, which is exactly why they are cheap.

## Is a 5kW Hybrid the Right Size for a 3BHK Home?

For most Indian 3BHK homes, yes. The typical 3BHK peak load runs 3.5 to 4.5 kW in simultaneous use. That covers one 1.5-tonne AC, four fans, a fridge, a TV, a washing machine, and full lighting. A 5 kW inverter serves that load with 10 to 15% headroom. A 5 kWp array behind it generates 18 to 22 units per day in most Indian cities. That matches or exceeds the daily consumption of a mid-sized family.

Three situations where 5 kW is the wrong size:

1. **Two or more ACs running together daily.** Two 1.5-tonne ACs plus household loads peak above 5.5 kW. Look at an 8 kW hybrid instead.
2. **Very light loads.** A 2BHK with no AC peaks near 2.5 kW. A 3 kW hybrid serves it at ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 less and earns a higher subsidy per kW.
3. **Three-phase supply with heavy loads.** Some larger 3BHKs and villas have three-phase connections. A single-phase 5 kW hybrid will not balance that load. Size up or go three-phase.

## Subsidy Eligibility: What a 5kW Hybrid Costs After PM Surya Ghar

Grid-connected hybrid systems qualify for the full PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana central subsidy on the same terms as on-grid systems. The inverter must be ALMM-listed. The installer must be an empanelled vendor on the national portal.

The central subsidy pays ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, according to the [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026. It stops there: ₹78,000 is the maximum for any residential system, so a 5 kW installation claims the same ₹78,000 as a 3 kW one. It is credited to your bank account by Direct Benefit Transfer after DISCOM commissioning.

Worked example, 5kW hybrid with battery in a Tier-2 city (labelled hypothetical, mid-market pricing):

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 5kW hybrid inverter (Qbits HS-5K) | ₹75,000 |
| 5 kWp panels, structure, cabling, installation | ₹2,05,000 |
| 5 kWh LFP battery | ₹1,25,000 |
| **Gross system cost** | **₹4,05,000** |
| Less: PM Surya Ghar central subsidy | ₹78,000 |
| Less: State subsidy (varies by state) | ₹15,000 |
| **Net homeowner cost** | **₹3,12,000** |

Drop the battery and the same system nets near ₹1,87,000. Note two things. First, the subsidy applies to system capacity, not inverter type. The hybrid premium is neither subsidised nor penalised. Second, the battery earns no central subsidy at all. That is why the battery-later strategy works: claim the full subsidy now, add storage when LFP prices fall further. ## Myth-Busting and Tradeoffs at the 5 kW Size

The 5 kW hybrid segment attracts three persistent myths. Each one costs buyers money.

**Myth 1: "Buy on-grid now, upgrade to hybrid later."** There is no upgrade path. Adding battery capability to an on-grid system means replacing the entire inverter at ₹60,000 to ₹90,000 plus rewiring. Buying the hybrid upfront with the battery port unused costs ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 extra. It works exactly like an on-grid unit until you add storage. If there is any realistic chance you want backup within five years, hybrid now is the cheaper path.

**Myth 2: "A hybrid without battery is wasted money."** Wrong. An unused battery port is an option you hold. The inverter still exports solar, still earns the full ₹78,000 subsidy, and still runs your home from the grid. When your grid worsens or LFP drops another 20%, you add storage for the battery cost alone.

**Myth 3: "The cheapest 5kW hybrid is the smart buy."** Divide price by warranty years before deciding. A ₹55,000 hybrid with a 5-year warranty costs ₹11,000 per year of coverage. A ₹75,000 Qbits HS-5K with a 12-year full replacement warranty costs ₹6,250 per year. The "expensive" unit is 43% cheaper per protected year. It also covers years 6 to 12, when inverters on 180 to 270 V grids in 40 to 45 °C summers start to fail.

> **What most buyers get wrong:** they compare inverter stickers and forget the battery platform. A cheap inverter locked to an expensive proprietary battery costs more over 10 years than a pricier inverter on an open 48 V platform. Price the inverter and the battery together, over the full warranty period.

The honest tradeoff: high-voltage platforms like Sungrow's genuinely charge and discharge more efficiently, and their monitoring is the best in class. Open 48 V platforms give you battery choice and cheaper storage. Neither is universally right. Efficiency-focused buyers in metros lean Sungrow. Value and flexibility buyers lean Qbits or Deye.
