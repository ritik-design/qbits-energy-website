## What is CAPEX

CAPEX, Capital Expenditure, is the total upfront cost of designing, procuring, installing and commissioning a solar PV project. It is one of the two big cost categories in project economics, alongside OPEX (operating expenditure). For solar specifically, capex dominates the lifecycle cost structure because operating costs are low.

Indian solar capex includes modules, inverter, mounting structure, DC and AC cables, transformer, switchgear, civil work, labour, project management, financing during construction, contingency, taxes and connection charges. For utility-scale plants land cost is also included. For rooftop projects existing roof eliminates that line item.

Capex per watt has fallen dramatically over the past decade. Utility solar fell from ₹15 crore per MW in 2012 to ₹3.5 to ₹4.5 crore per MW in 2026. The collapse was driven primarily by silicon module price reduction, with BOS and labour also falling.

## CAPEX explained simply

To build a solar plant, you need to buy panels, inverters, racking, wires, transformers, and pay for installation. Add it all up. That total is the capex. For a 1 MW utility plant in India today, that is roughly ₹3.5 to ₹4.5 crore. For a 3 kW home rooftop, around ₹1.65 lakh.

## Why CAPEX matters

Project budgeting. The customer needs to know the cost before committing.

Financial modelling. LCOE, IRR and payback all start from capex.

Tariff discovery. SECI tender economics rest on capex assumptions.

PPA negotiation. The developer must recover capex through PPA revenue.

Customer financing. Loans size against capex.

Subsidy quantification. PM Surya Ghar caps capex eligibility for subsidy.

## How CAPEX is calculated

Total CAPEX = Module + Inverter + Structure + Cables + Transformer + Switchgear + Civil work + Labour + Project management + Financing during construction + Contingency + Taxes + Connection charges

## Advantages

Single upfront cost easy to communicate.

Standardised across the industry.

Direct input to financial models.

Captures total project investment.

## Limitations

Hides lifecycle cost (including O&M and replacement).

Sensitivity to module price volatility.

Project-specific variation around averages.

Does not differentiate quality between low-cost and premium installations.

## Common problems

Capex overruns during construction. Buffer of 3 to 5 percent contingency mitigates.

Hidden costs (connection, transformer upgrade) discovered late.

Module price volatility affecting bid economics.

GST and customs duty changes during construction.

Quality compromises to hit aggressive capex targets.

## Best practices

Use detailed bills of quantity, not lump sum estimates.

Include 3 to 5 percent contingency.

Validate prices against current ALMM-listed brands.

Factor in transformer upgrade and connection charges separately.

Plan for monsoon delays in construction schedule.

Track capex against budget through project phases.

## Indian market context

Major capex drivers in 2026: ALMM-listed module prices ₹16 to ₹22 per Wp, BIS-certified inverter at ₹3.5 to ₹9 per Wp depending on size, mounting structure costs around ₹4 to ₹6 per Wp.

PLI scheme reduced reliance on imported cells and modules. Domestic capex has stabilised.

PM Surya Ghar subsidy effectively halves residential capex for systems up to 3 kW.

Major Indian EPCs publish typical capex bands for tender comparison.

## Common mistakes

Comparing capex across projects with different scope.

Excluding transformer or connection charges.

Underestimating monsoon delay impact.

Ignoring contingency.

Treating PM Surya Ghar subsidy as discount rather than reimbursement.

Using outdated capex assumptions in financial models.

## Key takeaways

CAPEX is the upfront cost of building a solar project. Indian utility-scale capex sits at ₹3.5 to ₹4.5 crore per MW (₹35 to ₹45 per Wp). C&I rooftop at ₹35 to ₹55 per Wp. Residential rooftop ranges ₹40 to ₹70 per Wp pre-subsidy, dropping to ₹10 to ₹30 post PM Surya Ghar. Capex has fallen 70+ percent since 2012 and has stabilised. Module, inverter, structure and labour are the four major components.
