## What IEC 62109 means

**IEC 62109 is the international safety standard series for power converters used in photovoltaic systems.** [IEC 62109-1](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/6470) covers general requirements. Part 2 addresses requirements particular to inverters. It is useful when assessing electrical, thermal and mechanical safety documentation for a PV converter.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - IEC 62109 is a two-part product safety standard: Part 1 (general requirements) and Part 2 (inverters), normally applied together.
> - It covers shock, fire, temperature rise and fault behaviour. It does not certify efficiency, islanding prevention, grid-code compliance or DISCOM approval.
> - In India, MNRE's order lists IS 16221 (Part 2) alongside IEC 62109-2 for inverters, with BIS registration as the practical proof.
> - Ask for the certificate and test report for the exact model, edition and hardware revision you are buying.

> [!IMPORTANT] A standard title is not a certificate
> A standard title is not a certificate. A buyer should ask what edition was applied, which model or family variants were tested, which laboratory issued the report, and whether the document covers the equipment revision being offered.

## The two parts and their editions

Table: The two parts of IEC 62109: title, edition and purpose

| Part | Title | Edition | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IEC 62109-1 | Safety of power converters for use in photovoltaic power systems, Part 1: General requirements | Edition 1.0, April 2010 | Sets minimum requirements for the design and manufacture of PV power conversion equipment to protect against electric shock, energy, fire, mechanical and other hazards |
| IEC 62109-2 | Part 2: Particular requirements for inverters | Edition 1.0, June 2011 | Applies to d.c. to a.c. inverters and products that include inverter functions, for grid-interactive and stand-alone use; supplements and modifies Part 1 |

Part 1 applies to equipment connected to PV source circuits up to 1,500 V d.c. and to a.c. mains circuits up to 1,000 V a.c. Part 2 refers to Part 1 by date, so an inverter report is normally written against both. The IEC webstore lists the editions currently on sale, so check it for later amendments or editions before quoting an edition in a contract.

## What the standard does and does not cover

IEC 62109 is a **product safety** standard. It looks at hazards such as insulation and clearance, protection against electric shock, earthing and bonding, temperature rise, fire and enclosure strength, fault behaviour, and marking and instructions. It does not certify energy yield or efficiency, and it is not the grid code.

Table: Which evidence answers which question about a PV inverter

| Question | Relevant evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Was this PV converter evaluated for product safety? | Model-specific safety report and certificate under the applicable edition |
| Will it cease energising a disconnected utility circuit? | Islanding-prevention evidence, commonly involving IEC 62116 testing (adopted by BIS as IS 16169) |
| How efficient is it, and how well does it track? | Efficiency under IEC 61683 and MPPT efficiency under IEC 62891, which are performance tests |
| Can it connect to this DISCOM network? | Current grid-interface requirements and utility approval |
| Is a solar module eligible under an ALMM requirement? | The applicable MNRE module/cell list and project rule |

## How the standard fits in India

BIS has an Indian standard for the same subject. MNRE's solar quality control order lists IS 16221 (Part 2):2015 alongside IEC 62109-2:2011 for inverters, and lists IS 16169 alongside IEC 62116 for islanding prevention. Inverters are expected to be registered with BIS against the standards the order specifies, using BIS-recognised laboratory reports. The order itself was reissued as the Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025, and MNRE has issued extensions and self-certification provisions for some larger inverter sizes, so confirm the position for your inverter size and date of supply on the current MNRE notice. The wider picture is in the [BIS and IEC compliance guide](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) and the [certification checklist](/blog/solar-inverter-certifications/).

Two points often confused: ALMM, which MNRE publishes as a list for solar modules and cells, does not list inverters, and a BIS registration number is not a THD or efficiency claim.

## What testing looks at

Without reproducing the standard's tests, the kinds of check a safety evaluation includes are:

- Insulation resistance, clearance and creepage, and voltage withstand.
- Touch current and protection against electric shock, including earthing continuity.
- Temperature rise under stated operating conditions.
- Behaviour under fault and abnormal operating conditions.
- Enclosure strength and, for outdoor products, the stated ingress protection.
- Marking, warnings and installation instructions.

Which tests apply to a given product depends on its design, so read the scope on the certificate rather than assuming every item above was run.

## How to read a certificate before buying

> [!TIP] Match the document to the nameplate
> Match the exact model code and variant on the document to the quote and nameplate. Check the issuing body, issue date, standard edition, test report number and any conditions or exclusions. Ask for clarification if a brochure quotes a series-wide standard but the certificate names only one variant.

For Qbits products, use the [datasheet library](/download-datasheets/) and the [on-grid inverter range](/on-grid-inverter/) to identify the model first. The "Standard" block on the published inverter datasheets lists IEC62109, IEC61000, IS16169 and IS16221 (BIS). Treat that as a pointer. Request the current underlying certification and local approval documents from the supplier for the actual project.

## Compliance steps a manufacturer follows

1. Choose an accredited test laboratory and certification body.
2. Submit representative samples for type testing against the standard's parts.
3. Receive a test report and, where applicable, a certificate for the tested models.
4. For India, apply for BIS registration using the laboratory report.
5. Re-evaluate when a design, component or firmware change could affect safety.

## Common problems with certification evidence

- The certificate names one variant while the brochure advertises a whole range.
- A certificate refers to an older edition or older hardware revision.
- The document is a test report, not a certificate, or vice versa.
- A model sold in India has no BIS registration shown for it.
- A claim of "certified" with no document supplied.

## Best practices for buyers

1. Ask for the certificate and test report for the exact model, not the family brochure.
2. Check the edition and date, and confirm nothing has changed since.
3. Keep the documents with the project file, because DISCOM or inspection stages may ask for them.
4. Verify BIS registration on the BIS record for the model.
5. Treat a certificate as one input alongside installation quality and approval of the grid connection.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the standard title in a brochure as a certificate.
- Accepting a family-wide claim when the certificate lists one model.
- Confusing a safety standard with an efficiency or grid-code approval.
- Expecting ALMM to cover inverters.
- Ignoring a hardware or firmware change since the certificate was issued.

## Key takeaways

IEC 62109 is the safety baseline for PV inverters, in two parts that are applied together. It speaks to safety, not performance, islanding behaviour or utility approval. In India the matching Indian standard and BIS registration are the practical proof, so ask for the document for the exact model you are buying.
