## The True Cost of Going Solar for a 1 BHK

The **True Cost of Going Solar** framework identifies five cost components for any solar system. For a 1 BHK:

1. **Solar panels**: 2 to 4 panels of 500–550 Wp. See the dated panel listings in the [solar panel price guide](/blog/solar-panel-price-india/).
2. **Inverter (on-grid)**: priced by model; ask for the exact model and its written warranty.
3. **Balance of System (BOS)**: Mounting structure, cables, junction box, SPD, [earthing](/glossary/earthing/), [net meter interface](/glossary/net-metering/).
4. **Installation and commissioning**: Labour, civil, DISCOM application, following the same residential solar installation process EPCs use nationwide.
5. **[GST](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/)**: the 12% slab no longer exists. Panels are named at Schedule I entry 437 at 5%, the inverter is 5% or 18% by classification, and a bundled turnkey contract uses the 70:30 split for an 8.9% effective rate.

Table: MNRE benchmark cost for a 1kW and 2kW system, before and after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy (a reference, not a quote)

| Item | 1kW System | 2kW System |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MNRE benchmark cost (₹50,000 per kW, first 2 kW) | ₹50,000 | ₹1,00,000 |
| Less: PM Surya Ghar subsidy | −₹30,000 | −₹60,000 |
| **Benchmark cost after subsidy** | **₹20,000** | **₹40,000** |

*The benchmark is the scheme's reference cost (13 February 2024), not a market price. Component-wise costs (panels, inverter, BOS, installation, GST) have no dated source in this guide, so none are shown; compare itemised written quotes.*

> **₹60,000.** The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana central subsidy for a 2kW residential system - ₹30,000 per kW. For a 1 BHK homeowner, this subsidy covers 60% of the MNRE benchmark cost at 1 and 2 kW, which makes solar affordable for modest homes. *Source - [MNRE PM Surya Ghar Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, 2026.*

## 1kW vs 2kW: The Sizing Decision for a 1 BHK

This decision deserves an honest table. Many installers push 2kW even for no-AC homes because margins are higher on larger systems.

Table: 1kW versus 2kW system for a 1 BHK, factor by factor


| Factor | 1kW System | 2kW System |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Best for** | No-AC 1 BHK, usage < 200 units/month | 1-ton AC 1 BHK, usage 250–400 units/month |
| **Daily generation** | 3.5–5 kWh | 7–10 kWh |
| **Monthly units generated** | 105–150 | 210–300 |
| **MNRE benchmark cost** | ₹50,000 | ₹1,00,000 |
| **Subsidy** | ₹30,000 | ₹60,000 |
| **Benchmark cost after subsidy** | ₹20,000 | ₹40,000 |
| **Payback at ₹7/unit** | 4.4 years (assumed ₹45,000 net) | 2.7 years (assumed ₹55,000 net) |
| **Roof space needed** | 6–9 sq.m. | 12–18 sq.m. |
| **Panels** | 2 panels | 3–4 panels |
| **Can run AC?** | ✗ Not reliably | ✓ 1-ton comfortably |

> [!TIP] 1kW or 2kW: the decision rule
> **Decision rule:** If your monthly bill is consistently below ₹1,200 and you have no AC, choose 1kW. If your bill is ₹1,500–₹3,000 and you have or plan to add a 1-ton AC, choose 2kW. Do not buy a 3kW system for a genuine 1 BHK unless you have extremely high consumption or plan to add a second AC within 2 years. Right-size to your actual consumption rather than to the subsidy cap, because exported units may earn a lower credit than the units you save on self-consumption.

## Payback Period for a 1 BHK Solar System

> [!EXAMPLE] Payback at ₹7 per unit, 1kW and 2kW
> **1kW system, no AC, at ₹7/unit:**
> - Daily generation: 1 kW × 5 peak sun hours × 0.8 = 4.0 kWh
> - Annual generation: 1,460 kWh
> - Annual savings: 1,460 × ₹7 = ₹10,220
> - Net cost (assumed): ₹45,000
> - **Payback: 4.4 years**
>
> **2kW system, with AC, at ₹7/unit:**
> - Daily generation: 2 kW × 5 peak sun hours × 0.8 = 8.0 kWh
> - Annual generation: 2,920 kWh
> - Annual savings: 2,920 × ₹7 = ₹20,440
> - Net cost (assumed): ₹55,000
> - **Payback: 2.7 years**

The 2kW system pays back faster in absolute years because the higher consumption home saves more per unit of solar generated. This is consistent with what the [solar payback period methodology](/blog/solar-inverter-payback-period/) shows for different consumption levels.

## Which Inverter for a 1 BHK?

For a 1 BHK solar installation, three things matter most in inverter selection (the [complete solar inverter buying guide](/blog/how-to-choose-solar-inverter-for-home-india/) covers the full evaluation framework for any home size):

- **[ALMM listing](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/)**: Mandatory for PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Verify before purchase.
- **Warranty length**: At ₹12,000–₹25,000 inverter cost, a 5-year warranty is the minimum; a model-specific written warranty means you will never replace the inverter during the panel's 25-year life.
- **Local service network**: For a small residential system, you need someone who can come out within 3 days of a fault. Remote support or a Delhi-based call centre is not sufficient for a Tier-2 city owner.

## Common Mistakes 1 BHK Buyers Make

- **Buying a [3kW system](/blog/3kw-solar-system-price-india/) on the salesperson's advice**: A 3kW system for a 200-unit/month 1 BHK will export most of its generation to the grid at a net metering rate that may be lower than the tariff you pay. The payback advantage disappears and you spend ₹50,000–₹70,000 more unnecessarily.

- **Not checking net metering eligibility**: Apartment dwellers need to confirm that the DISCOM meters their unit individually (not the entire building) and that net metering is permitted in the building's electricity connection terms.
- **Ignoring installation quality**: A 1kW system has only 2 panels. Saving ₹2,000 on a cut-rate installer who does not earth the system correctly creates a genuine safety risk, better to find a vetted local installer than the cheapest bidder. The [BIS certification mandate](https://bis.gov.in/) under IS 16221 and the CEA safety standards are non-negotiable for all grid-connected residential installations in India.
- **Forgetting to apply for net metering**: Without a bi-directional net meter from your DISCOM, surplus solar power is wasted. The [net metering application guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) walks through the process state by state.
