## Madhya Pradesh DISCOM Structure

MP's electricity distribution runs through three state-owned regional companies. Some sources abbreviate both the east and west companies as MPPKVVCL, so use the full name on your bill. District lists below follow public company and trade sources and may lag boundary changes.

Table: Madhya Pradesh DISCOMs, the areas each covers and the consumer portal

| DISCOM | Areas | Notes |

| **MPPoKVVCL** (Poorv Kshetra, brand MPEZ) | Jabalpur, Rewa, Satna, Sagar, Shahdol, Katni, Chhatarpur, Tikamgarh and other eastern districts | East MP |

Check your electricity bill header to identify your specific DISCOM. All are enrolled in the PM Surya Ghar national portal. The state nodal agency is MP Urja Vikas Nigam (MPUVN).

## Last Date and Scheme Validity

MNRE's guidelines run PM Surya Ghar through FY 2026-27, and the national portal shows the scheme closing on 31 March 2027. As of mid-October 2026, reports say the Centre is considering an extension and a successor phase, but nothing has been notified, so treat 31 March 2027 as the working deadline. The subsidy is released after commissioning, so installation alone before the date is not enough.

## MP PM Surya Ghar Application Process: Step by Step

### The MP PM Surya Ghar Application Framework

<!-- steps -->
1. **Identify your DISCOM**: Check your electricity bill header for your specific MP DISCOM entity.
2. **Register on national portal**: At pmsuryaghar.gov.in, select Madhya Pradesh and your specific DISCOM. Enter your consumer number.
3. **Aadhaar eKYC**: Complete OTP verification using your Aadhaar-linked mobile.
4. **Document upload**: Latest MP DISCOM bill, Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, property proof.
5. **Feasibility and approval**: The DISCOM assesses your site. Systems up to 10 kW are reported to need no feasibility study under the MPERC 2024 regulation as amended in 2026.
6. **Vendor selection**: Select an empanelled vendor from the portal. Vendor choice is widest in the larger cities and thinner in rural districts, so compare more than one quote.
7. **Installation and net metering**: After installation your vendor submits the completion report, the DISCOM installs the net meter and commissions the system, and the central subsidy follows verification on the portal.

## MP's Temperature Challenge: Inverter Considerations

MP summers are intense: in May and June, hot districts can exceed 45 °C. This creates thermal derating risk for inverters not designed for high-ambient operation.

- **Temperature derating**: Compare each datasheet's derating behaviour at your site's peak ambient temperature, not only the headline rating
- **Passive cooling design**: Heat-sink design matters in dusty conditions where fans clog
- **Dust protection**: Pre-monsoon dust storms are common, so check the ingress protection rating of the exact model for outdoor installation
- **Wide voltage handling**: Rural feeders can see wide voltage swings; compare the grid-voltage range on the datasheet (the QB 1.5/2.0/2.7/3.0/3.3/3.6/4.0KTLS datasheet lists 90 to 290 Vac)

The [inverter overheating guide](/blog/inverter-overheating/) covers how to identify and prevent thermal issues specific to high-temperature Indian states.

## Common Mistakes MP Solar Applicants Make

- **Wrong DISCOM selection**: MP has three DISCOMs with overlapping abbreviations. Select the one on your bill; the consumer number will not validate under a mismatched DISCOM.
- **Ignoring heat derating**: In MP's hottest months, an inverter that derates early loses output when irradiance is highest. Verify the inverter's derating curve before purchasing.

- **Undersizing system**: Because the subsidy caps at 3 kW but bill savings keep rising with generation, [upsizing from 3 kW to 5 kW](https://surgepv.com/residential-solar-design/) can be financially viable for larger roofs and higher consumption. The extra capacity earns savings, not extra subsidy.
- **Not applying for net metering promptly**: Some applicants delay the net metering step, so their system sits idle after installation. Ask your vendor to file the completion report as soon as installation ends.

## Net Metering in Madhya Pradesh: Rules and Process

MP's net metering framework is governed by MPERC (Madhya Pradesh Electricity Regulatory Commission). Trade press reports that the current rules are the MPERC Grid Interactive Renewable Energy Systems and Related Matters Regulations (Revision-II), 2024 (RG-39(II), 14 March 2024). MPERC published a draft first amendment on 15 May 2026 to simplify rooftop solar and align it with PM Surya Ghar; one installer guide reports it was finalised in June 2026, which we could not confirm on mperc.in. Key points:

- **PM Surya Ghar fees**: The May 2026 draft proposes that applicants seeking net metering under PM Surya Ghar are fully exempt from processing charges and meter testing fees, with the connection agreement folded into the application form.
- **Bidirectional meter**: Your existing meter is replaced with a net metering compatible import-export meter by your DISCOM after the completion report.
- **Capacity limits**: Trade summaries report that systems up to 500 kW can use net metering, subject to your sanctioned load or contract demand. Separately, residential central assistance under the scheme applies to systems up to 10 kW, with the cash capped at the 3 kW amount. Check the regulation text for the capacity rule that applies to you.
- **Reported timelines**: Trade summaries report a 15-day feasibility decision (none for systems up to 10 kW), approval within 30 days of acknowledging the application, and meter installation and commissioning within 15 days of the installation certificate. We could not verify these against the MPERC text.

## MP-Specific Documents and Compliance Notes

MP DISCOMs generally follow the standard PM Surya Ghar document set. Depending on your property, you may also be asked for:

- **Roof rights declaration**: For flats and apartments, confirming you have authority to install on the roof. Ask your housing society or DISCOM division what form it wants.
- **Land records (khasra/khatoni)**: Rural applicants without formal property title deeds may be asked for khasra or khatoni records from the tehsil office as property proof.
- **Business registration**: For rooftop solar on small commercial premises, the DISCOM may ask for business documents. Purely residential applications do not.

## MP Net Metering: How MPERC Rules Affect Your Bill

Under MPERC net metering rules, monthly surplus solar generation is carried forward as credit units, and what remains unadjusted at the end of the settlement year is paid at a rate set by the regulator rather than at the retail tariff. Sources disagree on whether that rate is the average power purchase cost or the lowest discovered solar tariff, so we do not quote a per-unit figure and do not count export income in the payback above.

The billing mechanics for MP homeowners:
- Generation above consumption in a month: credited as carry-forward units
- Year-end settlement: remaining credit units are settled under the MPERC rule at a rate below the retail slab rate
- Import tariff paid for grid consumption: set by slab, from about ₹4.71 to ₹7.24 per unit in the order's domestic category

The self-consumption premium matters. Avoiding a purchase at a ₹7 slab is worth far more than a settlement-rate credit for the same kWh, so right-sizing the system to your actual annual consumption maximises the financial return. A 3 kW system for a Bhopal household consuming 400 units a month fits the load profile well.

The [gross metering vs net metering comparison](/blog/gross-metering-vs-net-metering/) explains why net metering is usually preferable for MP's residential consumers and how banking works.

**What happens if MP revises tariffs upward?** MPERC revises tariffs through periodic orders. If residential import tariffs rise, the value of solar self-consumption increases in proportion. As a formula: a ₹1 per unit increase on a 3 kW Bhopal system generating about 4,794 kWh a year with 80% self-consumed adds roughly ₹3,800 of annual savings (4,794 × 0.8 × ₹1).
