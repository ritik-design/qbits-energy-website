Canada does not have a national solar proposal. It has ten of them. Export credit runs from $0.04390 per kWh under Manitoba net billing to full retail near $0.145 in Ontario, a spread of more than three times on identical hardware. Settlement periods range from monthly in Alberta to a 24 month reset in Quebec. Capacity caps run from 100 kW to 5 MW. On top of that sits a federal Clean Technology ITC that pays businesses 30% refundable and pays homeowners nothing, plus six live provincial programs and two that closed recently and still appear in circulating templates. This guide compares the platforms Canadian installers should shortlist, judged on whether they model the province rather than the country.

| Province | Authority | Max size | Export credit | Reset |

| Manitoba | Manitoba Hydro | 100 kW | $0.04390 per kWh net billing | Monetary credit |

> **What most Canadian quotes get wrong.** They carry one export assumption for the whole country. In Ontario at retail credit with a 12 month window, oversizing against annual consumption is the correct recommendation. In Manitoba at 4.39 cents, the same recommendation destroys value, because every exported kWh returns roughly a seventh of what an imported one costs. Two provinces, opposite advice, identical roof.

The settlement period compounds it. Alberta settles monthly, so summer surplus cannot offset winter consumption at all. Quebec runs a 24 month reset, which is unusually generous and allows meaningful banking. Saskatchewan credits are non-expiring. A proposal that shows an annual net figure without stating the settlement basis is hiding whether the surplus actually carries.

## The Incentive Stack, And The Two Closed Programs

The federal Clean Technology Investment Tax Credit is the anchor for commercial and agricultural work: 30% refundable on capital cost through 31 December 2033. Refundable is the important word. It pays out even where the business has no tax liability in the year, which materially changes the cashflow case for smaller operators and farms. It does not apply to homeowners.

| Program | Applies to | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean Technology ITC | Business only | 30% refundable to 31 Dec 2033 |
| BC Hydro | Residential | Up to $10,000, solar plus battery |
| Quebec grant | Residential | $1,000 per kW, launched March 2026 |
| Ontario HRSP | Residential | Up to $5,000, solar only |
| PEI | Residential | $1,000 per kW, max $10,000 |
| Manitoba | Residential | $0.50 per DC watt to 10 kW |

Two programs are closed and still circulate. The Canada Greener Homes Grant closed on 31 March 2024. The associated loan closed on 1 October 2025. Any proposal template still listing either is promising money that does not exist, and in a market where homeowners research heavily, that is a credibility problem as much as an arithmetic one. ## Worked Example: 60 kW Commercial Rooftop, Ontario Versus Alberta

An illustrative calculation, not a specific customer project. Inputs: 60 kW DC, 1,150 kWh per kW specific yield, 69,000 kWh generation, 44,000 kWh on-site consumption, installed cost $105,000.

| Line | Ontario | Alberta, monthly settlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generation | 69,000 kWh | 69,000 kWh |
| Self-consumed | 44,000 kWh | 38,000 kWh |
| Exported | 25,000 kWh | 31,000 kWh |
| Export rate | $0.145 retail | Retail below 150 kW |
| Export value | $3,625 | $4,495 |
| Self-consumption value at $0.145 | $6,380 | $5,510 |
| Year 1 energy benefit | $10,005 | $10,005 |
| Clean Tech ITC at 30% refundable | $31,500 | $31,500 |
| Net cost after ITC | $73,500 | $73,500 |

The interesting line is self-consumption. Alberta's monthly settlement means summer surplus cannot offset winter draw, so a larger share of generation leaves as export even though total output is identical. Where a province settles on a longer cycle, the same array behaves as though more of its output were consumed on site. That effect is invisible to any tool working from annual totals, and it is the reason two provinces with similar headline rates can still produce different answers.
