**An eligible Kerala household can apply for the central PM Surya Ghar rooftop-solar assistance through the [official national portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/).** Under MNRE's guidelines the assistance is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, which gives the usual ceiling of ₹78,000 at 3 kW of eligible solar-module capacity. KSEB Ltd handles the local grid steps: feasibility where required, inspection, the meter and commissioning. An installer quote should show the project cost and any assistance separately.

The figures below do not include a Kerala cash top-up, because none was found in official sources as of October 2026. Verify any advertised state benefit against a current government order before budgeting for it, and ask for a site-specific generation estimate and your current KSEB tariff before estimating payback.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS] Short version
> - The central subsidy applies in Kerala: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, with the usual ceiling of ₹78,000 at 3 kW.

> - Choose a vendor registered on the national portal.
> - Do not count on a state top-up unless you see an order; none was found in official sources as of October 2026.

## What PM Surya Ghar pays in Kerala

The [MNRE guidelines for central financial assistance (CFA) to residential consumers](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) (clause 5) set the standard calculation. Kerala is not one of the "special category" states, so the standard rates apply.

Table: PM Surya Ghar central assistance by eligible solar-module capacity, standard rates that apply in Kerala

| Eligible solar-module capacity | Central assistance (standard rates) |
| --- | ---: |
| First 2 kW (or part of it) | ₹30,000 per kW |
| Third kW (or part of it) | ₹18,000 per kW |
| Above 3 kW | No additional assistance |
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 |
| 3 kW and above | ₹78,000 (maximum) |

The guideline's own worked examples show how part-kilowatts count: 1.5 kW earns ₹45,000, 2.5 kW earns ₹69,000, and a 6 kW plant still earns the ₹78,000 maximum. Assistance is calculated on the rated DC capacity of the modules, not on the inverter's size (clause 5k). Adding a battery does not change the calculation (clause 5l).

Three conditions matter for a Kerala home:

- **Domestic content.** The modules must be domestically manufactured from domestically manufactured cells. Non-DCR modules in any form make the installation ineligible for CFA (clause 5m).
- **Residential connection.** The plant must be tagged to a residential KSEB connection and sit on a roof, terrace, balcony or elevated structure (clause 5b). Off-grid installations are not eligible (clause 6o).
- **One claim per installation.** CFA is available once for an installation, and a relocated plant is not eligible again (clause 5o).

The assistance is not a separate payment for each panel, inverter or battery, and it is not a promise. KSEB's inspection and the portal checks decide whether a given project is released. Read the [full PM Surya Ghar guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) and the [eligibility checklist](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-eligibility/) before signing a purchase contract.

## Is there an extra Kerala state subsidy?

Not that we could find. The MNRE guidelines allow a state to add its own residential support, but only if it follows the scheme guidelines and is disbursed through the national portal (clause 5i). We found no such Kerala order for homes as of October 2026. Secondary summaries of Kerala's rooftop-solar position also report no general state cash top-up, and describe the KSEB "Soura" programme as now working alongside the central scheme rather than as a separate payment. Treat that as a reading of third-party sources, not an official statement.

> [!WARNING] Before you count a Kerala state top-up
> What to do with this:
>
> - If a salesperson mentions a Kerala or ANERT add-on, ask for the current order number, the applicant conditions and the payment path in writing.
> - Check [ANERT](https://anert.gov.in/), the state renewable-energy agency, and your KSEB section office for any new notice.
> - Keep any possible state benefit out of your confirmed project economics until you have the order in hand.

## How the application runs in Kerala

KSEB Ltd is Kerala's main distribution licensee, so the DISCOM field on the national portal reads KSEB for most households. Kerala has two places to look, and they serve the same purpose:

Table: The two Kerala application routes, what each is and how to treat it

| Route | What it is | How to treat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PM Surya Ghar National Portal | The central scheme portal where you register, pick a registered vendor, update installation details and redeem the CFA e-token | Required for the central subsidy (MNRE guidelines, clause 6a) |
| KSEB rooftop solar portal | KSEB's own single-window rooftop solar portal, listed as ekiran.kseb.in, reported to offer "apply with subsidy" and "apply without subsidy" paths | Secondary-source detail: we could not open the site on 14 October 2026 (the connection was refused). Confirm with KSEB which route is current |

Whichever entry point you use, file one application per plant. Before you start, KSEB-linked portals reportedly want the mobile number and email on your KSEB consumer record to match the ones you use, so update them with KSEB first if they are out of date.

### The steps in order

<!-- steps -->
1. **Register on the national portal.** Use your mobile number and the KSEB consumer number from your bill, select Kerala and KSEB as the DISCOM, and generate your application ID (clause 6b).
2. **Check what KSEB requires for feasibility.** Under the central Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, as amended in February 2024, rooftop systems up to 10 kW do not need a technical feasibility study (see the [PIB release of 23 February 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2008289)). The MNRE guidelines add that until a state commission and DISCOM operationalise this, the consumer uploads the documents wherever the state regulation requires it (clause 6h). Ask your KSEB section office whether it asks for a feasibility step on your connection and get the answer in writing.
3. **Choose a vendor registered on the national portal.** There is no DISCOM tender for the scheme. You may pick any registered vendor at mutually agreed rates (clause 6c). The [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) shows how to check the record, and the [solar quotation checklist](/blog/solar-quotation-checklist/) covers the scope of the quote.
4. **Install, then update the portal.** After installation, you upload system details and geo-tagged photographs. The vendor may help you through your own login (clause 6j).
5. **KSEB inspects and commissions.** KSEB inspects the system, signs the metering agreement, and approves or returns the application (clause 6k). The meter is installed by KSEB, or you may buy one from enlisted meter vendors (clause 6l). The central Rules, as amended, set 15 days for the DISCOM's commissioning of rooftop PV.
6. **Redeem the e-token.** Once the inspection is complete, the e-token activates for the eligible amount and you redeem it on the portal (clause 6p). The CFA is processed within 15 days of DISCOM approval (clause 6t).

Those are the clocks written into the rules. They are limits for the authority, not a forecast of your elapsed time. Document gaps, inspection scheduling, meter availability and a returned application all add time. If a stage stalls, the consumer can raise a grievance on the portal, through the national call centre or on the app, and every grievance gets a tracking ID. MNRE's guidelines set a 30-day resolution period (clause 11).

### Kerala subsidy status check

People search for "Kerala solar subsidy status" mostly because the money has not arrived. Work through it in this order:

<!-- steps -->
1. Log in to the national portal with the registered mobile number and open your application. Note the stage: application, vendor, installation details, inspection, e-token or claim.
2. If the stage is "pending with DISCOM", ask the KSEB section office for the inspection date or the objection raised. Ask for it in writing or by email.
3. If the e-token is active but you have not redeemed it, redeem it with the bank details on your profile. A mismatch between the bank account holder name and the applicant name is a common reason for a claim to be returned.
4. If more than 15 days have passed since DISCOM approval and the claim is still unprocessed, raise a grievance on the portal and keep the tracking ID.
5. Keep the quote, invoice, photographs and inspection record in one folder.

Our [status-check guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-status-check/) lists each portal state in plain words, and the [rejection reasons guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-rejection-reasons/) explains what a returned application usually means. KSEB's customer-care line is reported as 1912 in secondary sources, so confirm it on KSEB's own site.

## Net metering in Kerala: the rules changed in late 2025

Kerala's net metering is set by the Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission (KSERC), not by the central scheme. The history matters because older guides describe the older rules:

- KSERC notified its Renewable Energy and Net Metering Regulations on 7 February 2020 and amended them in July 2022. In August 2024 the Commission issued a Second Amendment with a statement of reasons that said the draft did not introduce gross metering or net billing for any class of consumers.
- Secondary sources report that KSERC then notified the **KSERC (Renewable Energy and Related Matters) Regulations, 2025** in November 2025, with net metering, banking and grid support charges fully applicable from 1 January 2026. They report net billing for larger systems and no grid support charge for systems up to and including 10 kW.
- The same summaries disagree on the capacity limit for net metering and on battery requirements for larger plants, and one installer page mentions a High Court stay that we could not confirm. For these reasons, do not rely on a figure from a blog, including ours.

What you can do today:

- Ask KSEB, in writing, whether your proposed capacity qualifies for net metering or is treated as net billing under the regulation now in force for your connection.
- Ask what rate or credit is applied to exported units, and what the banking and settlement period is. These terms decide how the system's surplus is valued.
- Read the final regulation text on the [KSERC website](https://erckerala.org/) and match it against the answer you received.
- Use the [net metering complete guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) and the [gross metering versus net metering explainer](/blog/gross-metering-vs-net-metering/) for the differences in how each arrangement bills you, and the [application guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) and [approval timeline guide](/blog/net-meter-approval-timeline/) for the national picture.

> [!TIP] Budget on self-consumption
> Self-consumption is the safer assumption for budgeting. A unit you use directly offsets the tariff you would pay, while an exported unit is valued under whichever mechanism KSERC prescribes. Keep the two apart in any payback sheet.

## Monsoon, roof orientation and your generation estimate

Kerala gets two monsoon spells: the south-west monsoon (roughly June to September) and the north-east monsoon (October to November). Cloud cover cuts output in those months, and dense vegetation, coconut palms and neighbouring buildings add shading. A state-wide average cannot stand in for a site estimate, and we have deliberately not printed a table of monthly kilowatt-hours because those numbers depend on your roof.

> [!CHECKLIST] Ask the installer for
> - a monthly generation model that names the weather dataset (for example the [Global Solar Atlas](https://globalsolaratlas.info/), a World Bank-funded tool) and the loss assumptions;
> - the roof azimuth, tilt and a shading analysis for your plot;
> - a comparison with your own KSEB bills month by month, because Kerala households use less power in some monsoon months than in the summer; and
> - the payback arithmetic, not just the answer.

Our [irradiance data guide](/blog/solar-irradiance-data-india-statewise/) explains how to find location-specific inputs. If your backup needs matter, because your area has frequent outages, see the [on-grid versus hybrid comparison](/blog/on-grid-vs-hybrid/) and the [Qbits hybrid inverter range](/hybrid-inverter/). A hybrid inverter needs a battery, adds cost and changes the design, so size it against the loads you actually need to keep running.

## Equipment and the vendor contract

Ask for module and inverter makes and models in writing and check them against the scheme's conditions rather than a brand name. Domestic-content modules are a condition of the subsidy. MNRE's ALMM covers modules and cells, and it does not contain an inverter list, so ask separately for the inverter's grid-connection and safety documents that KSEB requires. The [ALMM guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains what the list covers.

Registered vendors owe the consumer repairs and maintenance free of charge for five years from commissioning under the scheme's comprehensive maintenance contract, and the manufacturers' component warranties are handed over to the consumer (clause 7d). Put the responsibility split in your contract: workmanship, component warranty and KSEB paperwork.

Coastal and humid conditions are real for Kochi, Kozhikode and Thiruvananthapuram. Compare the enclosure rating printed on each inverter's datasheet and the mounting-structure material in the quote, and read the [IP65 versus IP66 explainer](/blog/ip65-vs-ip66-solar-inverters-weather-protection-guide/) before deciding. ## Common mistakes Kerala applicants make

- **Paying a large advance before the paperwork is clear.** Tie milestone payments to the portal stages and KSEB's inspection, not to the installer's convenience.
- **Budgeting a state top-up that has no order behind it.** Ask for the order number.
- **Using a blog's metering rules.** The 2025 KSERC regulations changed the position, so get KSEB's answer in writing.
- **Planning from summer-month generation.** Use an annual model that accounts for both monsoon periods.
- **Ignoring shade from vegetation and neighbours.** A shading analysis belongs in the quote.
- **Skipping the vendor check.** Verify the registration yourself and compare the portal name, quote and GST invoice.
