Most reviews of [Sunbase](/blog/sunbase-alternative/) go wrong in the first paragraph, because they line it up against Aurora Solar or PVsyst and then mark it down for things it never set out to do. Sunbase is not a design engine. It is a CRM, field sales, and operations platform sold to solar, roofing, and construction contractors, and it should be judged on that job. This review covers what Sunbase actually is, what its own site says about pricing, where the product is genuinely strong, where it stops short of a dedicated design platform, and which buyer it fits. The useful question is not whether Sunbase beats a simulation tool. It is which layer of your stack it occupies, and what you still need beside it.

> **TL;DR**
> - Sunbase is a modular contractor management platform covering CRM, proposals, project management, inventory, financials, and reporting for solar, roofing, and construction.
> - Its published starting price is $59 per user per month, with no fixed public tiers and quotes built per business.
> - Real strengths sit in field sales: territory mapping, route planning, rep tracking, canvassing lead capture, and contract plus e-signature flow.
> - It is not a design or simulation tool, and it says so, positioning instead as something that integrates with separate solar [design software](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/solar-design-software-pricing/).
> - Most volume installers should run a CRM like Sunbase alongside a design platform, not instead of one.

**Short version.** Sunbase is a modular solar CRM and field operations platform starting at $59 per user per month, strongest at door-to-door sales workflow, pipeline management, proposals with e-signature, and post-sale project tracking. It is not an engineering design tool and does not claim to be. Judged as a CRM it is a credible option. Judged against Aurora or PVsyst it is the wrong category entirely.

That distinction matters commercially, not just semantically. Teams that buy a CRM expecting design depth end up disappointed by a product that was honest with them. Teams that buy a design tool expecting pipeline management end up running their sales process in a spreadsheet. Our [solar design software pricing comparison](/blog/solar-design-software-pricing/) covers the design layer in detail. This page covers the layer above it.

## What Sunbase Actually Is

Sunbase markets itself as an all-in-one contractor management platform, and the industry list is solar, roofing, and construction rather than solar alone. That breadth is a design decision with consequences in both directions, which we will come back to.

The platform is modular. According to Sunbase's own pricing page, businesses select from contractor CRM and sales management, proposal and estimation software, project management, inventory and asset management, financial management, and reporting and analytics dashboards. You are not buying one product. You are assembling a configuration.

Within the solar CRM module, Sunbase lists lead management, a visual sales pipeline, automated follow-ups, appointment scheduling, a proposal and quote builder, financing and incentive tracking, contract and permit workflows with e-signatures, dashboards and reports, territory mapping, and door-to-door canvassing support.

Read that list carefully and the centre of gravity is obvious. Almost every item is about moving a human transaction forward. Nothing in it is about irradiance, string sizing, or loss modelling. That is the product being coherent, not the product being incomplete.

## Sunbase Pricing in 2026

Sunbase publishes one number and declines to publish the rest. Its pricing page states that plans start from $59 per user per month, with customised pricing based on business requirements. There are no fixed tiers listed.

The stated factors that move the price are number of team members, industry workflow, which modules you select, and implementation plus training services. The page directs buyers to request a quote.

We are deliberately not extrapolating a five-seat annual figure from that floor, because the module structure means any such number would be invented. What we will say is the practical implication: $59 describes an entry configuration, and a solar contractor who wants CRM plus proposals plus project management plus reporting is buying more than the entry configuration. Ask for the quote in writing, itemised by module, before you build a budget around the headline.

## Where Sunbase Is Genuinely Strong

Credit where it is earned. These are the areas where Sunbase is doing something most design-first platforms do not attempt at all.

**Door-to-door and field sales workflow.** This is the standout. Sunbase provides territory mapping so areas can be defined and overlap prevented, route optimisation for canvassers, lead capture in the field, real-time rep location on a live map, and performance tracking per rep. For a company that grows by knocking doors, that is the operating system of the business. Design tools do not compete here because they never entered the category.

**Pipeline and lead routing discipline.** A visual pipeline with stages, automated follow-up sequences, and appointment scheduling solves a problem that kills more solar companies than bad designs do: leads going cold between the door knock and the sit. Follow-up automation is unglamorous and it is where revenue leaks.

**Deal-to-document flow.** Proposal and quote generation, financing plans attached to the deal, contract and permit workflows, and e-signature collection all sitting on the same record removes a lot of copy-paste. Collecting signatures on contracts and change orders inside the system, rather than through a separate e-sign product, is a genuine time saving for a coordinator handling twenty live jobs.

**Operations after the sale.** Project management, crew scheduling and task assignment, inventory and asset tracking, financial tools, and dashboards mean the record does not die at signature. Plenty of solar software stops the moment the customer says yes. The install, the inspection, the materials, and the invoice are where margin is won or lost.

**Modularity.** Being able to buy CRM without inventory, or proposals without financials, is a real advantage for a small contractor. It is also the reason the pricing is opaque, so the benefit comes with a cost.

**One platform across trades.** Solar, roofing, and construction in one system suits the many contractors who do two of the three. A solar-only tool forces those companies to run parallel systems.

## Where Sunbase Stops, Relative to a Design Platform

This section is not a list of failures. Sunbase does not claim these capabilities, and marking a CRM down for lacking a simulation engine would be dishonest. What follows is a map of what you still need, so nobody buys with the wrong expectation.

**Engineering depth.** Sunbase's proposal module references auto system sizing based on energy usage and roof dimensions, and remote rooftop assessment with site and PV layout visuals. That supports a sales conversation. It is a different thing from module-level layout with obstruction modelling, setback and fire-path handling, string configuration, and inverter matching against real hardware datasheets.

**Bankable simulation.** Sunbase's own material describes production and financial modelling that converts estimates into savings, ROI, and payback. The underlying simulation engine is not identified on the pages we reviewed. If a lender, an investor, or a C&I client wants an 8,760-hour hourly yield report with documented loss assumptions, that output comes from a dedicated design platform. Our [PVsyst review](/blog/pvsyst-review/) covers when a bankable report is contractually required rather than merely nice to have.

**Code and tariff libraries.** Electrical code handling, whether NEC, IEC, AS/NZS, or IS, and structured utility tariff or feed-in tariff libraries are the domain of design software. A CRM can store a financing plan. It does not decide whether your string voltage clears the design-temperature limit.

**Shading analysis.** We did not find a shading engine described in the Sunbase material we reviewed, so treat this as an open question to put to the vendor rather than a settled absence. For a clean gable roof that may not bite. For a roof with three vents, a chimney, and a neighbouring tree, a production number generated without shading analysis is a number you will have to defend later.

| Layer | What owns it | Sunbase role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead capture and canvassing | CRM | Core strength |
| Pipeline, follow-up, scheduling | CRM | Core strength |
| Array layout and shading | Design platform | Not its job |
| Simulation and yield reporting | Design platform | Not its job |
| Code compliance checks | Design platform | Not its job |
| Customer-facing proposal | Either, depending on stack | Supported |
| Contract, e-signature, permits | CRM | Core strength |
| Install, crew, inventory, invoicing | Ops platform | Core strength |

> **What most buyers get wrong.** They run a single evaluation and expect one purchase to cover design accuracy and sales operations. Those are two different products with two different buyers inside your company. Decide which problem is currently costing you more money, solve that one properly, and integrate rather than compromise.

**A word on the breadth trade-off.** Serving solar, roofing, and construction from one platform means solar-specific depth competes for roadmap attention with two other trades. That is a reasonable strategy and it wins the multi-trade contractor. It is also why a solar-only shop should check that the solar workflows are as deep as they need, rather than assuming breadth implies depth in their lane.

## Who Should Buy Sunbase

Sunbase is a sensible purchase if several of these describe you.

- You run a door-to-door or field canvassing sales motion and currently manage territories in a shared map or a spreadsheet.
- Your reps are numerous enough that routing, territory overlap, and activity tracking are real management problems.
- Leads are leaking between first contact and appointment because follow-up is manual.
- You want contracts, change orders, and e-signatures on the same record as the deal.
- Your jobs need tracking after signature, through scheduling, materials, and invoicing.
- You operate across solar and at least one of roofing or construction, so a multi-trade platform avoids running two systems.

For that company, the operational gain is not subtle. Organising a field sales team is the difference between growth and churn, and a design tool will never do it.

## Who Should Not Buy Sunbase

- **Engineering-led design consultancies.** Your output is drawings and yield reports. A CRM does not move that needle.
- **Teams whose bottleneck is design accuracy.** If proposals are being rebuilt because production numbers get challenged, buy design capability first.
- **Anyone needing lender-accepted simulation as standard output.** That is a separate tool regardless of which CRM you run.
- **Very small referral-led teams.** One to three people closing warm introductions will not use territory mapping, canvassing routes, or inventory modules, and a modular ops platform becomes shelfware.
- **Buyers who need a published, predictable price to get budget approval.** The quote-based model is workable, but it adds a procurement cycle that some buyers cannot absorb.

## Verdict

Sunbase is a competent modular CRM and operations platform for contractors, and it is strongest exactly where the solar industry is weakest: organising field sales and keeping the job moving after the contract is signed. Territory mapping, canvassing support, follow-up automation, e-signature flow, and post-sale project tracking are real capabilities that most design-first tools do not offer at all.

It is not a design platform, it does not claim to be one, and the biggest mistake a buyer can make is testing it as though it were. Scoring Sunbase against a simulation engine tells you nothing useful about either product. The pricing is the other thing to handle carefully. A $59 per user per month starting point is genuinely accessible, but a modular quote-based model means the entry number and the working configuration are different, and only a written itemised quote closes that gap.

Our recommendation for a growing installer with a field sales motion is to run both layers deliberately. Put design and proposals in a platform built for it, at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year with SurgePV, and put customers, contracts, and operations in a CRM like Sunbase. That costs less than most people expect, because neither product is being stretched into work it was not built for.

Three things to do next:

- Write down which of your two problems is currently more expensive, weak design output or a leaking pipeline, and buy for that one first.
- Get an itemised Sunbase quote listing every module you would actually enable, and confirm exactly which design tools it integrates with and what data crosses the boundary.
- Run one real canvassed lead end to end, from door knock to signed contract to scheduled install, before you commit to a seat count.
