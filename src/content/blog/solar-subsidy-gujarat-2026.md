## What Is the Gujarat Solar Subsidy — Both Schemes Explained

Gujarat homeowners in 2026 can access two distinct subsidy programmes that are designed to work in parallel. Many people apply for one and miss the other; understanding both from the outset prevents money from being left on the table.

**The Central Scheme — PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana**

Launched in February 2024 by the Government of India, PM Surya Ghar is the largest residential rooftop solar subsidy in India's history. It is administered nationally through [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and implemented through state DISCOMs. The subsidy is a direct bank transfer — paid after installation and commissioning — not a discount at the time of purchase. For a full overview of the scheme including eligibility and the five-step application process, the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) covers the national framework in detail.

**The State Scheme — Surya Gujarat**

Surya Gujarat is the Gujarat government's state-level rooftop solar programme for residential consumers, administered by [GEDA](https://geda.gujarat.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} (Gujarat Energy Development Agency) in coordination with [GUVNL](https://www.guvnl.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} (Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Limited) and the four Gujarat DISCOMs. The state scheme provides additional per-kW incentives on top of the central subsidy, making Gujarat's combined offer one of the most attractive in India.

The two schemes are applied for through slightly different channels but can be pursued simultaneously. In practice, the empanelled installer handles most of the paperwork across both channels; your job is to verify the documents are submitted correctly and to track the portal status for each scheme independently.

## Gujarat's Four DISCOMs — Who You Apply To and What Changes

Gujarat is unusual among Indian states in having four distinct distribution companies, each with geographic jurisdiction. Your electricity bill tells you which DISCOM supplies your connection, and that DISCOM owns your subsidy application process, net metering approval, and smart meter installation. The central PM Surya Ghar scheme flows through all four, but processing culture, timelines, and local empanelled vendor lists vary.

Understanding your [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) before applying prevents the common mistake of submitting to the wrong entity or expecting uniform timelines across the state.

### DGVCL — Dakshin Gujarat Vij Company Limited

**Coverage:** Surat, Navsari, Valsad, Bharuch, Tapi, Dangs, Narmada

Surat is DGVCL's largest load centre and has one of the highest concentrations of PM Surya Ghar applicants in India. The volume has created institutional familiarity — DGVCL's rooftop solar processing desk is among the more experienced in Gujarat. Expected timeline from application to commissioned net meter: 35–50 days in Surat city, 45–60 days in smaller DGVCL towns.

- **DGVCL portal:** Applications through the national PM Surya Ghar portal, with DISCOM verification via the DGVCL back-end system
- **Key document at DGVCL:** Consumer number from your electricity bill (mandatory for portal registration)
- **Surat applicant note:** Due to high volume, DGVCL sometimes runs batch commissioning inspections; coordinate with your installer on scheduling

### PGVCL — Paschim Gujarat Vij Company Limited

**Coverage:** Rajkot, Jamnagar, Junagadh, Porbandar, Amreli, Bhavnagar, Morbi, Surendranagar, Gir Somnath, Devbhumi Dwarka, Botad

PGVCL covers a large geographic area including significant rural and coastal zones. Coastal districts around Jamnagar and Porbandar experience above-average solar irradiance (4.9–5.5 peak sun hours), making solar economics especially favourable. Processing timelines are 45–65 days given the mix of urban centres and dispersed rural service areas.

- **Coastal note:** IP66-rated inverters are strongly recommended in coastal PGVCL areas; salt-laden air accelerates corrosion on lower-rated equipment
- **Rural connectivity:** Some remote PGVCL areas with weaker grid reliability may benefit from hybrid systems with battery backup

### MGVCL — Madhya Gujarat Vij Company Limited

**Coverage:** Vadodara, Anand, Kheda, Panchmahal, Dahod, Mahisagar, Chhota Udaipur

Vadodara is MGVCL's largest city. The company serves a mix of industrial-heavy and agricultural areas. Net metering approvals in Vadodara city typically run 40–55 days. The Anand and Kheda districts, with significant agricultural solar context (KUSUM schemes run parallel here), have MGVCL officers experienced with solar regulatory processes.

- **Documentation tip for MGVCL:** Property tax receipt is the preferred ownership document — accepted faster than some other proof types at the local DISCOM office

### UGVCL — Uttar Gujarat Vij Company Limited

**Coverage:** Ahmedabad, Gandhinagar, Mehsana, Banaskantha, Patan, Sabarkantha, Aravalli, Mahesana

Ahmedabad falls under UGVCL — the state's capital region and largest city. UGVCL handles the highest absolute volume of PM Surya Ghar applications in Gujarat. Despite the volume, UGVCL's processing has accelerated with dedicated rooftop solar desks in Ahmedabad. Expected timeline: 30–50 days for Ahmedabad city applicants, 50–65 days for smaller UGVCL towns in Banaskantha or Patan districts.

- **Gandhinagar applicants:** Being the state capital, Gandhinagar UGVCL offices have direct coordination with GEDA, often resulting in faster state scheme processing

## Net Metering in Gujarat — Rates, Banking, and What You Actually Earn

Once your rooftop solar system is commissioned in Gujarat, the financial relationship with your DISCOM is governed by Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (GERC) net metering regulations. Gujarat has one of India's more structured [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) frameworks — rules are consistent, grievance redressal is functional, and the banking mechanism provides genuine financial benefit.

**How Gujarat's net metering works:**

- Your existing meter is replaced with a bidirectional smart meter at no cost to you (DISCOM-supplied under the PM Surya Ghar scheme)
- During the day when your solar generation exceeds consumption, surplus units are exported to the grid
- The GERC-set [feed-in tariff](/glossary/feed-in-tariff/) (buyback rate) applies to exported units
- Surplus units are "banked" in your DISCOM account and can offset future bills for up to 12 months
- At the end of the banking period (typically March annually), any remaining credited units are settled in cash at the prevailing buyback rate

**Current Gujarat net metering buyback rates (2026):**

| DISCOM Zone | Retail Import Tariff (approx.) | Solar Export / Buyback Rate | Self-Consumption Advantage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DGVCL (Surat area) | ₹5.50–₹7.50/unit | ₹2.25–₹3.00/unit | ₹3.25–₹4.50/unit saved |
| PGVCL (Rajkot area) | ₹5.00–₹7.00/unit | ₹2.25–₹3.00/unit | ₹2.75–₹4.00/unit saved |
| MGVCL (Vadodara area) | ₹5.50–₹7.50/unit | ₹2.25–₹3.00/unit | ₹3.25–₹4.50/unit saved |
| UGVCL (Ahmedabad area) | ₹5.50–₹8.00/unit | ₹2.50–₹3.50/unit | ₹3.00–₹4.50/unit saved |

*Tariff rates are indicative for FY2025–26 based on GERC orders. Verify current rates at your DISCOM office or the GERC website before finalising financial projections.*

The gap between import tariff (what you pay for grid electricity) and export tariff (what your DISCOM credits for exported solar) is the reason system sizing and self-consumption behaviour matter. Every unit you consume directly from your own solar generation saves ₹5.50–₹8.00; every unit you export earns only ₹2.25–₹3.50. The design implication: size your system to match your consumption profile, not to maximise export.

## How to Apply for Solar Subsidy in Gujarat — Step-by-Step Process

The Gujarat solar subsidy application involves two parallel tracks — the national PM Surya Ghar portal and the GEDA/DISCOM state scheme — but in practice, your GEDA-registered empanelled installer handles the coordination between them. Your job is to understand each step so you can verify it is being done correctly rather than discovering a procedural gap after installation.

**Before you apply — prerequisites:**

- **Verify your DISCOM** — Check your electricity bill for DISCOM name (DGVCL/PGVCL/MGVCL/UGVCL)
- **Check your sanctioned load** — The proposed solar system cannot exceed your sanctioned load. Most 3 kW systems require a 5 kVA or higher sanctioned load
- **Confirm roof ownership** — You need ownership documents or an RWA no-objection certificate for apartment buildings
- **Gather documents** — Electricity bill, Aadhaar, PAN, bank details (Aadhaar-linked), property proof, roof photographs

**The six-step Gujarat application process:**

1. **Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in** with your electricity consumer number, Aadhaar, and mobile number. Complete e-KYC. Select "Gujarat" as your state and choose your DISCOM. This creates your national scheme application.

2. **Simultaneously register on the GEDA portal** at [geda.gujarat.gov.in](https://geda.gujarat.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} for the state Surya Gujarat component. You will need the same documents — the two registrations can run in parallel from day one.

3. **Obtain DISCOM technical feasibility approval.** The DISCOM reviews your application and confirms grid capacity at your connection point. MNRE mandates 15 days; Gujarat DISCOMs typically respond in 7–20 days depending on load on the approval desk.

4. **Select a GEDA-registered and DISCOM-empanelled vendor.** Your installer must appear on both lists. Verify on the respective portals — not on the vendor's printed literature. Confirm that the specific inverter model being proposed appears on the MNRE ALMM list; a non-ALMM inverter means subsidy rejection regardless of how smooth the rest of the process goes. Check [PM Surya Ghar eligibility requirements](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-eligibility/) if uncertain about which equipment qualifies.

5. **Install, commission, and notify DISCOM for inspection.** The DISCOM engineer visits to verify installation, confirm ALMM equipment, inspect earthing and safety, and arrange bidirectional smart meter installation. This step typically takes 7–20 days from notification.

6. **Upload commissioning documents to both portals.** Upload the DISCOM commissioning certificate, vendor invoice, and bank details to pmsuryaghar.gov.in for the central subsidy. Do the same on the GEDA portal for the state top-up. Both disbursements are direct bank transfers — expect 30–60 days for each.

**Documents checklist for Gujarat applicants:**

- Recent electricity bill (consumer number, DISCOM name visible)
- Aadhaar card (for e-KYC on both portals)
- PAN card
- Bank passbook or cancelled cheque (Aadhaar-linked account preferred)
- Property ownership proof: sale deed, property tax receipt, or society share certificate
- For apartment owners: RWA no-objection certificate
- Rooftop photographs (minimum 4 — all four orientations)
- Vendor quotation with model numbers and ALMM confirmation

## On-Grid vs Hybrid Inverter for Gujarat — Which Is Right?

Gujarat's grid quality is a key input into the inverter decision. Unlike states such as Uttar Pradesh or Bihar, where extended power cuts are common, Gujarat's distribution infrastructure — particularly in urban areas served by UGVCL, DGVCL, and MGVCL — delivers among the lowest outage hours in India. This has a direct bearing on the on-grid vs hybrid decision.

**Pros:**
- ✓ Lower upfront cost — on-grid inverters cost 25–40% less than equivalent hybrid models
- ✓ Simpler commissioning — fewer components, faster DISCOM approval
- ✓ Fully subsidy-eligible under PM Surya Ghar and Surya Gujarat
- ✓ Optimised for Gujarat's stable grid — anti-islanding function works as designed
- ✓ Fastest payback — typically 1–2 years shorter than a comparable hybrid installation

**Cons:**
- ✗ Zero power during outages — on-grid inverters shut down when the grid goes down, by regulation and design
- ✗ Cannot add battery backup without retrofitting the inverter
- ✗ Not recommended for PGVCL rural areas with frequent outages

## Common Mistakes That Delay or Deny Gujarat Solar Subsidy

Gujarat's subsidy process is well-administered, but the same procedural errors that derail applications nationally also appear here. These are the errors reported most frequently by Gujarat DISCOM officers and GEDA administrators.

- **Not verifying ALMM compliance before installation** — The installer's verbal confirmation is not sufficient. Check the exact inverter model number against the MNRE ALMM list before installation day. A single non-listed model means the full subsidy claim — both central and state — is rejected at the verification stage.
- **Installing before DISCOM feasibility approval** — DGVCL, PGVCL, MGVCL, and UGVCL all require formal feasibility approval before physical work begins. Installing first and applying after is grounds for commissioning refusal.
- **Choosing a non-empanelled vendor** — Verify the vendor on both the national PM Surya Ghar portal and the GEDA empanelment list. Some vendors are empanelled on one list but not the other; you need both to claim both subsidies.
- **Mismatched bank account details** — The account submitted must exactly match the Aadhaar-linked account. Even a minor name discrepancy (full name vs initials) causes transfer rejection and adds 30–45 days to disbursement.
- **Missing the GEDA state application** — Many homeowners complete the national PM Surya Ghar application but never register on the GEDA portal. The state top-up does not flow automatically from the national scheme — it requires a separate registration and document upload.
- **Sizing a system above sanctioned load without enhancement** — A proposed 3 kW system on a connection with a 3 kVA sanctioned load will fail feasibility. Apply for load enhancement in parallel if needed — it typically adds 15–20 days but does not block the main application.
- **Not tracking both portal statuses** — Each portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in and geda.gujarat.gov.in) has its own status tracker. Check both weekly. Portals sometimes require re-upload of documents without active notification.

## Frequently Missed Details That Change the Gujarat Solar Subsidy Maths

Several details about the Gujarat solar subsidy process are either not prominently communicated on the portals or require DISCOM-specific knowledge that first-time applicants often miss. These are worth knowing before you finalise your system design or vendor selection.

**The GERC banking year matters for cash flow.** Gujarat's net metering regulations allow surplus solar units to be banked for up to 12 months. The banking year typically resets in March. If your system is commissioned in February, you have only a few weeks of banking before the annual settlement. Commission your system between April and September to maximise the banking window before the next March reset — this can meaningfully improve first-year cash flow.

**Fixed charges remain regardless of solar generation.** Gujarat DISCOMs levy a fixed demand charge on every connection regardless of how much electricity you consume or how much your solar system generates. For a typical residential connection in Ahmedabad, fixed charges run ₹50–₹150 per month depending on sanctioned load. Factor this into your "zero bill" expectations — the actual minimum bill is the fixed charge component, not zero.

**Load enhancement and system sizing interact.** If your sanctioned load is 3 kVA and you want a 3 kW solar system, the DISCOM will typically reject feasibility. A 3 kW solar system with battery charging (hybrid scenario) may need a 5–7.5 kVA sanctioned load. Apply for load enhancement at the same time as the solar application — Gujarat DISCOMs process both concurrently, and the enhancement typically takes 15–20 additional days but does not restart the solar application clock.

**GEDA empanelment vs DISCOM empanelment are different lists.** A vendor may appear on the national PM Surya Ghar DISCOM empanelment list without being on the GEDA state scheme list, or vice versa. To claim both subsidies, your vendor must appear on both. Ask the vendor to show you their current registration status on both portals before signing any contract.

**The smart meter is DISCOM-supplied — you do not pay for it.** Under PM Surya Ghar, the bidirectional smart meter required for net metering is procured and installed by the DISCOM at no cost to the homeowner. Some unscrupulous vendors include a "meter upgrade charge" in their quotation. If you see this line item, question it before signing.
