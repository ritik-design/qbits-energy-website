A homeowner in a Tier-2 city sees an ad promising a 3 kW rooftop solar system for almost nothing after subsidy. A polite sales agent visits, shows a government-looking ID, and collects a Rs 50,000 booking advance. Then the phone goes silent. This script, with small variations, is playing out across India as rooftop solar demand surges under [PM Surya Ghar](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/pm-surya-ghar-2-0-analysis) [Muft Bijli Yojana](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana-explained/).

The scheme has put solar within reach of crores of households. It has also created the perfect cover for fraud. Most first-time buyers do not know how the subsidy flows, who may install, or what a real approval looks like. The Press Information Bureau itself has issued warnings about fraudulent websites imitating the official portal, according to [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=153495&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=1), 2024.

This guide maps the nine [red flags](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/red-flags-solar-quote) that expose a solar scam in India. It also covers verification steps that take under an hour, protective contract terms, and what to do if money has already gone. Read it before you pay anyone anything.

## Why Solar Scams Are Rising in India Right Now

Three forces have converged to make rooftop solar a target-rich market for fraud.

First, demand exploded. PM Surya Ghar targets 1 crore household installations, and registrations have moved into the lakhs. Every new entrant is a first-time solar buyer with no reference point for fair pricing or process.

Second, the subsidy creates confusion. The money goes from the government to the homeowner's bank account after installation and DISCOM verification. Scammers exploit this gap by inventing fees, faster-approval charges, and inflated subsidy figures that sound plausible to someone who has never read the scheme rules.

Third, enforcement lags the market. Vendor registration happens at the DISCOM level, and until recently there was no central mechanism to publicly blacklist violators. That changed when MNRE notified a standard operating procedure for vendor violations. Deactivated vendor names will now be displayed regularly on the national portal, according to [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/mnre-notifies-sop-to-address-vendor-violations-under-pm-surya-ghar), 2026. That is progress, but it also confirms the problem is large enough to need a formal SOP.

The fraud is not limited to individual homeowners. In Maharashtra, investigators found shops allegedly passed off as homes to claim subsidies of up to Rs 78,000, according to [Nagpur Today](https://www.nagpurtoday.in/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-scam-shops-passed-off-as-homes-to-grab-govt-subsidy/), 2026. When fraud operates at that scale, individual buyers need their own verification discipline.

## The 9 Red Flags That Expose a Solar Scam

Most solar scams in India are not sophisticated. They reuse the same nine patterns because the patterns keep working. Learn to spot them and the pitch falls apart in minutes.

**Red flag 1: A website or SMS that looks like PM Surya Ghar but is not.** The only official portal is pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Clones use lookalike domains, fake approval SMS messages, and phishing links that harvest your details or collect fake fees. PIB has publicly warned about fraudulent websites imitating the scheme, according to [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=153495&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=1), 2024.

**Red flag 2: Subsidy promises above Rs 78,000.** The central subsidy is Rs 30,000 per kW up to 2 kW, then Rs 18,000 more per kW. The cap is Rs 78,000. Anyone promising Rs 1 Lakh or more from the centre, or a secret quota, is lying. State top-ups exist but are small and documented in our [solar subsidy guide for all states](/blog/solar-subsidy-all-states/).

**Red flag 3: Pressure to pay a large advance today.** "Subsidy ends this week", "only two slots left", "prices rise tomorrow". Genuine vendors do not need urgency. Ghost installers live on urgency because the advance is the whole business model.

**Red flag 4: No verifiable portal registration.** If the vendor cannot give you a registration ID you can check yourself at pmsuryaghar.gov.in, walk away. The [empanelled vendor system under PM Surya Ghar](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) exists precisely so buyers can verify.

**Red flag 5: ALMM certificates sent as WhatsApp PDFs.** Certificate files are trivially forged. Verification happens on the MNRE website, against the exact model number, never against a document the seller provides.

**Red flag 6: Tier-1 claims for unbranded panels.** Cheap, unlisted modules get rebranded stickers and sold as Tier-1 stock. The tell is a quote far below market with vague panel specs like "540 Wp mono, Tier-1 make".

**Red flag 7: Generation promises that defy physics.** A 3 kW system in most of India generates 12 to 15 units per day. A vendor promising 20 units from 3 kW is either ignorant or lying, and either way the savings math they showed you is fake.

**Red flag 8: Free solar bait.** No system is free. The homeowner pays the cost minus subsidy. "Zero investment solar" usually hides an inflated quote, an undisclosed loan, or a pure advance scam. We broke this down in the [free solar panel scheme reality check](/blog/free-solar-panel-scheme-reality/).

**Red flag 9: A company with no service address and a one-page quote.** No GST number, itemized equipment list, or office address means no after-sales. The disappearing act starts before installation.

## Fake PM Surya Ghar Websites and SMS Scams

This is the fastest-growing pattern because it scales without any field presence.

The playbook is simple. A victim gets an SMS or WhatsApp message saying their PM Surya Ghar subsidy is approved. The message carries a link to complete verification or pay a small processing fee. The link opens a pixel-perfect clone of the official portal. The victim enters bank details, UPI credentials, or pays a "refundable" fee. Money and data are gone.

The official process never works this way. Subsidy is disbursed after installation, inspection, and grid connection, directly to your bank account through the portal you registered on. There is no stage where the government asks you to pay a fee to receive the subsidy.

Protect yourself with three rules. Type pmsuryaghar.gov.in yourself, never click links. Treat every unsolicited approval message as fake until verified on the real portal. Registration on the real portal is free. The correct process is laid out in our [how to apply for PM Surya Ghar online](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/) guide.

If you clicked a link and shared details, call 1930 right away. The sooner you report, the higher the chance banks can freeze the transfer before withdrawal. Then file the incident at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).

## Ghost Installers and the Advance Payment Trap

This is the pattern that costs homeowners the most money per case.

A "company" with a glossy brochure and a rented sales agent quotes 15 to 20 percent below market. It collects a large advance, often 50 percent or full payment, citing panel procurement. Then come delays, excuses, and finally a dead phone number. The office, if it existed, was a co-working desk.

The scale is not trivial. In one consumer case, a solar firm was ordered to refund Rs 11 lakh with interest for an incomplete installation, according to [SubsidySolar](https://subsidysolar.in/blog/solar-scam-alert-fake-installers), 2026. Consumer commissions do deliver relief, but only to buyers who kept written proof: invoice, receipt, agreement, and chat records.

The defense is a payment structure, not trust:

1. Pay 10 to 20 percent on signing, against a GST invoice to the company account.
2. Pay the material milestone, 40 to 50 percent, only after equipment is delivered to your site and you have verified the model numbers against the quote.
3. Pay the balance after installation, commissioning, and net-meter activation.

Any vendor who rejects milestone payments is telling you something. A genuine installer with real working capital accepts this structure because it is standard in the industry. The [solar quotation checklist](/blog/solar-quotation-checklist/) shows what a proper itemized quote looks like.

## Fake ALMM Certificates and the Tier-1 Panel Swap

The ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) is MNRE's quality gate. PM Surya Ghar subsidy is released only when the installed modules and inverter are on the current list. That makes the ALMM certificate a forgery target.

The scam has two variants. In the first, the vendor shows a genuine-looking ALMM certificate for a brand, but installs a cheaper unlisted model from the same brand. ALMM listing is model-specific, so the substitution still kills your subsidy. In the second, the certificate itself is fabricated, complete with logos and serials.

Verification takes ten minutes and costs nothing. Open the ALMM list on the [MNRE website](https://mnre.gov.in/), search the exact manufacturer name, and match the exact model number printed on the quotation. Do this again at delivery, against the nameplate on the actual equipment. Our [ALMM List Phase III guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains how the list is structured and where to find it.

The panel-swap variant is cruder. Unbranded Chinese modules with printed labels of a known Tier-1 manufacturer show up in quotes priced 20 to 30 percent below market. Beyond subsidy rejection, these panels carry no enforceable warranty in India. If a price looks too good, ask why, and check the [solar inverter buying mistakes](/blog/solar-inverter-buying-mistakes/) post for the equipment-side traps that pair with this one.

## Inflated Generation Promises and Forged DISCOM Papers

These two scams share a trait: they only surface months after payment, when the money is unrecoverable.

Generation inflation works like this. The vendor shows a savings projection based on 5.5 or 6 peak sun hours and a 25 percent performance overestimate. Your payback looks like 3 years instead of 5. The system works fine, technically, but the savings math you bought was fiction. The fix is to demand the assumption sheet: peak sun hours for your city, system losses, degradation, and the tariff used. Realistic Indian rooftop output is 4 to 5 units per kW per day on annual average. Cross-check any projection against that range.

Forged DISCOM papers are more dangerous. The vendor shows you a feasibility approval or net-metering sanction letter to trigger a payment milestone. The letter is fabricated, sometimes with a real DISCOM's letterhead. In the worst cases, the system gets connected without genuine approval, and the homeowner later faces disconnection plus subsidy forfeiture. The [PM Surya Ghar rejection reasons](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-rejection-reasons/) post catalogues what DISCOMs actually check.

Every genuine approval carries a reference number tied to your consumer number and application ID. Verify it directly with your DISCOM office or official portal before releasing any approval-linked payment. A screenshot is not verification.

## How to Verify a Solar Vendor Before You Pay

Run this checklist for every vendor on your shortlist. It takes under an hour per vendor and filters out nearly every fraud pattern described above.

| Check | Where to Verify | Pass Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portal registration | pmsuryaghar.gov.in vendor list for your DISCOM | Exact company name and registration ID found |
| GST registration | gst.gov.in search by GSTIN | Active registration, name matches invoice |
| Company existence | MCA portal (mca.gov.in) for Pvt Ltd firms | Active company, directors listed, not struck off |
| ALMM listing | MNRE website, current ALMM list | Exact quoted model numbers listed |
| Physical office | Visit or video call with location on | Real office matching the registered address |
| Past installations | Ask for 3 local references with addresses | At least 2 verifiable installs over 1 year old |
| Payment terms | Written quotation | Milestone-based, company account, GST invoice |
| Warranty terms | Written in the contract | Panel, inverter, and workmanship warranties stated |

Three additional habits close the remaining gaps. Get at least three itemized quotes, because fraud hides in pricing opacity. Never pay cash without a receipt, and never pay into a personal bank account. Search the company name plus the word "complaint" or "fraud" online, because cheated buyers usually post somewhere.

## What to Put in the Contract

A proper contract is your only real protection if things go wrong. Consumer commissions decide on documents, not on what the sales agent promised verbally.

Insist on these clauses, in writing:

- **Equipment schedule.** Exact brand, model number, wattage, and quantity for panels, inverter, structure, cables, and protections. Add a clause that any substitution needs your written approval.
- **Price and payment schedule.** Total price with GST breakup, tied to the milestone structure from the earlier section.
- **Timeline with penalty.** Installation start and completion dates, with a per-week delay penalty or refund trigger.
- **Warranty terms.** Panel product and performance warranty, inverter warranty, and workmanship warranty, each with duration and claim process. For context, the strongest inverter warranties in India now run 12 years full replacement, so treat anything unstated as nonexistent.
- **DISCOM and subsidy responsibility.** Who files the net-metering application, who follows up the subsidy claim, and what happens if vendor error causes rejection.
- **Exit clause.** Refund terms if the vendor abandons the project.

If a vendor refuses to put these in writing, that refusal is the review. A serious installer signs this contract in one meeting.

## What to Do If You Have Already Been Scammed

Speed matters more than anything else. Follow this order.

1. **Call 1930 now.** The national cybercrime helpline can initiate a freeze on the recipient account. Every hour of delay lowers the odds of recovering funds.
2. **File at cybercrime.gov.in.** Use the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and attach everything: receipts, bank references, chats, the contract, photos of any equipment delivered.
3. **Alert your bank.** Report the fraud, block linked cards or UPI handles, and get the complaint reference number in writing.
4. **File a police FIR or consumer complaint.** For a failed contract rather than pure cyber fraud, the consumer commission route through e-Daakhil is effective. Documented cases show refunds with interest are granted when buyers kept written proof.
5. **Report the vendor on the PM Surya Ghar portal.** With MNRE's violation SOP now in force, complaints can lead to vendor deactivation. That protects the next buyer.

One honest note: recovery is not guaranteed. That is why the hour of verification before payment is worth more than months of complaint-filing after it.
