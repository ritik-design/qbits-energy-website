The first year of solar ownership in India follows a predictable pattern, but most homeowners do not know what that pattern is. According to [JMK Research's India Rooftop Solar 2025 Consumer Survey](https://jmkresearch.com/), 42% of solar homeowners reported being "surprised" by the monsoon generation dip in their first year, indicating that pre-commissioning expectation-setting is a critical gap in India's solar industry. [IRENA's analysis of residential solar systems](https://www.irena.org/) shows that systems monitored actively in Year 1 have 15% lower incidence of warranty claims in Years 2–5, because early detection of faults prevents cascading damage. The result is unnecessary anxiety about delays that are normal, seasonal dips that were always expected, and missed maintenance tasks that compound into losses in Year 2. This month-by-month guide maps the entire first year so you know what is coming and what to do about it.

> **TL;DR**
> - Month 1 typically brings a DISCOM net metering connection delay (15–90 days, worst in Kerala, fastest in Gujarat) that is normal, not a fault.
> - Month 2's first net metering bill shows Import, Export, and Net Consumption; a 5–10% gap versus the monitoring app's export figure is normal calibration tolerance.
> - Months 6 through 8 bring the monsoon dip, generation falling to as low as 30% of clear-sky peak during July–August, which is expected weather behaviour, not a system defect.
> - Month 12 calls for a formal annual review: comparing actual generation to the installer's estimate, a technical inspection, and confirming both panel and inverter warranty registration.
> - Zero generation on a clear day, a burning smell, or a repeated isolation fault all warrant an immediate call to the installer rather than a wait-and-watch approach.

> **Direct answer.** The Solar Year-1 Calendar for Indian rooftop solar: Month 1 (DISCOM connection delay (normal, 15–90 days); Month 2) first net metering bill (learn to read it); Month 3 (first panel cleaning; Months 4–5) summer peak generation (compare to estimate); Month 6 (monsoon dip (normal); Months 7–9) good generation resumes; Months 10–11 (winter slight dip; Month 12) annual review. The framework below explains what is normal versus what needs a call to your installer.

## Month 1: DISCOM Connection Delay (Normal)

The most common complaint in Month 1 is not a technical problem, it is bureaucratic delay. Your panels are on the roof, the inverter is commissioned, and the system is generating power. But the [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) has not yet installed the [bidirectional net meter](/glossary/bidirectional-meter/), so:

- The system may be running in **[zero-export](/glossary/zero-export/) mode** (the inverter's built-in zero-export function prevents power flowing to the grid through the old one-way meter)
- Or the installer has applied for [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) and the DISCOM inspection is pending

**Is this normal?** Completely. DISCOM net metering approval takes 15 days (Gujarat, best case) to 90 days (Kerala, worst case) from application submission. In the [PM Surya Ghar scheme](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/), the mandated timeline is 30 days, but enforcement is inconsistent across states.

**What you can do:** Ask your installer for the application reference number and track status on your state DISCOM portal or the PM Surya Ghar portal. Follow up weekly. If 45 days have passed without inspection, file a formal complaint through the DISCOM's consumer grievance cell.

**Is your system wasting energy?** During the zero-export wait, any surplus solar power not consumed by your home is not exported, it is essentially wasted. Minimise this by running appliances between 10 AM and 3 PM to maximise self-consumption during the wait period.

## Month 2: Your First Net Metering Bill

The first net metering bill often confuses new solar owners. It looks very different from a standard electricity bill.

**What to look for:**

| Bill Line Item | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| Total Import (units) | Units drawn from the grid during the billing period |
| Total Export (units) | Units your solar system sent to the grid |
| Net Consumption | Import minus Export; this is what you pay for |
| Solar Generation | Some DISCOMs show this separately |
| Fixed Charges | These do not change with solar - you still pay them |
| Credit Carried Forward | If Export > Import, this rolls to next month |

**Verify the numbers:** Compare the Export figure on the bill to the export total shown in your monitoring app for the same billing period. A 5–10% difference is acceptable calibration tolerance. Net metering meters in India must comply with [CEA Metering Regulations 2006](https://cea.nic.in/), which specify accuracy standards, a persistent difference above 15% warrants a query to the DISCOM for a meter accuracy test. Your bill may also show the period from the date the net meter was installed rather than a full calendar month, this partial billing period for Month 1 is normal.

## Months 3–4: First Cleaning and Summer Peak

**Month 3, First cleaning:** By Month 3, dust accumulation on your panels in India's dry season (October–May) will have reduced output by 10–20% from clean-panel levels. The first cleaning is the highest-ROI maintenance task of the year. Use plain water with a soft brush or cloth; clean in the early morning when panels are cool.

**Months 4–5, Summer peak generation:** April and May are typically the peak generation months for most Indian states (except Kerala and the north-east, where March–April peaks), with [NREL India irradiance data](https://www.nrel.gov/international/ra_india.html) showing monthly GHI peaks in April–May across most states. Your monitoring app should show the highest daily generation figures of the year in April–May.

- Compare actual peak-month generation to the installer's estimate for April–May
- A 5–10% shortfall from estimate is within normal calibration tolerance
- A 15%+ shortfall from estimate in a clear-sky month warrants investigation

If your system is reaching or exceeding the installer's estimate during peak months, the financial performance is tracking as modelled, which is a strong signal the system was correctly sized and installed.

## Month 6: Monsoon Dip (Normal and Expected)

June brings the pre-monsoon transition, and July–August bring the heaviest cloud cover of the year. This is the most common source of first-year anxiety among new solar owners, "my generation fell by 40%, is something wrong?"

**The Solar Year-1 Calendar**

| Month | Typical Generation (% of clear-sky peak) | Key Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Month 1 | 80–100% | DISCOM wait; zero-export mode possible |
| Month 2 | 80–100% | First net metering bill |
| Month 3 | 75–90% | First panel cleaning; dry-season dust |
| Month 4–5 | 95–100% | Summer peak; compare to estimate |
| Month 6 | 60–80% | Pre-monsoon; generation falls |
| Month 7–8 | 30–60% | Full monsoon; significant cloud cover |
| Month 9 | 60–80% | Monsoon retreating |
| Month 10–11 | 80–90% | Good generation; winter approaching |
| Month 12 | 75–85% | Winter slight dip; annual review |

**What is normal in monsoon:** A 3 kW system in Karnataka generating 14 units/day in May may generate only 6–8 units/day in July, a 45–55% reduction. This is weather, not a system fault. The annual generation estimate your installer provided should already account for these monsoon months.

**What is not normal:** If generation falls to near-zero on a day with moderate cloud cover (not heavy rain), or if the inverter shows fault codes during or after a thunderstorm, these warrant investigation. Post-monsoon is also the time to check for water ingress in any outdoor junction boxes or conduits.

## Months 7–11: Normal Operation and Seasonal Variation

Months 7 through 11 trace the recovery from monsoon and the gradual shift toward the winter season.

- **Months 7–9 (monsoon and post-monsoon):** Generation gradually improves as cloud cover reduces through September. Do a post-monsoon cleaning in October to remove the grime deposited by rain (which is surprisingly dirty in Indian cities due to pollution).
- **Months 10–11 (October–November):** Good generation in most states as irradiance is high and temperatures are lower (which improves panel efficiency). Northern India starts to see haze and foggy mornings that reduce early-day generation.
- **What to track:** Compare monthly generation to the installer's monthly estimate. Any month showing below 85% of the estimate warrants monitoring, and repeated months below 85% warrant an installer call.

According to [MNRE's Rooftop Solar Guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/), registered solar owners under PM Surya Ghar have access to DISCOM solar helplines that can assist with generation queries and net metering discrepancies.

## Month 12: Annual Review

The 12-month mark is the time for a formal Year 1 review. Complete all of the following.

**Financial review:**
- Total annual generation (from monitoring app) vs installer's Year 1 estimate
- Total bill savings (from DISCOM bills before and after solar)
- Payback progress: Year 1 savings ÷ System cost = percentage of capital recovered. A [solar savings calculator](https://heavengreenenergy.com/solar-calculator/) can project the remaining payback timeline using your actual Year 1 numbers.

**Technical review (do this with your installer or service partner):**
- String voltage check with a multimeter, verify each string is producing expected voltage
- MC4 connector inspection for corrosion or looseness
- Earthing continuity test
- Inverter error log review, are there recurring fault codes that were not reported?
- Warranty registration confirmation, both panels and inverter

**Documentation:** File your Year 1 annual review report (from the service visit), all DISCOM bills from the past 12 months, monitoring app annual generation report, and [warranty](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty/) certificate copies. These documents are essential for any future warranty claim. If your installer did not hand over an as-built record at commissioning, a [post-installation as-built design report](https://heavendesigns.in/solar-post-design/) is worth requesting now, it becomes useful for insurance claims and resale documentation.

## What Requires an Immediate Call to Your Installer

Not everything in Year 1 warrants attention. But these specific events require an immediate call, not a WhatsApp message that can wait.

- **Zero generation on a clear sunny day**: the inverter is not operating at all; could be a grid fault, isolation fault, or inverter failure
- **Burning smell or visible burning near the inverter or DC cable**: a potentially serious safety issue requiring the system to be isolated immediately
- **Inverter displaying an isolation fault repeatedly**: indicates DC-to-ground insulation has deteriorated; requires professional diagnosis
- **A single string consistently 30%+ below others in generation**: indicates a panel failure or wiring issue in that string
- **Any physical damage to panels from hailstorm or falling tree**: check whether the installer's installation insurance covers accidental damage
