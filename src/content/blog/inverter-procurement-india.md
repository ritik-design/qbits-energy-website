A bulk inverter order is ready only when the EPC can match **each site, exact model, quantity, document, delivery milestone, acceptance test, and commercial responsibility** across the design, quotation, and purchase order.

Do not order from a family brochure or a verbal stock confirmation. Freeze the transaction in a controlled order schedule. Mark every missing answer as `clarification required`, and resolve it before dispatch or through an authorised written exception.

> **Order-control summary**
>
> - Qualify the supplier before asking the purchase order to carry supplier risk.
> - Use complete model designations, not labels such as `25 kW inverter` or `three-phase series`.
> - Match the proposed model to the approved design, current datasheet, applicable certificate, and project requirement.
> - Separate unit price from GST, freight, insurance, accessories, communications hardware, commissioning support, and other scope.
> - Put delivery evidence, inspection rights, rejection handling, and substitution control in writing.
> - Attach the exact-model warranty, service, and RMA schedules to the accepted commercial file.
> - Release the order only when the quotation and purchase order tell the same story.

## Start after supplier qualification, not before it

Procurement cannot repair a weak shortlist. Before commercial negotiation, establish the bidder's legal identity, authorised supply route, exact product platform, current evidence, support responsibility, and ability to answer technical clarifications.

Use the [solar inverter supplier selection checklist](/blog/inverter-suppliers-india/) for that decision. This guide begins after a supplier enters the shortlist. It covers the RFQ, quotation, purchase order, delivery, inspection, and acceptance controls for a specific order.

Keep the boundary clear:

| Decision | Owning question | Evidence produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier qualification | Should this organisation and product platform enter the shortlist? | Qualification file and approved bidder status |
| Bulk procurement | What exactly is being bought, supplied, inspected, and accepted? | Comparable quotation and controlled purchase order |
| Warranty and service | What happens after a covered issue is reported? | Exact-model warranty, service schedule, RMA process, and cost matrix |

Passing one decision does not pass the others. A qualified supplier can still submit an incomplete order. A detailed quotation does not prove that the warranty remedy is defined.

## Build one model and quantity schedule

The order schedule is the control sheet for the transaction. Give every bidder the same revision and require a response against every row.

| Field | What to record | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project and delivery location | Site name, state, consignee, and delivery point | Separates supply lots and logistics responsibility |
| Electrical system | Single-phase or three-phase, nominal AC conditions, and connection context | Prevents a phase or grid-interface mismatch |
| Exact inverter model | Complete manufacturer designation | Links the quote to one datasheet and document set |
| Quantity | Units by model and site | Controls allocation, delivery, and reconciliation |
| DC design inputs | Module model, string design, current, voltage, and temperature assumptions | Allows an engineering compatibility check |
| AC and protection interface | Output basis and required external protection scope | Separates inverter supply from balance-of-system scope |
| Communications | Logger, dongle, meter, gateway, SIM, cables, protocol, and account requirement | Prevents missing monitoring hardware or access |
| Accessories | Connectors, mounting items, meters, sensors, terminals, and model-specific items | Exposes what the base unit does not include |
| Documents | Datasheet, manual, certificates, drawings, settings, warranty, and service schedule | Creates an auditable submittal pack |
| Required date | Delivery need by site, with any split-lot sequence | Makes the schedule measurable without assuming stock |

Use `TBC` only with an owner and closure date. A blank cell is not flexibility. It is an undocumented assumption that can move from sales to engineering, then appear at site as a delay.

### Freeze the document revision with the model

A model name alone is not enough. Record the datasheet title or file, revision or issue date where shown, and the date the buyer checked it. If a certificate or manual has a different model string, ask the bidder to map the exact quoted model to the document in writing.

For Qbits models, begin with the [current public datasheet library](/download-datasheets/). That page separates model families and tells buyers to request the exact-model manual, certificate, battery compatibility confirmation, or written warranty when the public datasheet does not establish it.

## Create a project-specific evidence matrix

There is no safe universal label called `all approvals included`. Requirements can arise from the tender, scheme, regulator, DISCOM, customer specification, insurer, lender, or internal EPC standard. List the source of each requirement beside the evidence offered.

| Requirement row | Bidder response | EPC verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issuing or controlling body | Name the body or contract document | Confirm it is the correct authority for this project |
| Exact requirement | Quote the clause, standard, or approval requested | Check scope, jurisdiction, and effective date |
| Exact model covered | Give the full model designation | Match it to the order schedule and certificate scope |
| Current evidence | Provide the document, number, date, and status | Verify through the issuing body's route where available |
| Exceptions | State every gap or pending item | Assign an owner, decision, and closure date |

The [BIS Compulsory Registration Scheme portal](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/) provides a public registration-search route. Search the exact registration and model details relevant to the offered equipment. Do not treat a logo on a brochure as equivalent to the current record.

As checked on 26 September 2026, the [Ministry of New and Renewable Energy ALMM page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/) publishes List-I for solar PV modules and List-II for solar PV cells. It is not an inverter list. Verify module and cell requirements there when applicable, then check inverter requirements against the project's own documents.

If the project authority has not answered an approval question, record it as an open dependency. A supplier assurance cannot substitute for the authority's decision.

## Make every quotation comparable

Price comparison fails when bidders price different boundaries. Send a response table, not a request for one total.

Ask each bidder to state:

- seller legal name, GSTIN, quotation number, date, and validity;
- manufacturer and exact model for every line;
- quantity and unit basis;
- base equipment amount and GST treatment;
- freight, transit insurance, unloading, and site-delivery scope;
- included and excluded accessories;
- communications hardware, platform access, and any recurring dependency;
- documentation and support deliverables;
- dispatch and delivery milestones, with their start conditions;
- payment milestones and the evidence that releases each payment;
- warranty, service, and RMA document references; and
- deviation list against the RFQ.

Use one comparison basis:

`evaluated order amount = equipment + tax + freight + insurance + required accessories + required communications scope + other accepted supply items`

This formula is a normalisation tool, not a Qbits price or a total-cost forecast. Keep uncertain or excluded amounts visible rather than inserting estimates into a bidder's column. The [inverter quotation guide](/blog/why-inverter-manufacturers-quote-prices/) explains how scope changes a quote before price comparison begins.

## Convert the accepted quote into purchase-order controls

The purchase order should not rely on email history to explain its basic scope. Attach the accepted schedules and state which document controls when two documents conflict.

Include these controls:

1. **Document hierarchy:** purchase order, accepted quotation, technical schedule, deviations, warranty, service schedule, and other attachments in a defined order.
2. **Exact scope:** models, quantities, accessories, documentation, logistics, and support deliverables.
3. **Delivery milestones:** the event that starts each clock and the evidence that completes it.
4. **Payment milestones:** amount basis, tax treatment, due event, required evidence, and treatment of a disputed lot.
5. **Inspection and acceptance:** buyer rights, location, notice, sample or full-lot scope, acceptance window, and records.
6. **Non-conformance process:** quarantine, notice, investigation, correction, return, replacement, credit, and cost responsibility.
7. **Change control:** no model, document, component, accessory, firmware, logistics, or schedule substitution without the stated approval route.
8. **Cancellation and delay terms:** agreed consequences and exceptions, reviewed for the actual contract.
9. **Warranty and service attachments:** exact-model obligations, evidence, costs, contacts, and escalation.
10. **Records:** model and serial data, dispatch documents, delivery proof, inspection results, and acceptance status.

Legal and tax reviewers should confirm the final contract for the transaction. A procurement checklist does not decide title transfer, tax treatment, liability, or enforceability.

## Do not accept substitutions through a dispatch note

An alternative model can change string design, input current limits, MPPT allocation, protection coordination, communications, physical layout, approval evidence, and service documents. Even a model with the same AC rating is not automatically equivalent.

Require a written change request before dispatch. It should contain:

- original and proposed exact models;
- reason for the change;
- current datasheet and required certificate set;
- engineering compatibility review;
- accessory, monitoring, manual, and settings impact;
- price, tax, delivery, warranty, and service impact;
- affected sites and quantities; and
- approval by the authorised technical and commercial owners.

The same rule applies to a split shipment, changed seller, alternate communications device, or revised delivery location. No response should mean `not approved`, not implied consent.

## Define dispatch, delivery, and acceptance separately

These events are not interchangeable.

| Event | Evidence to define | Control question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready for inspection | Packing list, unit list, and inspection notice | Can the buyer inspect the stated lot? |
| Dispatch | Carrier document and serialised packing record | What left, when, and under whose risk? |
| Delivery | Proof at the agreed delivery point | Were quantity and visible condition recorded? |
| Inspection complete | Signed inspection or exception report | Which checks passed, failed, or remain open? |
| Acceptance | Authorised acceptance record | Has the buyer accepted the lot under the contract? |
| Commissioning handover | Agreed test and configuration records | Is this included in supply acceptance or a later stage? |

Link payment to the event the commercial agreement actually intends. `Against dispatch` and `after acceptance` transfer different risk. Do not use them as loose alternatives.

For multi-site orders, maintain a lot register. Link each unit's model and serial number to its dispatch, delivery site, inspection status, commissioning record, warranty document, and any open exception.

## Inspect the lot before it disappears into project stock

Set the inspection plan before ordering. The plan should be safe, proportionate, and agreed with engineering and quality teams.

### Document and identity check

- reconcile purchase order, invoice, delivery note, packing list, model, and quantity;
- capture serial numbers and lot identifiers;
- confirm required datasheets, manuals, certificates, warranty, and service documents;
- verify accessories and communications items against the schedule; and
- record document revisions and missing items.

### Packaging and visual check

- photograph packaging before opening when damage is visible;
- record seal, label, enclosure, connector, display, and accessory condition;
- keep damaged or mismatched units separate from accepted stock; and
- preserve the packaging and carrier evidence required by the agreed claim route.

### Agreed functional or electrical check

Use the manufacturer's instructions and a competent test team. Define the test sample, equipment, method, limits, safety controls, and record format in advance. Do not energise an unfamiliar unit or open an enclosure merely to complete a procurement checklist.

Acceptance should follow the written criteria. If the contract requires a factory or pre-dispatch inspection, record whether it replaces, limits, or sits alongside the incoming inspection.

## Attach warranty and service terms to the order

A datasheet icon or warranty duration does not settle remedy, exclusions, registration, freight, labour, site attendance, recommissioning, or response stages.

Use the [bulk-buyer inverter warranty and service checklist](/blog/inverter-warranty-service-checklist-bulk-buyers/) to request the exact-model warranty, service schedule, RMA procedure, cost-responsibility matrix, and escalation list.

Current Qbits public product data says its public datasheets describe an expandable warranty but do not establish the base term, remedy, registration deadline, or exclusions. Obtain the current written terms for the exact quoted model before purchase. Do not copy a term from another model, an old article, or a sales call into the order.

## The release test

Before issuing the purchase order, ask a colleague who did not negotiate the quote to review the file. They should be able to answer these questions without calling the salesperson:

1. What exact model and quantity goes to each site?
2. Which document revision and project requirement support every model?
3. What is included, excluded, taxed, shipped, insured, and paid for?
4. Which event proves dispatch, delivery, inspection, acceptance, and handover?
5. What happens to a damaged, late, missing, or substituted unit?
6. Which written warranty and service documents govern the supplied serial numbers?
7. Which document controls if two answers conflict?

If one answer is missing, keep the order in clarification. The goal is not more paperwork. It is one transaction record that design, procurement, accounts, logistics, site, and service teams can all follow.
