## The six gates for a power-cut system

A product belongs on a frequent-outage shortlist only when the proposed system passes all six gates:

1. **Backup output:** the exact backup or off-grid output covers the circuits that may operate together, including starting behaviour.
2. **Battery pairing:** the exact battery model, BMS communication, voltage, current, firmware, and cable interface are approved together.
3. **Grid isolation:** the design shows how the backup section is prevented from energising the utility connection after grid loss.
4. **Protected-load design:** the circuits that remain powered and the loads that stay excluded are fixed in the single-line diagram and distribution board.
5. **Transfer behaviour:** the exact mode, published transfer wording, test condition, and acceptable interruption for each load are known.
6. **Commissioning proof:** grid loss, backup loading, low-energy behaviour, overload response, grid restoration, alarms, and shutdown are witnessed and recorded.

Failure at any one gate can defeat the whole purchase. A large battery cannot correct an undersized backup output. A fast transfer label cannot correct an incompatible battery. A capable inverter cannot correct a backup board that accidentally carries a water heater, pump, and air conditioner together.

## Battery compatibility is more than 48 V

Frequent power cuts can cycle a battery repeatedly. The inverter and battery must agree on both electrical limits and control behaviour.

Check all of these for the exact pair:

- minimum, nominal, and maximum battery voltage;
- continuous and short-duration charge and discharge current;
- battery chemistry and permitted operating temperatures;
- usable state-of-charge window and reserve setting;
- BMS communication protocol, message set, cable pinout, and termination;
- inverter, battery, and BMS firmware versions;
- number of battery modules and parallel limits;
- fault, low-temperature, over-temperature, over-current, and communication-loss behaviour;
- who accepts warranty responsibility for the pairing.

The three inverters publish low-voltage battery information, but overlapping voltage is not compatibility. A battery can sit inside the nominal voltage window and still use the wrong protocol, current limit, pinout, or firmware.

Use the [battery-sizing guide](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/) for the energy calculation, then obtain the exact compatibility evidence from both manufacturers or the responsible system supplier.

## Size protected loads before sizing the battery

Start with the circuits that must remain energised, not the home's total sanctioned load. Record each appliance's running input, starting behaviour, operating schedule, and priority.

| Protected-load field | What to document |
| --- | --- |
| Circuit | Lights, selected sockets, refrigerator, router, pump, or another named circuit |
| Running demand | Nameplate value or measured input under the intended operating condition |
| Starting behaviour | Manufacturer data or measured start where relevant |
| Simultaneous operation | Which devices may start or run together |
| Required duration | Minimum and preferred hours for each priority group |
| Shedding rule | What disconnects first as power or energy becomes limited |
| Acceptance test | The actual load combination that will be demonstrated at handover |

For an illustrative planning check, a measured 0.8 kW average protected load required for four hours represents 3.2 kWh of AC load energy before battery operating-window limits, conversion losses, reserve, temperature, and ageing basis. It is not a battery recommendation.

Motors, compressors, pumps, and some power supplies cannot be approved by adding running watts alone. A refrigerator and water pump may each fit separately but fail when starting together. The inverter's backup-output and short-duration limits, the battery's power limit, and the circuit priorities must be checked as one system.

## Isolation and the protected-load board are safety requirements

Backup operation must not energise the utility network during an outage. [IEC 62116](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/6478) describes a test procedure for islanding-prevention measures used with utility-interconnected PV inverters. A standard title does not replace the installation design.

The single-line diagram should show the grid connection, inverter, battery, PV array, changeover or internal transfer path, backed-up distribution board, non-backed-up circuits, protective devices, neutral and earthing arrangement, metering, shutdown points, and generator or UPS interaction where present.

The protected-load board prevents a frequent design failure: transferring the whole home to backup even though the battery and backup output were sized for essentials. High-demand or non-essential circuits can be left on the ordinary grid board unless the system was explicitly designed and tested for them.

Do not bypass anti-islanding, alter grid settings, or improvise a changeover to keep ordinary on-grid solar operating. The existing [power-cut explainer](/blog/solar-inverter-power-cut-backup/) owns the grid-loss architecture in detail.

## Transfer time needs a test condition

GoodWe's page and Growatt's sheet both publish under 10 ms wording. Qbits' current catalogue publishes within 10 seconds. None of those lines tells the homeowner everything needed for acceptance.

Ask:

- Is the value measured from grid loss to stable backup output?
- Which operating mode and firmware were used?
- What battery state and load percentage applied?
- Was the load resistive, motor-based, or electronic?
- Does the value apply during grid loss, grid restoration, or both?
- What happens when the battery is unavailable, at reserve, or current-limited?
- Is there an independent or controlled test record for the exact model?

Then test the as-built system using the actual protected circuits. A light staying on does not prove that a computer, router, refrigerator, pump, or other required device saw acceptable voltage, frequency, interruption, or restoration behaviour.

Sensitive or safety-critical equipment may need a purpose-designed UPS even when a hybrid inverter publishes fast switching. The required continuity class comes from the load, not from a marketing label.

## Daytime and night-time cuts are different

During a daytime outage, the system may have PV energy available, but backup operation still depends on the supported architecture, battery state, array conditions, power limits, and control logic. Peak array nameplate power is not guaranteed backup power.

At night, the PV array contributes nothing. Runtime depends on the battery's usable energy at that moment and the protected-load profile. A system that survives a short afternoon cut may not survive an overnight outage.

Ask the designer to model at least:

- a short daytime cut with the battery near its reserve;
- a long daytime cut under poor solar conditions;
- an evening cut beginning before peak household demand;
- an overnight cut with no PV contribution;
- repeated cuts before the battery has fully recharged;
- grid restoration while protected loads are operating.

These are operating cases, not promises of duration. Each needs declared inputs and a defined pass condition.

## The commissioning test decides whether the design is real

Before final payment, record model and serial numbers, battery modules, firmware, settings, protected circuits, excluded loads, protective-device details, and the approved single-line diagram.

Witness and document:

1. normal grid-connected operation;
2. intentional grid-loss isolation;
3. transfer with the agreed protected loads operating;
4. starting of specified motors or compressors where safe and required;
5. load shedding or overload response;
6. low-state-of-charge and reserve behaviour where safely testable;
7. alarms, monitoring, shutdown, and emergency controls;
8. grid restoration and reconnection;
9. behaviour after communication or component faults covered by the test plan.
