To check a **PM Surya Ghar vendor**, start at the [official PM Surya Ghar National Portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), not an advertisement or a link forwarded on WhatsApp. Find the vendor through the registered-vendor pages for your state and district, then match the business shown there with the legal name, GSTIN, address and payment beneficiary on your quotation.

Do this before paying an advance. A portal match confirms one part of the decision. It does not approve the quote, authenticate the salesperson or guarantee subsidy release.

> **Short version:** MNRE's guidelines say central assistance is available only through vendors registered on the National Portal. Look the vendor up yourself, district by district. Match the exact business, not a familiar brand name. Put every component, payment milestone and responsibility in the quote. Pause if the name, contact, bank beneficiary or equipment does not reconcile.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - Central assistance is available only through vendors registered on the National Portal. Look the vendor up yourself, district by district, and match the exact legal name, GSTIN and payment beneficiary to your quote.
> - Vendors register through a state route (one ₹2.5 lakh performance bank guarantee for the state) or the national route via REC Limited (₹25 lakh guarantee, any district).
> - A registered vendor owes a five-year Comprehensive Maintenance Contract counted from commissioning. Hire an unregistered installer and none of the scheme protections follow you.
> - Non-DCR modules make the installation ineligible for CFA, and CFA follows the installed module capacity, not the quote.
> - No one can charge a fee to "release" your subsidy. Never share your portal password or OTP, and report fraud on cybercrime.gov.in or 1930.

This guide reflects the MNRE guideline documents and portal routes checked on **14 October 2026**. The portal, state processes and DISCOM instructions change, so confirm anything unclear on the live portal before acting.

## What an empanelled vendor is under PM Surya Ghar

"Empanelled" and "registered" are used interchangeably in everyday speech. MNRE's own documents say **registered vendor**. An empanelled vendor is a solar installation firm that has registered under the scheme's vendor process and appears on the National Portal. The firm usually registers in one of two ways:

Table: Two vendor registration routes under PM Surya Ghar: registering authority, bank guarantee and where each can work

| Route | Who registers it | Bank guarantee in MNRE documents | Where it can work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| State-level registration | The state DISCOM or a nodal state agency | One performance bank guarantee (PBG) of ₹2.5 lakh for the whole state (MNRE office memorandum, 17 May 2023) | The state concerned |
| National registration | REC Limited as registering authority (MNRE office memorandum, 7 May 2024) | ₹25 lakh PBG, valid at least five years | Any district of any state, with the vendor choosing the districts it lists |

The state process asks a firm to sign a declaration. It confirms, among other things, at least three trained technical staff, compliance with electrical-safety rules, installation to MNRE specifications, comprehensive maintenance for at least five years, a named complaint contact, and willingness to be blacklisted with the PBG encashed if its service fails. Installers who want the registration steps and the documents have their own page, covered in the box near the end of this guide.

For you as a homeowner, the point is simple: registration is a condition the vendor has met to take scheme work. It is not a quality certificate for any one job.

## Why the scheme requires a registered vendor

The July 2024 operational guidelines for residential Central Financial Assistance (CFA) state that the scheme is implemented only through registered vendors, and that a consumer has to choose one to receive CFA. The rule exists because the vendor carries scheme duties that the consumer cannot easily check alone:

- survey the roof and guide the consumer on capacity;
- design, supply, install, test and commission the system to MNRE's minimum technical specifications;
- run the safety checks and explain operation, maintenance and safety to the consumer;
- repair or replace non-performing components free of charge during a five-year Comprehensive Maintenance Contract (CMC) period counted from commissioning;
- give the consumer the manufacturers' warranty documents; and
- fix any system-quality or component problem that stops the DISCOM from commissioning the system or releasing CFA.

The guidelines also put the vendor under a performance rating based on the size of installations, quality of equipment, workmanship and service, and visible to consumers on the portal. A registering authority can inspect installed plants and, after notice, de-register a vendor or levy penalties when systems are non-functional because of poor installation or break the scheme's rules.

> [!IMPORTANT] Registration is what carries the protections
> These protections attach to the registration. Hire an unregistered installer and none of them follow you, and you lose access to CFA.

MNRE amended the residential CFA guidelines on 7 July 2025. The amendment is published as a scanned image PDF, so check it for later changes to vendor duties before you rely on any single clause above.

## How to check the vendor list district-wise: step by step

The checks below follow the National Portal route described in MNRE's guidelines and in secondary guides read on 14 October 2026. Portal screens and labels change, so treat the sequence as a map, not a script.

<!-- steps -->
1. **Type the address yourself.** Enter `pmsuryaghar.gov.in` in the browser. Do not use a link from a message or an advertisement.
2. **Open the registered-vendors page.** The public portal has a vendor listing that works without a login. A secondary guide read on 14 October 2026 describes selecting a state, then a district, then applying the filter. After you apply as a consumer, the portal also presents vendor selection inside your logged-in account.
3. **Pick your state and district.** Use the district of the roof, not your billing address, if they differ.
4. **Read the vendor card.** The guidelines say a profile can carry the company name, the system specifications offered, prices, key contacts, a rating and consumer feedback. Open the full profile for the contact details.
5. **Search for the exact name on your quote.** Use the company name, not only the brand. Two firms can trade under similar names with separate registrations.
6. **Check whether the vendor is still active.** Secondary guides describe a deactivated-vendor list on the portal. A vendor removed from the active list cannot be treated as registered for your project.
7. **Compare at least two vendors.** The guidelines describe vendor and system comparison tools on the portal. Use them alongside your itemised quotes.
8. **Save a dated record.** Take a screenshot or a PDF of the vendor profile and keep it with your quotation.
9. **Repeat the check before final payment** if weeks have passed.

Some state utilities link to the national list from their own sites. For example, [Delhi's solar portal](https://solar.delhi.gov.in/page/empaneled-vendors) points consumers to vendor pages for its DISCOMs. Your state DISCOM or nodal agency can confirm a vendor's registration if the national page is unclear.

A spreadsheet, a PDF "vendor list 2026" from a third-party site, or an old registration certificate can all be stale. The live record on the official portal is the only one that tells you the vendor's current status.

## The 5-point vendor verification checklist

Run all five points before you sign or transfer an advance.

Table: The 5-point vendor verification checklist: what to check, what a pass looks like and when to stop

| Point | What to check | Pass looks like | Stop when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Portal record | The vendor appears on the National Portal for your state and district | Name, contact and status match the quote | The vendor offers only a certificate image or refuses an independent check |
| 2. Legal name and GSTIN | The quote header, GST registration and invoice name match | One legal entity, one GSTIN, one address | Spellings differ, the GSTIN belongs to another firm, or no tax invoice is promised |
| 3. Quote scope | Capacity in kWp and kW, every component, exclusions, milestones | Each line is itemised and the scheme duties are assigned | The quote is a single "after subsidy" figure |
| 4. Module evidence | Make, model and DCR proof for solar modules | Model numbers and domestic-content documents are written down | Brand logo only, or the vendor says the rule does not apply |
| 5. Payment beneficiary | Who receives your money | Payment goes to the contracted seller's business account | A personal or unrelated account is named without a written explanation |

A personal payment destination is not automatic proof of fraud. It is a mismatch that needs a written explanation before money moves. The same applies when an agent or subcontractor says another company will invoice the work.

> [!CAUTION] Never hand over your OTP or portal password
> Call the vendor on a number you found independently. Ask it to confirm the salesperson, quote number, site address and amount. Never give anyone your portal password or OTP. The guidelines give the consumer rights to carry out every portal activity without relying on the vendor, although a vendor may help with entries through your login.

## Red flags of a fake or unregistered vendor

Table: Red flags of a fake or unregistered vendor, why each matters and what to do

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asks for a fee to "release", "approve" or "register" your subsidy | Central assistance is processed through the portal after DISCOM inspection and goes to your bank or loan account, not through the vendor | Stop. Report on cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 if you paid |
| Wants your OTP or portal password | You can run the portal steps yourself | Refuse and change the password |
| Not found on the portal, but shows a certificate image | An image proves nothing about current status | Ask for the legal name and check it independently |
| No business address, only a mobile or WhatsApp number | Hard to trace in a dispute | Ask for the registered office and verify it |
| Refuses to name the exact module and inverter models | You cannot check DCR or technical specifications | Do not sign until each model is in the quote |
| Quote is a single bundled "after subsidy" price | Hides cost, assistance and exclusions | Ask for the gross price, assumed assistance and your share separately |
| Urgency, such as "only a few slots left" | The scheme is not a district-level quota sale | Take your time and compare quotes |
| Asks you to pay the whole amount before survey | The model agreement allows milestone-based payment | Propose milestones tied to survey, delivery and commissioning |
| Price far below every other quote | Corners may be cut on modules, structure or labour | Ask what is excluded and verify the equipment |

## Questions to ask before signing

- What is your registered business name, and where can I see it on the National Portal for my district?
- Who is the legal seller, and does the GSTIN on the quote match it?
- Which module make and model will you install, and can you give me domestic-content documents?
- Which inverter make and model, and what are its per-model specifications and warranty documents?
- What is the total capacity in kWp, and how does it match the assistance slab for my case?
- Which work is excluded, such as roof strengthening, a phase upgrade, extra cabling or civil work?
- What are the payment milestones, and what is the refund rule if the DISCOM rejects the application for an equipment reason?
- Who uploads the documents, and who attends the DISCOM inspection?
- What does the five-year maintenance duty cover, who do I call, and how fast do you respond?
- Which person handles complaints, and what are their name, phone and email?

## Read the contract against the model agreement

MNRE's guidelines attach a model consumer and vendor agreement. The model is a suggested draft and the real contract can differ, but it is a useful benchmark. It covers design, supply, installation, commissioning and five years of comprehensive operation and maintenance, with the warranty counted from commissioning by the DISCOM.

Table: MNRE model consumer and vendor agreement: topics and what to look for in your contract

| Topic in the model agreement | What to look for in your contract |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Site survey, design, supply, civil and structure work, electrical work, net meter and grid connection |
| Modules and inverter | Domestically made modules and cells; inverter and modules conform to MNRE standards |
| Documents | Catalogues, warranty cards, BIS or IEC reports, serial numbers, single-line diagram, structure drawing |
| Warranty | Complete-system warranty for five years from commissioning, plus manufacturers' component warranties |
| Maintenance | Five years of overhaul, wear and tear, regular health checks, and cleaning guidance |
| Payment | Agreed between you and the vendor, with milestone payment permitted |
| Extra work | Separate estimate and invoice with GST for site-specific customisation |
| Performance | A performance ratio of 75 percent at commissioning, with a radiation sensor and calibration certificate |
| Disputes | Settled between you and the vendor; MNRE and the DISCOM are not parties |

Keep the contract price separate from any expected CFA. A vendor can explain the process, but cannot guarantee your eligibility, DISCOM approval, inspection result, bank validation or payment date.

## What to do if the vendor does poor work

Start with your own evidence file: the portal record, quote, GST details, payment proof, messages, application reference and the date of each check.

1. **Write to the vendor.** Describe what you can observe: the date, the defect, the name on the portal and the response you want. Ask for the complaint contact named in the vendor's registration declaration.
2. **Use the portal grievance route.** Per the guidelines, consumers and vendors can raise grievances on the National Portal, through the portal app or through the national call centre. Each grievance gets a unique tracking ID, and the guidelines say grievances are to be resolved within 30 days, with SMS and email updates. Secondary sources list 15555 as the national helpline number, but confirm the current number on the portal.
3. **Escalate to the DISCOM or nodal agency** if the problem blocks inspection, metering or commissioning. The model net-metering agreement also allows recourse to the DISCOM's Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum for supply and metering complaints.
4. **Report fraud separately.** If someone took money for a subsidy release or asked for an OTP, report it on [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or call 1930 and tell your bank.

A deregistration or penalty decision lies with the registering authority, not with you. A payment dispute under the contract is settled between you and the vendor or through the legal route. For an electrical or equipment fault, make the area safe and call a qualified person. Do not open an inverter or touch exposed conductors to gather evidence.

## Subsidy at risk: what can go wrong

Assistance is released only after the DISCOM physically checks the system and approves it on the portal. It then moves through an e-token that you redeem from your profile, and the guidelines say a claim is processed within 15 days of DISCOM approval. Vendor mistakes can break this chain at several points:

Table: Vendor mistakes that put the subsidy at risk, the result under the guidelines and what protects you

| Failure | Result under the guidelines | What protects you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-DCR modules | Installation is ineligible for CFA | Written module model and DCR documents before payment |
| System below minimum technical specification | DISCOM can send the application back or reject it | Per-model equipment schedule and vendor duty to rectify |
| Incomplete documents or geo-tagged photos | The application is sent back for corrections | Agreed owner for uploads and inspection |
| Capacity mismatch | CFA follows the installed module capacity, not the quote | Quote and portal entries match |
| Vendor removed or deactivated mid-project | Application may stall | Recheck the portal before milestone payments |

> [!TIP] Tie every payment to a milestone
> If the DISCOM rejects a claim for a system-quality reason, the guidelines place the duty to rectify it on the vendor. The consumer still carries the cash-flow risk, so tie payments to milestones.

## PM Surya Ghar registration and Qbits are different things

The **PM Surya Ghar National Portal** is the government route for the vendor check and the consumer application. The **Qbits Authorised Service Partner directory** is a Qbits-owned directory for service and partner enquiries. It is not an MNRE, REC or DISCOM registration record.

Qbits Energy is a solar inverter manufacturer. It does not install systems or hold a PM Surya Ghar vendor registration. If a quote includes a Qbits inverter, check the two records separately and make the exact model part of your written equipment schedule. A government-registered vendor is not automatically a Qbits partner, and a Qbits directory listing is not proof of scheme registration, subsidy eligibility or DISCOM acceptance. You can [search the Qbits Authorised Service Partner directory](/authorized-service-partners/) by state and district, then return to the portal for the government check. To see how Qbits grid-tied inverters are specified, read about the [on-grid inverter range](/on-grid-inverter/).
