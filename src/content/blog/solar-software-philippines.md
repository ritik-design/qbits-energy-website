> **What the tenfold increase actually demands.** Residential net metering work can be done reasonably well with layout tools and a tariff assumption. Commercial work at up to 1 MW cannot, because the value depends on matching generation to an industrial or commercial load curve, and because the buyer will interrogate the numbers. Teams moving into the segment the circular opened need a different class of modelling than the one that served them at residential scale.

## What Is Genuinely Philippines-Specific

**Export credits the generation rate, not retail.** For Meralco customers this is roughly PHP 5 to 6 per kWh, about 50 to 60 percent of retail. The design consequence is the familiar one: a self-consumed kilowatt-hour is worth close to double an exported one, so systems should track the daytime load curve. A tool applying retail to exports overstates the return substantially, and on a commercial project that error is large in absolute terms.

**The Philippine Electrical Code is based on NEC 2008.** The NEC lineage is convenient because US-derived design logic is closer to applicable here than in most non-US markets. The version gap is the trap. A tool defaulting to NEC 2023 is not automatically PEC-compliant, and the requirements that matter, anti-islanding within 2 seconds and DC arc fault protection on longer runs, are exactly what gets checked.

**Republic Act 9513 makes net metering a statutory right** for on-grid renewable energy end-users. Worth stating in a commercial proposal, because it removes doubt about whether the utility can decline on discretion. The real constraints are procedural.

**Defined approval timelines.** Distribution utilities must approve or reject a complete application within 10 working days, with LGU permit and CFEI following within 3 working days of inspection. These belong in the proposal because a commercial buyer is planning around an energisation date.

## Where Philippine Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Underbought: hourly load matching.** The capability the cap change made essential. Sizing a 500 kW commercial system without an hourly consumption profile is guessing, and the guess is expensive at that scale.

**Underbought: generation-rate export modelling.** Many tools apply a single tariff to all energy. That is roughly harmless on a small residential system and materially wrong on a commercial one.

**Underbought: bankable yield reporting.** Commercial buyers and their financiers ask for it, and residential-focused tools frequently do not produce it.

**Overbought: residential-optimised platforms for commercial work.** A tool that is excellent at rooftop layout and customer-facing design may have nothing to say about matching a manufacturing load curve.

**Overbought: seats for viewers.** Universal.

An illustrative calculation, not a specific customer project. A manufacturing facility with strong daytime load, now able to install at a scale the old 100 kW ceiling prohibited.

| Line | Quoted at retail rate | Quoted correctly |

| Export valued at | Retail tariff | PHP 5 to 6 per kWh generation rate |
| Self-consumed energy valued at | Retail tariff | Retail tariff, correctly |

| Overstatement of return | Substantial on exported share | None |
| Does the buyer's finance team accept it? | Unlikely under scrutiny | Yes |

The important line is the third. Pricing export at retail does not merely inflate the payback figure, it recommends a larger system than the site should have, because it makes surplus generation look as valuable as displaced consumption. The customer then buys capacity that earns roughly half what they were told.

At residential scale that error is embarrassing. On a 400 kW commercial project it is the sort of thing that ends a relationship, and commercial buyers in this segment have finance teams who will find it. The cap change is a genuine opportunity, and the businesses that capture it will be the ones whose numbers survive a procurement review.
