## Why Installers Look for a Pylon Alternative

Pylon's strength is residential proposal velocity. The tool moves quickly through a residential layout and produces a clean, sales-led proposal. Teams shopping for alternatives are typically not unhappy with the residential workflow. They are asking what happens past it.

### Engineering depth is not the headline

Pylon is positioned around sales velocity rather than engineering-first simulation, so lender-grade yield reporting is not what it markets itself on. For installers growing into C&I or PPA-funded work, that usually means pairing it with a second tool. Confirm the current simulation scope with Pylon before assuming either way. SurgePV ships the full [8,760-hour shade analysis](https://surgepv.com/shadow-analysis) engine on every paid plan, in the same licence as the proposal.

### Multi-country coverage is worth checking

Pylon is Australian-built and describes its high resolution imagery as available in select countries, which is the practical constraint on where it performs best. If you need documented code and tariff handling for India (IS), the UK (BS 7671), or Germany (VDE), ask the vendor to evidence it for your specific market rather than assuming coverage in either direction.

### The pricing shape

Pylon publishes per-project rates rather than per-seat licences, so the software bill tracks how many quotes you produce, including the ones that never close. Flat per-seat plans like SurgePV's $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year stop moving with quote volume, which is often cheaper and always easier to forecast at team scale. Confirm current Pylon rates with the vendor before modelling.
