**Short version.** Start with the circuits you need during a power cut and the battery you intend to use. Then compare only exact inverter models that document both requirements. For a 5 kW single-phase home, the three models below show how materially the published limits can differ. They are worked examples, not a claim that every other model has failed.

If the main reason for buying is repeated outages, the [power-cut buying guide](/blog/best-solar-inverter-frequent-power-cuts/) applies a stricter backup and transfer-time test to these exact models.

## How we selected the comparison examples

Qbits Energy publishes this article and sells the QBH hybrid range. That commercial interest is why Qbits receives the same evidence test as every other brand. It does not take first place by default.

We checked current manufacturer material on 26 September 2026. For this worked comparison, we chose three exact low-voltage, single-phase models whose public documents allowed the same five questions to be answered:

1. Is the exact 5 kW class model named?
2. Is it a single-phase hybrid inverter rather than a solar PCU or ordinary on-grid inverter?
3. Is the published battery voltage window clear?
4. Are the MPPT count and per-tracker input current stated?
5. Is the backup-output figure separate from the grid-output figure?

We did not score price, warranty, service, reliability, certification or battery compatibility. Comparable, current Indian evidence was not available for all three.

This is not a census of every hybrid inverter sold in India. Other models may meet the same evidence gates, and omission is not disqualification. The table is a reproducible example of how to compare the quoted models on common fields. Its order is based on how clearly each current source separates the decision-critical specifications, not sales, quality or reliability.

## Conditional readings of the three 5 kW examples

### GoodWe GW5000-ES-20: clearest 5 kVA low-voltage comparison point

GoodWe's current ES G2 page puts the exact GW5000-ES-20 battery window, current limit, grid output and backup output in one place. Its 5,000 VA nominal backup rating makes it the clearest starting point when the planned backup load approaches the 5 kVA class.

**Conditional reading:** keep it under consideration when a seller can provide the current approved battery document, India-market unit details and written warranty and service route. The global page alone does not establish those local facts.

### Qbits QBH-5KS48P: documented Qbits option for a 48 V design

The Qbits catalogue names the exact QBH-5KS48P and publishes two MPPTs, 18 A per tracker, a 48 V rated battery input, 120 A maximum battery current and 5,000 W rated off-grid apparent output. It also lists a 150 to 450 V MPPT operating window and IP66 enclosure for this model family.

The same document describes lithium or lead-acid support, but it does not name an approved battery make and model for closed-loop communication. Qbits' public files also do not establish the base warranty term, remedy, registration deadline or exclusions.

**Conditional reading:** keep it under consideration when you want a current Qbits model document and the quote names the exact battery pairing, firmware, supplied communication cable and written warranty terms. Those missing documents matter more than a brand-level promise.

### Growatt SPH 5000TL BL-UP: explicit 4 kW backup ceiling

Growatt's April 2026 datasheet is unusually useful because it stops a common sizing mistake. The SPH 5000TL BL-UP is a 5,000 W grid-side model with 4,000 W maximum backup output. Its battery range is 42 to 59 V, with 85 A maximum charge and discharge current.

That lower backup figure is not automatically a weakness. A home with a measured essential-load list below the limit may not need more. The correct question is whether the model supports the actual circuits and surge events assigned to backup.

**Conditional reading:** keep it under consideration when 4 kW of published backup output covers the designed load and the installer proves the exact battery and India-market model pairing. Do not buy it on the 5 kW label alone.

## Choose the capacity segment before choosing the brand

A hybrid inverter has two different sizing jobs. Its power rating must handle the loads that operate together. The battery must supply the energy those loads need over time. A monthly bill does not answer either question on its own.

### 3 to 4.6 kW, single phase

This segment can fit homes with a deliberately limited backup board, such as lights, fans, a refrigerator, router and selected sockets. The current Qbits catalogue covers QBH 3KS through QBH 4K6S48P. GoodWe's ES G2 page covers 3 to 6 kW variants, while Growatt's SPH TL BL-UP sheet covers 3 to 6 kW variants.

Do not use that overlap as proof that the models are interchangeable. Their charge-current limits and backup outputs change by variant. Ask the installer to mark the exact column used for the design.

### 5 to 6 kW, single phase

This is the segment compared in the main table. It gives more room for simultaneous essential loads, but it does not create whole-home backup automatically. Air conditioners, pumps and refrigerators can introduce starting surges that the running-watt total hides.

The [hybrid inverter selection guide](/blog/how-to-choose-hybrid-solar-inverter/) explains how to turn a load list into seller questions. Keep the final surge check with the designer who has the appliance and inverter manuals.

### 7 to 8 kW, single phase

The current [Qbits single-phase 7 to 8 kW document](/datasheets/products/Qbits-Hybride-Inverter-Catalogue-2.pdf) names QBH-7KS48P, QBH-7K6S48P and QBH-8KS48P. The 8 kW model publishes two MPPTs, 48 V rated battery input and 8,000 W rated off-grid apparent output.

That establishes the Qbits model envelope. The [8 kW hybrid inverter comparison](/blog/best-eight-kw-solar-inverter-india/) checks it against exact GoodWe and Deye models, including a conflicting Qbits battery-current statement that buyers should resolve before design approval.

### 5 to 12 kW, three phase

Qbits' current [three-phase QBH catalogue](/datasheets/products/Qbits-Hybride-Inverter-Catalogue-3.pdf) spans 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 12 kW. It uses a 48 V rated battery input and moves from one MPPT on the lower models to two MPPTs on the 8, 10 and 12 kW variants.

A three-phase grid connection does not decide how every backup circuit should be distributed. The installer must show the phase arrangement, protected circuits and off-grid behaviour in the single-line diagram. We do not rank this segment because the reviewed evidence set does not provide a comparable current competitor table.

## Battery compatibility can overturn an initial comparison

The words "lithium compatible" are not an approved-pairing document. A safe comparison needs six checks for the exact inverter and battery:

1. The battery's full operating voltage stays inside the inverter window.
2. Charge and discharge current limits agree in both directions.
3. The chemistry and charging profile are supported.
4. The Battery Management System protocol is implemented on both products.
5. Firmware versions and communication cable pinout match.
6. The battery make and model are approved in writing by the responsible supplier.

CAN or RS485 labels on both products do not prove they exchange the same messages. Ask for the approved pairing by model number. If the answer is verbal, treat compatibility as unresolved.

For energy-capacity planning, use the [battery sizing worksheet](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/). It separates load power from battery energy and makes the assumptions visible. Do not turn its planning output into a promised backup duration.

## What most best lists get wrong

Efficiency is easy to rank because every datasheet prints a percentage. It is rarely the first constraint in a hybrid purchase.

An inverter with the highest peak-efficiency figure can still be the wrong choice if its backup output is too low, the battery pairing is unsupported, or the local warranty route is unclear. Peak efficiency also says nothing about field reliability or service quality.

The same caution applies to warranty years. A longer headline does not tell you the remedy, exclusions, registration deadline, labour cover or who handles the claim. Obtain the current written terms for the exact model before comparing warranty value.

## The document pack to request with both final quotes

Give each seller the same request. The replies become more useful than another brand score.

| Document or answer | What it must identify |
| --- | --- |
| Exact inverter datasheet | Full model number, phase, MPPT limits, grid output and backup output |
| Approved battery document | Battery make, model, voltage, firmware and communication protocol |
| Single-line diagram | PV, grid, battery, backup board, protection, metering and isolation |
| Backup load schedule | Running load, starting surge and the circuit assigned to backup |
| Written warranty | Term, remedy, exclusions, registration, labour and claim route |
| Compliance documents | Exact model, issuing body, document number, validity and project relevance |
| Commercial quote | Inverter, battery, accessories, protection, wiring, commissioning and exclusions |
| Local service route | Named entity responsible for commissioning and after-sales support |

For price comparison, keep inverter-only offers separate from battery-inclusive and fully installed offers. The [hybrid inverter price guide](/blog/hybrid-inverter-price-india/) shows how quickly unlike scopes create a false bargain.

## Final decision

The three documented 5 kW examples make the decision pattern visible. GoodWe publishes a 5,000 VA nominal backup figure, Qbits publishes a 5,000 W off-grid figure, and Growatt clearly limits backup to 4,000 W. None wins every home, and models outside this table still deserve the same evidence test.

Remove any model that cannot prove the exact battery pairing or backup duty. Compare the survivors on written commercial and service terms. That sequence is slower than choosing the first brand on a list, but it protects the part of the system you actually need during an outage.
