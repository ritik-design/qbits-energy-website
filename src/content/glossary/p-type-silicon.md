## What is p-type silicon

P-type silicon is crystalline silicon intentionally doped with a small concentration of boron atoms (typically 1015-1017 atoms/cm³). Boron has three valence electrons (one less than silicon's four), creating "holes" or electron deficiencies that act as positive charge carriers. The result: silicon with hole carriers (p for positive).

P-type silicon has been the traditional base material for solar cells since the early decades of PV. PERC (Passivated Emitter and Rear Contact) cells, which dominated solar from 2017 to 2024, are built on p-type wafers. Earlier cell technologies (BSF, Al-BSF) also used p-type.

The Indian solar industry's gradual transition to n-type TOPCon from 2024 onward marks the end of p-type's dominance. P-type will persist in legacy installations, cost-sensitive markets and gallium-doped variants for the next several years, but new manufacturing capacity is increasingly n-type.

## Why p-type matters

Legacy installed base. Hundreds of GW of PERC p-type modules installed globally and in India.

Cost. P-type wafer manufacturing is mature and slightly cheaper than n-type.

Replacement supply. Existing PERC arrays need matching modules for repairs.

Cost-sensitive segments. Gallium-doped p-type offers efficiency gains without n-type premium.

Historical understanding. P-type technology informs ongoing cell development.

## How p-type works

1. Czochralski crystal growth with boron dopant added to silicon melt.
2. Ingot grown by slow pulling from melt.
3. Ingot sliced into wafers.
4. Wafers processed through PERC manufacturing flow.
5. Final cell: p-type base, n-type emitter on front, PERC rear passivation.

## Technical deep dive

### Comparison: p-type vs n-type

| Property | P-type | N-type |
|---|---|---|
| Dopant | Boron (or Gallium) | Phosphorus |
| Charge carriers | Holes | Electrons |
| LID | Significant (B-O) | None |
| LeTID | Present | Minimal |
| Bulk lifetime | Lower | Higher |
| Temperature coefficient | -0.36%/°C | -0.30%/°C |
| Cost | Baseline | 3-8% premium |
| Cells | PERC, BSF | TOPCon, HJT, IBC |
| Efficiency ceiling | ~23% PERC | 26%+ HJT |

### Boron-oxygen LID

P-type silicon with boron and dissolved oxygen forms boron-oxygen (B-O) complexes under light. The defects trap charge carriers, reducing efficiency by 1-2.5 percent in the first 100 hours of operation. Gallium-doped p-type silicon eliminates this mechanism.

### Cell technologies using p-type

| Cell | Status |
|---|---|
| BSF (Al-BSF) | Legacy, largely retired |
| PERC | Dominant 2017-2024, declining |
| Gallium-PERC | Low-LID variant |
| n-PERC (rare) | n-type with PERC structure |

## Key takeaways

P-type silicon is boron-doped silicon traditionally used as the base for PERC solar cells. Dominant from 2017-2024, p-type is being replaced by n-type TOPCon for new manufacturing. Susceptibility to LID is the main technical limitation. P-type will persist in legacy and cost-sensitive segments for several years. Gallium-doped p-type addresses LID for those who prefer p-type's cost advantage.
