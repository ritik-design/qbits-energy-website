The **solar battery vs no battery** decision starts with the job. If the goal is bill reduction while the grid is available, a compliant on-grid system may not need storage. If the goal includes outage backup, evening energy shifting, export limitation, or another supported service, model a compatible battery system against the actual loads, tariff, rules, and costs.

Do not let one financial claim answer a resilience question or one outage concern justify an uneconomic design without being explicit. This guide separates the goals, then provides a buy-now, add-later, or no-battery decision record.

## What changes when solar has no battery?

**A no-battery grid-tied system converts available PV power for onsite use and approved export while the utility connection is present.** It avoids battery cost, integration, operating limits, degradation, and replacement, but an ordinary grid-tied inverter stops energising the utility connection after grid loss and does not provide household backup by itself.

No-battery solar can fit when:

- the main objective is reducing grid purchases;
- substantial consumption occurs during solar hours or export receives acceptable treatment;
- outages are rare, tolerable, or covered separately;
- the grid and metering route supports the design;
- the roof and array are suitable; and
- a battery's additional capability does not justify its supported current and future costs.

This is not a promise of a zero bill. Fixed charges, unmatched consumption, seasonal generation, export rules and other bill components can remain.

## What can a battery add to a solar system?

**A compatible battery system can store energy and may support defined backup, self-consumption shifting, export control, peak management, or other documented modes.** Its actual value depends on inverter and BMS compatibility, usable energy, power, efficiency, operating limits, load timing, tariff, outage need, controls, approvals, degradation, warranty, service, and replacement assumptions.

| Possible value | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Outage backup | Critical loads, duration, starting demand, backup output and commissioned grid-loss test |
| Evening self-consumption | Time-aligned solar surplus and evening load, tariff and operating limits |
| Export limitation | Applicable rule, measurement point, controls, response and commissioning record |
| Peak management | Demand interval, control method, power capability and contractual tariff basis |
| Energy resilience | Supported operating scenarios and owner priority, not an invented cash value |

A battery cannot create solar energy. During long outages or poor solar conditions, stored energy and supported charging determine what remains available.

## How should daytime and evening consumption be compared?

**Use interval data where available, or build a documented time-of-use load profile.** Compare PV generation and household load in the same time steps to estimate direct self-consumption, surplus, evening demand, export, and potential battery cycling. Monthly units alone cannot show whether storage moves useful energy or sits underused.

Record:

1. daytime base load and variable loads;
2. evening and overnight consumption;
3. motor or appliance starting events;
4. weekday, weekend and seasonal changes;
5. expected EV, air-conditioning or occupancy changes;
6. modeled PV generation by time period;
7. applicable import and export values; and
8. battery charge and discharge constraints.

Do not assume every midday surplus unit can be stored and later delivered. Energy capacity, charge power, operating window, losses, state of charge, load, weather and controls constrain the path.

## How should outage backup needs be defined?

**List critical circuits, simultaneous and starting loads, required duration, outage timing, priority, acceptable load shedding, and behaviour when solar or battery is unavailable.** Then verify the exact inverter's backup output, phase, transfer, grid-forming, battery, protection, isolation, control, and restoration capabilities through documents and witnessed tests.

## How should battery costs and financial value be modelled?

**Model the battery as a separate cash-flow and operating case.** Include installed equipment, controls, protection, switchboard work, commissioning, finance, monitoring, maintenance, efficiency losses, usable operating window, degradation basis, supported repair and replacement scenarios, tariff value, export changes, and residual value only where credible.

Useful relationships:

- `shifted energy value = delivered battery energy × applicable avoided import charge - displaced export value`
- `annual battery net value = shifted energy value + other supported monetary value - battery operating and service costs`
- `battery project cash flow = annual battery net value - installation, finance, repair and replacement cash flows`

Inputs must use consistent units and actual time periods. Do not present a result without the calculation file. Outage resilience can be a legitimate reason to buy, but label it as a preference or use a buyer-supplied value rather than inventing a rupee saving.

## Which technical and policy checks apply?

**Verify exact inverter and battery models, electrical limits, BMS communication, firmware, operating modes, protection, environmental conditions, monitoring, warranties, service, single-line diagram, grid and meter arrangement, current scheme treatment, and applicable DISCOM requirements.** A technically compatible battery does not establish subsidy eligibility or utility approval, and policy eligibility does not prove compatibility.

The current cited PM Surya Ghar residential guideline allows battery storage as an additional component while calculating CFA from eligible installed solar-module capacity. It does not create a separate battery-capacity grant in that cited schedule. Read the [PM Surya Ghar battery-storage guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-battery-storage-update/) and recheck official amendments for the project date.

## Should the battery be bought now or added later?

**Buy now when the supported battery capability is needed now and the complete design, cost, compatibility, approvals, warranty, service, and commissioning case is acceptable.** Add later when current value is insufficient but a documented retrofit path is worth preserving. Choose no battery when storage does not solve a required or justified job.

| Decision | Conditions to document |
| --- | --- |
| Buy now | Current backup or tariff need, exact pairing, complete quote, approvals and accepted financial or non-financial value |
| Add later | Initial inverter and site preserve a documented path, with known replacement and compatibility risks |
| No battery | Grid-connected savings meet the goal and backup or shifting does not justify storage |
| Reassess | Tariff, export rule, outage pattern, load, battery offering, property or policy may change the result |

The [add-a-battery-later guide](/blog/add-battery-to-solar-system-later/) covers DC coupling, AC coupling, inverter replacement and site provisions.

## What is the final battery decision checklist?

**A decision-ready comparison uses the same PV design and separates battery capability, price, cash flow, replacement, and resilience.** It records exact models, load and generation profiles, grid and policy sources, technical limits, approvals, warranties, service, conservative and favourable cases, unresolved gaps, human reviewers, and the trigger for revisiting the decision.

Before signing:

1. State the goal: savings, backup, shifting, export control, or another supported use.
2. Build time-aligned load and PV profiles.
3. Define critical loads and outage behaviour.
4. Verify exact inverter, battery, BMS and firmware compatibility.
5. Obtain complete with-battery and without-battery quotes.
6. Check grid, meter, scheme and DISCOM treatment from current sources.
7. Model battery cash flow separately with no unconfirmed benefit.
8. Compare buy-now, add-later and no-battery scenarios.
9. Record technical, financial, policy and service review.
10. Put acceptance tests, handover and change control into the contract.
