Understanding these policies is not just about compliance. It is about margin protection and project eligibility, because many residential installations are linked to subsidy and net metering programmes.

## BCD on Solar Inverters: Why the Rate Depends on Classification

The [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) and BCD are two separate policy instruments that work in parallel. Understanding both is essential, and the rate for each product comes from the current tariff and notifications, not from a table in a guide.

### Customs Duty Structure for Solar Equipment

Table: Where to find the duty for each solar product (we quote no rate we could not verify)

| Product | Tariff heading | What we found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solar inverter (string/central) | 8504 40 (static converters) | Some tariff listings show BCD of 20%; some industry guides quote about 7.5%; classification decides. IGST depends on how the product is classified and supplied |
| Solar PV module and cell | 8541 | Rates were revised in recent Union Budgets; older figures in circulation may be out of date |
| Other BOS items (structure, cable, battery) | Various | Check each item's tariff line |

*Rates subject to change with Union Budget notifications. Verify current rates and exemption notifications at [CBIC](https://www.cbic.gov.in/) before procurement.*

Because the rate depends on the tariff line and on notifications, the same product can be assessed at different rates depending on how it is classified. A 2026 Mundra Customs adjudication file illustrates the point: a product the importer declared under a 7.5% line was treated as falling under HS 85044090 at 20% BCD and 18% IGST, with short-payment demanded. Treat any rate you find in a guide as a lead to verify.

### How Total Customs Incidence Is Calculated

The Import Duty Impact Calculator applies the following formula:

> [!FORMULA] Total customs incidence on an imported inverter
> **Step 1:** BCD = CIF Value × BCD Rate
>
> **Step 2:** Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) = BCD × 10%
>
> **Step 3:** IGST base = CIF + BCD + SWS
>
> **Step 4:** [IGST](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) = IGST base × IGST Rate
>
> **Step 5:** Total incidence = BCD + SWS + IGST

**Example (assumed 20% BCD for illustration):** 10 kW imported inverter, CIF value ₹40,000

Table: Worked duty calculation for a 10 kW imported inverter with a CIF value of ₹40,000

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BCD (20%) | ₹40,000 × 20% | ₹8,000 |
| SWS (10% of BCD) | ₹8,000 × 10% | ₹800 |
| IGST base | ₹40,000 + ₹8,000 + ₹800 | ₹48,800 |
| IGST (18%) | ₹48,800 × 18% | ₹8,784 |
| **Total duties** | | **₹17,584** |
| **Landed cost** | ₹40,000 + ₹17,584 | **₹57,584** |

The same inverter manufactured in India and sold domestically would attract IGST at the applicable rate on the ex-works price, with no BCD or SWS on the finished unit. Any landed-cost difference depends on the actual BCD rate, the importer's margin, freight and the two models' prices, so calculate it from dated quotes.

## The Import Duty Impact Calculator

### The Import Duty Impact Calculator

Apply this five-step framework to any inverter procurement decision involving an imported product:

<!-- steps -->
1. **Determine CIF value**: get the supplier's CIF India price (Cost, Insurance, Freight to Indian port). Do not use ex-works prices without adding freight, insurance, and port handling.
2. **Apply BCD**: multiply CIF by the BCD rate for your tariff line, taken from the current CBIC schedule and notifications.
3. **Calculate SWS**: multiply BCD by 10% (Social Welfare Surcharge is levied on BCD).
4. **Calculate IGST**: sum CIF + BCD + SWS, then multiply by the applicable IGST rate (5% or 18% depending on product classification).
5. **Compare with a domestically made alternative**: the domestic product's true cost is ex-works price + domestic freight + GST input credit. The net difference reveals the true import premium.

Pairing this framework with a broader [solar design and estimation toolkit](https://surgepv.com/tools/) lets EPCs model landed cost alongside system design and string sizing in the same workflow, rather than treating customs math as a separate spreadsheet exercise.

> [!EXAMPLE] Duty bill for an EPC doing 50 residential jobs a month
> For an EPC doing 50 residential installations per month using a 5 kW imported inverter at ₹25,000 CIF, at an assumed 20% BCD:
>
> - BCD: ₹5,000
> - SWS: ₹500
> - IGST (18%): ₹5,490
> - Landed cost: ₹35,990 vs ₹25,000 ex-works
>
> At 50 units/month, the monthly import duty spend = 50 × ₹10,990 = **₹5,49,500 per month** in duties alone. A domestically manufactured alternative avoids duty on the finished unit, but compare its price, certificates and service terms on the same basis before judging the margin effect.

## ALMM and Inverters: What to Check for PM Surya Ghar

The schemes below apply the ALMM requirement to solar modules (List-I) and cells (List-II). MNRE publishes no ALMM list for inverters, so the inverter checks further down are about the exact model's own documents.

- PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (residential subsidy programme)
- Central government-funded solar projects (SECI, CPSU schemes)
- State government solar schemes that adopt ALMM as a module eligibility criterion

### Inverter Verification Checklist for EPCs

Before confirming any inverter purchase for a PM Surya Ghar project:

> [!CHECKLIST] Inverter verification for a PM Surya Ghar project
> - **Step 1:** Ask the seller for the exact model's current BIS registration and test certificates. For modules, download the current ALMM list from [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/).
> - **Step 2:** Match the exact model number, wattage, and firmware version on the certificates to the invoice.
> - **Step 3:** Verify the manufacturer name on the certificates matches the invoice (brand name vs legal entity name sometimes differ).
> - **Step 4:** Check the certificate validity date, because certificates have an expiry and renewal cycle.
> - **Step 5:** Keep the certificates and, for modules, the downloaded ALMM list version and date as project documentation.

## Policy Risk: What EPCs Must Watch for in 2026 and Beyond

BCD rates are determined by Union Budget notifications and can change annually. EPCs with forward order books need to track these policy signals:

- **BCD revision**: if the government changes the BCD on inverters, the landed cost of imported inverters moves with it. Track Union Budget and CBIC notifications for your tariff line.
- **ALMM list expansion**: MNRE periodically adds new module and cell manufacturers, and has not published an inverter list. EPCs should watch for any change in the scope of the list and for new listed module brands.
- **BIS mandatory certification expansion**: currently BIS certification is required for certain inverter categories; expansion of mandatory BIS coverage would further restrict non-certified imported products.

## Common EPC Mistakes in Import Duty Management

- **Using ex-works prices in project BOMs**: ex-works pricing from a Chinese supplier is meaningless without adding the full duty incidence. BOMs submitted to clients should reflect landed costs, [quotation software that itemises landed cost line by line](https://quickestimate.co/features/quotation-system/) rather than ex-works pricing prevents this discrepancy from reaching the client.

- **Choosing an inverter on a small per-unit saving without checking certificates and DISCOM requirements**: the central subsidy is ₹30,000 to ₹78,000 per system. No unit-level saving justifies risking the homeowner's subsidy through a missed document check.
- **Not building BCD escalation risk into multi-year EPC contracts**: if BCD changes mid-contract, the EPC absorbs the margin hit unless the contract has a material cost escalation clause.
