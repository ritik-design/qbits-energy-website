A connected inverter should be judged at the exact model and installation level. An app-store listing shows that an application exists. It does not show that the logger is included, that a feature works on your model, or that an alert reaches you without setup.

This page compares the **hardware and connectivity** of five brands on a 5 kW single-phase inverter: **Qbits, GoodWe, Growatt, Sungrow and Solis**. For each, it names the exact model, the Wi-Fi or 4G logger options, the published data interval, what the datasheet says about gaps, and what to confirm before you pay. For the apps themselves, including setup, alerts, export and language, see the separate [solar inverter app comparison](/blog/solar-inverter-app-monitoring/).

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - There is no universal best. Each brand's logger talks only to its own cloud, so you are choosing a logger, an app and a support route together with the inverter.
> - Four of the five brands publish a plug-in logger datasheet. GoodWe, Growatt, Solis and Qbits also document a cellular or GPRS option. Sungrow's WiNet-S2 datasheet lists Wi-Fi and Ethernet.
> - Published data intervals are 5 minutes for the Growatt ShineWiFi-X and the Solis S3-WiFi-ST, and "seconds" for the Sungrow WiNet-S2. We found no interval published for the GoodWe kits or the Qbits product data.
> - Every Wi-Fi logger datasheet we checked lists 2.4 GHz only. A router that broadcasts only 5 GHz will not work.
> - Verify the logger part number, whether it is in the quote, and a reconnection test, before handover.

Qbits Energy publishes this page and sells one of the five inverters. Qbits is held to the same source rule as the others: a fact counts only if a manufacturer document states it, and Qbits appears in the table with its own published fields. The brands are listed in no ranked order, and no scores or winners are given.

## How we chose the five

1. **One class of inverter.** A 5 kW, single-phase, grid-tied residential model, or the 5 kW member of a named range, so the comparison is like for like.
2. **Exact models.** A brand-level claim such as "supports Wi-Fi" is not enough. We name the model and its logger.
3. **Official documents only.** We used datasheets, manuals, product pages and store listings from the manufacturer, opened on 14 October 2026. Where a document was silent, the table says "not found", which means we did not find it, not that it is absent.
4. **Not a market ranking.** Other brands such as Deye or SMA also offer connected inverters. Omission is not disqualification.

## Qbits QB 5KTLD

What the public documents do not say is which logger or Wi-Fi module sits inside the 5KTLD, whether it is included in a quote, what the data interval is, or how the app pairs with this model. For battery systems, the single-phase [QBH 3 to 6 kW hybrid](/our-products/QBH-3-6KS48P/) lists "Wi-Fi monitoring; verify battery interface", and the latest Qbits app release notes mention hybrid inverter details, including battery and energy values and a plant energy-flow diagram. ## GoodWe GW5K-DNS-G40

GoodWe's current [DNS G4 page](https://en.goodwe.com/dns-g4) lists the GW5K-DNS-G40 in a 3 to 6 kW, single-phase, two-MPPT range. The page does not itself describe Wi-Fi, so the communication facts come from GoodWe's own compatibility list (Version 18, dated 30 September 2026). It marks DNS G4 as in mass production and, in its communication table, as compatible with three module types, which we read as a Wi-Fi/LAN kit, the Ezlink3000 and the 4G Kit-G20, with a firmware condition for the Ezlink module (V1.6.8 or above). Read the table yourself for your own variant, because the columns are dense and conditions vary by family.

The module datasheets are specific:

- **Ezlink3000:** USB connection, Wi-Fi 802.11 b/g/n, Bluetooth 5.1, optional Ethernet up to 100 m, IP65, typical consumption 2 W or less.
- **4G Kit-G20:** up to 4 connected devices, IP66, integrated 4G and Bluetooth, breakpoint retransmission, on-site APN customisation.
- **Wi-Fi Kit-20 (a separate Wi-Fi-only kit; check the list for your variant):** Wi-Fi at 2.4 GHz only, Bluetooth V4.2 for setup, breakpoint retransmission, 1 connected device, IP65, plug and play.

The Wi-Fi Kit-20 datasheet explains breakpoint retransmission as automatically disconnecting and reconnecting broken links to prevent data loss. Remote upgrade of inverter programs without a site visit is also listed.

**Choose it when:** you want documented Bluetooth local setup and a cellular option from the same maker, and the seller names the module and firmware in the quote.

**Do not assume:** that the quoted kit is the one on the compatibility list for DNS G4, or that GoodWe's older DNS G3 module list applies, because that family is marked Phase Out.

## Growatt MIN 5000TL-X

Growatt's MIN 2500 to 6000TL-X datasheet lists the MIN 5000TL-X with two MPP trackers, IP65 protection and a display shown as OLED plus LED with Wi-Fi and app. For interfaces it marks RS485 and USB as standard, and Wi-Fi, GPRS, RF and LAN as optional. That wording matters: Wi-Fi is an optional accessory on this model, not a given.

The logger options are the **ShineWiFi-X** and the **Shine4G-X**:

- **ShineWiFi-X:** 802.11 b/g/n, 2.4 GHz band, USB to the inverter, 5 minute transmission interval, built-in memory for up to one month, 1.2 W rated power.
- **Shine4G-X:** 2G, 3G and 4G with a standard SIM card, USB, 5 minute interval, memory for up to one month, operating range -30 to +65 degrees C.

Both talk to ShineServer, and both are configured through the app. The setup guide for the ShineWiFi-X says the logger works on 2.4 GHz and dual-band routers but not on a single-band 5 GHz router, and it ties LED colours to states: flashing blue means connected to router and server, solid green means a failed connection, and flashing green means the router has no internet. The guide is dated 2020, so confirm against the current manual.

**Choose it when:** the quote names the ShineWiFi-X or Shine4G-X, you accept the web portal for reporting, and Growatt confirms the logger against your exact inverter.

**Do not assume:** that the MIN 5000TL-X ships with a logger. Optional means someone has to order it.

## Alerts: what to demand and how to test it

Alert types are only useful if they arrive. The published delivery routes differ:

Table: Published alert delivery routes by brand

| Alert route | Qbits | GoodWe | Growatt | Sungrow | Solis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App push | Not found | Notification setting in the SEMS Portal app manual | Not stated in documents checked | Yes, per Sungrow's 2023 release note | Yes, with selectable urgency |
| Email | Not found | Not stated for alarms | Yes, on ShineServer, with daily and monthly reports | Yes, per the release note | Yes |
| SMS | Not found | Not found | Not found | Not found | Yes |
| Alarm history | Alarm page in the app | Alarms tab with search | Not stated | Fault management | Search, filter, export |

Ask for the following to be shown on your own installed system, in this order:

<!-- steps -->
1. **Inverter offline.** Switch the router off and note whether and when a notification arrives.
2. **Grid fault.** Ask the installer to use the manufacturer's permitted test, not a real electrical fault.
3. **Fault with an explanation.** An alert that says only "E045" with no text sends you to a manual. See whether the app explains it.
4. **A recipient who is not you.** Add a second email or phone where the platform allows it.
5. **Delay.** Time the gap between the event and the message. Platforms rarely publish this.

## What data your connected inverter should show

Use this as a checklist at handover. Items marked model-specific need a live demonstration, not a brochure.

> [!CHECKLIST] Data the owner account should show
> - Current AC power and today's, month's and total energy
> - Plant status and inverter identity, with the serial number matching the nameplate
> - Dated alarm and event history
> - Per-MPPT voltage and current (model-specific, and useful with a dual-MPPT inverter on a split roof)
> - Grid voltage and frequency (model-specific)
> - Temperature (model-specific)
> - Consumption or export data, which usually needs a meter or current transformer
> - Battery state of charge and power on hybrids

If your roof has two orientations, per-MPPT data tells you whether one string is underperforming. The [dual-MPPT comparison](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/) explains why that matters.

## Cellular options for sites with weak broadband

Where home broadband is unreliable, a logger with its own SIM removes the router from the chain. The options documented above are the Qbits optional GPRS interface, the Growatt Shine4G-X, the Solis S3-GPRS-ST and the GoodWe 4G Kit-G20. Before choosing one, settle four things in writing.

> [!CHECKLIST] Four things to settle in writing for a cellular logger
> - **Band match.** Ask the local operator which bands it runs in your area, and compare with the module's list. For example, the Growatt Shine4G-X datasheet lists LTE-FDD B1, B3, B5, B7, B8 and B20, LTE-TDD B38, B40 and B41, WCDMA B1, B5 and B8, and GSM 900 and 1800 MHz.
> - **Who pays for the SIM and data.** A recurring cost should not be a surprise at month two.
> - **Signal at the inverter.** Test it where the inverter hangs, not where the installer stands.
> - **Replacement if the network changes.** A GPRS-only module depends on 2G service, which operators may withdraw.

## Adding monitoring to an inverter you already own

Most of the loggers above are USB or RS485 plug-ins built for their own brand's models. To add one:

<!-- steps -->
1. Find the inverter's exact model and firmware on the nameplate or display.
2. Look up that model in the manufacturer's current compatibility list. GoodWe's list carries firmware conditions, and Sungrow's manual says to confirm firmware compatibility with the module before use.
3. Order the listed logger part, not a look-alike from a marketplace.
4. Register the plant in your own name and add the logger by its own serial number.

If the inverter has no supported logger, a new inverter is a clearer route than an unverified workaround.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying for the app and ignoring the logger.** The plug-in stick is the link between inverter and cloud, so choose and test it first.
- **Assuming Wi-Fi is built in.** On several models it is an optional accessory.
- **A 5 GHz-only router.** Every Wi-Fi logger datasheet we checked lists 2.4 GHz. Use a dual-band router or a 2.4 GHz network.
- **Leaving the plant in the installer's account.** Register it under your own email.
- **Treating monitoring as proof of safety.** It reports what connected sensors see. It does not replace earthing, insulation or connector checks.
- **Taking the app total over the DISCOM meter.** The meter governs billing.
