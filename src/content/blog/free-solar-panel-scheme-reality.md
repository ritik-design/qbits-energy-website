> **Quick answers**
>
> - There is no government scheme that gives residential solar panels away at zero cost.
> - PM Surya Ghar pays central financial assistance against a published slab: ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp, ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000.
> - The money comes after installation, DISCOM inspection and approval on the portal, not before.
> - Assistance does not rise above 3 kWp, so any oversizing is funded entirely by you.
> - Zero upfront offers are loans or third-party contracts. Compare total cost, ownership and exit terms.
> - The scheme never asks for an OTP or an advance fee through a social media link.

**Short version.** The scheme is real, the free panels are not. Expect to fund the balance of the contract after assistance, verify the vendor and the application only through official channels, and treat any offer that needs your OTP as fraudulent. Facts below were checked against MNRE documents and PIB releases as of **14 October 2026**.

## Why the phrase persists

"Free solar panel scheme" is a search phrase, not a government programme name. It persists because assistance schemes get compressed into a headline as they travel through social media, and because some advertisers deliberately blur the line.

What exists is PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, a central scheme with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore, approved on 29 February 2024 and set to run until FY 2026-27 according to MNRE's [7 May 2024 office memorandum](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/process-of-vendor-registration-for-pm-surya-ghar-scheme-on-the-national-portal/). Its stated aim is to increase the share of rooftop solar and let households generate their own electricity. "Muft bijli" refers to the free electricity the system is expected to generate over time, not to free equipment.

That distinction is the whole article. Everything else follows from it.

As of 14 October 2026 we found no official announcement extending the scheme past its FY 2026-27 period. Treat any message that gives a fixed "last date" or a closing countdown as unverified until you see it on the portal or an MNRE notice.

## What the guidelines actually provide

The [MNRE operational guidelines for residential CFA](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) set out the standard residential slab. The figures below are from the July 2024 text, which states the slab as effective from 13 February 2024. MNRE amended the guidelines on 7 July 2025, and that amendment is a scanned image we could not read clause by clause, so check it for later changes.

| System capacity | Standard states and UTs | Special category states and UTs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 2 kWp or part thereof | ₹30,000 per kWp | ₹33,000 per kWp |
| Additional 1 kWp or part thereof, taking the system to 3 kWp | ₹18,000 per kWp | ₹19,800 per kWp |
| Beyond 3 kWp | No additional assistance | No additional assistance |

The guidelines include worked examples. A 1.5 kW system receives ₹45,000. A 2.5 kW system receives ₹69,000. A 6 kW system receives ₹78,000. Special category states in the guidelines are Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, the north-eastern states including Sikkim, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep.

These are published slabs rather than an entitlement. An individual application is still subject to eligibility, vendor, equipment and DISCOM conditions.

Four implications are frequently missed.

**The cap.** Assistance stops increasing above 3 kWp under the standard slab. A 5 kWp system receives the same central assistance as a 3 kWp one, so the additional capacity is funded entirely by you. Size the system for your consumption and your state's settlement rules, not to chase assistance that is not there.

**Modules decide the amount.** Assistance is calculated on installed module capacity, not on inverter size. The guidelines say adding a battery or other components does not change the calculation. Off-grid installations are not eligible.

**Domestic content.** Solar modules must be domestically manufactured from domestically manufactured cells. The guidelines say using non-DCR modules in any form makes the installation ineligible for assistance.

**Timing.** Assistance is released through an e-token after the DISCOM has physically verified the system and approved it on the portal, and the guidelines say the claim is processed within 15 days of DISCOM approval. You or your lender carry the full contract cost until then.

## Reading a quote properly

A quote should separate four figures: the gross contract amount, the eligible central assistance, any independently verified state support, and the amount you must fund.

Take an illustrative example. A vendor presents a ₹150,000 installed quote for an eligible 3 kWp system under the standard slab. Subtracting an assumed approved ₹78,000 leaves ₹72,000 to fund, before any finance cost.

That arithmetic is illustrative. It is not a current market price, and the ₹78,000 is assumed rather than approved. The actual project may differ on both numbers, which is exactly why a quote presenting a single "after subsidy price" without showing the components is doing you no favours.

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Itemised proposal | Shows modules, inverter, structure, wiring, labour and exclusions separately |
| Official scheme application | Identifies the applicant and the current status |
| Vendor registration record | Confirms the installer through the official route |
| Module and cell documents | Supports the domestic content check |
| Final invoice and commissioning record | Establishes installed equipment and contract amount |

The [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) explains how to check those records, and the [full scheme guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) covers eligibility and the application steps.

## What assistance does not cover

Applicants routinely budget for the panel and inverter and then meet costs nobody mentioned.

Structural work for an unsuitable roof, cable runs longer than standard, a connection upgrade from single to three phase, DISCOM application and meter charges, and any civil work are commonly outside a headline quote. The model consumer and vendor agreement in the MNRE guidelines says site-specific extra work is quoted separately and invoiced with GST on top of the standard plant cost. Ongoing cleaning is also your job, although the vendor must teach you how to do it.

Ask for exclusions in writing. A quote that lists what is included but not what is excluded leaves the gap for you to discover after the contract is signed.

## Zero upfront offers: the loan route

A no upfront payment offer can be legitimate. It is a financing arrangement and should be assessed as one.

MNRE's guidelines expect banks to offer standardised low-interest loans for residential rooftop systems up to 3 kW, priced at the repo rate plus 50 basis points, and to make them available through the National Portal and the Jan Samarth portal. Security terms differ by bank. SBI's page below lists hypothecation of the financed asset as the security, so ask your bank exactly what it requires.

Bank terms move, so use the bank's own page. The [SBI Surya Ghar loan page](https://sbi.bank.in/web/personal-banking/loans/pm-surya-ghar-loan-for-solar-roof-top), read on 14 October 2026, shows these terms:

| Term | What SBI's page states |
| --- | --- |
| Rate | Floating. 5.75 percent a year for loans up to ₹2 lakh and 7.90 percent for ₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh, as on 15 December 2025 |
| Maximum amount | ₹6 lakh. No income criterion for loans up to ₹2 lakh |
| Margin | At least 10 percent of project cost, paid upfront by the borrower |
| Tenure | Up to 120 months including a six-month moratorium |
| Process | Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in, apply for the loan on jansamarth.in or the bank's site, and disbursement goes to the vendor after feasibility documents |
| Assistance | Claimed on the National Portal using the loan account number |

Two things follow from the guidelines. If you take a loan, the portal credits the assistance to your loan account, up to the outstanding amount. And the loan does not remove the margin or the vendor checks.

Here is an arithmetic illustration, not a quote. Borrow ₹1,50,000 at 5.75 percent for 120 months. The monthly instalment is about ₹1,647, total repayment about ₹1,97,600, and interest about ₹47,600. At 7 percent the same loan costs about ₹1,742 a month and about ₹59,000 in interest. Rates here are floating, so your real numbers will differ.

"Pay only your EMI" means no down payment beyond the margin. It does not mean the system costs nothing. Ask for the full repayment figure on paper, and compare it with the quote for the project. If a salesperson will not show it, the word free is doing the selling.

## RESCO and utility-led models: free installation, not free power

The second legitimate no-upfront-cost route is the RESCO model. MNRE issued guidelines on 14 January 2025, reported in a [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2092717&reg=48&lang=2), for two models in addition to the standard consumer-owned route.

- **RESCO.** Third-party entities invest in rooftop solar, so consumers pay only for the electricity they use and bear no upfront cost.
- **Utility-led aggregation (ULA).** DISCOMs or state-designated entities install rooftop solar on behalf of households.

The guidelines also earmark a ₹100 crore Payment Security Mechanism corpus to de-risk RESCO investment. That protects developers' payments. It is not a consumer guarantee.

Before signing any such arrangement, settle these points in writing, because the public guidelines do not fix them for you:

- who owns the equipment during the contract and after it ends;
- the per-unit tariff, how it changes each year, and the total over the full term;
- what happens if you sell the house, rebuild the terrace or stop paying;
- who services the system, how quickly, and who pays for repairs;
- whether the developer or you claim central assistance; and
- the exit and buyout terms.

A long term payment obligation attached to your roof is a significant commitment. The monthly number is the least informative part of it. Where you can finance ownership at the loan rates above, compare the lifetime cost of owning against the lifetime cost of the tariff. Our guides to the [RESCO model](/blog/solar-resco-model-india-guide/) and [zero-cost RESCO and OPEX contracts](/blog/zero-cost-solar-resco-opex-india/) go through contract terms in more detail.

## Telling a real offer from a harvesting scam

The scheme is genuine. A substantial volume of advertising invoking it is not, and the tells are consistent.

| Red flag | Why it is a problem | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asks for an OTP or banking credentials | No application step needs it. It is account takeover | Refuse. Change passwords. Tell your bank |
| Advance "processing" or "release" fee to an individual account | Assistance is released through the portal to your bank or loan account after DISCOM approval | Do not pay. Report on cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 |
| Application through a social media link or unofficial form | Fake portals copy the official look | Type pmsuryaghar.gov.in yourself |
| An SMS saying subsidy is approved, with an APK or link | Links and app files can capture banking details or OTPs | Delete it. Check status only inside your own portal login |
| Guaranteed approval, amount or timeline | No installer controls the DISCOM decision | Treat as a sales claim |
| Urgency, such as a scheme closing in days or slots running out | The scheme has an official period, not district-level quota sales | Compare quotes and take your time |
| Vendor certificate shared as an image | Cannot show current status | Check the vendor on the official portal |
| No itemised quote or refusal to name module and inverter models | You cannot check domestic-content or specification rules | Do not sign |
| Calls from a "government solar department" offering a free system | The scheme runs through the portal, the registered vendor you choose and your DISCOM | Hang up and check the portal yourself |

For a wider fraud checklist, read [how to avoid solar scams in India](/blog/solar-scams-india-how-to-avoid/).

## How to verify an offer, step by step

1. **Type the portal address yourself.** Go to [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/). Registration is done by you. The guidelines say the consumer has user rights to do every portal activity without relying on the vendor.
2. **Check the vendor on the registered-vendor pages** for your state and district, and match the name to the quote. The [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) gives the five-point check.
3. **Get at least two itemised quotes** that separate contract value from assumed assistance.
4. **Ask for the module make and model and the domestic-content proof.** Non-DCR modules make the installation ineligible.
5. **Ask for the inverter make, model and datasheet.** Inverters must meet MNRE's minimum technical specifications, which the DISCOM checks at inspection. If your quote names a Qbits model, see the [on-grid inverter range](/on-grid-inverter/) for datasheets. Qbits supplies inverters and does not install systems.
6. **Confirm the payment milestones** are tied to survey, delivery and commissioning, and that money goes to the contracted business.
7. **Confirm the five-year maintenance duty.** Registered vendors must provide repair and maintenance free of cost for five years from commissioning under the guidelines.
8. **Write down the complaint route:** the vendor's named complaint contact, the portal grievance section, and the national call centre.

## State support sits on top, and varies

Central assistance is only one layer. The guidelines allow States and UTs to supplement central assistance, and some run their own residential rooftop support administered separately and on their own conditions.

Because these change by order and differ widely, treat any state figure quoted in a sales conversation as unverified until you have seen it on an official state or DISCOM source. A vendor combining a central slab with an assumed state amount to produce an attractive net price is presenting two assumptions as one certainty.

Ask which specific state scheme is being claimed, under which order, and where it is published. If that cannot be answered, price the project on central assistance alone and treat anything further as an upside rather than a plan. State by state details are in our [all-states subsidy guide](/blog/solar-subsidy-all-states/).

## From application to payment: the real sequence

The guidelines describe the order of steps, and each one is a place where a free-solar story falls apart.

1. You register on the National Portal and apply through your DISCOM details.
2. The DISCOM feasibility step applies where your state still requires it. The guidelines refer to the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, under which rooftop systems up to 10 kW are deemed accepted without technical feasibility approval once the regulator or DISCOM puts that into practice.
3. You choose a registered vendor and agree design, components and financial terms.
4. The vendor installs, runs the safety checks and educates you on maintenance.
5. You update the system details and upload documents, including geo-tagged photographs. The vendor may help through your login.
6. The DISCOM inspects the system, completes the net-meter agreement and approves, sends back for corrections, or rejects the claim with reasons.
7. The e-token is activated with the eligible amount for the actual installed capacity. You redeem it on the portal and the assistance is credited to your bank account, or to your loan account if you borrowed.

None of those steps involves paying an agent who contacted you first.

## After approval, keep the paperwork

Assistance is disbursed against a completed and verified installation, so the records created at commissioning are what close the process.

Keep the sanctioned application, the final itemised invoice, the commissioning record, the equipment serial numbers, the warranty documents and the DISCOM approval together. If disbursement is delayed, those documents are what an escalation rests on, and reassembling them months later is considerably harder than filing them at the time. If something goes wrong, the portal's grievance route gives each complaint a tracking ID and is meant to resolve it within 30 days according to the guidelines.

## What to do next

Confirm your DISCOM and sanctioned connection, get at least two itemised quotes that separate contract value from assumed assistance, verify each vendor through the official channel, and check the equipment documents for the exact models proposed. Then decide on the arithmetic in front of you rather than on a headline. A properly quoted system with a clear balance to fund is a better outcome than an advertised free one.
