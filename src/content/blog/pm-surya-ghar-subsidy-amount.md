> **Direct answer.** The PM Surya Ghar subsidy amount is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kilowatt — giving a maximum central subsidy of ₹78,000 for any system 3 kW or above. The subsidy is capped at 3 kW per household. Systems above 3 kW receive the same ₹78,000; group housing societies receive ₹18,000 per kW capped at ₹50,000 per unit. ## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Amount Slab Table — Every System Size Calculated

The central subsidy structure has two slabs defined by MNRE, applied on a per-kilowatt basis to the first 3 kW of installed capacity. Every homeowner should know both slabs before sizing their system.

**Slab 1:** ₹30,000 per kW — applies to the first 2 kW of system capacity.

**Slab 2:** ₹18,000 per kW — applies to the third kilowatt only.

**Above 3 kW:** No additional central subsidy. The maximum is fixed at ₹78,000 regardless of whether you install a 4 kW, 6 kW, or 10 kW system.

### Why 3 kW Is the Economic Sweet Spot

The 3 kW threshold is not arbitrary. According to [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/) data on residential solar sizing patterns in India, over 72% of home installations in the 1–5 kW segment land between 2 kW and 4 kW. A 3 kW system in India's median irradiance zone (4.5 to 5.5 peak sun hours per day) generates approximately 360 to 420 units per month — enough to offset the consumption of a typical 2 to 3 BHK household including air conditioning.

Installing exactly 3 kW maximises the subsidy per rupee invested. Going to 4 kW or 5 kW is sensible if your consumption is higher, but the additional kilowatts receive no central subsidy, lengthening payback. Going below 3 kW leaves subsidy money on the table. For most urban homeowners with an electricity bill above ₹4,000 per month, 3 kW is the starting point for the financial case.

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Amount for Group Housing and RWAs

Individual homeowners and apartment flat owners face different subsidy structures, and the group housing rules are where most flat owners get confused.

### Individual Flat Owner Route

A flat owner who has a separate residential DISCOM connection and roof access — or a portion of the terrace — can apply individually at the standard residential slab: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the third, up to ₹78,000. The flat owner needs an NOC from the Resident Welfare Association confirming exclusive terrace access for the installation area. This is the most financially attractive route for flat owners.

### Resident Welfare Association Common-Area Route

When the entire society applies collectively for a common-area system — powering lifts, common lighting, pumps, and shared loads — the subsidy structure changes:

- **Rate:** ₹18,000 per kW
- **Per-unit cap:** ₹50,000 per dwelling unit in the society
- **Maximum system size eligible:** Up to 500 kW per society

A 100-unit housing society with 100 flats can claim up to ₹50,00,000 (₹50 lakh) in central subsidy for a system of up to 500 kW. The lower per-kW rate reflects that common-area systems typically export a large proportion of their generation (export at the DISCOM's feed-in tariff) rather than self-consuming at the full retail tariff — reducing the financial benefit relative to individual rooftop systems.

| Applicant Type | Subsidy Rate | Cap Per Unit | System Size Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Individual homeowner | ₹30,000/kW (first 2 kW) + ₹18,000/kW (third kW) | ₹78,000 | No limit (subsidy capped at 3 kW) |
| Individual flat owner (own terrace) | Same as above with RWA NOC | ₹78,000 | 3 kW per flat owner |
| RWA / group housing (common area) | ₹18,000 per kW | ₹50,000 per unit | 500 kW per society |

## The PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Stack — Central + State + Depreciation Layers

Most homeowners calculate only the central ₹78,000 and stop there. The full financial picture has three distinct layers that stack on top of each other — and in states like Gujarat and Maharashtra, the combined benefit is nearly double the central headline number.

### The PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Stack

1. **Central Government Layer** — ₹30,000/kW (first 2 kW) + ₹18,000/kW (third kW) = up to ₹78,000. Funded by MNRE, disbursed directly to homeowner. This is the base layer available to every eligible residential applicant across all 28 states and 8 union territories.

2. **State Government Top-Up Layer** — Additional state-level subsidy disbursed through State Nodal Agencies or DISCOMs. Amounts vary by state and are revised annually. In Gujarat, this layer adds ₹30,000–₹60,000 for a 3 kW system. In Maharashtra and Rajasthan, it adds ₹15,000–₹30,000. This layer is only available in states with active state solar policies — check your State Nodal Agency before assuming you qualify.

3. **Accelerated Depreciation Layer** — Applicable primarily to non-residential applicants (small businesses, professionals, shops with domestic-plus-commercial connections). Under Section 32 of the Income Tax Act, solar equipment qualifies for 40% accelerated depreciation in Year 1, reducing the effective tax outgo. For a 3 kW system at ₹1,50,000 gross, a taxpayer in the 30% bracket saves approximately ₹18,000 in the first year through depreciation alone. This layer does not apply to pure residential consumers, but is significant for self-employed homeowners who operate from home or have mixed-use properties.

Applying all three layers, the effective net cost for a qualified Gujarat homeowner in the 30% tax bracket installing a 3 kW system at ₹1,60,000 gross drops to approximately ₹22,000–₹40,000 — a payback period of well under 12 months when electricity savings begin.

## How to Calculate Your Exact PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Amount in 4 Steps

The calculation is straightforward once you know the slabs. Work through these four steps before calling any vendor.

### Step 1 — Determine Your Optimal System Size

Find the last 12 months of your electricity bills. Sum the units consumed and divide by 12 for your monthly average. Divide that monthly average by 120 (approximate units per kW per month in median Indian conditions) to get the kW capacity that offsets 100% of your consumption.

**Example:** 480 units per month ÷ 120 = 4 kW needed for full offset.

Round down to 3 kW if your roof area or sanctioned load constrains you, or if you prefer the faster payback of a smaller system with maximum subsidy efficiency.

### Step 2 — Apply the Slab Formula to Your Chosen Capacity

Use this formula:

- **1 kW:** 1 × ₹30,000 = **₹30,000**
- **2 kW:** 2 × ₹30,000 = **₹60,000**
- **3 kW:** (2 × ₹30,000) + (1 × ₹18,000) = **₹78,000**
- **4 kW or above:** (2 × ₹30,000) + (1 × ₹18,000) = **₹78,000** (capped)

### Step 3 — Add Your State Top-Up (If Applicable)

Look up your state in the table in the previous section. Add the confirmed state top-up amount to your central subsidy figure. If your state has no top-up, use zero.

**Example (Gujarat, 3 kW):** ₹78,000 + ₹30,000 minimum state top-up = ₹1,08,000 minimum combined subsidy.

### Step 4 — Calculate Net Cost and Payback

Subtract the combined subsidy from the vendor quote to get your net cost. Divide net cost by annual electricity bill savings to estimate payback years.

**Example (Pune, 3 kW, no state top-up):**
- Gross system cost: ₹1,60,000
- Central subsidy: ₹78,000
- Net cost: ₹82,000
- Annual savings at ₹8/unit on 400 units/month: ₹38,400
- Payback: ₹82,000 ÷ ₹38,400 = **2.1 years**

## ALMM Compliance — The Hidden Condition That Controls Your Entire Subsidy

Every rupee of PM Surya Ghar subsidy — central and state — is conditional on a single technical requirement: your solar inverter and panels must be on the [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) maintained by [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/). A non-ALMM inverter disqualifies the full claim, not just the inverter component.

This is not a technicality that vendors can work around. The DISCOM commissioning engineer cross-references the installed inverter model number against the current ALMM list during the site inspection. If the model is absent or the listing has expired, the inspection fails, the commissioning certificate is withheld, and the entire subsidy disbursement chain stops.

- **Verify before installation begins** — Do not take the vendor's word that the inverter is ALMM-listed. Go to [mnre.gov.in/almm-list/](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/) and search the exact model name and wattage yourself.
- **Check for active status** — ALMM listings expire. An inverter listed six months ago may have had its listing lapse if the manufacturer missed a compliance update. Always check the revision date of the list.
- **Match model numbers exactly** — The model number on the physical inverter, the vendor invoice, and the ALMM listing must all match. Variants (e.g., TLS-3K vs TLS-3KD) are treated as separate models.
- **Panel ALMM equally matters** — The solar panels must also be on the ALMM panel list. Both inverter and panel ALMM compliance are required; one without the other is insufficient.

Qbits on-grid and hybrid inverters across the TLS, TLD, HS, and HT series are ALMM-listed, making every Qbits-based installation fully subsidy-eligible. For homeowners evaluating inverter options specifically for subsidy protection, the [best solar inverter for home India guide](/blog/best-solar-inverter-for-home-india/) covers ALMM status alongside performance benchmarks and warranty comparisons.

> **The ALMM rule is absolute.** There are no appeals or exceptions once an installation is commissioned with a non-listed inverter. The cost of replacing a non-ALMM inverter post-commissioning — to salvage the subsidy claim — typically equals or exceeds the subsidy amount itself. Verify first; install second.

## Common Errors That Reduce or Cancel Your PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Amount

Errors in the application process are more common than DISCOM officers would prefer. The following mistakes are the ones most frequently cited in MNRE's own grievance data and in reporting by [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/).

- **Installing before DISCOM feasibility approval** — The DISCOM must approve your application before physical installation begins. Systems installed before approval cannot be commissioned under the scheme, and the subsidy is denied with no recourse. The portal must show green "feasibility approved" status before your vendor touches the roof.
- **Wrong system size on the application versus actual installation** — If your application states 2 kW and you install 3 kW (or vice versa), the DISCOM commissioning report will not match the portal application. The discrepancy triggers a rejection or extended review. Always amend the application if the system size changes.
- **Vendor empanelment lapse** — DISCOM empanelments are renewed periodically. A vendor who was empanelled when they gave you a quote may have lapsed by the time installation begins. Verify current empanelment on the portal on the day you sign the contract.
- **Bank account mismatch** — The account holder name, account number, and IFSC submitted on the portal must match the bank's records exactly. A single character discrepancy — even an initial vs full name — causes the direct transfer to fail. The correction process adds 30–45 days.
- **Missing NACH mandate** — The portal requires a signed NACH mandate even though the subsidy is a one-time credit rather than a recurring payment. Skipping this form stalls disbursement at the final stage.
- **Uploading illegible documents** — Electricity bills must clearly show the consumer number. Property documents must be legible to the survey or flat number. Portal verification teams return blurry uploads without processing them.

## Net Metering and Your Monthly Saving After Subsidy

The subsidy is a one-time benefit. The ongoing financial return from your PM Surya Ghar installation comes from [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) — the billing mechanism through which your electricity account is credited for every unit your solar system exports to the grid.

A bidirectional smart meter records both the power you import from the grid and the power you export. At each billing cycle, the DISCOM subtracts export units from import units. You pay only for the net difference plus applicable fixed charges. In summer months with long daylight hours, a well-sized system often generates a surplus that carries forward as credit.

The financial arithmetic of net metering is straightforward:

| Scenario | Monthly Generation (3 kW, avg.) | Monthly Consumption | Net Bill Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low consumption (200 units) | 390 units | 200 units | Credit of 190 units carries forward |
| Matched consumption (380 units) | 390 units | 380 units | Near-zero bill (only fixed charges) |
| High consumption (550 units) | 390 units | 550 units | Pay for 160 net units + fixed charges |

Export tariffs in India range from ₹2.50 to ₹5.00 per unit depending on state and DISCOM, set by State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs). Because the export tariff is lower than the retail import tariff (typically ₹6 to ₹10 per unit), self-consuming solar generation is always more valuable than exporting it. Size your system and time high-consumption appliances (dishwasher, washing machine, water heating) to run during the solar generation window.

For a state-by-state breakdown of net metering tariffs, banking rules, and annual settlement processes, refer to the [net metering India complete guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/).

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Amount — Frequently Missed Nuances

Most online content about PM Surya Ghar covers the headline ₹78,000 and stops. The following nuances are the ones that catch homeowners off guard after they have already applied or installed.

### The Subsidy Is Not a Discount — It Is a Post-Installation Reimbursement

Many homeowners assume the ₹78,000 is deducted from the vendor invoice at the time of purchase, similar to a consumer goods rebate. It is not. You pay the vendor the full system cost upfront (or via a loan), install the system, complete commissioning, upload documents to the portal, and then receive the subsidy into your bank account 30 to 90 days later. Budget for the full gross system cost before assuming the subsidy offsets it at the start.

### Loans for PM Surya Ghar Are Available — and Structured Around the Subsidy

MNRE has partnered with SBI, Canara Bank, PNB, and Bank of Baroda to offer concessional home loans for PM Surya Ghar applicants at interest rates of 7% to 9% — below standard personal loan rates. These loans can be structured so the ₹78,000 subsidy repayment is applied directly to the loan principal when it arrives, reducing the effective loan tenure. Homeowners who cannot arrange upfront capital should explore this route before considering the RESCO model, which sacrifices the subsidy benefit.

### Subsidy Is One Per Consumer Number — Not One Per Property

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy is attached to the electricity consumer number, not the property address. A property with two separate electricity connections (for example, a house with one domestic connection and one small commercial connection) can only claim the subsidy on the domestic connection. If a homeowner moves and gets a new electricity connection at a new address, they are eligible for the scheme again — since the new consumer number has no prior subsidy claim.

### The 3 kW Cap Applies to Subsidised Capacity, Not Total Installed Capacity

There is no restriction on the size of system you install under PM Surya Ghar. A homeowner can install a 10 kW system and still claim ₹78,000 in central subsidy — they simply receive the same amount as a 3 kW installer. The restriction is on subsidised capacity, not permitted capacity. Systems above 3 kW benefit fully from net metering, DISCOM commissioning, and all operational scheme protections — they just do not receive additional central subsidy beyond the 3 kW slab.
