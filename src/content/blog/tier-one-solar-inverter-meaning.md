**Tier 1 is not a solar inverter certification.** When the phrase refers to BloombergNEF, it describes a quarterly manufacturer classification based on qualifying financed projects. It does not certify an exact model, prove reliability, replace BIS evidence, or approve an inverter for a tender, scheme, or grid connection.

The words still carry useful information when the publisher, list, quarter, legal manufacturer, and methodology are clear. Without those fields, `Tier 1 inverter` is only an unsupported label.

> **Procurement answer**
> - Identify who issued the Tier 1 claim and which equipment list it covers.
> - Verify the quarter because manufacturer status can change.
> - Keep the ranking at manufacturer level. Do not apply it automatically to every model.
> - Run exact-model compliance, design, quality, warranty, service, and project checks separately.
> - Never convert bankability into a reliability or approval claim.

## First ask which Tier 1 label the bidder means

The phrase has no useful procurement meaning until the bidder names its source. A solar module list, a power inverter list, and a seller's own supplier tier are different records.

Ask for these five fields:

1. **Publisher:** Who owns the classification?
2. **List:** Does it cover power inverters, solar modules, storage systems, or something else?
3. **Period:** Which quarter and year apply?
4. **Entity:** Which legal manufacturer or brand name appears?
5. **Evidence:** Can an authorised subscriber check the status against the publisher's current list rather than a sales slide?

This catches a common substitution. A bidder may show a module maker's Tier 1 status while offering an inverter from the same group. That module record says nothing about the inverter maker or exact inverter model.

BloombergNEF's current [Tier 1 reports page](https://about.bnef.com/tier-1-reports/) separates PV modules, power inverters, and energy storage. Its methodology also says press releases and manufacturer claims do not prove current status. The latest quarter is the relevant one.

## What the BNEF power inverter classification measures

As of 26 September 2026, the current public [BNEF Tier 1 Power Inverter Methodology](https://assets.bnef.com/public/tiering/invertermethodology) is dated 18 March 2026. It defines a finance and project-use screen at manufacturer level.

Under that methodology, a qualifying maker supplies own-brand, own-manufactured inverter products to at least four qualifying projects in the preceding two years. Those projects must have closed non-recourse financing from at least two commercial banks. Relevant solar, storage, or hybrid project financings need at least 5 MW of inverter capacity. If a maker supplies only part of a project, its own share must reach 5 MW. For a portfolio financing, the maker must supply at least 5 MW across the portfolio.

Those thresholds are methodology inputs, not product test results. BNEF reviews the classification quarterly and publishes only a Tier 1 list. It does not publish Tier 2 or Tier 3 inverter lists.

| BNEF input | What an EPC may reasonably infer | What the EPC cannot infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use in qualifying financed projects | The maker has recent acceptance in projects meeting the methodology | Every inverter model was reviewed or accepted |
| Non-recourse finance from commercial banks | Qualifying project finance exists in BNEF's tracked evidence | A bank guarantees the product or supplier |
| Own-brand, own-manufactured products | The company meets BNEF's maker definition | A specific factory, bill of materials, or shipment is unchanged |
| At least 5 MW supplied by the maker for a relevant partial project or portfolio | The maker's contribution meets the published capacity threshold | The offered model was tested, certified, or used in every project |
| Quarterly classification | Status belongs to a defined period | An old badge proves current inclusion |
| Manufacturer-level list | The named maker meets the classification | Reliability, safety, performance, service, or legal compliance |

The India caveat is especially important. The methodology says extra evidence is needed to establish non-recourse financing for projects in India and several other markets. It explains that non-recourse finance is not the norm in those markets, so a bank may not always require full inverter technical due diligence. That makes the label useful for context, but unsafe as a shortcut for product qualification.

## Bankability, certification, and approval answer different questions

Procurement teams often place unlike evidence in one `certification` folder. Keep each evidence class separate.

| Evidence type | The question it answers | Correct scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 classification | Has the manufacturer met the publisher's current classification method? | Named maker, list, and quarter |
| Bankability review | Will a named lender or financing structure accept this risk? | Lender, project, transaction, and date |
| BIS licence or registration under an applicable QCO | Does the covered product follow the stated Indian conformity route? | Exact model or declared series scope in the authority record |
| Certificate or test report | Was the named model or tested family assessed against a stated standard and edition? | Document scope, issuing body, laboratory, date, and limitations |
| Datasheet and manual | What does the manufacturer publish for the offered model? | Exact model, document revision, and selected options |
| Tender or owner acceptance | Does the offered evidence meet this procurement specification? | Bid and project |
| DISCOM or other authority decision | Does the specific installation meet that authority's process? | Site, jurisdiction, design, and date |
| Field reliability evidence | What failure or availability outcome was observed? | Defined population, model mix, period, environment, and failure definition |

For India, use the [BIS versus ALMM stocking checklist](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) for the current national product route and ALMM boundary. A Tier 1 claim cannot replace any evidence that route requires.

The same separation applies to project finance. A lender may use brand acceptance as one screening input, then require an independent engineer to review the model, design, energy assessment, contracts, warranties, insurance, and counterparties. `Bankable` is not a permanent engineering property attached to a logo.

## Do not use Tier 1 as a reliability score

BNEF's inverter methodology is unusually direct on this point. It says purchasers should not use the list as a measure of quality and that the classification does not directly account for product quality or manufacturer financial strength.

An EPC still needs evidence for the risks it will carry after commissioning:

- exact model and hardware revision;
- current datasheet and installation manual;
- voltage, current, MPPT, environmental, derating, and grid-function fit;
- model-scoped conformity and test evidence required by the project;
- manufacturing location, bill-of-material change control, serial traceability, and release records;
- written warranty scope, remedy, exclusions, and cost allocation;
- named service and escalation responsibilities;
- spares, firmware, monitoring access, and lifecycle support; and
- any field dataset, including its population, time window, climate, model mix, and failure definition.

A clean badge cannot fill a blank row in that file. The [solar inverter supplier selection checklist](/blog/inverter-suppliers-india/) covers the organisational and evidence hard gates. The [datasheet-to-BOQ guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) carries exact-model values into the technical submission.

## Verify a Tier 1 claim without copying an online list

Do not build a tender schedule from a screenshot, search snippet, forwarded spreadsheet, or an undated `top Tier 1 brands` article. BNEF states that its current list is client-only and that versions released online may be unauthorised or altered.

Use this review sequence instead:

1. Ask the bidder to name the publisher and the Power Inverter Makers list.
2. Record the claimed quarter and the exact legal manufacturer name.
3. Request evidence that an authorised subscriber can check against the current BNEF list; a timestamped badge may help where applicable.
4. Have an authorised subscriber confirm the current list when the condition is material. BNEF says it cannot confirm Tier 1 status to non-clients.
5. Check whether the offered model is made and branded by the entity being claimed.
6. Record the Tier 1 result as one manufacturer-level row.
7. Complete every exact-model and project row independently.

If verification is unavailable, mark `clarification required`. Do not replace the missing status with an older quarter or a manufacturer announcement.

## Write the tender clause so bidders cannot substitute evidence

A vague clause such as `inverter shall be Tier 1 and certified` combines two undefined conditions. Split it into an eligibility row and separate technical evidence rows.

Use wording like this as a starting point:

> Where Tier 1 manufacturer status is required, the bidder shall state the publisher, exact power inverter list, applicable quarter, and named legal manufacturer. The bidder shall provide evidence that an authorised subscriber can check against the current publisher list. Tier 1 status shall not substitute for the exact-model datasheet, applicable conformity evidence, test reports, warranty and service documents, or project approvals required elsewhere in this specification.

Then define the acceptable quarter, any permitted status-change window, and the consequence if the maker is absent at bid submission or before supply. Procurement and legal teams should adapt the clause to the project. There is no universal Tier 1 tender rule.

## The Qbits evidence boundary

This article does not claim that Qbits appears on a current Tier 1 list. The public BNEF methodology explains the classification, while the current maker list is available to clients. No retained primary record establishes a current Qbits listing, so the claim remains unmade.

For a Qbits bid, start with the offered model rather than a brand label. [Download the current Qbits datasheet](/download-datasheets/), freeze its model and revision, and add the required conformity, test, warranty, service, and project evidence as separate controlled records.
