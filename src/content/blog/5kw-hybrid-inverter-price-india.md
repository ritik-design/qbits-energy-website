## What actually moves a 5 kW hybrid price: feature-level cost drivers

Two 5 kW hybrids with a ₹70,000 gap are not the same machine at different margins. The listings above show five engineering and commercial differences.

**Battery voltage and architecture.** Luminous lists 48 V, Devsol and Nexus list 24 V, and the Solis and Deye units accept low-voltage batteries in the 40 to 60 V range. A 24 V platform needs roughly twice the battery current of a 48 V one for the same power, so cable, fuse and busbar ratings change. High-voltage platforms exist in other product lines; check which type a quote assumes, because batteries from one platform do not work on the other.

**Battery current.** The Deye page lists 135 A maximum charge or discharge current and the Solis page 112 A. At 48 V, 112 A is about 5.4 kW of DC power and 135 A is about 6.5 kW before conversion losses (48 V × 112 A = 5,376 W; 48 V × 135 A = 6,480 W). A unit with a lower battery current may not deliver its full rated output from the battery alone.

**MPPT count.** Both imported-brand listings show 2 MPPT inputs. A second tracker matters on east-west roofs or partial shade and adds hardware cost. If your array faces one direction with no shade, you may not use it.

**Phase.** A three-phase 5 kW hybrid, such as the Qbits QBH-5KS48P3, serves a three-phase connection. The single-phase listings above do not. Match the unit to your sanctioned connection before comparing prices.

**Warranty wording and channel.** The pages print 36 months (Luminous), 4 years (Nexus) and a 2-year tag in the Devsol title, while the Devsol FAQ describes longer terms. A warranty is only as useful as the document behind it, so ask for the written terms for the exact model and who services it locally.

Working with only the page-printed ratings:

- **Deye lithium:** 5.12 kWh × 0.90 = 4.61 kWh usable. ₹1,23,000 ÷ 4.61 = about ₹26,700 per usable kWh. Over 6,000 cycles, ₹1,23,000 ÷ (4.61 × 6,000) = about ₹4.45 per delivered kWh.
- **Luminous tubular, four units:** 4 × ₹17,999 = ₹71,996 for 7.2 kWh nominal. At 80% depth, 5.76 kWh is usable, which is about ₹12,500 per usable kWh. Over 1,500 cycles, ₹71,996 ÷ (5.76 × 1,500) = about ₹8.33 per delivered kWh.

The tubular bank costs less to buy and more per kWh delivered over its rated life, but those cycle ratings come from different test conditions and neither is a measured result. The point is the method. Divide the installed battery cost by usable kWh multiplied by rated cycles, using the datasheet for the exact battery. ## Why two "5 kW" listings may not be comparable

Start with these five checks before comparing the rupee figure.

1. **kW versus kVA.** kW is real power. kVA is apparent power. A 5 kVA name is not enough by itself; check the rated output in kW and power factor.
2. **Single-phase versus three-phase.** The Luminous and Deye listings state single-phase. The QBH-5KS48P3 is three-phase, so check the phase on any listing that does not say.
3. **Grid output versus backup output.** Some documents show different continuous ratings for the grid and backup ports. Use the rating for the load you expect that port to carry.
4. **Battery voltage and protocol.** 24 V and 48 V systems are not interchangeable. A CAN or RS485 socket alone does not prove that a specific battery-management system is approved.
5. **Maximum versus everyday operation.** Maximum PV input, short overload and peak efficiency are limits or test results. They are not continuous output promises.

## Subsidy eligibility: what a 5 kW hybrid changes and what it does not

The MNRE [operational guidelines for the residential component](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) (PDF [here](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3716e1b8c6cd17b771da77391355749f3/uploads/2024/07/202407021768035484.pdf), checked 14 October 2026) say four things that matter for a 5 kW hybrid buyer.

- **CFA is capped at 3 kWp.** The central assistance is ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp and ₹18,000 for the third kWp, with no additional assistance beyond 3 kWp. A 5 kWp system is worth the same ₹78,000 as a 3 kWp one under the standard calculation.
- **It follows module capacity, not inverter size.** The CFA is irrespective of the inverter size and is computed on the rated DC capacity of the modules.
- **Battery storage is allowed but not paid for.** The guidelines name battery storage as an additional component and state that the CFA is calculated on module capacity only.
- **Battery hybrid systems that connect to the grid** are eligible subject to approval by the respective Electricity Regulatory Commission, with DISCOM inspection. Off-grid installations are not eligible.

The benchmark cost in the same document is ₹50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹45,000 for each additional kW, which makes the benchmark for 5 kW (2 × ₹50,000 + 3 × ₹45,000) ₹2,35,000. That is a scheme reference for a grid-connected rooftop system, not a selling price and not a battery-inclusive price. The [PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/) covers state top-ups, and the [glossary entry](/glossary/pm-surya-ghar/) defines the terms.

Worked illustration with a listed kit: the Waaree kit at ₹4,12,999 less the ₹78,000 central assistance leaves ₹3,34,999 before any state top-up, which this guide does not estimate. Treat it as arithmetic. Approval, DCR compliance, registered-vendor requirements and the final amount are decided through the portal and your DISCOM, and cannot be promised from a listing.

On GST, the rate for the specified renewable energy devices is 5% from 22 September 2025, with some components and standalone supplies treated differently. The [GST guide](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) cites the notification and explains how to read an invoice. Ask for the HSN code and rate line by line.

## Is a 5 kW hybrid right for a 3BHK? A checklist, not a verdict

Room count does not size an inverter. Work through this list with your own figures.

1. **List the loads that must stay on.** Write the running watts from each appliance's nameplate. For example, if the fans, lights, fridge and router total 450 W, that is the baseline.
2. **Add the starting surge of the largest motor load.** A compressor or pump can draw several times its running power for a moment. Compare the total with the inverter's surge rating and how long it holds.
3. **Check continuous battery discharge.** The inverter cannot deliver more than the battery bank can supply. Use the battery current rating discussed above, not the nameplate kW.
4. **Pick the outage hours.** Multiply the backup load by the hours you want to cover, then convert to nominal battery size as below.
5. **Check phase.** If your connection is three-phase, a single-phase unit does not balance it.
6. **Check whether one air conditioner or two.** Two running together on backup may exceed a single 5 kW unit's continuous rating.

Planning example (assumed round figures, not a product claim): a steady 500 W essential load for four hours is 0.5 kW × 4 h = 2 kWh of AC energy. With an assumed 90% conversion efficiency and 80% usable battery capacity, the nominal battery is 2 kWh ÷ (0.90 × 0.80) = 2.78 kWh. That result still needs checks for starting current, temperature, reserve and ageing. The [battery sizing guide](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/) walks the same method load by load, and the [best 5 kW inverter guide](/blog/best-5kw-solar-inverter-india-2026/) compares the 5 kW fit across on-grid and hybrid options.

## Buy hybrid now and battery later, or on-grid now?

The decision comes down to the premium and how likely backup is. The same-brand Deye gap above (₹37,999 on-grid versus ₹1,35,888 hybrid) shows the premium can be large, so paying it for a battery you might never add is not free. On the other hand, a unit with an empty battery port works like a grid-tied unit and can take a battery later without replacing the inverter, provided the battery protocol and voltage match. The [add a battery later guide](/blog/add-battery-to-solar-system-later/) lists what to specify now so the retrofit stays simple. Ask each supplier for both options in writing.
