Solar Ladder is one of the few solar software products built in India, for Indian solar businesses, rather than adapted from something written for a US installer. That matters more than most feature lists admit. This review looks at what the platform actually is, what it costs (and what it does not disclose), where it earns its place in an EPC's stack, and where a buyer should stop expecting it to help. The single most important thing to establish up front is category, because most of the confusion around Solar Ladder comes from people evaluating it against the wrong competitors and then judging it unfairly in both directions.

> **TL;DR**
> - Solar Ladder is an India-focused solar business operations and CRM platform, not primarily an engineering design tool.
> - Its own site publishes 600+ companies, 70,000+ projects, and 500+ MW managed, along with 85,000+ proposals created.
> - Pricing is not published on the website at the time of writing, so budget from a direct quote rather than a third-party figure.
> - Genuine strengths: Indian EPC workflow, project and site tracking, PM Surya Ghar and net metering paperwork, cost tracking, lead pipeline.
> - It is not the tool for bankable simulation, 8,760-hour shading, IS code clearance, or CAD handoff, and it does not claim to be.
> - Many Indian EPCs should run it alongside a design platform such as SurgePV at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year, joined by API.

**Short version.** Solar Ladder is an India-built operations and CRM platform for solar installers, covering leads, proposals, project tracking, site surveys, costing, and operations and maintenance. It is strong where Indian EPCs actually bleed time, which is paperwork and coordination. It is not a substitute for a dedicated design and simulation platform, and buying it expecting one is the most common category error we see.

## What Solar Ladder Actually Is

Solar Ladder describes itself as the operating system for solar. That phrase is doing real work, and it is accurate about the ambition. The published module list covers projects, proposals, design, tasks, project management, books, operations and maintenance, site surveys and forms, a customer dashboard, and integrations with tools including Google Calendar, Google Forms, Zoho, and IndiaMART.

Read that list again and note what dominates it. Seven of the ten modules are about running a business. One is about design. The product's shape tells you where the engineering investment went.

The company is headquartered in Mumbai and its published traction figures, taken from its own website, are 600+ companies using the platform, 70,000+ projects managed, 500+ MW of project capacity, and 85,000+ proposals created. Those are vendor-stated numbers and we present them as such, but they are consistent with a product that has real adoption in the Indian rooftop market rather than a launch-stage tool.

The buyer it is built for is an Indian EPC running residential and small commercial rooftop work at volume, where the constraint is not "can we design this" but "can we get forty concurrent jobs through survey, DISCOM approval, install, and subsidy claim without losing three of them."

## Solar Ladder Pricing in 2026

Solar Ladder does not publish a price list on its website at the time of writing. There is no public pricing page with tiers and per-seat numbers of the kind Aurora, HelioScope, or SurgePV expose.

We are not going to invent one. Any specific rupee or dollar figure you find quoted for Solar Ladder in a comparison article should be treated as unverified until the vendor confirms it for your configuration.

What that means practically is that the evaluation burden shifts to you. When you request a quote, ask these things explicitly:

- Is billing per user, per company, per project, or tied to installed capacity?
- What happens when you add a seasonal salesperson or a site surveyor who logs in twice a week?
- Which modules are included in the base and which are separate?
- Is there a minimum contract term, and what is the renewal escalation?
- Is the procurement or financing layer bundled, priced separately, or commission-based?

That last one matters. Solar Ladder has been described in coverage of the company as operating a procurement layer for buying modules and materials and a financing layer offering working capital to installers and loans to end customers. Those are adjacent businesses to the software. Understand whether they are optional or entangled with your software commercials before you sign.

Unpublished pricing is not a scandal. Plenty of India B2B SaaS sells this way because deal sizes vary hugely between a three-person installer and a 50 MW-a-year EPC. But it does mean you cannot benchmark it from your desk, and you should not pretend otherwise in a board deck.

## Where Solar Ladder Is Genuinely Good

These are real strengths. Credit is owed and we are not going to hedge it.

**It was designed around Indian EPC workflow, not translated into it.** This is the strongest thing about the product and it is hard to replicate. A US-first platform assumes a permit office, an AHJ inspection, and a utility interconnection queue. An Indian rooftop job assumes a DISCOM feasibility approval, a meter testing appointment, a state nodal agency, and a subsidy claim that pays after commissioning. Those are different shapes. Solar Ladder is built to the second shape.

**Project and site tracking that matches how the work happens.** Custom site survey forms that a surveyor fills on a phone at the site, feeding straight into the project record, remove a genuinely stupid loss of time. Most Indian EPCs still run this as a WhatsApp photo dump followed by a re-typing exercise. Removing that is worth money every single week.

**Subsidy and documentation workflow.** Solar Ladder markets support for the PM Surya Ghar path, including helping customers through net metering applications and uploading completion reports that trigger the DBT subsidy transfer, and it publishes practitioner guidance on registering as a PM Surya Ghar vendor. Paperwork throughput is the actual bottleneck for high-volume residential EPCs in India. A tool that treats it as a first-class workflow rather than a notes field is solving the right problem. If subsidy process is your pain point, our guides on [PM Surya Ghar vendor empanelment](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) and the [documents required](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-documents-required/) cover what the platform is automating.

**Lead and pipeline management built for solar, not generic CRM.** A generic CRM makes you model a solar job as an "opportunity" with custom fields you invent yourself. A solar CRM already knows a job has a kW size, a roof, a survey, an approval, and a subsidy stage. The IndiaMART integration is a small detail that tells you the team understands where Indian solar leads actually come from.

**Costing visibility.** The Books module targets job-level cost tracking. Margin leakage on Indian rooftop work is rarely a pricing problem, it is a "we do not know what job 47 cost" problem. Bringing cost against project in the same system that holds the project is the right structural answer.

**Regional language output.** Producing customer-facing documents in Hindi, Tamil, or Marathi is not a gimmick in semi-urban India. It is the difference between a homeowner reading the proposal and a homeowner asking their nephew about it.

## Where Solar Ladder Stops

Now the limits. The fair way to frame these is not as failures, because Solar Ladder never positioned itself as a simulation engine. It is a question of which layer of the stack it occupies.

**Engineering depth.** A design module that lays out modules and shows a shadow at a time of day is a sales-support design tool. It is not the same instrument as a platform doing 8,760-hour module-level irradiance and loss modelling. Both are legitimate. They answer different questions and they are trusted by different audiences.

**Bankable output.** If a lender, a C&I customer's technical consultant, or an investment committee is reviewing your numbers, they will want P50, P75, and P90 yield figures with a documented loss chain. That is the standard the [PV yield simulation](/blog/pv-yield-simulation-software/) category exists to meet. An operations platform does not produce it and should not be blamed for that.

**Detailed shading analysis.** Indian rooftops are unusually hostile. Water tanks, staircase mumties, parapets, and neighbour buildings on three sides are normal. Once shading materially drives the yield answer, you need the analysis depth described in our [solar shading analysis software](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software/) guide, not a visual sun path.

**Engineering deliverables.** Single line diagrams, IS code clearance checks, structural and BOQ documentation, and DXF or DWG export for a consultant are all outside the operations layer.

**Segment ceiling.** As an EPC moves from 5 kW residential into 500 kW and 2 MW C&I, the technical scrutiny per project rises sharply while the number of projects falls. The value of an operations platform is highest at high job count. The value of an engineering platform is highest at high scrutiny. Most growing Indian EPCs cross that line and need both.

**India focus cuts both ways.** The depth that makes Solar Ladder good in India is not portable. If you start quoting work in the Gulf, Africa, or Southeast Asia, the compliance and tariff assumptions stop helping you.

## Who Should Buy Solar Ladder

Buy it if most of these describe you.

- You are an Indian EPC doing residential or small commercial rooftop at meaningful volume, say 15 or more jobs a month.
- Your projects live across WhatsApp groups, a spreadsheet, and one person's memory.
- Subsidy claims and DISCOM follow-ups slip, and slipped claims are costing you cash flow.
- Site surveys arrive as photos that someone re-types into a quote.
- You cannot state the true delivered cost of a completed job without an evening of reconciliation.
- Your design requirement is a clean, credible homeowner-facing proposal rather than a lender-facing yield study.

At that profile, the platform is aimed directly at your problem, and the India-native design of the workflow will save you more time than a more famous international tool would.

## Who Should Not Buy Solar Ladder

- **Engineering-led teams.** If your work is reviewed by consultants and lenders, an operations platform does not address the scrutiny you are under.
- **C&I and utility-heavy pipelines.** Fewer, larger, more technically interrogated projects invert the value equation.
- **Anyone who needs bankable yield reports as a standard deliverable.** That is a different product category entirely.
- **Teams selling outside India.** The India-specific depth becomes dead weight.
- **Teams whose only real problem is design speed.** Buying a business operating system to solve a design bottleneck is expensive and slow.

## Verdict

Solar Ladder is a good product doing an underserved job. Indian solar EPCs have been badly served by software for years, mostly because the international platforms treat India as an export market and the generic CRMs treat solar as just another pipeline. Solar Ladder is built for the actual shape of an Indian rooftop business, and the parts of the workflow it targets, subsidy paperwork, site surveys, project coordination, job costing, are exactly the parts that quietly destroy margin.

The two honest caveats are category and price transparency. On category, it occupies the operations layer, and a buyer who walks in expecting engineering-grade simulation will be disappointed by a product that never promised it. On price, the absence of a published figure means you cannot benchmark it before a sales conversation, which slows evaluation and makes comparison articles unreliable.

Our recommendation for a growing Indian EPC is to stop treating this as a single-vendor decision. Run a design and proposal platform for the technical and customer-facing half, and an operations platform for everything after the signature. If your engineering layer is the weak one, SurgePV at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year covers it with India-specific compliance included. If your operations layer is the weak one, Solar Ladder deserves a serious look and a real pilot.

Three things to do next:

- Decide, honestly, whether coordination or engineering is costing you more this quarter, and buy for that layer first.
- Request a Solar Ladder quote with your real headcount and ask directly how procurement and financing are commercially tied to the software.
- Run five real jobs, including one messy one, through any shortlisted tool before signing anything annual.
