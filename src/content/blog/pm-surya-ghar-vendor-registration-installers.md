As of **26 September 2026**, a solar installer can pursue PM Surya Ghar vendor registration at state level or national level. The right route depends on where the business can install and maintain residential rooftop systems, not on which application looks faster.

MNRE's current process calls this vendor registration. Earlier documents also use the terms registration and empanelment.

The [Ministry of New and Renewable Energy's vendor-registration notice](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/process-of-vendor-registration-for-pm-surya-ghar-scheme-on-the-national-portal/) names REC Limited as the national registering authority. It also continues the earlier state-registration process through the relevant state authority. Before arranging a performance bank guarantee, or PBG, verify the current instructions with that authority. A dated PDF should not be treated as a bank instruction.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS] Short version
> - Choose the operating geography first. MNRE documents state a Rs 2.5 lakh PBG for state registration and Rs 25 lakh for national registration.
> - Align the firm's legal name across GST, PAN, the declaration, the PBG, and the portal.
> - Prepare evidence of technical and service capacity. Submit only through the current official route.
> - Registration creates continuing installation, documentation, maintenance, and complaint-handling duties.
> - It does not guarantee leads, subsidy release, project approval, or a Qbits partnership.

> **Homeowner checking a vendor, not registering one?** Use the [homeowner guide to finding and verifying a PM Surya Ghar vendor](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/). This page is for installers.

## State or national registration: choose before preparing documents

**State registration suits an installer that can serve and maintain projects within one state. National registration suits a business with genuine multi-state delivery and service capacity.** The wider badge carries a much larger PBG requirement in the MNRE process document, so it should not be chosen for appearance alone.

MNRE documents checked on 26 September 2026 state the following:

Table: State registration compared with national registration for PM Surya Ghar vendors, per MNRE documents checked 26 September 2026

| Decision point | State registration | National registration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registering authority | Designated state DISCOM or state agency | REC Limited |
| Operating scope | The relevant state, with districts identified in the declaration or current process | Any district in any state, while the vendor may declare its active states and districts |
| PBG stated in the referenced documents | One Rs 2.5 lakh PBG for the entire state | Rs 25 lakh PBG |
| PBG validity stated | At least five years | At least five years from registration or renewal |
| Registration term stated | Initially one year, then extended year by year when the PBG condition is met | Five years, renewable for another five years when the PBG condition is met |
| Practical fit | One-state installation and service operation | Multi-state operation with matching delivery and maintenance capacity |

These figures come from MNRE's 2022, 2023, and 7 May 2024 memoranda referenced in the current notice. They can be amended. Confirm the amount, beneficiary, prescribed wording, validity, claim period, issuance method, and submission route before asking a bank to issue anything.

Do not assume an old Phase II certificate needs a fresh application without checking. The 7 May 2024 memorandum deemed earlier Grid Connected Rooftop Phase II vendors registered for PM Surya Ghar. It also carried their existing documents, including bank guarantees, into the new scheme process. The registering authority must confirm how that transition applies to the firm's current record.

## The registration workflow an installer can safely follow

Portal labels and field sequences can change. That makes the safest order **authority first, paperwork second, PBG last**. This guide therefore does not reproduce a button-by-button screen flow or promise a processing time.

<!-- steps -->
1. **Define the service area.** List only the states and districts where the firm can survey, install, commission, maintain, and attend complaints.
2. **Identify the registering authority.** For a state route, confirm the designated DISCOM or state agency. For national registration, use the current REC and National Portal instructions linked from the MNRE notice.
3. **Reconcile the legal identity.** Use the same firm name, entity status, GSTIN, PAN, authorised signatory, and address across every current record. Resolve spelling or entity-name differences before submission.
4. **Read the current application and declaration.** Compare them with the referenced MNRE declaration, but treat the live authority's format as controlling.
5. **Prepare evidence behind each declaration.** A tick box is not enough if the authority later asks for proof of staff, electrical-safety capability, service arrangements, or statutory records.
6. **Verify the PBG instructions.** Confirm the beneficiary, amount, prescribed text, validity, claim conditions, issue format, delivery address, and renewal process directly with the authority.
7. **Submit through the official route.** Retain the acknowledgement, submitted version, attachments, correspondence, and PBG record under one application reference.
8. **Answer queries against the submitted record.** Do not create a second version of the firm's name, districts, staff count, or service contact merely to satisfy a follow-up.
9. **Confirm the live registration.** Check the legal name, registration status, operating area, public contact, and profile details after approval. A successful login alone is not proof of active scope.

> [!CAUTION] Use only the official route
> Start from the [official PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) rather than a saved login page, message link, or third-party form. Never share an OTP or portal password with someone offering paid registration assistance.

## Vendor login: first sign-in and what the dashboard does

The login is the second stage, after the firm has a registration record. MNRE's **Vendor Manual dated 26 June 2024** (hosted by a state DISCOM) describes it as follows.

Table: Vendor login: first sign-in stages as described in the MNRE Vendor Manual of 26 June 2024

| Stage | What the manual describes |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Open the route | Go to Vendor Login on pmsuryaghar.gov.in and choose Update Vendor Credentials |
| 2. Verify PAN | Enter the firm's registered PAN number to verify the email linked to it |
| 3. Verify email | Enter an email address, request an OTP and confirm it. The verified address becomes the registered email for login and later communication |
| 4. Set a password | Use at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one number and one special character |
| 5. Sign in | Enter the registered email and password on the Vendor Login page |

After sign-in, the manual shows the vendor dashboard doing these jobs:

- seeing the list of consumers who have shown interest in the vendor;
- updating the firm profile, the operational districts and the product rates;
- viewing the applications mapped to the vendor for installation and tracking project status;
- uploading the signed consumer agreement; and
- submitting installation details such as latitude and longitude, inverter capacity, quantity, manufacturer and serial numbers, and module capacity, quantity, manufacturer and serial numbers.

Two practical points follow. The PAN used to create credentials must be the PAN the firm registered with, so a PAN or entity-name mismatch is the first thing to fix if verification fails. And the serial numbers you enter at installation should match the equipment schedule in the consumer contract, because the DISCOM inspects against the portal record.

For national and multi-state firms, the same manual describes registering on the portal by entering company type, PAN with an uploaded copy, organisation details and documents, then choosing a registration type: national, at ₹25 lakh, or multi-state, at ₹2.5 lakh per state up to ₹25 lakh. The firm then submits the bank guarantee in the format provided to REC Limited and receives empanelment on the National Portal. The manual dates from 2024 and the portal has been updated since, so follow the live labels.

Login is not proof of live registration. A firm can hold credentials and still be deactivated or limited to certain districts, so confirm the public record after approval.

## Documents and details to have ready

The June 2022 and May 2023 state-registration memoranda and the 2024 national process point to the same core set. Which items are uploaded, signed or posted in original depends on the registering authority, so use its checklist as the final word.

Table: Documents and details to have ready for vendor registration, with the common mismatch to avoid

| Item | Why it is needed | Common mismatch to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAN and company type | Identity step of national registration and login credential creation | Entity name differs from GST or bank records |
| GST registration | Declaration asks for the firm's GSTIN | Trade name used instead of legal name |
| Provident Fund number, where applicable | Listed in the state declaration | Left blank when staff are on payroll |
| Evidence of at least three trained technical staff | Declaration item on trained manpower | Training records missing or expired |
| Electrical-safety and statutory compliance proof | Declaration item on statutory requirements | Licence held by a person who has left the firm |
| Organisation details, email, mobile and address | Registration form and public profile | Different addresses across records |
| Performance bank guarantee in the authority's format | ₹2.5 lakh per state or ₹25 lakh national in MNRE documents | Wrong beneficiary, validity or wording |
| Authorised signatory details | Signs the declaration | Signatory authority not documented |
| Districts willing to work in | Declaration and consumer search list | Claiming districts the firm cannot service |

## What the referenced state declaration asks a firm to confirm

The referenced declaration is more than a list of identity documents. It tests whether the installer has technical, safety, maintenance, and complaint-handling capacity.

The 2022 declaration attached to MNRE's current process notice asks for or confirms:

Table: What the referenced 2022 state declaration asks a firm to confirm

| Declaration area | Preparation check for the installer |
| --- | --- |
| Firm identity | Legal name and legal status such as company, proprietorship, partnership, or LLP |
| Tax identity | GSTIN and PAN |
| Employment record | Provident Fund number, where applicable |
| Technical team | At least three people trained for rooftop solar installation work |
| Statutory capability | Ability to meet electrical-safety and other applicable legal requirements |
| Technical compliance | Commitment to install to the applicable MNRE specifications and standards |
| Maintenance | Commitment to comprehensive maintenance for at least five years |
| Consumer handover | Information on the installation plus operating and safety guidance |
| Complaint handling | Named contact, phone number, and email for complaints |
| Operating area | Urban or rural districts where the firm is willing to work |
| Accountability | Authorised signature and acceptance of consequences for misinformation or service failures |

This table describes the referenced declaration. It does not claim that every item is a separate upload on today's portal. Use the current application and authority checklist to determine what must be entered, uploaded, signed, notarised, or delivered in original form.

Build a controlled application folder before submitting. Keep the final declaration, supporting evidence, PBG correspondence, authority queries, registration confirmation, renewal dates, and named owner for each obligation together. If a staff member, complaint contact, district, or legal record changes, check whether the portal or authority requires an update.

## Check the PBG before the bank issues it

**Most PBG mistakes are expensive because a nearly correct guarantee can still be rejected.** The amount is only one field. The issuing bank must work from the authority's current accepted wording and instructions.

Use this pre-issuance check with the registering authority and bank:

> [!CHECKLIST] PBG pre-issuance check
> - exact beneficiary name and address;
> - applicant's exact legal name and constitution;
> - required amount and currency wording;
> - prescribed guarantee text and reference number;
> - issue date, validity date, and any separate claim period;
> - stamp, signature, electronic messaging, or confirmation requirements;
> - original-document delivery or portal-upload requirement;
> - amendment process if the authority finds an error;
> - renewal trigger and internal reminder owner; and
> - release, return, or cancellation evidence at the end of the obligation.

> [!WARNING] A nearly correct PBG can still be rejected
> Do not copy a PBG from another state, another firm, or an old application. Do not describe the guarantee as a registration fee. The cited MNRE documents describe it as a performance bank guarantee connected to the vendor's continuing obligations.

The older state process also allows action including blacklisting and PBG forfeiture for misleading information or failure to meet declared conditions. The [PM Surya Ghar residential CFA operational guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) separately allow deregistration or penalties for non-compliant systems after notice. Treat every declaration as an operating commitment, not formality.

## Registration is the start of the operating obligation

Approval puts the installer inside an accountable consumer-delivery process. It does not end the compliance work.

MNRE's residential CFA guidelines state that a registered vendor profile can include system specifications, prices, consumer contacts, feedback, ratings, and performance information. The same guidelines place several continuing duties on the vendor:

- survey the site and guide the consumer on technically and financially appropriate rooftop capacity;
- design, supply, install, test, and commission the system within the applicable scheme and utility rules;
- conduct safety checks and explain operation, maintenance, and safety to the consumer;
- assist with required approvals, portal documentation, inspection, metering, and grid-connectivity steps;
- provide comprehensive maintenance for five years from commissioning under the scheme requirements;
- repair or replace non-performing components as required during that maintenance period;
- provide the consumer with the relevant original-equipment-manufacturer warranties;
- rectify system-quality or component issues that prevent commissioning or CFA disbursal; and
- receive complaints and maintain enough records to resolve or escalate them.

Responsibilities should be mapped before accepting a project. A simple control sheet can prevent hand-off failures:

Table: Control sheet mapping each project stage to its record and a named owner

| Stage | Record to control | Named owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enquiry | Consumer request, service area, proposed capacity, site constraints | Sales or survey lead |
| Survey and design | Site notes, shadow review, drawings, equipment schedule, assumptions | Design authority |
| Contract | Scope, price, exclusions, milestones, scheme responsibilities | commercial team |
| Installation | Serial numbers, photographs, test records, safety checks | Site supervisor |
| Commissioning | Inspection record, corrections, metering and portal documents | Commissioning lead |
| Handover | Manuals, warranties, contacts, maintenance guidance | Customer handover owner |
| Maintenance | Complaint log, visit notes, diagnosis, parts, closure evidence | Service owner |

The most dangerous gap is usually ownership, not a missing spreadsheet. If no one owns a portal correction, a consumer update, or a service escalation, the registration badge will not protect the firm from a complaint.

## What vendor registration does not guarantee

**Registration establishes entry into the scheme process, subject to current rules. It does not promise commercial success or approval of every project.** Keep these decisions separate:

Table: What PM Surya Ghar vendor registration does not guarantee, and why

| Registration does not guarantee | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer leads | A profile can make the firm discoverable, but it does not promise enquiry volume or conversion |
| Subsidy release | The consumer, system, documents, inspection, bank validation, and authority decisions must still meet current requirements |
| Grid permission | State regulations, DISCOM processes, inspection, metering, and project facts still apply |
| Profit or margin | Project price, equipment, labour, travel, corrections, maintenance, tax, finance, and collection determine the result |
| Equipment approval | Each proposed model must meet the applicable current technical and scheme requirements |
| Qbits partner approval | Qbits runs a separate manufacturer partner-review process |

Do not market registration as a government endorsement of workmanship or a guarantee that the consumer will receive CFA. Do not promise a disbursal date controlled by the portal, DISCOM, bank, or scheme authority.

## Government registration and Qbits partnership are separate

**PM Surya Ghar registration authorises scheme participation within its approved scope. A Qbits partnership concerns a commercial relationship with an inverter manufacturer. Neither substitutes for the other.**

The current Qbits partner form lets an applicant choose EPC or System Integrator. It asks for company, contact, and GST details. EPC applicants can provide their PM Suryaghar registered name. The form also asks about current inverter brands, technical and service teams, and previous solar-inverter experience.

That field helps Qbits understand the applicant. It does not register the applicant with MNRE, REC, a state agency, or a DISCOM. Qbits does not guarantee government approval, consumer leads, territory, margin, credit, or acceptance into its partner programme.

## Final application review

Before submitting, confirm that:

> [!CHECKLIST] Final application review
> - the chosen registration level matches the service operation you can sustain;
> - the legal name is consistent across GST, PAN, declaration, portal, PBG, and bank records;
> - every declaration can be supported by current evidence;
> - the service districts are realistic for five-year maintenance and complaint work;
> - the current authority has confirmed the PBG format and submission route;
> - the business has named owners for design, installation, commissioning, portal records, and service;
> - no marketing material promises subsidy, approval, leads, or income; and
> - the government registration and manufacturer-partner applications remain separate records.
