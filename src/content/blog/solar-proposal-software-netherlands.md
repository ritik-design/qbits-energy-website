Every Dutch solar proposal written between now and 1 January 2027 has a shelf life problem. The salderingsregeling ends on that date, confirmed when the Senate approved the termination in December 2024. A system quoted today will spend a few months under [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) and roughly twenty-five years without it. Yet the standard Dutch quote still leads with a payback figure calculated under netting, which describes the shortest and least representative period of the customer's ownership. This guide compares the platforms Dutch installers should be using to quote honestly through the transition, and explains why the battery line moves from optional extra to the centre of the document.

## What A Dutch Proposal Has To Show From Now On

**Two scenarios, not one.** A quote issued in 2026 covers a system that operates under two different regimes. Showing only the netting figure is not conservative or optimistic, it is simply describing the wrong period. Showing only the post-2027 figure understates the first months. Both belong in the document, with the post-2027 case as the headline because it governs the asset's life.

**Self-consumption and export as separate lines.** Under netting these were the same thing, which is why Dutch quotes historically collapsed them into a net annual figure. From 2027 they are different businesses at different prices. A customer who cannot see the split cannot understand why load shifting or a battery would help.

**Terugleverkosten stated explicitly.** Several suppliers now charge customers for exporting. Where that applies, export carries a net cost rather than a net income, and a proposal listing export as revenue will not match the first bill.

**A battery case with real sizing.** Not a bolt-on line item, but a modelled option showing how much surplus the battery actually captures and what that is worth. Our [solar plus storage design software](/blog/solar-plus-storage-design-software/) comparison covers the dispatch modelling this requires.

## The Payback Conversation Dutch Installers Are About To Have

> **What most Dutch quotes will get wrong through 2026.** They will present a seven year payback because that is what the salderingsregeling delivers, and the customer will discover in January 2027 that the number no longer holds. The installer will not have lied. They will have quoted the current rules. The customer will not care about that distinction.

The commercially interesting point is that honesty here is an advantage rather than a cost. A homeowner comparing three quotes, two showing seven year paybacks and one showing a twelve year payback with a clear explanation and a battery option, is being given something the other two did not provide. In a market where the policy change is widely reported and half a million social housing tenants are already being told they will lose money, credibility is worth more than an attractive number.

There is also a practical sales consequence. The post-2027 economics make batteries, heat pump scheduling, and EV charge timing genuinely valuable rather than marginal. Installers who can model those are selling a larger, higher-margin system on a defensible basis. Installers who cannot are selling panels into a market where panels alone stopped being the answer.
