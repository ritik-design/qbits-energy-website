## What is conduit

Conduit is the protective tubing through which electrical cables are routed in buildings, plants and outdoor installations. It shields cables from mechanical damage, UV exposure, water, dust, rodents and fire. For solar PV installations, conduit serves dual purposes: physical protection of the cable run and code compliance with IS 732 wiring code and CEA Measures Relating to Safety.

> **TL;DR**
> - Conduit is protective tubing that routes and shields [DC](/glossary/dc-cable/) and [AC](/glossary/ac-cable/) solar cables from UV, mechanical damage, rodents and water.
> - Indian solar uses rigid PVC for most runs, flexible PVC for short equipment connections, GI in industrial settings, and HDPE underground.
> - Common sizes run 20 to 25 mm for residential, 32 to 50 mm for C&I, and 50 to 100 mm for utility DC trunk runs.
> - Cable fill ratio should stay under roughly 40 percent so cables can dissipate heat properly.
> - Outdoor conduit and cable glands need IP65 minimum; GI conduit must be electrically bonded to system earth.
> - PVC softens above 60°C, so higher-temperature variants are needed for hot inverter exit points.

Indian solar installations use rigid PVC conduit for most cable runs, flexible PVC for short connections to equipment, GI (galvanised iron) for industrial environments and HDPE for buried installations. Selection depends on environment, cable types and mechanical requirements.

## Why conduit matters

Cable protection. Prevents physical damage and rodent attack.

UV shielding. Protects standard cables from sunlight degradation.

Code compliance. Required by IS 732 for certain installation methods.

Aesthetic. Tidy installation without exposed cables.

Future flexibility. Cables can be drawn out and replaced.

Fire safety. Some conduit types provide fire containment.

### Fill ratio

| Number of cables | Maximum fill ratio |
|---|---|
| 1 | 53% |
| 2 | 31% |
| 3+ | 40% |

Beyond fill ratio causes cable heating and reduces ampacity.

### IP rating for outdoor

| Component | Required IP |
|---|---|
| Conduit + glands | IP65 |
| [Junction box](/glossary/junction-box/) | IP65 |
| [Combiner box](/glossary/combiner-box/) | IP65 or IP66 |

### Bonding

GI conduit must be electrically bonded to system earth at multiple points. Continuity provides earth path during fault.

PVC conduit is non-conductive; cables inside use separate earth conductor.

## Common conduit problems

UV degradation in non-UV-rated PVC.

Heat damage near hot inverters.

Water ingress through inadequate seals.

Rodent damage to thin-walled conduit.

Sharp internal edges damaging cable insulation.

Inadequate bend radius causing cable stress.

## Best practices

Use UV-stabilised PVC for outdoor runs.

GI in fire-sensitive or industrial environments.

Maintain fill ratio under 40 percent.

Use cable glands rated IP65 at all entries.

Apply sealant at junction box entries.

Bond GI conduit to [earth](/glossary/earthing/).

Document conduit routes on the [single line diagram](/glossary/single-line-diagram/).

Use proper deburring tools on cut ends.

## Indian market context

Major Indian conduit brands: Polycab, Anchor, Schneider, Anchor Roma, Havells, Finolex, Honeywell Press-on.

ALMM does not list conduits. BIS certification under IS 9537 (PVC) and related standards.

PM Surya Ghar residential installations use 20 to 32 mm UV-rated PVC conduit.

C&I rooftops use combination of rigid PVC for cable runs and flexible PVC for equipment connections — routing decisions that EPCs typically evaluate during [solar EPC bid evaluation](/blog/how-to-evaluate-solar-epc-bids/).

Utility plants use HDPE for buried runs and GI or PVC overhead, often specified as part of turnkey solar EPC installation scope.

## Common mistakes

Using non-UV-rated PVC outdoors.

Exceeding fill ratio.

Inadequate IP-rated cable glands at entries.

Skipping conduit on long DC runs through hot sun.

Wrong conduit material for environment.

Sharp internal edges from poor cutting.

Failing to bond GI conduit.

## Key takeaways

Conduit protects and routes electrical cables in solar installations. Indian standards (IS 9537, IS 14930, IS 732) govern PVC, flexible and GI conduit selection. Rigid PVC dominates rooftop installations; HDPE underground; GI in industrial. Maintaining fill ratio, IP-rated cable glands and proper bonding ensures 25-year cable protection. Documentation on SLD enables future maintenance.
