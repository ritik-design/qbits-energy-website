Component A provides ₹6,600 crore of central financial assistance to the residential sector for 4,000 MW of capacity. Search for the MNRE rooftop solar scheme and you get a subsidy calculator. That answers the wrong question. MNRE does not inspect your roof and does not clear your file. It writes the rules. Other bodies execute them, and the one that decides whether your claim moves is your DISCOM.

This guide covers the institution, not the application form. It maps Rooftop Solar Programme Phase II and its two funding components. It shows how the residential arm folded into PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana in February 2024. It sets out who does what across MNRE, REC Limited, SECI, state nodal agencies, DISCOMs, and vendors. Then CFA routing, consumer categories, and the component rules that govern hardware. Last comes the part most pages skip: where the programme sits against target, and why approvals stall.

For the consumer journey, the steps and documents, use the [PM Surya Ghar guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/). Rules vary by state and DISCOM, so verify every figure here yourself.

> **TL;DR**
> - The Union Cabinet fixed the 40 GW rooftop target on 17 June 2015 (PIB). Rooftop reached 32.59 GW as on 31.08.2026 (MNRE).
> - CCEA approved Phase II on 19 February 2019 with ₹11,814 crore: ₹6,600 crore of residential CFA for 4,000 MW, ₹4,985 crore of DISCOM incentives capped at 18,000 MW (PIB, 2019).
> - PM Surya Ghar launched 13 February 2024, Cabinet approved 29 February 2024, outlay ₹75,021 crore for 1 crore households (PIB, 2024). Phase II "stands subsumed" within it (MNRE, 2024).
> - REC Limited is the National Programme Implementing Agency, DISCOMs are the State Implementation Agencies, and CFA is processed 15 days after DISCOM approval (MNRE, 7 June 2024).
> - ALMM covers modules, cells, and from 2028 ingots and wafers, with no inverter list. MNRE states ALMM orders do not relax domestic content rules (MNRE, 4 August 2026).
> - Net metering up to 500 kW comes from the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Amendment Rules, 2021, but the arrangement follows your state commission.

**Short version.** The MNRE rooftop solar scheme is the Grid Connected Rooftop Solar Programme, whose residential arm now runs as PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana. MNRE sets the rules and the central financial assistance slabs. REC Ltd implements nationally. Your DISCOM sanctions, inspects, meters, and verifies the claim. A vendor registered on the National Portal must do the installation.

## The 40 GW objective, and how Phase II splits the money

The programme is a capacity target plus a funding mechanism, not a product scheme. MNRE states the objective as "a cumulative installed capacity of 40,000 MW from Grid Connected Rooftop Solar (RTS) projects". The Union Cabinet fixed that on 17 June 2015, raising the national solar goal to 100,000 MW by 2022. The split was 40 GW rooftop and 60 GW large and medium projects, according to PIB (2015).

No official notification declares the 2022 date missed. MNRE's own data puts grid connected rooftop at 32.59 GW of 168.04 GW total solar as on 31.08.2026. The ministry's page has dropped the year while keeping the 40,000 MW figure. India's Panchamrit pledges at COP26 on 1 November 2021 then moved the wider goal to 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030 (PIB, 2021).

The CCEA approved Phase II on 19 February 2019 with ₹11,814 crore of central support. MNRE launched it on 08.03.2019, according to PIB (2019). Only the residential component reaches a household.

| Phase II component | Capacity | Central support | Who receives it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Component A, residential CFA | 4,000 MW | ₹6,600 crore | The residential consumer |
| Component B, DISCOM incentives | Ceiling of 18,000 MW | ₹4,985 crore | The distribution company |

Read 18,000 MW as a ceiling, not a target. The CCEA wording is that incentives "will be available only for initial capacity addition of 18,000 MW", counted across all sectors rather than residential alone. Incentives accrue above the previous year's base, so a DISCOM has reason to process volume, but it also absorbs metering and network cost. MNRE extended the programme till 31.03.2026 (PIB, 8 December 2022).

## How central financial assistance reaches a household

One online pipeline, with a DISCOM checkpoint in the middle. MNRE routes release through an e-token activated after DISCOM inspection. After that, "the CFA shall subsequently be released to the consumer account", per MNRE Office Memorandum 318/17/2024-GCRT dated 7 June 2024. The DISCOM runs a checklist-based physical inspection and approves the application on the National Portal. CFA is processed within 15 days of that approval.

Two consequences follow. Your [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) is the State Implementation Agency and the gate, so the 15 day clock starts at its approval, not your commissioning. And if the system is bank financed, CFA goes to the loan account first.

## Who does what in the MNRE rooftop programme

Most confusion in this scheme is role confusion. Read the table before escalating a stuck file.

| Body | What it does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| MNRE | Scheme, guidelines, CFA slabs, ALMM and quality control orders | Inspect roofs, clear files |
| REC Limited | National Programme Implementing Agency, runs the portal | Supply or install equipment |
| National Portal | Application, vendor registration, net metering agreement, transfer | Decide technical feasibility |
| State nodal agency | State top-ups, Model Solar Village, the state RTS cell | Pay central assistance |
| DISCOM | State Implementation Agency: sanction, inspection, meter, portal approval | Set CFA slab amounts |
| SECI | Phase II vendor empanelment, government-building tenders, CPSU tranches | Implement PM Surya Ghar CFA |
| Registered vendor | Supply, installation, portal paperwork, warranty | Receive the CFA itself |

The SECI row trips people up. Under Phase II, SECI's own tender document said "SECI's role is limited to the empanelment process", with second-stage bidding left to the state agency. SECI is not named in the PM Surya Ghar guidelines.

## What changed when PM Surya Ghar launched in February 2024

February 2024 turned a modest residential component into a mass programme. The Prime Minister launched PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana on 13 February 2024. The Union Cabinet approved it on 29 February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore (PIB, 2024). The commitment is free electricity up to 300 units a month for 1 crore households. MNRE's administrative approval followed as Order 318/17/2024-GCRT dated 16 March 2024, running till 31 March 2027.

The old scheme did not run alongside it. MNRE's guidelines record that Phase II "stands subsumed within the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana". There was no new tender-mode allocation from 12.02.2024, and claims closed after October 2024.

Of the ₹75,021 crore, ₹65,700 crore is residential CFA and ₹4,950 crore is DISCOM incentives. The rest includes ₹1,000 crore for local bodies, ₹800 crore for Model Solar Villages, and ₹100 crore for RESCO payment security (MNRE, 7 June 2024).

MNRE then removed steps that were slowing files. It waived technical feasibility and allowed sanctioned load to be raised automatically up to 10 kW. It also folded the net metering agreement into the portal application and simplified vendor registration (PIB and MNRE, 25 March 2026). RESCO and Utility Led Aggregation models arrived by Office Memorandum of 28.12.2024.

## Eligibility by consumer category, and what sits outside

Category decides which component you are in, and that fixes the amount. The residential component is open to any consumer with a grid connected domestic connection. MNRE calls the scheme demand driven, so no state quota rations households.

Group housing societies and resident welfare associations have their own provision. MNRE Office Memorandum 318/17/2024-GCRT dated 7 June 2024 gives ₹18,000 per kWp for common facilities, including electric vehicle charging. It caps that at 3 kWp per house and 500 kWp for the society. Special category states carry higher residential rates of ₹33,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp and ₹19,800 for the next. Rates took effect on 13 February 2024, and no later revision is published as of September 2026.

Three categories fall outside residential assistance: commercial and industrial consumers, institutional and social sector buildings on non-domestic tariffs, and government buildings. For a society, the [apartment and RWA solar guide](/blog/solar-for-apartment-complex-rwa-india/) covers the ownership questions first.

## Worked example: CFA on a group housing society roof

This is arithmetic on published MNRE parameters, not a quote or field data. It shows how the two caps interact. Take a general category society of 60 flats planning solar for lifts, pumps, and corridor lighting.

1. Per house cap: 3 kWp x 60 flats = 180 kWp notional ceiling.
2. Overall cap: 500 kWp. The lower binds, so 180 kWp applies.
3. Proposed common facility capacity: 40 kWp.
4. CFA at ₹18,000 per kWp: 40 x 18,000 = ₹7,20,000.
5. Now assume 12 flat owners already installed 2 kWp each, so 24 kWp. The ceiling is inclusive of individual plants on the same premises, so headroom becomes 156 kWp. The 40 kWp proposal still fits.
6. Had the society proposed 200 kWp, only 156 kWp sits inside the ceiling. CFA becomes 156 x 18,000 = ₹28,08,000, and the remaining 44 kWp carries nothing.

Step 5 carries the lesson. Individual installations eat society headroom, so sequence matters. And the cap is on capacity, not cost.

## Why a non-registered installer costs you the CFA

The rule is procedural, not technical. MNRE states that subsidy is transferred after "successful installation (by empanelled vendors) and verification by State DISCOMs". A system built outside the registered vendor route has no valid claim path on the portal, however good the hardware is.

Sequence is where this goes wrong. Registration must be current at the agreement stage, not produced afterwards. A vendor listed last year, listed in another state, or trading under a related firm name does not pass. Match the portal entity to the one on your agreement and invoice.

MNRE has been widening the vendor pool by simplifying registration (PIB and MNRE, 25 March 2026), which also means untested firms appear. Registration proves scheme eligibility, not workmanship, so run the checks in the [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) too.

The risk runs both ways. A false domestic content claim is not a paperwork slip. MNRE's enforcement memorandum of 16 October 2023 provides for an IPC 420 case, 10-year blacklisting, and forfeiture of the bank guarantee.

## Component rules: ALMM, domestic content, and the BIS order

Three separate rulebooks decide whether equipment is scheme eligible. They cover different products, carry different dates, and get collapsed into one.

[ALMM](/glossary/almm-list/) is the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers. MNRE notified it for solar PV modules from 10.04.2021 and for cells from 01.06.2026. The cell order is dated 09.12.2024 and was amended on 28.07.2025 (PIB and MNRE, 2025). It extends to ingots and wafers from 1 June 2028. No ALMM inverter list is published. For the cell timeline and its exemption window, see the [ALMM List-II exemption guide](/blog/almm-list-ii-exemption-net-metering-open-access/).

Domestic content runs on a separate track, and MNRE has said so in terms. An ALMM order "does not relax any provisions about usage of domestically manufactured solar PV modules and domestically manufactured solar PV cells". That is MNRE OM 283/54/2026-GRID SOLAR dated 4 August 2026. ALMM dates have moved. DCR for PM Surya Ghar has not.

Verification is mechanical now. Since 1 December 2024, a module whose DCR credentials cannot be verified on the NISE portal "shall not be accepted". The National Portal will not take an installation without a 16-digit DCR certificate number (MNRE, 11 November 2024 and 12 April 2025). MNRE's model consumer-vendor agreement requires that "the solar modules, including the solar cells, should be manufactured in India".

Inverters sit under the quality control order, which has itself been superseded. MNRE's 2017 compulsory registration order named IS 16221 (Part 2):2015 and IS 16169:2014 for PV inverters. The Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025 replaced it, gazetted 27 January 2025 under the BIS Act, 2016. MNRE has since extended the inverter items on a self certification route to 31 December 2026. That relaxation has been extended more than once, so check MNRE's lab policy page for the live notification. The [inverter regulations guide](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) explains which standard does which job.

Qbits states it is ALMM Phase III listed. Treat that as a supplier statement and verify it against the current MNRE list and the model on your quote. Standards cited in published Qbits documents include IS 16221, IS 16169, IEC 62109, and IEC 62116. Qbits publishes an expandable warranty, and its public datasheets do not define the base term, remedy, registration deadline or exclusions, so obtain the current written warranty for the exact quoted model. Pull the [on-grid inverter](/on-grid-inverter/) documents and match identifiers.

## Net metering is a state decision, not a central one

Net metering is not an MNRE subject. It comes from the Ministry of Power and your state regulator. The Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Amendment Rules, 2021, notified 29 June 2021, permit net metering up to 500 kW or the sanctioned load, whichever is lower. Above that comes net billing or gross metering.

The same rules then hand the working arrangement to the states. Metering type, banking period, settlement of surplus, and carry forward follow State Electricity Regulatory Commission regulations. Two identical 5 kW systems in different states can settle surplus on different terms. Gujarat's regulator allowed rooftop net metering from 1 kW to 1 MW through its 2022 amendment regulations, beyond the central line. Others apply tighter sanctioned load or transformer limits. Read your own tariff order before sizing. The [net metering guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) covers the mechanics.

## Where the programme stands against target, and why files stall

The honest position is real momentum plus a real approval gap. Grid connected rooftop capacity stood at 32.59 GW as on 31.08.2026, according to MNRE's physical progress data. PM Surya Ghar accounted for 39,38,805 systems serving 47,47,341 households as on 20.07.2026, with ₹27,323.55 crore disbursed. That is MNRE's own figure in Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 580 (2026). Two traps sit there. The 32.59 GW is all rooftop, including commercial and pre-2024 systems. And systems are not households, because one society installation serves many homes.

Now the pipeline. Over 1 crore households have registered and more than 65 lakh applications were pending (MNRE, 4 June 2026), against roughly 39 lakh completed systems. Targets are 75 lakh households by December 2026 and 1 crore by 31 March 2027.

The bottleneck is not the ministry. MNRE gives CFA processing 15 days from DISCOM approval, so every week before that approval falls outside the published clock. MNRE's remedy list reads the same way: each measure targets friction at the utility stage. State performance varies enormously too. The [net meter approval timeline](/blog/net-meter-approval-timeline/) separates published stages from observed delays, and the [statewise capacity page](/blog/india-rooftop-solar-capacity-state-2026/) shows where deployment sits.

## The Bottom Line

The MNRE rooftop solar scheme is a rulebook, not a counter. MNRE writes the orders, REC Limited coordinates, and your DISCOM decides whether your file moves. Every slab, cap, and metering rule here can change by notification.

- Identify your component first: residential, group housing common facility, or non-residential. That choice fixes which rules apply.
- Verify three documents before you sign: the vendor's current portal registration, the module's ALMM and domestic content evidence, and the inverter's model-specific BIS and IEC papers.
