To apply for a **Qbits solar inverter dealership in India**, use the official partner form and choose `Dealer` as the business type. You will need basic company and contact details. The form also gives you room to explain your warehouse, team, working capital, sales plan, and previous inverter experience.

That is the application route, not an automatic appointment. Qbits reviews each submission. The public page does not publish a minimum investment, guaranteed margin, exclusive territory, credit limit, or approval timeline. If your application moves forward, confirm those points in the proposed agreement before you buy stock or describe your business as an authorised Qbits dealer.

## Who is this dealer route for?

The dealer route is for a business that wants to sell Qbits solar inverters in a defined market and can explain how it will reach customers, handle stock, and support sales after delivery. It may suit an electrical-products dealer, solar retailer, or established local business adding an inverter line.

Choose a different option on the form if the work is different:

| Route on the Qbits form | Use it when this best describes your business |
| --- | --- |
| Dealer | You plan to sell to customers or installers in a local market |
| Distributor | You plan to stock products and supply a wider downstream network; use the [Qbits distributor application walkthrough](/blog/become-qbits-distributor/) to prepare the role-specific file |
| EPC | You survey, design, procure, install, and commission solar projects |
| System Integrator | You combine inverters with other equipment, controls, or project systems |

These descriptions help you choose a route. They are not a substitute for the role definition in a final agreement. A dealer appointment does not by itself create service authorisation, distribution rights, or an exclusive territory.

If more than one description appears to fit, compare the [Dealer, Distributor, EPC, and System Integrator roles](/blog/dealer-vs-distributor-vs-epc/) by customer, stock, project responsibility, and service boundary before selecting the business type.

## Is there a published eligibility threshold?

Qbits does not publish a pass or fail score for dealer applicants on the current partner page. It does not say that a particular turnover, warehouse size, team size, working-capital figure, or monthly target guarantees approval.

The sensible test before applying is whether you can describe a real, supportable business:

- a registered business identity and reliable contact person;
- a market you already understand or can credibly develop;
- a sales route that fits the dealer role;
- enough financial capacity for the terms eventually offered;
- a practical approach to stock, delivery, customer queries, and escalation; and
- truthful information about your current team and inverter experience.

Do not inflate a turnover figure, team size, or sales forecast to make the application look stronger. A smaller, specific plan is more useful than a target the business cannot explain.

## What does the current Qbits partner form ask for?

The live Qbits form groups its questions into company information, business profile, infrastructure and team, financial details, and business targets. As of 26 September 2026, the visible fields marked as required are:

- company name;
- registered address;
- official business email;
- a 10-digit WhatsApp number; and
- GST number.

The form also asks for information that is not visibly marked as required:

| Section | Information requested |
| --- | --- |
| Business profile | Website, business type, PM Surya Ghar registered name for EPCs, annual turnover, and inverter brands currently used |
| Infrastructure | Owned or rented office, warehouse availability and size, sales team, technical-support team, service team, and service-centre location |
| Financial details | Available working capital, bank-loan amount, cash-credit limits, other capital, and hypothecated bank |
| Business targets | Target monthly sales, readiness to promote Qbits, and previous solar-inverter experience |

Use the [field-by-field Qbits partner application guide](/blog/qbits-partner-application-guide/) to prepare each answer, understand the current required markers, and review the data-handling notice before submission.

EPC applicants completing the PM Suryaghar field can use the separate [PM Surya Ghar vendor registration guide for installers](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vendor-registration-installers/). Government vendor registration and the Qbits partner review are independent processes.

There is an important distinction here. A form asking for warehouse size or working capital does not prove that Qbits requires one published minimum. It means the team wants that information for its review. If Qbits later sets a threshold for your proposed appointment, ask for it in writing.

## Which documents should you prepare?

The public form asks mostly for entered information rather than listing a document-upload pack. Prepare records that let you complete it accurately and answer follow-up due diligence without scrambling:

- legal name, entity type, registered address and authorised signatory evidence;
- current GST details;
- the official email and WhatsApp number monitored by your business;
- office, warehouse, and service-centre details, if applicable;
- an up-to-date staff list showing sales, technical, and service roles;
- current inverter brands and relevant market experience;
- financial records supporting any turnover, capital, loan, or credit-limit figure you provide; and
- a realistic monthly sales plan with its assumptions.

The form does not ask you to upload all of these records on the first screen. Keep them available in case the partner team requests verification. Share sensitive financial or identity documents only through a confirmed Qbits process, and never share passwords or one-time passcodes.

## How does the Qbits dealer application process work?

The public process is straightforward:

1. Review the [Qbits inverter families](/our-products/) and [current datasheets](/download-datasheets/) so you know what you are applying to sell.
2. Open the official [Qbits partner application](/become-our-partner/).
3. Select `Dealer` and complete the form with current, accurate information.
4. Save a copy of the information you submitted and note the date.
5. Wait for the Qbits team to review the application and contact you about the relevant route.
6. If discussions continue, provide verification through the confirmed channel.
7. Review the complete appointment proposal and schedules before making commitments.

The page confirms that applications are reviewed. It does not promise automatic acceptance or a fixed response time, so do not base a stock order, lease, or hiring decision on the form submission alone.

Evaluate the manufacturer through current exact-model documents, compliance evidence, quality and change-control processes, written warranty, service workflow, parts and escalation, training, stock support, data systems, channel policy, and references that can be verified. Marketing reach or a headline warranty should not substitute for product and operating evidence.

Before approaching one brand, use the [Indian inverter partner programme comparison](/blog/indian-inverter-partner-programmes/) to separate product-resale, retail-franchise, project-execution, and lead-generation routes using the same public-evidence standard.

## How should stock, working capital and credit be planned?

Model stock and working capital from the actual proposed terms and conservative operating scenarios. Include opening inventory, model mix, replenishment, supplier payment, customer collection, taxes, freight, warehousing, insurance, staffing, demo equipment, marketing, service tools, warranty cash flow, slow stock, returns, bad debt, and contingency without assuming credit or margins.

The [solar dealer working-capital guide](/blog/working-capital-solar-dealers/) provides a blank stock cash-cycle schedule, downside cases, and finance-comparison questions without assuming a Qbits facility.

Use the [solar dealership investment worksheet](/blog/solar-dealership-investment-india/) to separate registration, demo, stock, setup, cash-cycle, and contingency amounts before choosing a working-capital figure.

Build at least these scenarios:

1. cash purchase with slower-than-expected customer collection;
2. approved credit with the actual limit, due date, security and suspension rules;
3. lower sales with stock ageing or a model transition;
4. higher sales with replenishment and service capacity constraints;
5. warranty or return cases that delay cash recovery; and
6. termination or territory change with remaining inventory and receivables.

Obtain accounting and tax review before treating a rebate, credit note, finance facility, stock return, or customer advance as available cash. A salesperson's estimate is not an approved credit line.

Before putting a percentage into that model, use the [solar dealer margin disclosure guide](/blog/solar-dealer-margin-india/) to separate list-price discount, product gross margin, project margin, contribution, and net profit.

## Which product, training and service support should be verified?

Verify what support is delivered, to whom, by which team, under what conditions, and with what evidence. Product training, design assistance, commissioning support, sales material, monitoring access, warranty intake, service authorisation, spares, escalation, and continuing updates should be distinguished rather than bundled into an undefined promise of full support.

Ask:

- Which courses are required before sale, installation, commissioning, or service?
- Does completion grant any authorisation, and when does it expire or change?
- Which tools, test equipment, software accounts and records are required?
- Who approves designs, settings, battery pairings and field repairs?
- Which support channels, hours, priorities and escalation steps are written?
- How are parts, labour, freight, travel and customer communication allocated?
- Who updates the partner when specifications, firmware, warranties or policies change?
- Which customer data can the partner access, and how is it protected and returned?

Use the [solar inverter warranty guide](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty/) to test whether the commercial discussion matches the current written coverage.

## What should be reviewed before signing an agreement?

Review the parties, products, territory, channels, targets, prices, rebates, credit, stock, delivery, returns, marketing, data, installation, warranty, service, liability, insurance, term, termination, post-termination duties, and disputes with qualified advisers. Carry every material promise into the final text or record it as unavailable, conditional, or rejected.

The [solar dealer agreement checklist](/blog/solar-dealer-agreement-checklist/) supplies the full document review. Do not begin selling from a letter, message, or application acknowledgement if the programme requires a signed appointment and completed onboarding.

## What makes a dealer application decision-ready?

**A decision-ready dealer application is complete, consistent, and easy to verify.** It shows where you operate, which customers you serve, how you expect to sell, who owns each function, what capacity exists today, and which capabilities still need to be built. It avoids invented forecasts and makes dependencies visible.

Before submission, confirm:

- the role matches actual capability;
- every statement is current and supportable;
- the territory plan distinguishes demand evidence from estimates;
- working-capital cases do not assume unapproved credit or return rights;
- technical and service claims match available staff and authorisations;
- existing brand or channel conflicts are disclosed where requested;
- sensitive documents use the verified submission route; and
- internal reviewers understand that application is not appointment.

If that describes your business, complete the [Qbits partner application](/become-our-partner/) and choose `Dealer`. Treat any commercial discussion after submission as a proposal until the final terms are approved in writing.
