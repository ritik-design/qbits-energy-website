## The 5-Document Warranty Claim Pack: Assemble Before You Call

The single most effective step any homeowner can take before filing a solar inverter warranty claim is assembling every required document before making the first contact. Brands routinely add days or weeks to a claim timeline by requesting documents one at a time. Arriving with all five eliminates that delay entirely.

This framework, the **5-Document Warranty Claim Pack**: covers every document that any brand operating in India can legitimately require for a residential inverter claim:

### Document 1: Original Purchase Invoice

The purchase invoice must show the brand name, inverter model, serial number, purchase date, seller name and address, and the sale price including [GST](/glossary/gst-on-solar/). This is the foundational document for every warranty interaction, without it, the brand cannot confirm that the unit was sold through an authorised channel or that the warranty period is still active.

If you purchased through an installer who bundled the inverter into an overall system cost, request a line-item invoice that separates the inverter from other equipment. A combined invoice without individual product serial numbers is not sufficient for a warranty claim.

Keep one physical copy and one digital copy (cloud-stored or emailed to yourself) the day you receive it. Most homeowners only look for the invoice when they need it, by which time it has been lost, water-damaged, or simply misplaced.

### Document 2: Installation Certificate or Commissioning Report

The [commissioning](/glossary/commissioning/) report is the document your installer generates when the system is brought live. It records the installation date, the installer's credentials, the system configuration, component serial numbers, and the commissioning test results.

This document performs two critical functions in a warranty claim. First, it establishes the warranty start date, the day the system was commissioned, not the day the inverter was manufactured. Most reputable brands start the warranty clock from the commissioning date; without this document, a brand can default to the manufacture date, which may have been six to twelve months earlier. Second, it records the installer's credentials, which most brands require to confirm the installation was performed by a certified or authorised party.

Request the commissioning report the day the system is handed over. Do not accept verbal confirmation that the installation is complete.

### Document 3: DISCOM Interconnection or Net-Metering Approval

For grid-tied systems (which covers the majority of residential installations in India) your [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) issues a formal interconnection approval or net-metering sanction letter before the system is allowed to export power to the grid. This letter confirms that the installation met the DISCOM's technical requirements at the time of commissioning.

In warranty disputes, this document serves as independent third-party confirmation that the system was installed to regulatory standards. Brands sometimes attempt to void claims on the grounds of "improper installation", the DISCOM approval letter is the strongest counter-evidence available. Getting the underlying electrical and CEIG drawings right at the design stage is what makes this DISCOM approval straightforward to obtain in the first place.

### Document 4: Fault Evidence File

The fault evidence file is the one document you create at the time of failure. It should contain:

- **Photographs** of the inverter display showing any error codes, warning indicators, or abnormal readings.
- **A short video** (60–90 seconds) demonstrating the fault condition, the inverter failing to start, the error code cycling, or the unusual sound or behaviour.
- **A brief written log** noting the date and time of the fault, any grid events in the preceding 24 hours (power cuts, voltage fluctuations, storms), and the system's generation history from the monitoring app in the days before the fault.

The monitoring app log is particularly valuable. An AI-monitored system that shows a gradual anomaly in MPPT tracking or AC output frequency over several days before complete failure provides strong evidence of a manufacturing defect rather than a sudden external event.

### Document 5: Warranty Card or Registration Confirmation

The warranty card (physical or digital) or the warranty registration confirmation email establishes the coverage terms. If the brand required online warranty registration within 30 days of commissioning (which many brands do) this document confirms that the registration was completed on time.

Without a registered warranty, some brands downgrade the buyer to a shorter or more limited "standard" coverage that differs from the marketed terms. The registration confirmation is the proof that the advertised warranty terms apply.

## Covered Faults vs Excluded Causes: Knowing the Difference Before You Call

Filing a warranty claim for a fault that is excluded wastes time and weakens your position with the brand for subsequent legitimate claims. Understanding the coverage boundary before you call the service line prevents this.

### What Is Covered: Manufacturing Defects

A solar inverter warranty covers failures that originate in the manufacturing process, faults in components or assemblies that would not have failed under normal operating conditions within the warranty period. Common covered faults include:

- **IGBT or MOSFET failure**: power transistors that fail due to manufacturing variance, not due to overcurrent from misuse.
- **Capacitor degradation**: electrolytic capacitors that lose capacity faster than normal due to substandard components or manufacturing defects.
- **Control board failure**: processor, firmware, or communication board failures where no external cause (lightning, liquid ingress, physical impact) is identifiable.
- **MPPT circuit malfunction**: [MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) tracking failures that cause the inverter to operate at significantly below optimal efficiency without any external cause.
- **Display or communication module failure**: error displays, Wi-Fi modules, RS485 interfaces, and app connectivity failures not caused by physical damage.

The common thread: if the fault is internal, non-physical, and occurred under operating conditions that fall within the inverter's rated specifications, it is a manufacturing defect.

### What Is Excluded: The Common Denial Grounds

Understanding the excluded causes is equally important. The following failure modes appear in the exclusion clauses of virtually every solar inverter warranty sold in India:

- **Lightning damage without SPD**: a direct lightning strike or a major induced voltage transient that damages the inverter is excluded. However, if a [Surge Protection Device](/glossary/spd/) was installed and functioning correctly, the brand must prove the SPD failed rather than simply citing "lightning damage."
- **Physical damage**: damage from impact, improper handling, pest ingress (rats, lizards, insects), water ingress above the rated IP protection level, or animal interference.
- **Improper installation**: installation by an uncertified technician, incorrect wiring configurations, or non-compliance with the manufacturer's installation manual. Choosing a [verified solar installer](/blog/find-reliable-solar-installer/) or a full-service solar EPC contractor at the outset keeps this exclusion from becoming relevant at all.
- **User modification**: any internal repair, component substitution, or firmware modification performed by someone other than an authorised service technician.
- **Operation outside rated parameters**: DC input voltage or current exceeding the inverter's rated maximums, which can occur when installers aggressively [DC-oversize](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/) the panel array.
- **Natural calamities**: flood damage, fire damage, and direct lightning strikes are typically excluded even with proper protective equipment installed.

The most important distinction for Indian homeowners is between **lightning damage** (legitimately excluded, but requiring the brand to prove causation) and **grid voltage fluctuation damage** (which should not be excluded for inverters marketed as India-grid compatible). This distinction is where many valid claims are wrongly denied.

| --- | --- | --- |

## Step-by-Step: How to File a Solar Inverter Warranty Claim in India

The claim process follows a predictable sequence across all major brands. Knowing each step before you begin prevents the procedural errors that lead to unnecessary delays.

### Step 1: Assemble the 5-Document Warranty Claim Pack

Before making any call or sending any message, gather all five documents described in the previous section. Place them in a single folder (physical and digital) so that every piece of evidence is available the moment a brand representative requests it. A buyer who can provide all five documents in the first interaction resolves claims measurably faster than a buyer who retrieves documents reactively.

### Step 2: Open the Ticket Through the Correct Channel

Contact the brand's service channel using the method specified in your warranty document. This is where channel matters:

- **Brand-direct brands** (Qbits), open the ticket directly with the brand via WhatsApp monitoring platform, the dealer portal, or the national service line. No installer co-ordination required.
- **Dealer-routed brands**: contact your original dealer first. If the dealer is unavailable, escalate to the brand's national service contact, citing that the dealer is unreachable.
- **App-based brands**: initiate the claim through the inverter's monitoring app, which typically generates a ticket automatically.

Request a **written ticket number** in every case. If the brand communicates verbally, follow up immediately with a written email or WhatsApp message summarising the conversation and asking for confirmation of the ticket reference. This written record is your evidence of the claim being filed on a specific date.

### Step 3: Technician Visit and Fault Diagnosis

Most brands will either dispatch a technician for an on-site inspection or request photographs and monitoring data for a remote diagnosis before approving the claim. Cooperate fully with the diagnostic process and retain copies of all information you provide.

During the technician visit:
- Ask the technician to document their findings in writing, even informally.
- Photograph the unit before and after any on-site work.
- Do not authorise the technician to repair or disassemble the unit without first confirming whether the repair will be covered under warranty.

If the technician determines the fault is not covered (attributing it to lightning damage, improper installation, or another excluded cause) ask for the determination in writing before the technician leaves the site.

### Step 4: Claim Approval and Parts or Replacement Dispatch

Once the brand's service team confirms the fault is covered, they will initiate either a repair cycle or a replacement dispatch, depending on the coverage type.

For **replacement warranty** (Qbits standard): a new unit is dispatched within 72 hours of claim approval. You receive a tracking number. The failed unit is returned to the brand via the same logistics partner.

For **repair warranty** (most other brands): the failed unit is collected, transported to a service centre, repaired, and returned. This cycle averages 6 to 8 weeks in India when spare parts must be sourced domestically or imported.

> **6–8 weeks.** The typical repair turnaround for solar inverter warranty service in India when a brand provides repair-only coverage. At ₹200 per day in lost generation for a 5 kW system, this represents ₹8,400 to ₹11,200 in unrecovered electricity value per claim event. *Source - [Mercom India Solar Market Report 2025](https://www.mercomindia.com/).*

### Step 5: Resolution Confirmation and System Recommissioning

When the replacement unit arrives or the repaired unit is returned, verify that it matches the specification of the original, same rated power, same firmware version (or newer), same MPPT count and configuration. Do not accept a unit with a lower specification than the original.

After installation, obtain a recommissioning report from the technician. This documents the replacement unit's serial number, the installation date (which may restart or continue the warranty period depending on the brand's terms, confirm this explicitly), and the test results confirming the system is generating normally.

## What Qbits' 72-Hour RMA SLA Looks Like in Practice

The Qbits RMA process is built around a specific commitment: from the moment a warranty claim is approved, the replacement unit is dispatched within 72 hours. This is a published, contractual SLA, not a marketing aspiration.

### How the Process Works

**Hour 0, Fault detection.** The AI-monitored WhatsApp platform flags an anomaly, a sudden drop in output, a recurring fault code, or an MPPT efficiency deviation outside normal parameters. The homeowner receives an alert. Alternatively, the homeowner notices a fault and initiates contact via WhatsApp or the dealer portal.

**Hour 0–2, Ticket issuance.** A unique ticket number is issued within 2 hours of the fault being logged. This number is the reference for every subsequent interaction.

**Hours 2–48, Diagnosis.** Depending on the fault, the service team either performs a remote diagnosis using the monitoring data or dispatches a technician for an on-site inspection. For faults that are clearly manufacturing defects based on monitoring data, remote diagnosis is typically sufficient.

**Hours 48–72, Approval and dispatch.** Once the fault is confirmed as covered under warranty, the replacement unit is packed and dispatched via a tracked courier. The homeowner receives a dispatch confirmation with a tracking number.

**Days 4–6, Delivery and recommissioning.** The replacement unit is delivered and installed by an authorised service partner. A recommissioning report is issued. The failed unit is returned to Qbits through the same logistics partner.

The homeowner does not need to co-ordinate with the original installer at any point in this process. The claim is entirely brand-direct.

### What Enables This SLA

A 72-hour dispatch commitment is only possible when the brand maintains physical stock of replacement units in regional depots across India, rather than relying on a single central warehouse or on-demand manufacturing. Brands that cannot commit to a specific RMA timeline are typically running low-inventory or import-dependent supply chains that cannot support a contractual turnaround commitment.

Qbits' service network covers 280+ Indian cities, with regional stock depots enabling same-day or next-day dispatch across most major service zones.

## When the Brand Drags Its Feet: Escalation Paths for Indian Homeowners

Some brands acknowledge claims promptly but allow them to stall indefinitely at the diagnosis or approval stage. Others deny claims on technical grounds that do not withstand scrutiny. The following escalation path covers both situations.

### Stage 1: Formal Written Notice to the Brand

If the brand has not responded within its stated SLA (or within a reasonable timeframe if no SLA is stated) send a formal written notice via registered email to the brand's national service head, copying the regional service manager if the contact is available. The notice should:

- State the ticket number and date the claim was filed.
- Specify the fault condition and the documents provided.
- Cite the applicable warranty clause (replacement within X days, or the warranty coverage clause).
- State clearly that you expect resolution within 7 business days and will escalate to regulatory bodies if the deadline is not met.

This notice creates a documented timestamp of escalation that is necessary for any subsequent regulatory filing.

### Stage 2: DISCOM and MNRE Grievance Filing

For systems installed under PM Surya Ghar or any MNRE scheme, the [MNRE consumer grievance portal](https://mnre.gov.in/) is the appropriate regulatory escalation point. MNRE-registered installers and empanelled brands are subject to scheme-level compliance requirements that give MNRE oversight authority over warranty disputes.

For systems where [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) approval is involved (which is every grid-tied net-metered system) the DISCOM's consumer grievance cell can be notified of the dispute. DISCOM involvement does not directly resolve the warranty claim, but it creates a regulatory record of the dispute that strengthens a subsequent consumer court filing.

### Stage 3: Consumer Forum Filing via e-DAAKHIL

The [Consumer Protection Act 2019](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/) gives Indian homeowners access to consumer courts that are specifically designed for disputes like warranty claim denials. The relevant forum for a residential solar inverter claim (typically ₹40,000 to ₹2,00,000 in value) is the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.

Complaints are filed online at [edaakhil.nic.in](https://edaakhil.nic.in/) without requiring a lawyer. Filing fees are nominal (₹100–₹2,500 depending on claim value). The filing requires:

- Your identity proof and address.
- The purchase invoice, warranty document, and correspondence record.
- A statement of the deficiency of service, specifically, that the brand denied or failed to resolve a valid warranty claim.
- The relief sought, replacement unit, compensation for lost generation, or both.

Consumer courts in India consistently rule in favour of buyers when the warranty documentation is clear and the brand's denial cannot be supported by the exclusion clauses in the warranty document. The [BIS certification](/glossary/bis-certification/) and [commissioning](/glossary/commissioning/) records serve as strong corroborating evidence in these proceedings.

> **₹50 lakh.** The claim value ceiling below which Indian consumers can file at the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission without a lawyer, using the e-DAAKHIL portal. For residential solar inverter disputes (typically ₹40,000–₹2,00,000) no legal representation is required. *Source - [Consumer Protection Act 2019, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Government of India](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/), 2019.*

## Common Mistakes That Derail Solar Inverter Warranty Claims

The majority of delayed or denied warranty claims in India trace back to one of the following procedural errors, all of which are avoidable before the fault ever occurs.

- **No commissioning certificate**: the single most common reason a brand invokes the manufacture date instead of the installation date for warranty calculations. Request this document at system handover without exception.

- **Verbal-only communication with the brand**: a phone call to a service number produces no documented evidence of the claim being filed. Always follow up every verbal interaction with a written message that includes the date, the fault description, and the ticket number.

- **Allowing non-authorised repairs**: if a local electrician opens the inverter casing to "take a look," the physical warranty is voided across virtually every brand in the market. Call the brand's service line before any internal work is performed.

- **Installing without an SPD**: while a [Surge Protection Device](/glossary/spd/) does not affect the warranty directly, its absence provides the brand with a plausible attribution pathway for electrical faults. A ₹3,000 SPD installed at commissioning removes this ambiguity. The [how-to-choose solar inverters with surge protection](/blog/how-to-choose-solar-inverters-with-surge-protection/) guide covers SPD selection in detail.

- **Missing the warranty registration window**: many brands require online registration within 30 days of commissioning. An unregistered warranty may default to less favourable coverage terms. Check your warranty card for registration requirements and complete the registration the day the system is commissioned.

- **Accepting verbal denial**: if a technician or service agent says the claim is not covered, ask for the determination in writing with the specific exclusion clause cited. A verbal denial is not a final determination under consumer law.
