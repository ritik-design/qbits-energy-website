## Is There Any Government Scheme That Gives Free Solar Panels?

No. There is no central or state scheme in India that gives a homeowner a free rooftop solar system with no payment at any stage. This is the single most important fact in this article. It is worth stating plainly because search results for this keyword are engineered to obscure it.

What the government actually offers is **Central Financial Assistance (CFA)**, a partial reimbursement. PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana launched on February 13, 2024. Under it, the central government pays you back a fixed amount per kW. The money comes after you have bought, installed, and commissioned a system. The scheme has a budget outlay of Rs 75,021 crore and targets solarising 1 crore households by March 2027, according to [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/), 2024.

The word that causes the confusion is "muft" in the scheme's own name. Muft Bijli means free electricity, not free panels. The promise is that a correctly sized rooftop system generates up to 300 units a month, which zeroes out most household bills. The electricity becomes free after you have paid for the equipment that generates it. That is a genuinely valuable outcome, but it is a very different thing from free hardware.

Why does the myth persist? Because a "subsidy of Rs 78,000 on a Rs 2 lakh system" ad is harder to write than "free solar panels, apply now." The first sentence is true. The second one gets clicks.

## What the Government Actually Pays: PM Surya Ghar Numbers for 2026

The CFA structure is fixed, national, and published. Here is exactly what a residential homeowner receives, according to the [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026:

| System size | Central subsidy (CFA) | Approx. gross system cost (2026) | Your net cost after CFA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | Rs 30,000 | Rs 70,000 to Rs 90,000 | Rs 40,000 to Rs 60,000 |
| 2 kW | Rs 60,000 | Rs 1.3 lakh to Rs 1.6 lakh | Rs 70,000 to Rs 1 lakh |
| 3 kW | Rs 78,000 | Rs 1.8 lakh to Rs 2.4 lakh | Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1.6 lakh |
| 5 kW | Rs 78,000 (capped) | Rs 2.8 lakh to Rs 3.5 lakh | Rs 2 lakh to Rs 2.7 lakh |
| 10 kW | Rs 78,000 (capped) | Rs 5 lakh to Rs 6.5 lakh | Rs 4.2 lakh to Rs 5.7 lakh |

Gross costs are industry-observed ranges for 2026 including panels, inverter, structure, and installation. Your quote will vary with panel brand, inverter choice, roof type, and state. For a component-level breakdown, see the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy amount](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/) guide.

Three details in this table kill most "free solar" claims on contact:

1. **The subsidy caps at Rs 78,000.** On any system of 3 kW or larger, the central payment stops there. Nobody receives a free 5 kW or 10 kW system.
2. **It covers roughly 30 to 40 percent of cost.** On a typical 3 kW install, CFA pays about a third. You fund the rest.
3. **It arrives after the money is spent.** The subsidy is a direct bank transfer released only after installation, DISCOM inspection, and commissioning. You or your financier carry the full cost first.

Several states add a top-up on top of the central CFA. Gujarat offers an additional Rs 40,000 for eligible residential consumers under its state rooftop programme. Rajasthan offers Rs 8,000 per kW, according to state policy summaries tracked in 2026. Even in the most generous state, the combined benefit pays for roughly half the system. Never all of it. ## Where the "Free Solar" Myth Actually Comes From

The myth has four distinct sources, and each needs a different response. Understanding which one you are looking at is the core skill this post teaches.

**Source 1: The scheme's own name.** Muft Bijli Yojana translates as Free Electricity Scheme. Politicians' speeches and headlines shortened that to "free solar," and the nuance died in translation. The government's intent was honest: free electricity bills. The market heard: free panels.

**Source 2: Zero-down EMI marketing.** Installers and loan companies advertise "free solar, pay only your EMI." What they mean is no down payment. It is a legitimate product, but it is a loan, not a gift. More on this in the next section.

**Source 3: RESCO and PPA models.** A company installs a system on your roof at zero upfront cost. It then sells you the power it generates for 15 to 25 years. "Free installation" is technically accurate. "Free solar" is not, because you pay per unit for decades. MNRE formalised this route under PM Surya Ghar in January 2025. Its operational guidelines for RESCO models added a Rs 100 crore payment security corpus, according to [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/), 2025.

**Source 4: Outright scams.** These include fake portals mimicking pmsuryaghar.gov.in and SMS messages announcing "your subsidy is approved" with a malicious download link. Callers may also claim to be from a government solar department and demand processing fees. These are not marketing exaggerations. They are crimes, and they are the reason the trust angle of this post exists. Fake PM Surya Ghar approval SMS scams were still circulating in July 2026, according to consumer fraud trackers.

> **What most homeowners get wrong:** they assume the word "free" in an ad is either fully true or fully false. In practice it is usually a loan or a lease wearing the government's branding. Ask first: "Do I own this system, and what will I pay in total over its life?" The answer separates all four sources instantly.

## Zero-Down EMI: A Good Product With a Dishonest Name

Let us be fair to the EMI model, because it is the most common legitimate "free solar" offer and it genuinely helps cash-constrained households go solar.

The mechanics: a bank or the installer's finance partner lends you the system cost. Collateral-free rooftop solar loans near 7 percent interest are available from nationalised banks. Loans up to Rs 2 lakh need only your electricity bill and KYC. Repayment runs up to 10 years, according to the [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026. The Rs 78,000 subsidy, when it arrives, either reduces your principal or comes to you as cash depending on the loan structure. Your monthly outgo is an EMI that installers deliberately size at or below your current electricity bill.

Here is a realistic worked example (labelled hypothetical; your quote will differ):

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 3 kW system gross cost | Rs 2,10,000 |
| Loan at 7 percent, 7 years, zero down | EMI approx. Rs 3,170 |
| Typical 3 BHK electricity bill before solar | Rs 2,800 to Rs 3,500 |
| Subsidy received after commissioning | Rs 78,000 |
| Total paid over loan tenure | approx. Rs 2,66,000 |
| Interest cost over 7 years | approx. Rs 56,000 |

The homeowner pays nothing on day one, and the EMI roughly replaces the old bill. After seven years, the system generates near-free electricity for its remaining 18 or more years. That is a sound financial decision for many families. But note the bottom row: the "free" system cost Rs 2.66 lakh in total outflow. Free was the down payment, not the solar.

## RESCO and PPA: Free Installation, Not Free Solar

The RESCO model is the second legitimate "free" offer, and it is more structurally honest about what it is: you are renting out your roof.

In a RESCO arrangement, a third-party developer pays for, installs, owns, and maintains the solar plant on your roof. You sign a **Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)** and buy the solar electricity it generates at an agreed per-unit tariff, usually for 15 to 25 years. Your upfront cost is genuinely zero. Your long-term cost is the tariff, which typically starts below your DISCOM rate and escalates by a fixed percentage each year.

The tradeoffs are real and worth stating plainly:

- **You do not own the asset.** The panels on your roof belong to the RESCO company for the contract term. Buyout options exist but are often priced steeply in early years.
- **The roof is locked in.** Selling the house, redeveloping the terrace, or adding a floor becomes contractually complicated.
- **Escalation clauses compound.** A 3 percent annual tariff escalator turns a Rs 4 per unit starting tariff into roughly Rs 7 per unit after 20 years.
- **No subsidy to you.** In most RESCO structures the developer claims available benefits because they own the asset.

MNRE's January 2025 guidelines added a Payment Security Mechanism with a Rs 100 crore corpus. The goal is to de-risk RESCO investments under PM Surya Ghar, according to [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/), 2025. This makes the model safer for developers, and indirectly more available to you. It does not make it free.

For a homeowner who can access a 7 percent loan, owning the system almost always beats leasing it over 20 years. RESCO makes sense mainly for households that cannot or will not take on any debt. ## The Scams: Red Flags That Should End the Conversation

This is the section to share with parents and grandparents. Solar subsidy fraud works because it wraps itself in a real scheme's name. The victims are usually first-time buyers who have heard "the government is giving free solar."

These are the red flags, each one sufficient reason to walk away:

1. **Any fee before installation.** Registration fees, processing fees, file charges, or "subsidy release" charges. The official portal charges nothing to register, and no legitimate vendor collects money on behalf of MNRE. This is the single most common fraud pattern.
2. **A website that is not pmsuryaghar.gov.in.** Scam portals clone the official design and buy ads to rank above it. Check the URL character by character. The real portal ends in .gov.in and takes no payment.
3. **An SMS saying your subsidy is approved, with a link.** The link installs malware or harvests banking details. DISCOM and portal updates come through your registered login, not through APK download links. This scam was actively reported through mid-2026.
4. **Callers claiming to be from the "government solar department."** There is no such department calling households. MNRE does not sell systems, and it does not authorise vendors by phone. MNRE has clarified that vendors are empanelled by DISCOMs, not appointed by the Ministry, according to [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/).
5. **Pressure on OTPs and Aadhaar.** No legitimate solar process needs your bank OTP. Ever.
6. **"Only 3 free systems left in your district."** Quotas and urgency are sales pressure, and in scam cases, pure fiction. The real scheme is open to 1 crore households and does not run out on a Tuesday.
7. **No empanelled vendor ID.** A genuine installer has a DISCOM registration you can verify on the official portal. Our post on [empanelled vendors under PM Surya Ghar](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) shows exactly how to check in two minutes.

If you or a family member has already paid into one of these, act fast. Report it on the national cybercrime portal at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or call the 1930 helpline. Fast reporting materially improves the chance of freezing the transferred funds.

## How to Claim What the Government Actually Offers

The genuine process has five steps, and none of them involves paying a stranger:

1. **Register free on the official portal.** Go to pmsuryaghar.gov.in, register with your mobile number, and submit your application with your DISCOM and consumer account number. Our [PM Surya Ghar apply online](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/) walkthrough covers each screen.
2. **Confirm your eligibility.** You need to be an Indian citizen with a house, a suitable roof, and a valid electricity connection. You must not have availed a rooftop solar subsidy before. Full criteria are in the [PM Surya Ghar eligibility](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-eligibility/) post.
3. **Get DISCOM feasibility approval, then install through an empanelled vendor.** Verify the vendor's registration ID on the portal before paying any advance.
4. **Complete inspection and commissioning.** The DISCOM inspects the system, installs the net meter, and issues a commissioning certificate.
5. **Receive the subsidy by direct transfer.** Up to Rs 78,000 lands in your bank account, typically within about 30 days of commissioning and document submission.

Notice what is absent from this list: agents, middlemen, processing fees, and anyone who contacted you first. The scheme is designed for a homeowner to navigate directly or through one verified installer.
