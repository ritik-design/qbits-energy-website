For commercial and industrial buyers investing in solar across India, understanding inverter depreciation is the single highest-leverage financial decision in the procurement process. Inverters represent **15-20% of total system investment**, and depreciation strategy alone can shift a project from a marginal seven-year payback into a compelling five-year return.

This guide consolidates the regulatory framework, calculation methods, year-by-year cash impact, commissioning timing, and selection criteria that determine whether you capture the full tax shield or leave 25-40% of project economics on the table.

## Why Depreciation Matters More Than Subsidies for Commercial Solar

Capital subsidies (PM Surya Ghar, state programmes) get most of the press attention, but for any business with a meaningful tax liability, accelerated depreciation is the larger lever. A ₹40 lakh inverter investment yields:

- **₹4.8 lakh of immediate tax savings in year one** (₹16 lakh depreciation × 30% corporate tax rate)
- **₹10+ lakh cumulative tax shield over five years** (typically 26%+ of original investment)
- **1.5-2 years compressed off the payback period** vs the same project without depreciation

For a manufacturing plant, cold storage, hospital, or commercial complex, depreciation converts solar from a sustainability spend into a working-capital strategy. The Income Tax Act treats solar inverters as plant and machinery under Section 32, which is the most favourable asset class for depreciation purposes.

## The 40% WDV Rate Explained

Solar inverters - both on-grid and hybrid - qualify for the accelerated **40% per annum depreciation rate under the Written Down Value (WDV) method**. This rate is one of the highest in the Income Tax Act and is specifically designed to incentivise renewable energy adoption.

### Eligibility Criteria

To claim accelerated depreciation, the system must satisfy:

- **Business or professional use** (residential systems do not qualify)
- **Entity ownership** (the inverter must be on your balance sheet, not leased or under a PPA)
- **Operational during the financial year** (commissioned and generating)
- **Minimum 180-day operation** for full first-year claim (less than 180 days = 50% claim)
- **Proper documentation** maintained (invoice, commissioning certificate, asset register)

## WDV vs SLM: Which Method to Choose

Indian tax law allows depreciation under two methods. For solar inverters, the choice has material cash-flow consequences.

| Method | Annual rate | Pattern | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **WDV (Written Down Value)** | **40%** | Front-loaded - large early-year deductions | Most businesses; matches loan repayment cash flow |
| Straight Line Method (SLM) | ~13.91% | Equal annual deductions | Businesses that prefer predictability over IRR |

### WDV pros and cons

| Pro | Con |
| --- | --- |
| Front-loads tax shield into early years | Lower deductions in later years |
| Better time-value-of-money alignment | Requires year-by-year recalculation |
| Improves project IRR materially | Year 5+ tax shield diminishes |

### SLM pros and cons

| Pro | Con |
| --- | --- |
| Equal annual deduction | Lower total tax benefit |
| Simple to forecast | Worse cash-flow alignment |
| Predictable expense pattern | Slower payback compression |

**Recommendation for most C&I buyers:** WDV. The front-loaded shield matches the heaviest cash outflow years (loan principal repayment, working capital tied up in installation), and the IRR uplift typically exceeds 200 basis points compared to SLM.

## Book Value vs Actual Asset Value

A critical point that confuses many CFOs and operations heads: **book value does not equal physical value** for solar inverters.

| Measure | Year 10 status |
| --- | --- |
| Book value (after WDV) | ~₹4,239 on a ₹4 lakh original (0.6%) |
| Actual operational performance | 96-97% of rated efficiency |
| Remaining useful life | 5-8 years |
| Replacement cost (new equivalent) | Similar to original |

The inverter on your balance sheet at near-zero book value is still generating revenue. Treat book value as a tax instrument; treat physical condition as the operational reality. This is why **inverter warranty length matters strategically** - a 12-year manufacturer warranty protects the physical asset through the critical depreciation period (years 1-7) when project IRR is most sensitive to downtime.

## Strategic Timing Decisions

### Commissioning before the end of FY

- Operations more than 180 days = 100% first-year depreciation claim
- Operations less than 180 days = 50% claim only (remaining 50% deferred to year 2)
- Target commissioning by **late September** to safely cross the 180-day threshold for the Indian financial year (April-March)

### Coordinating with capital subsidies

PM Surya Ghar, PM-KUSUM, and state capital subsidies all reduce the depreciable base. A ₹40 lakh project receiving ₹6 lakh of subsidy is depreciated on ₹34 lakh, not ₹40 lakh. Net effect on tax shield is approximately ₹1.8 lakh reduction in first-year savings - still a strong positive, but plan around it.

### Tax holiday and MAT considerations

For SEZ units, eligible manufacturing under Section 115BAB (concessional rate), or businesses paying Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT), the effective depreciation benefit can be different. Run the numbers with your CA before assuming the standard 30% tax shield applies.

## Documentation Checklist for Tax Audit Defence

Maintain the following as a single bound file per system, accessible during any tax officer review:

1. **Purchase invoice with GST** showing inverter classified as plant and machinery
2. **Commissioning certificate** from the EPC contractor
3. **DISCOM net metering activation** certificate showing energisation date
4. **Annual operation logs** or monitoring system data (proves continued business use)
5. **Year-by-year depreciation schedule** prepared and signed by a chartered accountant
6. **Asset register** with unique serial numbers per inverter unit
7. **Subsidy disbursement records** (if applicable) showing reduced depreciable base

This documentation set defends the claim in a routine tax audit and is also required for warranty claims, insurance, and future asset sales.

## Inverter Selection Criteria for Depreciation Planning

Not all inverters are equal when viewed through the depreciation lens. The financial model assumes the asset operates reliably through the entire depreciation period. Selection should favour:

- **Extended warranty coverage** - 12+ years preferred (matches the critical depreciation window plus buffer)
- **Proven field reliability** - low MTBF, transparent failure-rate data
- **High efficiency** - 98%+ rated; protects generation revenue that pays back the asset
- **ALMM Phase III listed** - mandatory for any government-linked project and subsidy eligibility
- **Strong documentation and digital warranty systems** - reduces audit friction and supports warranty claims
- **Indian manufacturer support** - faster RMA SLA protects revenue during the critical first 5 years

A premium inverter at 10-15% higher upfront cost typically pays back through reduced downtime alone within the depreciation window, before factoring in the longer warranty tail.

## Common Mistakes That Forfeit the Tax Shield

- **Commissioning in late October-March** without realising the 180-day rule cuts the first-year claim in half
- **Choosing PPA/RESCO** without modelling the depreciation opportunity cost on CAPEX
- **Failing to maintain the asset register** at serial-number level, weakening any future audit defence
- **Not coordinating subsidy disbursement** timing with the commissioning date, creating reconciliation complexity
- **Treating the 40% WDV as a guaranteed tax saving** without confirming the business's actual tax position (MAT, tax holiday, loss carry-forward all affect the effective rate)
