## What the premium over an on-grid inverter actually buys

The hybrid premium is ₹18,000 to ₹45,000 depending on size. Compare a 5 kW on-grid unit such as the QB 5KTLS at roughly ₹40,000 to ₹62,000 against the QBH 5KS48P at ₹58,000 to ₹95,000 and the delta is visible. Here is what sits inside it.

A **bidirectional DC-DC converter** moves energy both ways between the 48V battery bus and the DC link. An on-grid inverter only pushes one direction. This is the single largest piece of added hardware cost.

A **battery management interface** speaks CAN or RS485 to the pack's own BMS. Without it, the inverter guesses at state of charge and charge limits, and a guessing inverter is how lithium packs get damaged and warranties get voided. Confirm the exact pack model appears on your inverter's supported battery list before you buy either.

An **automatic changeover relay** disconnects from the grid and re-energises a backup output within about 10 to 20 milliseconds. That speed is what keeps a desktop, a router, and a compressor running through a cut instead of restarting. An on-grid inverter does the opposite by design, because anti-islanding rules require it to stop feeding a dead line so that DISCOM linemen are safe.

A **generator input** lets a diesel or petrol genset charge the battery and carry load without fighting the inverter. Small commercial users in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar rely on this more than any other feature.

**Deeper monitoring** rounds it out. An on-grid portal shows yield. A hybrid portal has to show state of charge, charge and discharge power, backup load, and grid import and export as separate flows, because troubleshooting storage without those four numbers is guesswork. That is a fair amount of engineering for the money. It is still the small half of the budget.

## The battery is the decision, not the inverter

Here is the part most price guides bury. Storage is priced per kWh, and you need several kWh for the system to be worth having, so the multiplication runs away from you fast.

For useful home backup, most families land between 5 kWh and 15 kWh. At LFP prices of ₹24,000 to ₹32,000 per kWh installed, that is ₹1.2 Lakh to ₹4.8 Lakh in a single line item. The inverter that manages it costs ₹58,000. The ratio is not close.

This is why the chemistry question matters more than the brand question. Our [lithium versus lead acid comparison](/blog/lithium-vs-lead-acid-solar-battery/) covers the technical differences. The commercial version fits in one table.

| Metric | Tubular lead acid | LFP (lithium iron phosphate) |
|---|---|---|
| Installed cost per kWh nameplate | ₹10,000 to ₹14,000 | ₹24,000 to ₹32,000 |
| Practical depth of discharge | 50% | 90% |
| Usable kWh per 10 kWh nameplate | 5.0 kWh | 9.0 kWh |
| Cycle life at that depth | 1,200 to 1,500 | 4,000 to 6,000 |
| Round trip efficiency | 75% to 82% | 92% to 96% |
| Typical service life, daily cycling | 4 to 6 years | 10 to 15 years |
| Cost per usable kWh cycled | ₹13 to ₹20 | ₹6 to ₹9 |

The last row is the one to argue with your installer about. Lead acid looks cheaper because the sticker compares nameplate kWh, but you only ever use half a lead acid bank, it gives back four fifths of what you put in, and it needs replacing around year five. LFP costs more than twice as much on day one and roughly half as much per unit of work delivered over its life. For any system that cycles daily, which describes nearly every hybrid installation, LFP now wins on cost.

Lead acid still has two honest uses: a low cycle standby application where the bank sits full most of the year, and a tight upfront budget where the buyer accepts a known replacement in year five. Neither is a cost win. Both are cash flow decisions, and they should be described that way.

## An illustrative worked example

*The following is a clearly labelled illustrative example built on the price ranges in this guide. It is not a specific customer installation. Substitute your own tariff and outage hours.*

A household in a Tier 2 city with a single phase connection uses about 500 units a month at ₹8.20 per unit, so roughly ₹4,100 monthly. Power cuts average two hours a day, mostly between 7 pm and 10 pm, and the family currently runs a petrol generator for about 90 minutes on those evenings at an estimated fuel cost of ₹1,600 a month.

They install 5 kW of panels, a QBH 5KS48P hybrid, and a 10 kWh LFP pack. Take the midpoint of the table above and call it ₹6.6 Lakh installed. Central subsidy reaches ₹78,000 at the 3 kW cap, so net outlay is about ₹5.82 Lakh.

Solar offsets roughly 6,000 units a year at ₹8.20, which is about ₹49,200, though a share of that is export credited at a lower rate in most states. Call the realised electricity saving ₹42,000. Displaced generator fuel adds ₹19,200. Total annual benefit is roughly ₹61,200, so simple payback lands near 9.5 years.

Run the same numbers for on-grid. At a ₹3.15 Lakh midpoint less ₹78,000 subsidy, net outlay is ₹2.37 Lakh, the electricity saving is the same ₹42,000, and the generator keeps running. Payback is about 5.6 years.

The on-grid system pays back nearly four years sooner. The hybrid buys three quiet, powered evening hours a day and a house that does not go dark. That is a real product. It is not a cheaper kilowatt hour, and any quote presenting it as one is selling you something.

## When a hybrid genuinely pays in India, and when it does not

The honest test has three conditions. Hybrid makes financial sense when at least two hold.

**Outages are frequent and long.** Two or more hours on most days, or unplanned cuts that regularly exceed an hour. Large parts of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, and rural Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Tamil Nadu meet this comfortably. If you already burn diesel or replace a conventional inverter battery every four years, the hybrid absorbs a cost you are paying today.

**Your load is concentrated after sunset.** A family running air conditioning from 8 pm to midnight consumes when the panels are asleep. Storing midday surplus for night use converts export credit into avoided import, which is worth real money wherever export is credited below the retail tariff.

**Net metering is poor or unavailable.** Several DISCOMs have shifted to gross metering, imposed low export rates, or capped sanctioned export. If your surplus earns ₹2.50 while your import costs ₹8.20, self-consumption through a battery is worth ₹5.70 per unit and the economics change materially.

Now the cases where it does not pay. A stable urban grid with fewer than four outage hours a month and clean 1:1 net metering leaves almost nothing for the battery to do. It cycles rarely, ages anyway, and reaches end of life having saved less than it cost. A daytime-heavy load profile, which describes most offices and many shops, is worse still, because those loads already match generation and an on-grid inverter serves them at a much lower price. Put the ₹2.5 Lakh you saved on the battery into more panels instead.

There is a middle path. Buy a hybrid inverter now with a small pack, or none at all, and add capacity in year three when your consumption pattern is known and cell prices have fallen further. Most hybrid units, including the QBH 5KS48P, run on grid and solar with the battery terminals unpopulated. You pay the inverter premium up front and defer the large expense.

> **What most buyers get wrong.** They compare hybrid inverter prices across five brands for a week, then accept whatever battery the installer bundles. The inverter is a ₹58,000 decision with a datasheet. The battery is a ₹3 Lakh decision with a cycle life claim that almost nobody verifies. Spend your scrutiny where the money is.

## PM Surya Ghar treatment for hybrid systems

The scheme rules matter here because they are widely misdescribed by sellers. The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana central subsidy reaches ₹78,000 and is capped at the 3 kW band. A 3 kW system, a 5 kW system, and a 10 kW system all receive the same ₹78,000. There is no additional central payout for larger arrays.

Three conditions apply. The system must be grid connected, which a hybrid is, so storage does not disqualify you. The inverter model must be ALMM listed. And the work must go through a vendor empanelled on the national portal, with application, inspection, and net meter installation completed in sequence before the subsidy is released.

What the scheme does not do is fund the battery. The subsidy is calculated on the grid connected solar system. Adding 10 kWh of LFP increases your bill by ₹2.4 Lakh to ₹3.2 Lakh and increases your subsidy by nothing. In effective terms, that means the subsidy covers roughly 22% of an on-grid system at the 3 kW band and closer to 12% of a comparable hybrid. A handful of states run separate storage incentives, so check your DISCOM's current circular before you assume none exists.

## How to read a hybrid quote without getting caught

Ask for five separate numbers: inverter, battery, panels, balance of system, and labour with liaison. Any quote presented as one lump sum hides which component the margin sits on, and in hybrid quotes it usually sits on the battery.

Then check three specifics. Confirm the battery's usable kWh, not its nameplate kWh, because a 10 kWh lead acid bank is a 5 kWh product. Confirm the cycle life figure and the depth of discharge it is quoted at, since 6,000 cycles at 80% and 6,000 cycles at 100% are different claims. Confirm that your exact battery model appears on the inverter's supported list, in writing, because mismatched pairs are the most common cause of warranty disputes in Indian storage installs.
