**The best 15 kW solar inverter for an installer is the one whose two trackers carry the strings of the module you are actually fitting, and whose voltage window suits your string length.** All five units compared here are three-phase, on-grid and rated at 15 kW, so power does not separate them. Tracker current does: it runs from 20 A to 32 A across the five datasheets.

That matters more with today's larger modules. A 700 Wp class module runs near 17 A, so two paralleled strings overload most 15 kW trackers. Units that look equal on a quotation stop being equal once you do the string arithmetic.

## What should an installer check before buying a 15 kW inverter?

Eight checks decide a 15 kW purchase. Run every shortlisted model through them before you look at brand or price.

1. **MPPT count and per-MPPT current against your module current.** Compare the module's operating current (Imp) to the tracker's maximum input current, and the module's short-circuit current (Isc) to the tracker's short-circuit rating. A strict design keeps strings per tracker multiplied by module current under both limits. See [dual versus single MPPT](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/) for when a second tracker earns its place.
2. **DC window against string length.** The cold-morning open-circuit voltage must stay under the maximum. The hot-afternoon operating voltage must stay above the lower edge of the MPPT window, and the unit has to start at all.
3. **1000 V or 1100 V maximum.** Read it together with the MPPT window and the connector rating, because the figure on the front page is not the voltage you design to. The section on voltage below shows how.
4. **Three-phase output and your connection limits.** A three-phase unit feeds all three phases, so the phase-balance question that dogs large single-phase inverters does not arise. What remains is sanctioned load, the capacity ceiling for your consumer category, and any inspection threshold set by your state. Our [three-phase inverter guide](/blog/3-phase-solar-inverter/) covers the connection side, and the [state-wise inspection thresholds](/blog/ceig-inspection-solar-plant-inverter-checks/) are listed separately. Neither is a figure we can give you for your DISCOM.
5. **Enclosure rating, cooling and weight.** IP65 and IP66 both claim dust-tight enclosures, and IP66 covers stronger water jets. Fan-cooled units add a part that can fail. Read the [IP65 versus IP66 limits](/blog/ip65-vs-ip66-solar-inverters-weather-protection-guide/) before you decide a rooftop wall or a terrace is acceptable.
6. **Communication and monitoring.** What is built in, what needs a paid accessory, and whether your client can see it on a phone.
7. **DISCOM acceptance of the exact model.** India has no national approved list, and acceptance is decided per DISCOM, per model and per revision, as the [DISCOM approval guide](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) explains. Treat every standards list below as evidence you will be asked for, not as approval.
8. **Service route.** Who attends a fault at your customer's site, and who pays for freight and labour. None of the datasheets answers this. Get it in writing.

## How many strings does each 15 kW inverter take? A worked check

The same two illustrative modules, run through each of the five inverters, show where the units split. The first module is illustrative, not a product. Assume a 700 Wp module with Voc 48.6 V, Vmp 40.3 V, Isc 18.3 A, Imp 17.4 A, and a -0.26% per °C coefficient applied to both voltages. A second illustrative module, a 545 Wp class with Isc 13.9 A and Imp 13.0 A, shows the smaller-current case.

**Strict rule.** Strings per tracker times Imp must stay at or under the tracker's input current, and strings times Isc at or under its short-circuit rating. Some designers accept a few per cent over on input current. If you do, get the brand's written tolerance first.

| Inverter | 700 Wp class: strings allowed on tracker 1 and 2 | 545 Wp class: strings allowed on tracker 1 and 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growatt MID 15KTL3-X | 1 and 1 | 2 and 2 |
| GoodWe GW15K-SDT-30 | 1 and 1 (one string per tracker on the sheet) | 1 and 1 (one string per tracker on the sheet) |
| Qbits QB-15KTLC | 1 and 1; Isc not checkable | 1 and 2; Isc not checkable |
| Solis S5-GR3P15K | 1 and 1 | 2 and 2 |
| Sungrow SG15RT | 1 and 1 | 1 and 1 (two strings would be 4% over 25 A) |

The honest reading is that the units split by roof. A 15 kW array of 700 Wp modules is about 26 modules, laid out as 13 plus 13 on two trackers, and that works on all five. GoodWe's 22 A trackers carry one 17.4 A string each, and its sheet prints one string per tracker, so two 545 Wp strings (26.0 A) would exceed both 22 A and the string limit. With 545 Wp modules, Growatt and Solis accept two strings on each tracker, Qbits accepts three strings in total, and Sungrow and GoodWe accept one per tracker under the strict rule.

String length for the 700 Wp case, at a 5 °C design minimum and 65 °C cell temperature:

- Cold Voc per module: 48.6 x (1 + 0.0026 x 20) = 51.1 V.
- Hot Vmp per module: 40.3 x (1 - 0.0026 x 40) = 36.1 V.
- A 13-module string reads 664 V cold and 469 V hot. That sits inside every window above, including GoodWe's 140 to 1000 V.
- A 5-module string reads about 181 V hot, which is no margin over a 180 V floor. Six modules read 217 V hot.

Run your own module and your site's coldest recorded temperature through the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/) before you accept a layout. Assumptions here are illustrative, and your module datasheet governs.

## Which DC voltage should you design to: 1000 V or 1100 V?

Design to the top of the MPPT window, not to the 1100 V maximum on the front page. That maximum is what the unit can survive, and the MPPT window stops at 1000 V on all five sheets.

At 5 °C the 700 Wp example gives 971 V for a 19-module string, under 1000 V, and 1022 V for 20, over it. At -10 °C, the 19-module string reads 1007 V. That passes a 1100 V rating but is outside every 1000 V window, and on the GoodWe and Sungrow sheets the unit goes to standby above 1000 V. For hill-station or north India sites with sub-zero mornings, the extra 100 V of headroom is useful, but it only protects the unit. It does not make the string productive above 1000 V.

Check the DC connector's own voltage rating before you rely on any system voltage above 1000 V, and keep cold Voc under the lower of the maximum DC voltage and the connector rating.

## Which 15 kW unit suits which installer?

The picks below are conditional readings of the datasheets. They do not rank quality or reliability.

**Growatt MID 15KTL3-X** suits an installer using large modules who wants the highest quoted European efficiency here (98.6%) and room for two strings per tracker on 545 Wp class modules. It trades that for IP65, 23 kg and the largest enclosure by volume on these five sheets.

**GoodWe GW15K-SDT-30** suits an installer who wants a fanless unit (natural convection, under 30 dB printed) in IP66 at 16.2 kg, a wide 140 to 1000 V window, integrated string current monitoring, Modbus-RTU and Modbus-TCP on the sheet, and low winter temperatures (its sheet lists -30 C). It trades that for one string per tracker, Type III surge protection as standard (Type II optional), AFCI as an option, and no maximum DC power row or standards list. Its 22 A trackers carry one string of a 700 Wp class module but not two of a 545 Wp class module.

**Qbits QB-15KTLC** suits an installer who wants IP66 and a 1100 V maximum in the lightest unit here, with Wi-Fi as standard, and whose layout fits one string per tracker or a 1 plus 2 split. Its limits on the published sheet are the missing short-circuit rating, no AFCI row and no string-monitoring row. Its datasheet does not establish the warranty terms or the service route. Ask Qbits for both in writing, as you would from any brand.

**Solis S5-GR3P15K** suits an installer who needs the most string capacity (32 A trackers, four strings in total) in an IP66 unit, using an India-specific sheet that lists IS 16169 and IS 16221. It weighs 20 kg and is fan cooled, and its AFCI needs activation.

**Sungrow SG15RT** suits an installer who wants a longer protection list on the sheet (AFCI, string current monitoring, Type II surge protection, C5 corrosion class) for a coastal or humid site, and who can live with IP65, 21 kg and 25 A trackers.

## What do the 15 kW datasheets not tell you?

The five documents are the brands' own papers, and some are older than the unit you will be handed. Solis's is dated August 2022, GoodWe's India sheet carries file token 20250814 and Sungrow's is version 18. The Qbits file does not print a date. Ask each supplier to confirm that the sheet governs the exact model and regional variant in the quote.

Nothing here covers price, stock, warranty length or remedy, certificate validity, DISCOM acceptance or firmware. Those decide the purchase too. Request the same documents from every seller:

| Request | What it settles |
| --- | --- |
| Datasheet for the exact model code and region | Current limits for the unit in the quote |
| Short-circuit current per MPPT in writing | Whether your module passes the Isc check |
| Current certificates for the model | Evidence your DISCOM may ask for |
| DISCOM acceptance in writing | That the model is accepted by the utility that will connect it |
