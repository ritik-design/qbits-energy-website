**Short version.** Get two exact model numbers intended for the same job, put their datasheets side by side against your design, then compare written India warranty terms and two installed quotes on identical scope. That process decides it. The brand names do not.

**Disclosure.** This is a buyer's comparison published by Qbits, which also sells inverters. It does not claim independent field testing or an audited service ranking for either competitor, and it does not rank them against each other.

## Why the brand level question does not work

Both Sungrow and Growatt sell multiple inverter families in India, spanning residential and commercial capacities, on-grid and hybrid architectures, single and three phase.

That means a statement like "Sungrow is premium and Growatt is mid market" compares positioning rather than products, and positioning tells you nothing about whether a specific model has the MPPT count your roof needs or supports the battery you intend to buy.

It also means the comparison changes entirely depending on what you are buying. Two models can be close on one project and not comparable at all on the next.

So the useful question is never which brand. It is which of these two specific models better fits this specific design, and are both quotes covering the same work.

## Comparing on-grid models

Check each model's rated and maximum AC output, phase, PV voltage window, current per tracker, and the operating temperature and derating information.

Be careful with efficiency figures. A peak efficiency number describes one operating point under favourable conditions. Weighted efficiency, where published, reflects performance across a realistic spread of loads and is the more useful figure. In practice the gap between two current generation inverters is usually smaller than the effect of a well or badly designed string layout.

For a roof with more than one orientation, count independent MPP trackers rather than DC connectors. The [single versus dual MPPT guide](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/) explains why that distinction matters, and the [sizing guide](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) covers the string checks that constrain both candidates equally.

Ask each installer to document string allocation and current per tracker against the model they are proposing. If only one of them does, that tells you something useful about the quotes as well as the inverters.

## Comparing hybrid models

If a battery is part of the purchase, this section usually decides the outcome.

Obtain each brand's current approved compatibility document for the exact battery pack and firmware version you intend to use. Then match nominal and operating voltage across the full state of charge range, maximum charge and discharge current in both directions, continuous backup output, surge capability with its duration, and transfer behaviour.

A CAN or RS485 connector on both devices does not establish approved BMS operation. Those are interface standards describing wiring and signalling, not proof that two products exchange meaningful instructions.

The [hybrid buying guide](/blog/best-hybrid-solar-inverter-india-2026/) sets out the common evaluation framework, and the [battery sizing worksheet](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/) starts from your loads rather than from either brand.

## Warranty and service in India

Ask each authorised seller for the written warranty document rather than a duration quoted in conversation. Read the remedy, whether labour and transport are covered, the registration requirement, and the exclusions.

Then ask a more specific question than most buyers do: which service location would handle a claim at my address, and what is the process. A national centre count is a marketing figure. The route that serves your district is the thing that determines how a failure in year seven actually goes.

Document the model, the invoice and the commissioning results at handover. These matter if the installer and the manufacturer later need to establish where responsibility sits. Global shipment rankings are not included here, because they do not inform a model level decision for a single project.

## Making two quotes genuinely comparable

Most brand comparisons are decided by a price difference that is not real, because the two offers cover different work.

Before comparing totals, confirm both quotes state the same array: identical module model, count and layout. Confirm both assume the same mounting structure and the same cable runs, since a longer AC run changes both cost and voltage rise. Confirm both include or both exclude the DISCOM application, meter charges, protection equipment, monitoring hardware and commissioning.

Then check what each one excludes. Exclusions are where quotes diverge most, and they are usually listed less prominently than inclusions.

Only once the scope matches does the remaining difference tell you anything about the inverters. At that point you are comparing two products. Before it, you are comparing two documents.

## Scheme and utility requirements

Use the exact inverter certificates and grid interface documentation applicable to your project. The [MNRE ALMM page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/) concerns module and cell lists and should not be presented as a universal inverter brand approval.

Check current utility and scheme conditions with the installer, and confirm which party is responsible for satisfying them. Where a requirement is not met, the practical question is who bears the cost of correcting it, and that is a contract question rather than a product one.
