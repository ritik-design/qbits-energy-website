## Why Installers Look for a Scanifly Alternative

Scanifly carved out a strong niche by leading with drone-based 3D capture. The output is high fidelity, the as-built models are excellent, and installers who already run a drone fleet got a clear workflow. Most installers shopping for an alternative are not unhappy with Scanifly's accuracy. They are unhappy with the operational overhead, the per-project economics, and the fact that Scanifly only does one part of the job.

### Drone dependency

Every Scanifly project requires drone capture, which requires a pilot, FAA Part 107 compliance in the US (or local equivalents), insurance, weather windows, and travel. For a 100-system-per-year installer, that is real money and real calendar friction. Teams that try to scale find drone ops becomes a bottleneck.

### Per-project pricing

Scanifly's pricing model centres on per-project fees. That is fine at low volume and gets painful at high volume. Installers crossing 100 projects a year typically pay several times what a flat per-seat license costs for the rest of the [solar design platform](https://surgepv.com/solar-designing).

### Measurement-only scope

Scanifly is a measurement product. Once the 3D model exists, the design, 8,760-hour shading, financial model, BOQ, single-line diagram, AutoCAD export, and proposal happen in other tools. Each handoff is a copy-paste step.

### Site-visit cost for residential

For residential rooftops, satellite imagery is now sharp enough that AI-driven 3D modelling reaches drone-grade accuracy on most homes. Paying a drone fee plus a pilot's time for a 6 kW residential system rarely pencils out.
