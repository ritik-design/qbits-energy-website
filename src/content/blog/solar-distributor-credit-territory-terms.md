**Supplier credit and territory exclusivity are separate commercial terms.** Credit decides when approved invoices must be paid and how much unpaid exposure is allowed. Territory wording decides where, what, and to whom a distributor may sell, plus whether any channel protection applies. Neither term follows automatically from the title `Distributor`.

Review them together because they can pull cash in opposite directions. A wider or protected territory may come with stock, coverage, reporting, or target conditions. Credit may reduce the immediate cash needed for approved orders, but it does not remove inventory risk, downstream collection risk, or payment liability.

> **The practical answer:** do not negotiate only for more days or a bigger map. Define the credit calculation, territory scope, exceptions, performance test, change process, and exit treatment. Then run the proposed terms through the same downside cash-flow scenario.

This guide is an evaluation framework. It does not describe Qbits commercial terms and is not legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice.

## Start with two separate term sheets

Prepare one term sheet for credit and another for territory before looking at the agreement as a whole. This stops an attractive label in one section from hiding an expensive condition elsewhere.

The credit sheet should answer:

- which legal entity receives credit;
- which orders, products, invoices, taxes, and freight count towards the facility;
- the approved limit and available headroom calculation;
- when the payment clock starts and when an invoice becomes due;
- which security, guarantee, deposit, insurance, or bank document applies;
- how payments and credit notes are allocated;
- how disputed invoices affect usage and suspension;
- who can change, reduce, suspend, or withdraw the facility; and
- what becomes payable when the appointment ends.

The territory sheet should answer:

- the exact geography and boundary test;
- covered products, customer types, sales channels, and project types;
- whether the appointment is exclusive, non-exclusive, sole, or conditional;
- reserved accounts, direct sales, tenders, marketplaces, and existing customers;
- how inbound and cross-territory enquiries are assigned;
- what counts towards any target;
- which conditions keep channel protection active; and
- what happens to registered leads, quotations, orders, stock, and customers after a change.

If either sheet points to a schedule, policy, email, portal, or price list, record its title, version, effective date, and change process. `Standard terms apply` is not enough information for a cash-flow model.

## Map the territory before discussing exclusivity

A territory is not complete until its geography, products, customers, channels, exceptions, and attribution rule are all defined. A state name alone does not settle who can quote a national account, an online lead, or a project delivered across a district boundary.

Use a scope map like this:

| Scope element | Question to resolve in writing | Why it changes the economics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography | State, district, pin code, named accounts, or another boundary? | Determines the actual addressable market and fulfilment cost |
| Product scope | All current models, named families, or a schedule that can change? | A protected area may cover only part of the proposed stock |
| Customer scope | Dealers, EPCs, installers, resellers, projects, or end customers? | Two partners may operate in one area without serving the same customer class |
| Sales channel | Offline, direct sales, tenders, marketplaces, ecommerce, or referrals? | A geographic right may not cover every route to market |
| Attribution | Billing address, delivery address, installation site, lead origin, or registered opportunity? | Determines which sale counts and which partner receives protection |
| Reserved business | Existing accounts, national accounts, government orders, OEM supply, or strategic projects? | Exceptions can materially reduce the commercial value of the map |
| Cross-border process | Can the distributor sell outside the area or fulfil an inbound request from elsewhere? | Affects lead handling, freight, commission, and conflict risk |
| Change control | Who can redraw the scope, on what evidence, with what notice? | Determines whether current stock and pipeline remain protected |

The word `exclusive` still needs a second layer. Exclusivity may apply only to a product, channel, customer segment, period, or performance state. It can also coexist with named exceptions.

Use four status labels in your internal model:

1. **Proposed:** discussed but not yet approved in authorised documents.
2. **Active:** all stated start conditions have been met.
3. **Conditional:** protection continues only while defined requirements are satisfied.
4. **Changed or ended:** the agreement says what happens to pipeline, stock, customer support, data, and brand use.

Do not call a market `exclusive` in sales material while its status is proposed, conditional on an unmet requirement, suspended, or expired.

## Build the cash-flow model without inventing credit norms

**No universal credit period or limit can replace a dated cash-flow model.** The useful question is not `What do distributors normally get?` It is `What funding gap does this proposed operating plan create, and which written facility actually covers it?`

Start with the distributor's own operating inputs. Leave every supplier-specific field blank until an authorised proposal supplies it.

| Input | Evidence to use | Do not substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening stock | Proposed SKU quantities and landed invoice values | A round investment target |
| Replenishment | Order trigger, lead time, minimum order, and demand scenario | Hope that stock will sell before payment |
| Freight and handling | Current route quotations and insurance terms | Product price alone |
| Downstream collections | Customer terms, actual collection pattern, advances, and overdue cases | Invoice date as assumed cash receipt |
| Returns and damage | Written eligibility, approval, freight, valuation, and credit-note timing | A verbal `return support` statement |
| Supplier credit | Approved limit, eligible exposure, due-date rule, and security | A requested facility or sales discussion |
| Existing finance | Current usable cash and sanctioned facilities at one date | Undrawn requests or future investor money |
| Taxes | Treatment confirmed by the accountant for the actual transaction | Gross and net figures mixed in one model |

One useful funding view is:

`Peak operating funding gap = stock at landed cost + downstream receivables + unrecovered variable costs - customer advances - supplier credit actually available`

This is a planning formula, not a Qbits outcome. Define every input on the same date and tax basis. Run at least a base case and a downside case using your own evidence. The downside case should test slower customer collections, delayed stock movement, rejected returns, credit withdrawal, or a territory change.

### Calculate credit usage the way the document calculates it

A stated limit has little meaning until the usage formula is known. Ask whether usage includes:

- unpaid invoices only, or accepted orders as well;
- taxes, freight, debit notes, interest, and disputed amounts;
- related companies or multiple branches under one limit;
- goods in transit;
- payments not yet allocated; and
- approved credit notes not yet posted.

Your worksheet can use:

`Available headroom = approved limit - exposure counted under the agreement`

Do not add an order merely because a spreadsheet shows headroom. Confirm that the supplier has accepted it, the relevant products are eligible, no suspension applies, and the delivery will not make another invoice due before downstream cash arrives.

## Review the payment clock, not just the number of days

A payment period is incomplete without a start event, due-date rule, document requirements, and treatment of disputes. The same number of days can create different cash dates depending on whether it starts from invoice, dispatch, delivery, acceptance, installation, or another event.

Ask the commercial and finance reviewers to mark each item as confirmed, unresolved, or unacceptable:

| Credit question | What the written term should resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Start event | Which dated event starts the payment period? |
| Due date | How are weekends, holidays, partial deliveries, or missing documents handled? |
| Payment allocation | Which invoice is closed when one payment covers several balances? |
| Disputes | Must the undisputed amount be paid, and does the disputed amount consume limit? |
| Credit notes | When do returns, rebates, or corrections restore headroom? |
| Security | Amount, form, issuing party, invocation conditions, expiry, return, and release |
| Consequence of delay | Interest, order hold, facility suspension, acceleration, or another stated remedy |
| Review | Evidence, frequency, decision authority, notice, and effective date |
| Withdrawal | Treatment of accepted orders, goods in transit, outstanding invoices, and security |
| Termination | Final payment, set-off, reconciliation, surviving obligations, and document release |

Also separate **order acceptance** from **credit approval**. A credit facility does not necessarily require a supplier to accept every purchase order. The order process should identify availability, allocation, price, delivery, cancellation, and acceptance authority.

## Test exclusivity against the target formula

**Conditional exclusivity is only measurable when the target formula and consequence process are written.** A target can refer to orders, invoices, deliveries, payments, downstream sales, active dealers, or another measure. Those are not interchangeable.

For each target, record:

- unit or value basis;
- product and customer scope;
- measurement period;
- order, invoice, delivery, payment, or downstream-sale event;
- treatment of taxes, freight, returns, cancellations, shortages, and credit notes;
- evidence source and dispute process;
- seasonality or force-majeure treatment if agreed;
- review date and amendment authority;
- notice and cure process; and
- the exact consequence of a miss.

Then connect the target to cash. A target measured on invoices can increase stock and payment exposure before downstream customers have paid. A target measured on supplier receipts can be affected by allocation or dispatch delays. A target measured on paid invoices makes collection timing part of territory protection.

The point is not to remove every condition. It is to make the condition measurable and fundable.

## Run five tabletop scenarios before signing

Before signing, test what happens when the plan does not run perfectly. Use the actual draft documents and trace these scenarios from trigger to final accounting entry.

### A large customer pays late

Which supplier invoices still fall due? Does the facility remain available? What security can be used? Who approves a temporary change, and does late customer payment affect the territory target?

### Stock becomes slow-moving after a model change

Can unopened stock be returned, exchanged, transferred, or sold during a defined transition? Who sets the valuation? When does any credit note restore credit headroom?

### A direct or national-account opportunity appears in the territory

Is the account reserved? Who owns the quotation and customer communication? Does the transaction count towards the distributor's target, and is any fulfilment or referral arrangement defined?

### The performance target is missed

Is protection reduced immediately, after notice, or after a cure period? Does the appointment continue on a non-exclusive basis? What happens to registered leads and accepted orders?

### The credit facility is reduced or withdrawn

Which accepted orders continue? Are all outstanding invoices accelerated? How are deposits, guarantees, credit notes, returns, and open disputes reconciled? Can the existing territory obligation still be performed with the revised funding?

If the documents produce different answers, list the conflict and identify which document controls. Do not solve it by choosing the most favourable interpretation.

## Keep the three commercial owners separate

Channel, finance, and legal reviewers answer different parts of this decision. One person's email should not silently override another team's authorised document.

| Owner | Decision to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Channel or sales | Role, market plan, products, customers, targets, lead process, and coverage expectations |
| Finance or credit | Limit, usage, due dates, security, payment allocation, suspension, and reconciliation |
| Legal or authorised signatory | Contract hierarchy, rights, exceptions, changes, termination, remedies, and signature authority |

Add tax, accounting, insurance, and competition-law advice where the actual structure needs it. This article cannot assess whether a particular restriction, security document, guarantee, or remedy is suitable or enforceable.

## What Qbits currently publishes

**As of 26 September 2026, the public Qbits partner route invites dealers and distributors to describe their business and discuss territory and commercial terms.** Its application asks for current working capital, bank loans, cash-credit limits, other capital, hypothecated bank, infrastructure, team, and proposed sales commitment.

Those fields do not publish a Qbits credit offer or an approval threshold. The public page does not state a credit limit, payment period, security requirement, exclusive territory, target-linked protection, margin, stock-return right, acceptance rule, or response time.

If wider downstream supply is your intended role, first use the [Qbits distributor registration walkthrough](/blog/become-qbits-distributor/) to prepare an accurate operating file. Do not spend against an expected territory or facility while the application is under review.

## Prepare one discussion pack

Bring one controlled pack to the commercial discussion:

1. Distributor legal entity and authorised contacts.
2. Proposed territory scope map with customer and channel boundaries.
3. Product and stock plan with base and downside demand cases.
4. Dated cash-flow model with blank supplier-term inputs clearly marked.
5. Requested credit structure and the calculation supporting it.
6. Target definition, evidence source, review date, and cure proposal.
7. Reserved-account, direct-sale, tender, online, and cross-territory questions.
8. Slow-stock, return, credit-note, security-release, and exit scenarios.
9. List of documents and schedules that must be supplied before commitment.
10. Internal legal, finance, tax, accounting, and operational review owners.

When that pack reflects your current business rather than hoped-for support, use the [official Qbits partner application](/become-our-partner/) and select `Distributor`. Submission begins a review. Confirm every credit, territory, exclusivity, target, stock, return, and exit term in the current authorised documents before making commitments.
