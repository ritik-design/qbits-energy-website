# Qbits Energy — 12-Month Off-Page SEO & Link Acquisition Strategy

> 100% white-hat. Built for an Indian brand with a moderate marketing budget (~₹6–10L/year for PR + content + outreach). Pair with the on-page SEO framework.

---

# Strategic North Star

Qbits' link profile in month 12 should look like:
- **150–250 referring domains** (from ~5–15 today)
- **Mix:** 40% editorial/PR · 25% guest posts · 15% directories & gov · 10% YouTube/podcast · 10% partner network
- **Median DA of referring domains:** 35+
- **Indian TLDs:** 70%+ (.in, .co.in)
- **No PBN, no link-farms, no fiverr packages.** One single Google penalty erases 18 months of work.

# 1. Digital PR Campaigns (3 Link-Earning Assets)

The principle: build something journalists and bloggers *want to reference.* Each asset should earn 30–80 referring domains over 12 months.

## Campaign 1 — "India's State-wise Solar Payback Calculator"

**The asset:** An interactive tool at `/calculator/state-payback` that lets a user input their state + monthly bill and returns: payback period, total 25-year savings, CO₂ offset, subsidy applicable. Pre-loaded with current DISCOM tariff data for all 28 states.

**Why it earns links:** Every solar/personal-finance/sustainability article needs a "see how much you'd save" embed. Calculators are the most-linked content type in any vertical because they save journalists from doing math.

**Distribution play:**
- Pitch as exclusive embed to *ET Energy World, Mongabay India, Mercom India, Saur Energy*
- Open-license the embed code (with attribution link) — homepages and blogs will embed it
- Annual update press release each February (post-Union-Budget tariff changes)

**Expected outcome:** 40–80 referring domains in 12 months; long-tail rank for "solar payback calculator India" and "solar savings calculator [state]."

## Campaign 2 — "The India Rooftop Solar Index" (annual research report)

**The asset:** A 30-page annual PDF research report analyzing rooftop solar adoption across 50 Indian cities. Data points: installation cost trends, average payback by city, subsidy disbursal speed by DISCOM, top-performing installer networks. Sourced from Qbits' own installer network data + RTI requests to DISCOMs + public MNRE filings.

**Why it earns links:** Original Indian solar data is rare. *Mercom*, *Bridge to India*, and *CEEW* are the only credible sources today and they cost money. A free, well-designed, methodologically transparent annual report becomes citation-default.

**Distribution play:**
- Tease 5 headline findings as a press release ("Tier-2 cities now pay back solar 14 months faster than Tier-1")
- Send embargoed copy to 15 business-desk journalists 48 hours before public release
- Submit findings to MNRE press secretary + IREDA
- LinkedIn long-form post by founder
- Permanent landing page with download form (lead capture bonus)

**Expected outcome:** 50–100 referring domains lifetime; rapid Bridge-to-India-style authority signal; ongoing citations.

## Campaign 3 — "Diesel Generator Replacement Tracker"

**The asset:** A live dashboard showing how many DG-hours have been displaced by Qbits-installed systems across India — by state, by industry. Updated monthly with anonymized fleet data. Frames solar as *fuel displacement,* not just bill savings — angle no competitor owns.

**Why it earns links:** Sustainability journalists, ESG publications, and industrial trade media have been searching for hard data on diesel displacement for years. Provide it, and become the cited source.

**Distribution play:**
- Quarterly press release ("Qbits-installed systems displaced 3.2M liters of diesel in Q1 2026")
- Pitch to *Down To Earth, Mongabay, Carbon Copy, IndiaSpend* (climate desks)
- Open API + embeddable widget for trade publications

**Expected outcome:** 30–60 referring domains; positions Qbits as climate-credible, not just product-focused.

# 6. Broken Link Building

**Method:** Use Ahrefs/Semrush "broken backlinks" report on competitor domains + use the Wayback Machine to find dead URLs that other sites still link to. Offer Qbits replacement content.

**Categories of dead links to hunt:**

1. **Discontinued competitor product pages** — Sungrow/Growatt frequently kill old model URLs; press articles still link to them. Offer comparable Qbits SKU page.
2. **Dead subsidy info pages** — old MNRE scheme URLs (Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission, etc.) are linked from hundreds of blog articles. Offer Qbits' current `/subsidy/pm-surya-ghar` hub.
3. **Removed calculator/tool pages** — once-popular EPC startup calculators that 404 today. Offer Qbits' calculator.
4. **Broken case study links** — defunct solar EPCs that have closed. Offer Qbits replacement case study.
5. **Outdated state-policy pages** — pre-2023 DISCOM net-metering policy URLs. Offer current Qbits state hubs.

**Workflow:** Ahrefs → Site Explorer → Broken Backlinks → filter outbound from .edu/.gov/.org → contact webmaster with "noticed your link is broken, here's a current replacement" email.

**Conversion rate:** 5–10% — but the links are high quality. Aim 8–12 placements in 12 months.

# What NOT to do

- **Never buy links.** Fiverr, gigs, "1000 backlinks for ₹5,000" — all penalty bait.
- **No PBNs** (private blog networks). Google detects pattern-based footprints.
- **No exact-match anchor stuffing.** 70%+ branded/natural anchors; <15% exact-match.
- **No commenting on random blogs** with link in body. Dead tactic since 2015.
- **No bulk press release distribution** to 500 generic sites. Targeted outreach beats spray-and-pray every time.
- **Never disavow without certainty.** Bad disavows have killed otherwise healthy sites.
- **Don't chase DR for its own sake.** A DR-30 niche solar blog beats a DR-70 generic directory.
