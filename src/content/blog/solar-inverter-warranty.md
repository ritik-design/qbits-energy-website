> **Quick answers**
>
> - Duration is the headline. Remedy, cost liability and exclusions decide what you actually receive.
> - Full unit replacement, repair and parts only are three different promises.
> - Labour, freight and reinstallation are frequently excluded on rooftop claims.
> - The start date can be invoice, commissioning or registration, and the difference can cost months.
> - A product warranty does not cover installation workmanship. That is a separate contract.
> - Ask for the written policy before paying, not after a fault.

**Short version.** Compare warranties on four things: what remedy you get, who pays to deliver it, what voids it, and when the clock starts. A shorter term with a clear replacement remedy and stated cost cover can be worth more than a longer term with vague remedies.

## Why the headline number tells you least

Inverter warranty length became a competitive marketing variable, and duration is the easiest thing to advertise and the hardest to interpret.

Two products can both advertise a long term and deliver entirely different outcomes. One replaces the whole unit and ships it at the manufacturer's cost. The other repairs at a service centre, expects you to fund removal and freight, and excludes several of the failure modes that actually occur on Indian rooftops.

The number of years is the start of the comparison, not the conclusion.

## The five terms that decide a claim

| Term | The question to ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start date and registration | Invoice, commissioning or registration? Is registration mandatory within a window? | Storage and delay can consume months of cover |
| Covered faults | Which defects and which components? | Determines whether your specific failure qualifies |
| Remedy | Full unit replacement, repair, or parts only? | The practical difference on a rooftop is large |
| Cost liability | Who pays labour, removal, freight and reinstallation? | Often the biggest uncovered expense |
| Exclusions and process | What voids cover, and what are the claim steps and timelines? | Decides whether a valid fault becomes a paid claim |

Note that a panel performance warranty, a battery capacity warranty and an inverter product warranty are three unrelated instruments. They cover different equipment, use different measures, and are frequently conflated in sales conversations. Do not carry an assumption from one to another.

## Replacement, repair and parts are not the same promise

This distinction deserves separating out because it is where expectation and document most often diverge.

**Full unit replacement** means a working unit is supplied in place of the failed one. For the owner this is the cleanest outcome, because downtime depends mostly on logistics rather than diagnosis and repair turnaround.

**Repair** means the unit is fixed, often at a service centre. That introduces removal, transport in both directions, queue time and reinstallation. The system is down throughout, and generation lost during that window is rarely compensated.

**Parts only** means a component is supplied and somebody else pays for the labour to fit it.

A warranty document that uses the phrase "at the manufacturer's option" is reserving the choice. That is common and not inherently unreasonable, but you should read it as "repair is possible" rather than assuming replacement.

## The costs that catch people out

On a residential rooftop, the replacement unit is often not the largest line item in a claim.

A site visit, safe isolation, removal from a wall at height, packing and freight to a service location, return freight, reinstallation, recommissioning and any DISCOM notification all carry cost. A warranty that covers the hardware and excludes the rest leaves those with you.

Ask for a written answer to one specific question: if this unit fails in year seven, what will I pay? A supplier who can answer that clearly is describing a warranty they understand. One who cannot is describing a headline.

## Start dates and registration traps

Cover that begins at invoice behaves differently from cover that begins at commissioning.

Equipment frequently sits between purchase and installation while approvals are processed. If the clock started at invoice, that period is deducted from your protection without any generation in return.

Separately, some warranties require registration within a defined window, and treat failure to register as a reduction or loss of cover. If registration is mandatory, do it immediately and keep the confirmation with the invoice.

## Exclusions that actually bite in India

Read the exclusions with your own site in mind rather than as boilerplate.

- **Surge and lightning.** Frequently excluded or limited, and a genuine risk on Indian distribution networks. Confirm whether surge protection is required for cover to remain valid, and whether its absence voids the claim.
- **Grid abnormality.** Sustained over voltage or under voltage outside the rated window may be excluded even though the inverter is the component that fails.
- **Water and dust ingress.** Usually excluded where the installation did not follow the specified mounting and clearance instructions.
- **Unauthorised opening or repair.** Opening the enclosure commonly voids cover outright.
- **Environmental conditions.** Operating outside the rated ambient range, or in a location the manual excludes, can remove cover.
- **Installation defects.** Cable sizing, protection selection and mounting errors belong to the installer, not the manufacturer.

## Manufacturer and installer responsibility are separate

Most disputed claims are really arguments about category rather than about the fault.

A product warranty addresses manufacturing defects in the product. An installer contract addresses workmanship, design choices, protection, cabling and mounting. A fault caused by an undersized cable or a missing protective device is an installation matter even though the inverter is what stopped working.

Keep both documents, the commissioning test results and all later service reports. Where a fault is disputed, ask for the diagnosis in writing rather than accepting a verbal conclusion. ## Building a claim file that can be assessed

Assemble this before opening a claim.

1. Invoice with date, and the commissioning date and records.
2. Model designation and serial number read from the unit label.
3. The written warranty document that applied at the time of sale.
4. The exact fault message or event code, copied rather than paraphrased, with timestamps.
5. Photographs of the installation and the displayed fault, taken without opening the enclosure.
6. Any support ticket reference and the agreed next step.

Avoid repeatedly resetting the unit or changing protection settings to keep it running. It feels productive and it erases the fault history that would otherwise support your claim. ## Comparing competing warranties fairly

Put the candidates side by side on remedy, cost liability, exclusions and start date rather than on duration alone.

EPC procurement teams comparing fleet supply need a different level of control. Use the [bulk-buyer inverter warranty and service checklist](/blog/inverter-warranty-service-checklist-bulk-buyers/) to define RMA stages, cost allocation, spares and contract evidence.

A shorter term with full unit replacement, stated freight cover and narrow exclusions can be worth more over the life of a system than a longer term with discretionary remedies and broad exclusions. The only way to see that is to read both documents before purchase.
