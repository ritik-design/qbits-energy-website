## ICP #1 — Residential Homeowner (Primary B2C Buyer)

**Demographics:** Age 35–55, male primary decision-maker (wife strongly influences), Tier-1/Tier-2 city + emerging Tier-3, owns a 2–4 BHK independent house with rooftop, household income ₹12–40 LPA, graduate+, 1–2 ACs running, monthly electricity bill ₹4,000–₹15,000.

**Psychographics:** Status-conscious but value-driven. Risk-averse with capex but proud of "future-ready" purchases. Reads Moneycontrol, watches YouTube reviews, joins WhatsApp groups before any ₹2L+ purchase. Treats solar as a 25-year decision, not an appliance.

**Goals:** Cut electricity bill 70–90%, hedge against tariff hikes, future-proof against EV charging needs, signal modernity to neighbors, qualify for PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

**Pain Points:** Confused by 15+ inverter brands. Burned by past experiences with installers who vanished. Worried about roof leakage, panel theft, monsoon performance, and "what if the brand shuts down in 5 years?"

**Buying Triggers:** Bill crosses ₹8,000/month · neighbor installs solar · subsidy announcement · planning to buy EV · summer of intense load-shedding.

**Objections:** "Payback is too long" · "What if I sell the house?" · "Roof will leak" · "Battery is too expensive" · "Will the company exist in 10 years for warranty?"

**Decision Criteria:** (in rank order) Trust in brand → installer reputation → warranty length → subsidy eligibility → app/monitoring → price → aesthetics.

**Information Sources:** YouTube (Electrical India, Solar Subsidy Guru), WhatsApp society groups, Google "best solar inverter for home India," neighbors who installed, Just Dial.

**Buying Journey:** 3–6 months. Awareness (bill shock) → Research (YouTube, blogs) → Site survey (2–4 vendors) → Family discussion → Subsidy paperwork → Install.

**Budget Range:** ₹1.8L–₹5L total system; inverter share ₹35k–₹90k.

**Key Messages That Convert:** "Bill will drop from ₹10,000 to ₹800/month" · "Govt pays ₹78,000 of it" · "10-year warranty, Indian service team" · "Your kids' AC, free."

**Channels to Reach Them:** Meta ads (geo-targeted, household-income lookalikes), YouTube pre-roll, Google search ads on subsidy queries, society WhatsApp influencer kits, vernacular content (Hindi/Marathi/Tamil/Telugu).

## ICP #2 — Solar EPC Contractor / Installer (B2B Channel Partner)

**Demographics:** Age 28–45, male, owns a 3–25 person EPC firm in Tier-2/3 cities, installs 5–80 systems/month, mostly residential + small commercial (≤100 kW), often ex-electrician or engineering diploma background, runs business on WhatsApp + Tally.

**Psychographics:** Margin-obsessed and reputation-protective. One bad inverter failure can kill 6 months of referrals in a small town. Skeptical of glossy marketing — believes specs and field data. Loyal to brands that "pick up the phone" during a site issue.

**Goals:** Maintain 15–25% project margin, minimize service call-backs, win subsidy-eligible projects, build a brand reputation locally, get exclusive territory rights.

**Pain Points:** Brands dump stock then discount on Amazon (kills his margin). Slow RMA from imported brands. Customers blame him, not the brand, when an inverter fails. Distributor delays on spares. Net-metering paperwork hell.

**Buying Triggers:** Existing brand had a batch failure · brand undercut him online · competitor installer got a better deal · new product category (hybrid/battery-ready) demand · PM Surya Ghar pipeline surge.

**Objections:** "Will you protect my pricing online?" · "What's your spare-part SLA?" · "Show me 3 installer references" · "What's the dealer margin vs Growatt?" · "Do you have ALMM listing for subsidy projects?"

**Decision Criteria:** Margin → service SLA → ALMM/BIS compliance → MOQ flexibility → marketing co-op support → product reliability data → app/monitoring quality.

**Information Sources:** Distributor reps, WhatsApp EPC groups, Intersolar India, SolarQuarter events, peer installers, Saur Energy magazine.

**Buying Journey:** 2–8 weeks. Sample unit → 1–2 trial installs → field performance monitored 30–60 days → channel agreement → rolling orders.

**Budget Range:** ₹2L–₹50L/month inverter spend depending on scale.

**Key Messages That Convert:** "Channel-protected pricing — never on Amazon" · "Spare board at your door in 72 hrs or it's free" · "Co-branded site survey kit + lead share" · "Higher margin than Growatt, faster service than Sungrow."

**Channels to Reach Them:** Direct sales rep visits, Intersolar/REI booths, LinkedIn outreach, WhatsApp broadcast lists, distributor cascade, dedicated installer portal, training certifications.

## ICP #3 — Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Buyer

**Demographics:** Factory owner / school trustee / hospital admin / mall facility head, age 40–60, decision involves CFO + facility head + promoter, plant load 50 kW–2 MW, monthly bill ₹2L–₹40L, often family-owned MSME or mid-market institution.

**Psychographics:** ROI-first and risk-managed. Won't sign anything without a payback model on paper. Trusts CA/CFO inputs heavily. Worried about CAPEX vs OPEX (PPA/RESCO) trade-off. Values silent operation (hospital, school) and uptime (factory).

**Goals:** Cut power cost 30–60%, hit ESG/CSR targets, lock in tariff for 15–25 years, gain export credit (if industrial), reduce DG dependency.

**Pain Points:** Confused between CAPEX, OPEX (PPA), and lease models. Worried about DISCOM net-metering caps for >500kW. Past bad experience with developer who left site mid-project. Compliance complexity (CEIG, electrical inspector, fire NOC).

**Buying Triggers:** Tariff hike notice · diesel cost spike · ESG audit · new factory expansion · CSR mandate · bank offering green-loan rates · competitor in same industrial estate installs solar.

**Objections:** "What if my plant load drops 30%?" · "Can your inverter handle harmonics from VFDs?" · "Who owns the system in OPEX?" · "What's the IRR vs putting cash in FD?" · "Liability if my workers get shocked?"

**Decision Criteria:** Bankability of the inverter brand → IRR/payback model → O&M guarantee → harmonics/grid-quality specs → references in same industry → ALMM listing → roof structural fit.

**Information Sources:** CFO peer network, industry associations (CII, FICCI), banking RM (Tata Capital, IREDA), Saur Energy, EPC RFPs, plant engineer recommendations.

**Buying Journey:** 4–12 months. Internal need → consultant/EPC RFP → 3–5 bidder shortlist → site survey → bank financing → DISCOM approval → install (6–10 weeks).

**Budget Range:** ₹35L–₹15Cr project; inverter share ₹3L–₹1.5Cr.

**Key Messages That Convert:** "27% IRR, 3.8-year payback, bankable to SBI/IREDA" · "Industrial-grade THD <3%, handles your VFD load" · "5MW+ deployed in F&B / pharma / textile" · "Single-window EPC + brand warranty under one PO."

**Channels to Reach Them:** LinkedIn ABM, CII/FICCI events, CFO roundtables, industry-vertical case study microsites, EPC partner co-selling, banker referrals, on-site demos.

## ICP #4 — Solar Product Distributor / Dealer

**Demographics:** Age 35–55, owns a regional electrical/solar distribution firm covering 1–3 states, turnover ₹5Cr–₹150Cr, stocks 3–6 inverter brands + panels + BoS, 5–40 sales reps, family business often in 2nd generation.

**Psychographics:** Portfolio strategist. Thinks like a fund manager picking brands — wants a "challenger brand" alongside his Tier-1 anchor (Sungrow/Growatt) to capture price-sensitive volume without cannibalizing. Politically managed relationships with brand RSMs.

**Goals:** Expand product portfolio gap (e.g., needs a strong hybrid brand), increase regional market share, lock exclusive territory, get higher trade margin, secure marketing development funds (MDF).

**Pain Points:** Existing brands are slow on credit notes, dump inventory at quarter-end, give same dealership to his competitor 2 streets away, take 90+ days to settle warranty claims, no co-marketing investment in his region.

**Buying Triggers:** Anchor brand reduced his margin · competitor distributor got his territory · new category gap (hybrid/storage) · brand exited Indian market · PM Surya Ghar pipeline needs more SKUs.

**Objections:** "Territory exclusivity?" · "Credit terms — 30/60/90?" · "Will you give MDF for in-shop branding?" · "What's your QoQ volume commitment?" · "How is your service team staffed in my region?"

**Decision Criteria:** Trade margin → territory exclusivity → credit terms → MDF/marketing support → product fit in portfolio → brand owner's financial stability → ALMM listing.

**Information Sources:** Brand RSM relationships, Intersolar/REI, distributor WhatsApp groups, industry rumor mill, peer distributors in non-competing geographies.

**Buying Journey:** 1–4 months. Brand approach → portfolio gap analysis → margin/term negotiation → trial PO (₹10–25L) → 6-month performance review → annual agreement.

**Budget Range:** ₹50L–₹15Cr annual inverter purchase per brand.

**Key Messages That Convert:** "District-level exclusivity for 12 months" · "60-day credit + 2% MDF on sell-out" · "Hybrid SKU your current portfolio is missing" · "Founder-led brand with Indian manufacturing, here to stay."

**Channels to Reach Them:** Direct National Sales Head outreach, industry trade shows, brand-hosted dealer meets in resort destinations, structured channel programs, LinkedIn, distributor referral cascade.

## CROSS-ICP INSIGHTS

### The ONE universal message that resonates across all 4 ICPs

> **"Built in India, backed in India — so when something goes wrong on a Tuesday afternoon, someone actually picks up the phone."**

After-sales reliability is the single anxiety shared by the homeowner (warranty fear), installer (RMA SLA), C&I buyer (uptime), and distributor (service network). Every ICP is buying "future certainty" more than they're buying an inverter.

### Biggest language difference: Homeowner vs Installer

- **Homeowner speaks in outcomes:** "bill," "savings," "subsidy," "neighbors," "warranty years."
- **Installer speaks in inputs:** "margin," "MOQ," "RMA TAT," "ALMM," "channel-protected pricing."

The homeowner wants to be told *what they'll feel.* The installer wants to be shown *the unit economics on a spreadsheet.* Same product, two completely different vocabularies — never mix them in the same ad.

### Top 10 Emotional Triggers for Headlines & CTAs

1. **Loss aversion** — "Your bill will double by 2030. Lock your rate today."
2. **Subsidy urgency / FOMO** — "₹78,000 government subsidy ends soon."
3. **Status / neighborhood signal** — "Be the first on your street."
4. **Family security** — "Power your kids' future, literally."
5. **Made-in-India pride** — "Designed in Bengaluru, built for Indian grids."
6. **Trust through specificity** — "10-year warranty, 72-hour service SLA."
7. **Expert validation** — "Recommended by 1,200+ installers."
8. **ROI greed (B2B)** — "27% IRR. Beats every other capex on your books."
9. **Fear of obsolescence** — "Battery-ready. EV-ready. Future-ready."
10. **Anti-import skepticism** — "Why pay Tier-1 prices for Tier-3 service?"

### State-Wise ICP Variation

**GUJARAT** (mature solar market, high installer density)
1. Buyer is most price-aware — Surat/Rajkot homeowners benchmark 4–5 quotes ruthlessly.
2. Existing solar penetration high → buyers ask sophisticated questions (string sizing, MPPT count).
3. Net-metering policy is favorable and well-understood — selling point is *speed of approval*, not subsidy education.
4. Gujarati-language collateral + Surya Gujarat scheme references convert better than generic Hindi.
5. Distributor network is dense and price-competitive — channel margin compression is severe; territory exclusivity matters more than in any other state.

**UTTAR PRADESH** (high-growth, low-literacy market)
1. Buyer needs subsidy education first — most don't know PM Surya Ghar exists or how to apply.
2. Power-cut frequency is the #1 buying trigger, not bill savings → **hybrid + battery** sells better than on-grid.
3. Trust deficit is highest — buyers want to see physical office, factory photos, founder face.
4. Hindi-first content non-negotiable; English brochures get ignored.
5. Tier-3 town installers dominate; B2B strategy must reach Meerut, Gorakhpur, Varanasi — not just Lucknow/Noida.

**TAMIL NADU** (industrial-heavy, policy-complex)
1. C&I segment dominates buying — textile mills (Tiruppur, Coimbatore), auto ancillary (Chennai) drive volume more than residential.
2. TANGEDCO net-metering policy is the most restrictive in India → wheeling/banking expertise is a differentiator.
3. Tamil-language content + Tamil-speaking field sales is mandatory — non-negotiable cultural barrier.
4. Buyers heavily value third-party certifications (TUV, IEC) and lab test reports over marketing claims.
5. Diesel-replacement framing (for industries on DG backup) outperforms bill-savings framing — speak the language of *generator displacement,* not just *grid savings.*
