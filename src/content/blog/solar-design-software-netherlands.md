Dutch residential solar is five months from the largest change in its economics since the salderingsregeling was introduced. Net metering ends on 1 January 2027, confirmed when the Senate approved the termination in December 2024. From that date, exported electricity earns whatever compensation the supplier sets, expected in the range of 5 to 10 cents per kWh, while imported electricity continues to cost retail. Payback periods that run seven to nine years today are projected to move to 12 to 17 years. ## What Changes On 1 January 2027

Under the salderingsregeling, exported kWh were netted against imported kWh at full retail value across the billing year. The consequence was that timing did not matter. A household could export all summer, import all winter, and settle the difference at retail rate. Array sizing was a simple function of annual consumption, and the load profile was irrelevant.

From 1 January 2027 the netting disappears. Suppliers set the export compensation, with a government requirement that it be reasonable and not negative. Expectations cluster around 5 to 10 cents per kWh against retail import prices several times higher. Timing becomes the whole game.

> **What most Dutch quotes will get wrong in 2027.** They will keep sizing arrays against annual consumption. That was correct under netting and is wrong without it. After the change, a system sized to cover 100% of annual usage on a household that is empty during the day will export most of its output at a fraction of what the imported evening electricity costs.

There is a second effect already visible. Several suppliers now apply terugleverkosten, feed-in charges levied on customers who export significant volumes. For those customers, export can carry a negative net value even before the scheme ends. A design tool that treats every exported kWh as income is overstating the return today, not just from 2027.

## What Dutch Installers Now Need From Design Software

**8,760-hour simulation against a real load profile.** This is the requirement that replaces everything else. Monthly or annual modelling cannot compute self-consumption, and self-consumption is now the return. The load profile should come from smart meter data where the customer can supply it, since Dutch smart meter penetration is high and half-hourly data is generally available. Our [PV yield simulation software](/blog/pv-yield-simulation-software/) guide covers the methodology, and [solar shading analysis software](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software/) covers why module-level resolution matters.

**Battery dispatch modelling.** The battery case in the Netherlands moves from marginal to central on 1 January 2027, because shifting a kWh from a 6 cent export to a 30 cent avoided import is worth roughly five times the export value. Sizing a battery correctly requires simulating charge and discharge against the actual hourly surplus, not applying a rule of thumb. See our [solar plus storage design software](/blog/solar-plus-storage-design-software/) comparison.

**Orientation reconsidered.** Under netting, south-facing maximised annual yield and annual yield was the objective. Without netting, an east-west split flattens the curve and lifts self-consumption, which can return more money from less electricity. Our [east-west roof solar design](/blog/east-west-roof-solar-design/) guide covers the trade-off.

**NEN 1010 and network operator conditions.** NEN 1010 is the Dutch low-voltage installation standard. Connection conditions come from the regional operator, principally Liander, Stedin and Enexis. Grid congestion is now a genuine constraint in parts of the country, and commercial connections in congested regions can face long waits or export restrictions that belong in the design assumptions rather than surfacing after the sale. ## Worked Example: 4.8 kWp Near Utrecht, Before And After 2027

An illustrative calculation using published expectations for post-2027 compensation, not a specific customer project. Inputs: 4.8 kWp, 950 kWh per kWp specific yield, 4,560 kWh generation, 3,400 kWh consumption, import at 30 cents per kWh, post-2027 export compensation at 7 cents per kWh, self-consumption 32% without a battery.

| Line | Under salderingsregeling | From 2027, no battery | From 2027, with 5 kWh battery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generation | 4,560 kWh | 4,560 kWh | 4,560 kWh |
| Self-consumed | 1,459 kWh | 1,459 kWh | 2,918 kWh |
| Exported | 3,101 kWh | 3,101 kWh | 1,642 kWh |
| Value of self-consumption at 30c | EUR 438 | EUR 438 | EUR 875 |
| Value of export | EUR 930 (netted at retail) | EUR 217 | EUR 115 |
| Total year one benefit | EUR 1,368 | EUR 655 | EUR 990 |

The same system loses 52% of its annual benefit when netting ends. A 5 kWh battery recovers roughly half of what was lost. That is the conversation every Dutch installer will be having through 2026 and 2027, and it cannot be had with a tool that reports annual kWh.

Note the second-order point. Under netting, the battery had almost no financial case, because export already paid retail. From 2027 the battery becomes the primary lever. Installers whose software cannot size one against a real load profile will be quoting batteries by rule of thumb in a market where the sizing decides the payback.
