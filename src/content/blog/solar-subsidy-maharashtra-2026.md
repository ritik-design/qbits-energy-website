## Solar Subsidy in Maharashtra: Two Schemes, One Installation

Maharashtra homeowners benefit from two distinct solar subsidy schemes running in parallel in 2026. Understanding the difference between them — and how they interact — is the starting point for any subsidy application.

The **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** is the central government scheme administered by MNRE through the [PM Surya Ghar national portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}. It provides ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kilowatt, capping at ₹78,000 for a 3 kW or larger residential system. The subsidy is disbursed directly to the applicant's Aadhaar-linked bank account after the DISCOM completes commissioning. Maharashtra ranks among the top five states in PM Surya Ghar registrations nationally, according to [Mercom India's](https://www.mercomindia.com/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} tracking of state-wise registration data.

The **MEDA (Maharashtra Energy Development Agency) scheme** is the state-level counterpart. MEDA operates under the Maharashtra state government and administers the residential rooftop solar incentive component of the Maharashtra Solar Energy Policy. Applications are submitted through the MEDA portal. The MEDA incentive is a per-kW benefit paid on top of the central subsidy — both are claimable on the same system, and neither disqualifies the other.

| Scheme | Administered by | Max Benefit | Application Portal | Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PM Surya Ghar** | MNRE / Central govt | ₹78,000 (3 kW) | pmsuryaghar.gov.in | 30–60 days post-commissioning |
| **MEDA Rooftop Solar** | Maharashtra state govt | Per-kW state incentive | meda.gov.in | 30–45 days post-commissioning |
| **Net Metering Credits** | DISCOM (MSEDCL / BEST / Tata / Adani) | Ongoing monthly bill offset | DISCOM portal | Lifetime of system |

Both central and state subsidies require the same baseline: an ALMM-listed inverter, an empanelled installer, and DISCOM commissioning. Install once — claim both. ## The Maharashtra Solar Savings Stack — 3 Layers of Financial Benefit

Every Maharashtra homeowner who installs rooftop solar in 2026 taps into three distinct financial layers simultaneously. Most applicants understand the subsidy — fewer understand how all three layers compound to deliver the actual payback.

### The Maharashtra Solar Savings Stack

1. **Central Subsidy (Layer 1)** — PM Surya Ghar provides up to ₹78,000 cash-back directly to your bank account after DISCOM commissioning. This is a one-time capital reduction. On a 3 kW system costing ₹1,50,000, it cuts your net investment to ₹72,000.

2. **MEDA State Incentive (Layer 2)** — The Maharashtra state layer adds a further per-kW benefit on top of the central amount. Claimed through meda.gov.in, this is disbursed as a second direct bank transfer after you submit commissioning documents and your MEDA application. The combined central plus state benefit makes Maharashtra one of the more generously incentivised states in India.

3. **Net Metering Savings (Layer 3)** — The [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) mechanism under MERC (Maharashtra Electricity Regulatory Commission) credits every unit your solar system exports to the grid at the retail import tariff rate. For MSEDCL consumers, this means ₹5.50 to ₹8.00 per unit exported — the same rate they pay to import. This is not a one-off payment but a 25-year stream of monthly bill reductions.

What makes Maharashtra particularly favourable is that Layer 3 — net metering — operates at retail-rate export crediting. States that credit at avoided cost (around ₹2.50–₹3.50 per unit) deliver materially lower lifetime savings. Maharashtra's retail-rate net metering is a structural financial advantage for anyone installing solar here today.

The three layers do not simply add — they interact. The capital reduction from Layers 1 and 2 lowers the payback period, which means Layer 3 starts generating pure profit earlier. A system that costs ₹2,00,000 gross but requires only ₹1,00,000 net investment after subsidies reaches payback approximately twice as fast.

## Maharashtra's Four DISCOMs and Their Net Metering Processes

One of the most common mistakes Maharashtra solar applicants make is treating MSEDCL as the only distribution company in the state. Maharashtra actually has four active DISCOMs serving distinct geographies, and each has its own net metering application process, portal, and timeline. Your electricity bill header tells you which DISCOM you are on.

### MSEDCL (Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company Limited)

MSEDCL is Maharashtra's largest [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/), serving the vast majority of the state including Pune, Nashik, Nagpur, Aurangabad, Solapur, Amravati, Kolhapur, and all rural Maharashtra. MSEDCL's net metering process runs through its dedicated portal at [msedcl.in](https://www.msedcl.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}.

The MSEDCL net metering process has a reputation among installers as one of the more procedurally demanding in India. Applicants must submit a detailed technical package including a single-line diagram, inverter datasheet (ALMM-listed), panel layout, structural certificate, and electrical completion certificate from a CEIG-empanelled engineer. Each document must match the installed configuration exactly — mismatches cause rejection and restart the timeline.

Realistic timeline: **60 to 90 days** from application submission to bidirectional meter installation. Urban MSEDCL divisions (Pune City, Nashik City) process closer to 45 to 60 days. Rural sub-divisions can exceed 90 days due to meter supply backlogs.

### BEST (Brihanmumbai Electric Supply and Transport)

BEST supplies electricity to the island city of Mumbai — essentially the south Mumbai area bounded by the Mahim Creek. BEST has a smaller consumer base than MSEDCL but a well-staffed urban utility team. BEST customers apply for net metering through BEST's consumer portal. The process is markedly faster than MSEDCL, with most approvals completing in **30 to 50 days**. BEST also has fewer document escalations due to its smaller, more urban geographic footprint.

### Tata Power (Mumbai Eastern and Western Suburbs)

Tata Power Distribution serves the eastern and western suburbs of Mumbai — Bandra, Andheri, Goregaon, Powai, Chembur, Kurla, and surrounding areas. Tata Power has one of the more digitised net metering application processes among Indian DISCOMs, with online status tracking and dedicated rooftop solar helpline. Typical approval time: **30 to 55 days**. Tata Power customers applying under PM Surya Ghar are also supported through the national portal with a linked DISCOM workflow.

### Adani Electricity (North-Western Mumbai)

Adani Electricity Mumbai serves Dharavi, Vikhroli, and parts of north-western Mumbai. Adani Electricity's net metering application is processed through Adani's consumer portal. Timeline is comparable to Tata Power at **30 to 55 days**. Adani Electricity customers following the PM Surya Ghar route should ensure their installer is empanelled with both the national portal and Adani Electricity's own vendor registry.

## Step-by-Step: How to Apply for Solar Subsidy in Maharashtra

The application process has a specific sequence. Deviating from this sequence — especially installing before receiving DISCOM feasibility approval — can void the subsidy claim entirely.

**Step 1 — Register on the PM Surya Ghar National Portal**

Visit [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and register using your mobile number, state (Maharashtra), and DISCOM name. Enter your electricity consumer number from your bill. Complete Aadhaar-linked e-KYC. You will receive a registration reference number.

**Step 2 — Obtain DISCOM Technical Feasibility Approval**

Submit the online feasibility application through your DISCOM portal (linked to the national portal for MSEDCL). A DISCOM engineer will assess your sanctioned load, transformer capacity in your area, and technical suitability for grid-connected solar. Do not begin any physical installation until this approval letter arrives.

**Step 3 — Appoint an Empanelled Installer and Select ALMM Equipment**

Choose a DISCOM-empanelled solar installer. Verify that the proposed inverter model appears on the MNRE [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) at mnre.gov.in — this is the single most common cause of rejected PM Surya Ghar claims. Panels must also be from ALMM-listed manufacturers. For MSEDCL, the installer must additionally be registered on the MSEDCL empanelled vendor list, not just the national portal.

**Step 4 — Installation, Electrical Certification, and MEDA Application**

After installation, obtain the electrical completion certificate from a CEIG-empanelled contractor. Simultaneously, file your MEDA state incentive application through meda.gov.in with your DISCOM consumer number, installer details, system specifications, and bank account details.

**Step 5 — DISCOM Commissioning Inspection and Net Metering Activation**

The DISCOM will schedule an on-site inspection, verify the installed system against approved specifications, remove the old unidirectional meter, and install a smart bidirectional meter. Once commissioning is complete, the DISCOM uploads the commissioning report to the PM Surya Ghar portal.

**Step 6 — Subsidy Disbursement**

After commissioning verification, PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ₹78,000) is transferred to your bank account within 30 to 60 days. The MEDA state disbursement follows within 30 to 45 days of MEDA application processing.

- **Central PM Surya Ghar:** ₹78,000 (3 kW) — via national portal DBT
- **MEDA state incentive:** per-kW amount — via MEDA direct transfer
- **Net metering:** ongoing monthly bill credits — via DISCOM billing adjustment

## MSEDCL Net Metering — Setting Realistic Expectations

For the majority of Maharashtra homeowners, MSEDCL is the relevant DISCOM, and MSEDCL's net metering process deserves a frank discussion. The MSEDCL process works — but it rewards applicants who prepare their documentation meticulously and follow up proactively.

The most common causes of delay or rejection in MSEDCL net metering applications are:

- **Mismatched technical documents** — the single-line diagram must match the actual installed configuration. Any deviation (inverter model change, panel brand substitution) requires a fresh submission.
- **Non-ALMM inverter or panel** — MSEDCL inspectors check ALMM compliance at the commissioning visit. A non-ALMM inverter fails inspection regardless of its technical quality.
- **Sanctioned load mismatch** — if your proposed system exceeds your sanctioned load, MSEDCL will reject the feasibility application. Apply for a load enhancement first, then apply for net metering.
- **Missing CEIG certification** — for systems above a certain capacity threshold, an electrical certificate from a CEIG (Chief Electrical Inspector to Government)-empanelled contractor is mandatory in Maharashtra. This is often overlooked by new installers.

> **60 to 90 days.** The realistic MSEDCL net metering timeline from application submission to bidirectional meter installation for most Maharashtra residential applicants. Urban divisions typically complete in 45–60 days. *Source — installer community data compiled by [Saur Energy India](https://www.saurenergy.com/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, 2025–2026.*

Once the bidirectional meter is installed, net metering works automatically through the DISCOM billing system. You receive a monthly statement showing units imported, units exported, and net payable. Credits accumulate monthly and are settled annually — any surplus credits at the end of the settlement year are typically adjusted against fixed charges or carried forward per MERC regulations.

## KUSUM Scheme in Maharashtra — Agricultural Solar Briefly Explained

Maharashtra has a large agricultural economy, and the PM Surya Ghar scheme (which targets residential rooftops) is entirely separate from the KUSUM (Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan) scheme for farmers.

The [KUSUM](/glossary/kusum/) scheme's Component B covers standalone solar-powered agricultural pump sets. In Maharashtra, KUSUM is implemented through MEDA and MSEDCL jointly. Eligible farmers receive:

- **30% central government subsidy** (MNRE)
- **30% Maharashtra state subsidy** (MEDA/state budget)
- **40% farmer contribution** (own funds or agricultural loan)

This means a farmer in Nashik or Vidarbha installing a solar pump pays only 40% of the system cost, with the remaining 60% covered by government subsidies. Applications for KUSUM Component B in Maharashtra are routed through the MEDA portal. KUSUM is not relevant to residential urban homeowners — it is specifically for agricultural connections with pump loads.

For urban and peri-urban homeowners on MSEDCL residential connections, the PM Surya Ghar + MEDA residential scheme combination described throughout this guide is the applicable pathway.

## Common Mistakes Maharashtra Solar Applicants Make

Installers and consumers who have navigated the Maharashtra solar subsidy process report a consistent set of avoidable errors. Knowing them before you apply saves weeks of delay.

- **Installing before DISCOM feasibility approval** — the PM Surya Ghar portal requires a valid feasibility letter before the commissioning report is accepted. Systems installed without prior approval are ineligible for the central subsidy regardless of equipment quality.
- **Using a non-ALMM inverter** — even a technically sound inverter from a reputable brand will fail MSEDCL's commissioning inspection if it is not on the MNRE ALMM list. Verify every proposed inverter model at [mnre.gov.in](https://mnre.gov.in/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"} before signing any installation contract.
- **Applying to MEDA after MSEDCL has already processed the commissioning** — some applicants forget to file the MEDA application simultaneously with the commissioning process. MEDA applications should be submitted at Step 4 (installation complete, commissioning pending) rather than after commissioning.
- **Choosing a non-empanelled installer** — MSEDCL maintains its own empanelled vendor list which sometimes differs from the national portal's empanelled list. Confirm your installer is on both.
- **Ignoring the sanctioned load** — if your proposed solar system capacity exceeds your sanctioned load, MSEDCL will reject the feasibility application. Check your bill for "sanctioned load" or "contracted demand" before sizing the system.
- **Not linking Aadhaar to the bank account** — PM Surya Ghar subsidy is disbursed via DBT (Direct Benefit Transfer) to the Aadhaar-linked bank account. If Aadhaar is not seeded with your bank account, the transfer fails silently.
