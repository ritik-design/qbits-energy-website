There is no reliable public price range for a 50 kW solar inverter in India, and this post does not invent one. On 30 October 2026 we searched government e-marketplace bids, state agency rate contracts, tender documents, brand shop pages and marketplaces. The only dated, openable government figure we found is a 2021 benchmark from CREDA, the Chhattisgarh state renewable energy agency: ₹2,34,000 excluding GST for a three-phase 50 kW string inverter, delivered anywhere in Chhattisgarh with a five-year onsite warranty. It is five years old, it is one state's tender yardstick, and it is not a 2026 market price.

For an EPC buyer, one unit price says little. What matters is what sits inside the number: the model, the tax entry, delivered or ex-works, the documents, the warranty paperwork and the payment terms. Below you will find the evidence we could open, the quote drivers and a bid-comparison sheet you can reuse.

> **Short version**
> - No dated, independent public price series for 50 kW inverters was found. Three or more such points would be needed to state a range, and we have one.
> - The one dated government number is a 2021 CREDA benchmark of ₹2,34,000 ex-GST, delivered, five-year onsite warranty. Do not use it as today's price.
> - Brand shop pages and marketplace listings we opened carry no price date or contradict themselves, so we did not use them.
> - Qbits quotes per model and quantity. This post gives no Qbits price.
> - Compare quotes only after you normalise tax entry, delivery basis, documents, accessories and commissioning scope.

## The one dated 50 kW inverter benchmark: CREDA, 2021

CREDA's bid document 79770 of 30 July 2021 ran a tender to standardise rates for inverters "with 05 years onsite warranty conforming to the IEC/BIS and MNRE standards anywhere in the state of Chhattisgarh". Annexure IV gives a benchmark cost per unit for each size, in rupees, "excluding GST". Clause 11 says bids below 90% of the benchmark are treated as unbalanced and may need extra performance security. The benchmark exists to judge bids, so it is a buyer's yardstick, not a record of what anyone paid.

Three-phase solar string inverter rows from Annexure IV (CREDA, 30-07-2021, ex-GST, delivered, five-year onsite warranty):

| Size | Benchmark per unit | Per kW (our arithmetic) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 kW | ₹1,14,260 | ₹7,617 |
| 20 kW | ₹1,34,125 | ₹6,706 |
| 30 kW | ₹1,64,000 | ₹5,467 |
| **50 kW** | **₹2,34,000** | **₹4,680** |
| 60 kW | ₹2,49,000 | ₹4,150 |

Three points are worth taking from it, and none is a 2026 number.

1. **Size moves the price per kW, not in a straight line.** In this table the step from 50 kW to 60 kW adds ₹15,000, about 6.4% more for 20% more capacity. The schedule is not smooth either: the 8 kW row (₹91,930) sits above the 10 kW row (₹83,200). A benchmark is a judgment, so treat each row as one reading.
2. **Topology moves it far more than size.** In the same Annexure IV, a 50 kW off-grid PCU is ₹4,25,000 and a 50 kW hybrid inverter/PCU is ₹4,75,884, against ₹2,34,000 for the string inverter. That is about 1.8 and 2.0 times. A "50 kW inverter" request that does not say on-grid string, hybrid or off-grid cannot be priced.
3. **The basis is written into the number.** Excluding GST, delivered to site, five years of onsite warranty. Take any of those away or change them and the figure is a different thing.

It cannot tell you today's price, any other state's price, any brand's price, or what a retail buyer pays. Five years separate it from now, with a GST reform in between. The benchmark excludes GST, so that reform does not change the figure itself.

## How a tender price is built: the 2026 CREDA bid

CREDA's August 2026 rate contract publishes no rate, but it shows which conditions a bidder must price in. The price sheet reads "Rate of system (Excluding GST, In Rs.)" and says rates are "FOR anywhere in the State of Chhattisgarh inclusive of roadworthy packing, loading, unloading, all types of incidental expenses, insurance, duties". Each condition below is a cost somebody carries.

| Condition in the 2026 bid document | Why it moves a unit price |
| --- | --- |
| Rates ex-GST, GST paid extra at the rate applicable at billing | Tax is kept out of the comparison, so every bidder is compared on the same base |
| Delivered to any location in the state, with packing, loading, unloading and insurance | Freight to remote sites is averaged into one rate |
| Five-year onsite warranty from commissioning and acceptance, with repair or replacement at the supplier's cost | A bidder prices expected service visits and spares over five years |
| Faults to be made operational within 3 days (minor) or 7 days (major) or the buyer may rectify at the bidder's cost | Response commitments need service staff and spares stock |
| Service station at headquarters, spares, manpower and a toll-free complaint number | A service network is a fixed cost spread over the units sold |
| 90% paid after supply and a site performance test, 10% held as security for 60 months | Money tied up for five years has a financing cost |
| Delay penalty up to 0.5% per week, capped at 5% of cost | Schedule risk is priced in |
| OEM authorisation form and test certificates before supply; test reports from an MNRE or NABL lab at bid stage | Documentation and type-test cost sits behind each unit |

These are one state agency's terms, and a private commercial and industrial (C&I) buyer will set different ones. The method carries over: a unit price with these conditions stripped out is not comparable to one that carries them. The [GeM sourcing guide](/blog/buy-solar-inverters-on-gem/) walks through how an EPC separates GeM's layer from the buyer's added terms.

## What moves a 50 kW solar inverter price quote

Technical drivers are fixed by your design, and commercial drivers are negotiable. Keep them apart: the first group changes which inverter you buy, and the second changes only what you pay for the same one.

### Technical drivers

- **MPPT count and per-tracker current.** MPPT (maximum power point tracking) inputs let each group of strings run at its own best voltage. A design that needs six strings on one roof plane may need more tracker current than a three-tracker unit offers. Choosing a unit with more trackers, or two smaller inverters, changes the bill. The [50 kW datasheet comparison](/blog/best-fifty-kw-solar-inverter-india/) reads six brands' tracker counts and currents, so this post does not repeat them.
- **DC:AC ratio.** More module capacity per inverter kW improves yield in weak light and adds clipping at noon. The ratio you design to decides whether a 50 kW or a 60 kW unit fits, and the [DC oversizing guide](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/) covers the trade-off. Ask each bidder to quote against the same string layout.
- **Maximum DC voltage and MPPT window.** Longer strings need higher maximum DC voltage and a window that still covers cold-morning voltage and hot-afternoon voltage. A unit with a narrower window may force shorter strings and more combiner hardware.
- **Enclosure rating and cooling.** IP rating decides rooftop and coastal suitability, so compare the stated rating, not the word "outdoor".
- **Communication.** In the Qbits product data, both 50 kW families list Wi-Fi, with RS485 or GPRS as options. When a communication option is optional, one bidder's price can include it and another's can leave it out. List it as its own line.
- **Certification and test reports.** India's Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025, notified on 27 January 2025, covers inverters used in solar PV applications. According to the PIB release of 29 January 2025, products must conform to the latest Indian Standards and carry the BIS Standard Mark under a BIS licence. Compliant sourcing costs something, and non-compliant sourcing carries a risk the quote will not show. Ask for the licence number and the model it covers. The [inverter regulations guide](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) tracks current status, including the route for larger units.

Two Qbits 50 kW families show how fields differ inside one capacity class, as listed in the Qbits product data. The [QB-40-50KTLC-Plus](/our-products/QB-40-50KTLC-Plus/) has 4 MPPTs and a 200 to 1000 V MPPT range. The [QB-50-60KTLC](/our-products/QB-50-60KTLC/) has 3 MPPTs and a 250 to 1100 V MPPT range. Both state 1100 V maximum DC and IP66. Whether either suits a given roof is a design question for the [50 kW comparison](/blog/best-fifty-kw-solar-inverter-india/), not a price question.

### Commercial drivers

- **Delivered or ex-works.** Freight on a heavy three-phase unit to a distant site can be a visible line or hidden in the rate. Insist on one basis.
- **Quantity.** Four units for one 200 kW roof and forty for a portfolio are different enquiries. Quantity breaks, where offered, belong in writing against a stated quantity.
- **Payment terms.** Advance, on delivery or retention against performance each price the supplier's cash differently. The CREDA 90/10 split with a 60-month hold is an extreme case.
- **Quotation validity.** An old figure reused as a current offer is a common error. Record the validity date.
- **Warranty paperwork.** Ask for the controlling written document, its term, its remedy and what any extension option covers. A headline number is not a document.
- **Installation scope.** Supply only, supply plus commissioning, or supply plus a service contract are three different prices. Mark each exclusion.
- **Tender or retail.** Retail single-unit pricing, a private project quote and a government rate contract each carry different volume, documentation and payment risk. They are not interchangeable benchmarks.

### Which GST rate applies to a 50 kW inverter

GST on a 50 kW solar inverter is not settled by the notification text, so this section records only what the text supports. Notification 9/2025-Integrated Tax (Rate), dated 17 September 2025 and corrected by a corrigendum dated 18 September 2025, came into force on 22 September 2025. We opened the text. Schedule I (5%) entry 437, chapters 84, 85 or 94, covers "renewable energy devices" and their parts, listing among them "solar power-based devices", "solar power generator" and photovoltaic cells. Schedule II (18%) entry 477 covers heading 8504, "electrical transformers, static converters (for example, rectifiers) and inductors". The notification does not name solar inverters in either entry.

What follows is a reading, not a ruling. An inverter is a static converter, so heading 8504 is the starting point, but whether a given product description falls in entry 437 or entry 477 turns on how the supplier describes and classifies it. Ask each bidder to state the HSN, the entry and the rate in writing. The [GST guide](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) works through the entries, the 70:30 explanation for bundled supplies and input credit. We did not verify any customs duty rate for this post. For an imported unit, ask whether the price is duty-paid.

If you are not sure which rate applies, compare quotes ex-GST and settle the tax entry separately. The next section shows why.

## How to compare two 50 kW inverter quotes: a worked example

**Every figure in this table is invented for the exercise.** The amounts are not market prices, not attributed to any brand, and not from any source above. Only the method is the point. Two bidders quote four "50 kW three-phase" inverters for a 200 kW roof, and the cheaper headline wins until you normalise.

| Line item | Unit | Quote A (as received) | Quote B (as received) | Quote A normalised | Quote B normalised |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inverter, 4 units, ex-GST | ₹ | 7,60,000 | 8,20,000 | 7,60,000 | 8,20,000 |
| Communication option | ₹ | Not included | Included | 24,000 | 0 |
| Freight to site | ₹ | Ex-works | Delivered | 18,000 | 0 |
| Spare connector and fuse set | ₹ | Not mentioned | Included | 9,000 | 0 |
| Commissioning visit, 4 units | ₹ | Excluded | Included | 20,000 | 0 |
| Documents and test reports | list | Datasheet only | Datasheet and test reports | Request, price pending | 0 |
| **Comparable total ex-GST** | ₹ | 7,60,000 | 8,20,000 | **8,31,000** | **8,20,000** |

The ₹60,000 gap in headline price reverses once Quote A's missing lines are added at assumed costs: A comes to ₹11,000 above B. The documents row stays open for A, so the true gap may be larger.

Now add tax. Take B's ₹8,20,000 as an illustrative base. At 5% the tax is ₹41,000. At 18% it is ₹1,47,600. The ₹1,06,600 difference is why the entry and the rate must be written on the quote and not assumed. Apply one treatment to both columns before you compare.

What to normalise, row by row:

1. Same model class and quantity, stated by exact model.
2. Same tax basis, ex-GST first, with the HSN entry written down.
3. Same delivery basis, to your site.
4. Same accessories, communication hardware and spares.
5. Same commissioning and training scope.
6. Same document set, including licence number and test reports.
7. Same validity date, payment terms and warranty document.
