## What A UK Solar Proposal Has To Get Right

**BS 7671 and the certificate.** All UK solar electrical work must comply with BS 7671, 18th Edition with Amendment 2. Solar-specific requirements sit in Part 7, Section 712, covering DC isolators, cable sizing, surge protection, and earthing. A qualified electrician issues an Electrical Installation Certificate on completion. SurgePV's [BS 7671 solar guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/uk/guides/bs-7671-solar) covers the clauses that bite at design time.

**The G98 or G99 route.** The threshold is 16 amps per phase. G98 is fit-and-inform for systems up to 3.68 kW single-phase or 11 kW three-phase, with the DNO notified after commissioning and 28 days to object. G99 needs formal pre-approval with a 45 working day review. Most UK residential at 3 to 6 kW single-phase falls under G98. The proposal timeline has to match the route, and SurgePV's [G98 vs G99 guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/uk/guides/g98-vs-g99) sets out the boundary.

**MCS eligibility, enforced at specification.** MCS is not a legal requirement to install. It is a hard requirement for the customer to receive SEG payments, and both the installer and the products must be certified. Specifying a non-MCS module quietly destroys the export income the proposal is built on. See SurgePV's [MCS certification guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/uk/guides/mcs-certification).

**VAT, stated with its basis.** Qualifying residential installations in Great Britain currently attract 0% VAT on supply and installation. The relief is time-limited and has been extended before, so a proposal should state the rate applied and the date it was prepared. Commercial work follows standard treatment.

## The SEG Trap: Why Export Rate Is The Wrong Headline

Under the old Feed-in Tariff, export was a known national number and quoting was arithmetic. Under SEG it is a commercial variable set by each supplier, and it sits far below the import price.

> **What most UK installers get wrong.** They quote a single blended SEG rate and let the customer assume export drives the return. It does not. Every kWh used in the house avoids the full import price. Every kWh exported earns the SEG rate. The gap between those two numbers is where the entire savings case lives, and a proposal that does not separate them is not describing the economics.

This has a direct design consequence. Under FiT, oversizing was rational because export paid well. Under SEG, the return comes from self-consumption, which means the useful levers are load shifting, hot water diversion, EV charging schedules, and batteries. A 6 kW array on a house that is empty all day performs very differently from the same array on a house with a heat pump and a home worker, and only an hourly model shows the difference.

The practical test on any vendor demo: ask them to show the same system on two different load profiles and report the self-consumption percentage for each. Tools running an 8,760-hour engine answer immediately. Tools running annual totals cannot answer at all. ## Worked Example: 4.5 kW In Leeds, Two Households

An illustrative calculation showing why the load profile matters more than the array under SEG. Inputs identical in both cases: 4.5 kW array, 900 kWh per kW specific yield, 4,050 kWh generation, import at 26p per kWh, SEG export at 6p per kWh.

| Line | Empty weekdays | Home worker plus EV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual generation | 4,050 kWh | 4,050 kWh |
| Self-consumed | 1,215 kWh (30%) | 2,633 kWh (65%) |
| Exported | 2,835 kWh | 1,417 kWh |
| Value of avoided import at 26p | £316 | £685 |
| SEG export income at 6p | £170 | £85 |
| Total year one benefit | £486 | £770 |

Same hardware, same roof, same town. A 58% difference in year one benefit, driven entirely by when the electricity is used. A proposal that reports one number for both households is wrong for at least one of them, and probably both. This is also the honest argument for a battery on the first profile: shifting even half the exported energy into evening use is worth more than the entire SEG income.
