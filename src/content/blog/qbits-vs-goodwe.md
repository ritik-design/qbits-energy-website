**In a Qbits vs GoodWe choice, the question is whether you need GoodWe's tracker current and cold-end range or the maximum PV power and standards list that Qbits prints.** GoodWe prints 40 A per tracker at 25 kW and 50 kW and 42 A on all eight inputs of its 100 kW unit, with -30 C at the cold end on its three-phase sheets. Qbits prints a maximum PV power in every on-grid class, a standards list, and a lower weight in some classes. GoodWe's India sheets also print more protection and communication detail. Check your modules and string layout against both before you shortlist.

**Disclosure.** Qbits publishes this article and sells the Qbits models in it. GoodWe is a competitor, so weigh our reading accordingly. Both brands got the same treatment: the brand's own current datasheet, the version or date printed on it, and the day we read it. The GoodWe files came from GoodWe's India page (en.goodwe.com/india) and the Qbits files from the public datasheet downloads on qbitsenergy.com, all on 30 October 2026. Everything in a GoodWe column is GoodWe's own claim. We did not test either brand's units and have no field data. Because the Qbits sheets are image files, we read them visually and give each Qbits figure as printed.

This page differs from the class guides on this site in one respect. Those read five or six brands each and took GoodWe's figures from its global site, for example the GW25K-MT sheet code 20230703 and the GW50KS-MT sheet code 20260512. Here every GoodWe column comes from the documents GoodWe lists for India, which differ in places, and the two brands sit on identical rows.

## Worked checks: do the strings fit, and does the battery current hold?

These checks are **illustrative**. They use a 700 Wp class module with assumed values. Use your own module's datasheet.

**Assumed module (illustrative):** Voc 48.6 V, Vmp 40.3 V, Isc 18.3 A, Imp 17.4 A. Both voltages move by -0.26% per C, and we design for 5 C cold and a 65 C hot cell. The other comparisons on this site use the same assumptions. That gives 51.1 V per module cold (48.6 V x 1.052) and 36.1 V per module hot (40.3 V x 0.896).

**5 kW, one string.** Cold voltage by module count: 10 modules read 511 V, 11 read 562 V and 12 read 614 V. Hot operating voltage for 11 modules is 397 V.

| Unit | Maximum DC voltage | 10 modules (511 V) | 11 modules (562 V) | 12 modules (614 V) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qbits QB-5KTLD | 550 V | Fits | Over 550 V | Over |
| GoodWe GW5K-DNS-G40 | 600 V | Fits | Fits | Over 600 V |

The current is not the issue here: 17.4 A at Imp is under the 20 A tracker on both. One more check applies to Qbits: 10 modules is 7.0 kWp, which is under the 7500 W maximum PV power the Qbits sheet prints. GoodWe prints no such figure, so ask GoodWe for the maximum PV power in writing. Neither result says one inverter is better. It says that a longer string suits GoodWe's higher voltage limit.

**50 kW, six strings of 16 modules (67.2 kWp).** Cold voltage is 16 x 51.1 V = 818 V, under 1000 V and 1100 V on both. Hot operating voltage is 16 x 36.1 V = 578 V, inside both windows. The current rule: strings on a tracker x Imp must not exceed the tracker's printed maximum input current, and strings per tracker must not exceed the printed number. Two strings sharing a tracker draw 34.8 A at Imp and 36.6 A at Isc.

| Unit | Trackers as printed | Strings carried at 17.4 A | Six strings fit? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qbits QB-50KTLC-Plus | 40 / 40 / 20 / 20 A | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 | Yes, with no spare string place; 67.2 kW is under the 75 kW printed ceiling |
| GoodWe GW50K-SDT-C30 | 4 x 40 A | 2 per tracker = 8 | Yes, with two spare string places; Isc 36.6 A is under the 52 A short-circuit rating |

The Qbits sheet prints no short-circuit rating, so the Isc check stays open for that unit, and the order of its 40 A and 20 A trackers is our reading. GoodWe prints no maximum PV power, so 67.2 kWp (134% of 50 kW) is a figure to confirm with GoodWe.

**100 kW, twelve strings of 16 modules (134.4 kWp).** Voltages are as above. Qbits carries 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 12 strings, with no spare, and 134.4 kW is under its 150 kW ceiling. GoodWe carries 2 per tracker on eight trackers, which is 16 places, so twelve strings leave four spare. A layout that needs 14 or 16 strings of 700 Wp modules passes on the GoodWe GT sheet's tracker current and not on the Qbits sheet's, subject to the maximum PV power that GoodWe's sheet does not print (16 strings is 179 kWp, 179% of 100 kW).

**8 kW shop backup, 48 V battery.** The [three-phase hybrid comparison](/blog/best-three-phase-hybrid-inverter-homes-shops/) works this method in full, and we apply it here. An 8 kW load for 3 hours at 93% conversion draws 8.6 kW from the battery, which is 179 A at 48 V and 195 A late in discharge at 44 V.

| Model | Backup rating against 8 kW | Battery current limit against 179 A and 195 A |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qbits QBH-8KS48P3 | 8 kW rated, no margin | 190 A passes 179 A, fails 195 A |
| Qbits QBH-10KS48P3 | 10 kW rated | 210 A passes both |
| GoodWe GW8K-ET-L-G10 | nominal 8 kVA, max 8.8 kVA | 175 A fails even 179 A |
| GoodWe GW10K-ET-L-G10 | nominal 10 kVA, max 11.0 kVA | 220 A passes both |

The Qbits 8 kW unit clears the 179 A case with little margin, the GoodWe 8 kW unit does not clear it, and the 10 kW unit of each brand clears both cases. This is arithmetic on printed figures, not a sizing approval, and the battery pack and cable must be rated for the same current.

## What we could not compare

- **Reliability in the field.** Failure rates, return rates and uptime appear on neither sheet, and we hold no data on them.
- **Service where you work.** GoodWe lists an office in Navi Mumbai plus service and sales contacts, and Qbits is based in Surat, Gujarat. Neither route was tested for this page, and a sheet cannot tell you how fast a fault gets attended.
- **Price.** Qbits quotes per model and quantity. We have no GoodWe quote, and this page states no price for either brand.
- **Warranty.** Terms are issued in writing per model and differ by region and date, so no warranty term of either brand is compared here.
- **Certificates and registrations.** The Qbits sheets list standards, whereas the GoodWe India sheets list none and refer readers to GoodWe's website. A listed standard is not proof of registration or certification for your DISCOM (the state power distribution company). Get current certificates and registration numbers for the exact model from both brands and confirm them independently.
- **Net-metering acceptance.** Acceptance is the DISCOM's call. Our post on [what DISCOMs check on an inverter](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) covers the usual review, and both brands should put their approved-model position on paper.
- **Software and monitoring.** Apps, portals and API access were not compared.
- **GoodWe's high-voltage hybrids and its other India models.** We read one model per class.

## Questions to put to Qbits and GoodWe

1. **Which exact unit will I get?** Ask for the model code, the sheet version and date, and the country version. GoodWe's India sheets carry no version, so ask for the sheet's date.
2. **What are the tracker limits?** Ask each brand for current per terminal, short-circuit current and strings per tracker, then check them against your layout with the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/).
3. **What is the PV ceiling?** The GoodWe India sheets print no maximum PV power, so ask GoodWe for the maximum DC input power of your model.
4. **Which certificates exist?** Ask for the test certificates and registration numbers for that model, each with its issue date.
5. **Is the model on my DISCOM's list?** Ask whether it appears on the accepted list, and get the reply in writing.
6. **Who comes when it faults?** Ask who attends in your district, whom to call, and how a replacement unit travels.
7. **Where are the spares?** Ask which are held in India and which are shipped on request.
8. **What does the warranty say?** Request the written warranty for the exact model, covering the base term, the remedy, the claim steps and the exclusions.
9. **Which sheet rows are missing?** Ask Qbits for short-circuit current, string monitoring, AFCI (arc-fault protection), hybrid switch-over time, battery compatibility and communication. Ask GoodWe which of its two 25 kW models you are being quoted.
10. **Can I check my own reading?** The [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) lists the lines that matter.
