A school is probably the best-suited institution for rooftop solar in India. The reasons are structural, not coincidental: electricity consumption is concentrated during school hours (8 am–5 pm), which maps perfectly onto solar generation times. The school is largely unoccupied during summer vacation (precisely when solar generation is at its highest) and [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) credits that surplus against the academic term's bills. Maintenance requirements are minimal. The roof is large, typically flat, and unshaded.

The resulting economics are compelling: systems that pay back in 3–5 years, followed by 15–20 years of free electricity that directly reduces operating costs, and ideally, school fees.

> **Direct answer.** Solar for schools India delivers exceptional ROI because daytime-only loads match solar generation perfectly and summer vacation surplus is credited via net metering against academic-year bills. The School Solar Load Audit in this guide provides a structured framework to size the system correctly and quantify the savings before committing to installation.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - School electricity consumption is concentrated during daytime hours, which maps almost exactly onto solar generation, making schools one of the best-matched building types for rooftop solar. Summer vacation coincides with peak solar irradiance; a 1,000-student school can export roughly 34,500 kWh during April-June, credited via net metering against the academic term's bills.
> - Most schools should choose an on-grid system; hybrid with battery is only worth the 30-50% cost premium for schools with more than 2 hours of daily outages.
> - Government schools can sometimes access solar at zero upfront cost through RESCO arrangements or state programmes, paying a contracted per-unit rate; check the rate against your grid tariff before signing.
> - A representative 1,000-student private school with a 50 kW on-grid system pays back in about 5.7 years in the worked example, and under a 7-year MSME loan the EMI is slightly above the monthly saving, so plan a small top-up.
> - Solar adoption also contributes points toward the CBSE Green School Programme's renewable energy criterion, though it is not mandatory for certification.

This guide covers the school solar load profile, on-grid vs hybrid decision, CBSE Green School requirements, RESCO and state programmes, financing options, and a worked ROI model for a representative 1,000-student school.

## School Solar Load Audit

### School Solar Load Audit

A systematic load audit identifies what the solar system needs to power and during which hours. For a typical school, map loads into four categories:

**Category 1: Core Academic Loads (Running During School Hours)**
- Classroom fans, lights, and projectors
- Lab equipment (physics, chemistry, computer labs)
- Administrative office, computers, printers, copiers
- CCTV and security systems (24/7)
- Library lighting and computers

**Category 2: Scheduled High-Loads**
- Canteen or tiffin service kitchen (morning peak)
- Audio-visual equipment for assembly and events
- Sports facility lighting (if evening use)
- Air conditioning (in AC schools, much higher load)

**Category 3: Infrastructure Loads**
- Water pump (motor pump for overhead tank)
- Central inverter/UPS for networking
- Lift (if multi-storey building)

**Category 4: Minimal Vacation Loads**
- Security lighting
- Maintenance equipment (occasional)
- Server room cooling

## On-Grid vs Hybrid: The School Decision

For most schools in India, an on-grid system is the correct choice. Here is why:

Table: On-grid and hybrid systems compared for a school

| Factor | On-Grid | Hybrid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Lower | 30–50% higher |
| Daytime load matching | Excellent | Excellent |
| Grid outage during school hours | System shuts down | Battery continues essential loads |
| Summer vacation surplus | Exported (net metering credit) | Exported or stored |
| Best for | Urban schools, <2 hr outages/day | Schools with frequent >2 hr outages |
| Subsidy eligibility | PM Surya Ghar (if residential meter) | PM Surya Ghar (if residential meter) |

> [!IMPORTANT] Outage frequency decides on-grid or hybrid
> Hybrid is only worth the 30 to 50% cost premium for schools with frequent outages of more than 2 hours a day.

The decision pivot is grid outage frequency. In urban and semi-urban India, grid reliability has improved dramatically with 24×7 power supply targets under the Saubhagya scheme. Schools in these areas gain minimal additional value from battery backup. Schools in rural areas with scheduled load shedding of 4+ hours per day should consider hybrid systems.

For hybrid schools, the essential load circuit typically includes: computer labs, CCTV, library, and administration, the loads where interruption causes maximum academic disruption.

## Summer Vacation: The Net Metering Dividend

This is the strongest financial argument for school solar that most people miss.

> [!EXAMPLE] April–June reality for a 1,000-student school
> - Solar generation: 50 kW × 8 PSH × 90 days = 36,000 kWh (peak summer generation)
> - School consumption during vacation: ~1,500 kWh (security + minimal admin)
> - **Net export to grid: 34,500 kWh**
> - Net metering credit at ₹8/unit: **₹2,76,000 credited to the school's account**

This ₹2.76 Lakh credit is used against the school's electricity bills during August–March when consumption is high and solar generation is lower. The economic model is self-reinforcing: the highest-generation period (April–June) produces the largest credit, which subsidises the highest-consumption period (academic term).

> **Grid impact.** MNRE's target of installing rooftop solar on all government school buildings in India was included in the broader PM Surya Ghar programme framework, with state SREDAs as the implementing agencies. Karnataka, Rajasthan, and Gujarat have been among the most active in government school solar rollouts. *Source - [MNRE Annual Report](https://mnre.gov.in/), 2025.*

## CBSE Green School Solar Requirements

The CBSE Green School Programme (GSP) provides a framework for schools to assess and reduce their environmental impact. Solar adoption contributes to the energy category of the assessment:

- **Energy audit criterion**: schools that conduct a formal energy audit and identify efficiency measures earn points.
- **Renewable energy criterion**: schools generating 10–25%+ of their electricity from renewable sources (solar, wind, biogas) earn points in the renewable energy sub-section.
- **Solar as visible sustainability signal**: beyond points, solar panels on the school roof serve as a tangible educational resource, students can study photovoltaic technology in context.

CBSE GSP certification is voluntary and does not directly affect school recognition or affiliation. However, top CBSE schools increasingly use GSP certification as a differentiator in their admissions and parent communications.

For government schools, CBSE's technical partner TERI has published green school implementation guides that include solar sizing protocols compatible with the School Solar Load Audit methodology in this guide.

## RESCO for Government Schools

Government schools typically cannot commit capital expenditure on a non-curriculum item. For these schools, the RESCO model can be the most accessible pathway where a state programme or tender offers it:

**How a RESCO arrangement for a school works:**
- A developer installs solar at the school at zero upfront cost to the school.
- The school signs a [PPA](/glossary/ppa/) at a contracted per-unit rate that should be below its grid tariff, a rate schools can benchmark using a [solar financial modelling tool](https://surgepv.com/generation-financial-tool/) before signing.
- The school's electricity cost falls only if the PPA rate is below the grid tariff it replaces, so compare the two on a bill before signing.
- The RESCO developer owns the system, handles maintenance, and is responsible for grid connectivity.

Availability differs by state: ask your state nodal agency or DISCOM which government school solar programme is open.

For the full RESCO model explanation, see the [zero cost solar RESCO guide](/blog/zero-cost-solar-resco-opex-india/).

School management committees sizing their own scenario can cross-check the numbers below with a solar savings calculator.

Table: Worked ROI for a 1,000-student private school with a 50 kW on-grid system

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual electricity bill | ₹15,00,000 |
| System size | 50 kW on-grid |
| Annual generation | 50 kW × 5 PSH × 330 days × 0.8 performance ratio = 66,000 kWh |
| Annual saving (₹8/unit) | ₹5,28,000 |
| System cost | ₹30 Lakh |
| Simple payback | **5.7 years** (₹30 Lakh ÷ ₹5.28 Lakh) |
| Savings over 20 years | **₹1.06 crore** (₹76 Lakh after the ₹30 Lakh system cost, before degradation and tariff changes) |

> [!EXAMPLE] Loan EMI against monthly savings
> If the school qualifies for a MSME loan at 9% for 7 years, the EMI is approximately ₹48,000/month. Monthly electricity saving is about ₹44,000 (₹5.28 Lakh ÷ 12). The EMI is therefore about ₹4,300 a month higher than the saving while the loan runs, so the school needs a small top-up (or a longer tenure or a down payment) until the loan ends; after that the full saving is the school's.

## Common Mistakes in School Solar Projects

- **Undersizing for summer vacation**: not accounting for summer generation surplus and net metering credit. The system should be sized to generate maximum surplus during April–June for net metering benefit.
- **Not exploring RESCO for budget schools**: private unaided schools with thin margins immediately dismiss solar as a capital expense. RESCO eliminates the capex and delivers savings from month one.
- **Skipping the DISCOM net metering application**: a school that installs solar but does not complete net metering loses the summer surplus credit, a significant portion of the annual return.
- **Not factoring in CBSE GSP points**: for schools actively pursuing CBSE Green School certification, solar should be a line item in the sustainability strategy, not a separate decision.
- **Installing on a structurally inadequate roof**: older school buildings with aging concrete roofs may require a structural and civil engineering assessment before solar installation. Do not skip the structural check.
