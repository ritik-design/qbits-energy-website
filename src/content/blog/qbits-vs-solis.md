**In a Qbits vs Solis choice, settle tracker count and tracker current first, because that is where the two sets of datasheets differ most.** Solis lists three trackers at 25 kW against two on Qbits, four or five at 50 kW against four, and ten at 100 kW against eight. Qbits prints 20 A per tracker on its 5 kW unit where Solis prints 16 A, and a lower weight at 25 kW. Solis prints more safety and communication rows. Your module current, roof layout and paperwork decide the rest.

**Disclosure.** Qbits publishes this article and sells the Qbits models in it. Solis is a competitor, so read our summary with that in mind. Both brands went through one method: the brand's own current datasheet or product page, the version or date printed on it, and the day we read it. The Solis files came from solisinverters.com/in and the Qbits files from the public datasheet downloads on qbitsenergy.com, all on 30 October 2026. Every Solis figure is Solis's own claim. We ran no test and hold no field data for either brand. The Qbits sheets are images, so we read them by eye and quote the figures as printed.

The class guides linked below read five or six brands each. This page does something different: it sets two brands against each other class by class, using Solis's current India documents.

## What Solis does well

These strengths come from Solis's own pages and documents, opened on 30 October 2026.

- **A long-established, listed company.** Solis's India company profile says Ginlong Technologies was founded in 2005 and listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange (code 300763) in 2019. It says its products are used in more than 100 countries and regions. These are Solis's statements. We did not check them against any other source and we do not use any market-share or bankability ranking that the profile page quotes.
- **A wide range.** The India product list runs from mini single-phase models of 700 W to 3.6 kW, through single-phase, three-phase, hybrid and C&I classes, up to a 275 to 350 kW utility class.
- **Documents on the product page.** Each India product page we opened links a datasheet or flyer. The S6-GR1P(4-5)K-S page also links certificate PDFs by standard and a manual. We opened one: a TUV Rheinland certificate of conformity dated 24 March 2023 for the S6-GR1P single-phase family, tested to IEC 61727:2004 and IEC 62116:2014. Its own wording says it covers engineering samples and does not assess production. The manual file on that page is named for the European region and we did not read it.
- **India contact lines on the flyers.** The India flyers (the S6 models) print a sales and a service phone number and email. We did not test whether they are answered or how fast.
- **Print detail.** Solis's three-phase sheets print rows that the Qbits sheets do not: short-circuit current per tracker, string monitoring, I/V curve scan, surge protection type and an AFCI row. A buyer can check those before ordering, which is a real advantage when you are writing a specification.

Qbits publishes model datasheets on its [datasheets page](/download-datasheets/). ## A worked check: do the strings fit the trackers?

This check is **illustrative**. It uses a 700 Wp class module with assumed values. Use your own module's datasheet.

**Assumed module (illustrative):** Voc 48.6 V, Vmp 40.3 V, Isc 18.3 A and Imp 17.4 A. We apply -0.26% per C to both voltages and design for 5 C cold and 65 C hot cell temperature. The 15 kW to 100 kW class guides on this site use the same figures.

**50 kW, six strings of 16 modules (67.2 kWp).**

- Cold open-circuit voltage: 16 x 48.6 V x (1 + 0.0026 x 20) = 818 V. That is under 1000 V on all three units.
- Hot operating voltage: 16 x 40.3 V x (1 - 0.0026 x 40) = 578 V. That is inside every printed MPPT window.
- Current rule used: strings on a tracker x Imp must not exceed the tracker's printed maximum input current. A pair of strings on one tracker carries 34.8 A at Imp and 36.6 A at Isc.

| Unit | Trackers as printed | Strings the trackers carry at 17.4 A | Six strings fit? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qbits QB-50KTLC-Plus | 40 / 40 / 20 / 20 A | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 | Yes, with no spare tracker; DC power 67.2 kW is under the 75 kW printed ceiling |
| Solis S5-GC50K | 5 x 32 A | 1 per tracker = 5 (two strings read 34.8 A against 32 A) | No on current; five strings is 56 kWp |
| Solis S6-GC3P50K04-NV | 4 x 42 A | 2 per tracker = 8 | Yes; two spare string places |

Three limits apply. The Qbits sheet prints no short-circuit rating, so the Isc check is open for that unit. The Solis S5 would take 36.6 A on a 32 A tracker, and the sheet does not say what the unit does above its rating, so do not rely on that. Last, which Qbits trackers are the 40 A ones and which the 20 A ones is our reading of the printed row, so get it confirmed in writing.

**5 kW single-phase, ten modules in one string (7.0 kWp).** Cold voltage is 10 x 48.6 V x 1.052 = 511 V and hot operating voltage is 361 V, which fits 550 V and both DC windows on both brands. The current is where they differ: 17.4 A is under the Qbits 20 A tracker and over the Solis 16 A tracker, and Solis's flyer headline says string current up to 16 A. A 545 Wp class module at about 13.0 A Imp fits both. Neither result says one inverter is better. It says that a 700 Wp class module changes the shortlist.

## What we could not compare

- **Failure rates, returns and uptime.** Neither datasheet reports them, and we have no figures for either brand.
- **Service in your district.** Solis prints India sales and service numbers on its flyers. Qbits's office is in Surat, Gujarat. We tried neither route, and no datasheet shows how quickly a fault gets attended.
- **Price.** Qbits quotes by model and quantity. We hold no Solis quote and print no price for either brand.
- **Warranty.** Each brand sets its terms in writing for the exact model, and the wording changes with region and date. This page compares no warranty term for either brand.
- **Certificates and registrations.** A standard named on a datasheet does not show that your unit is registered or certified for your DISCOM (the state power distribution company). Request the current certificates and registration numbers for the exact model from each brand, and verify them yourself.
- **Net-metering acceptance.** Your DISCOM alone decides whether a model is accepted. [What DISCOMs check on an inverter](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) explains the review, and each brand should state its approved-model position in writing.
- **Software and monitoring.** We did not compare apps, portals or API access.

## A checklist to take to Qbits and Solis

1. **Model and sheet.** Note the exact model code, the sheet's version and date, and which country version ships with your order.
2. **Tracker map.** Request the current rating, short-circuit rating and per-string limit for each terminal. Test your layout in the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/), then compare it with the printed tracker current.
3. **Certificates.** Ask for the latest test certificates and registration numbers for that model, with the issue date of each.
4. **DISCOM status.** Check whether the model is on your DISCOM's accepted list, and get the answer in writing.
5. **Fault support.** Find out who attends a fault in your district, whom to call, and how a replacement unit is routed.
6. **Spares.** Learn which spares sit in India and which ship on request.
7. **Warranty in writing.** Get the base term, remedy, claim process and exclusions for the exact model in a written document.
8. **Rows the sheets leave out.** From Qbits, ask for short-circuit current, string monitoring, AFCI (arc-fault protection), hybrid switch-over time and communication. From Solis, ask which sheet version covers the model supplied and which grid standard applies to a 100 kW unit.
9. **A cross-check.** The [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) lists the lines worth checking if you want to test your own reading of either sheet. If GoodWe is also on your list, [Qbits vs GoodWe](/blog/qbits-vs-goodwe/) uses the same rows, and the [Qbits and Havells inverter comparison](/blog/qbits-vs-havells-solar-inverters/) works the same way.
