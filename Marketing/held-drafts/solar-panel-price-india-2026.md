Any of those changes the panel line on your quote more than a ₹1 per watt drift in the headline rate. This post is the 2026 update layer. ## What Actually Changed in Module Pricing Going Into 2026

Ask an installer what panels cost this year and the honest answer is "about the same, but I am selling you something different."

The per watt bands drifted within a couple of rupees. What moved was the composition of what gets quoted. A 2024 residential quote defaulted to DCR mono PERC around 540 Wp. A 2026 quote defaults to TOPCon in the 560 to 620 Wp class, often DCR, and increasingly from a manufacturer whose cells are made in India.

Three forces drove that.

**Domestic cell capacity ramping.** India spent 2024 and 2025 with a structural mismatch: plenty of module assembly capacity, far less cell capacity. That gap is why DCR modules carried such a wide premium. New cell lines commissioning through 2025 and 2026 narrowed it, though the premium did not vanish, because DCR demand grew alongside supply as PM Surya Ghar volumes scaled.

**ALMM Phase III.** The Approved List of Models and Manufacturers keeps extending its reach up the supply chain, with the compliance boundary moving toward cells rather than stopping at modules. Our [ALMM Phase III verification guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) covers how to check a model number before you sign.

**TOPCon crossing over.** Indian lines converted to N-type TOPCon at scale. Once a technology becomes the volume product rather than the premium product, its cost structure changes. That is why the TOPCon premium collapsed toward ₹2 to ₹4 per watt.

> **What most buyers get wrong about 2026 pricing.** A flat headline rate does not mean nothing happened. The rate stayed flat while the product improved. Paying 2024 money for a 2026 TOPCon module buys more generation per rupee. Flat nominal pricing on a better product is a real price cut.

## The 2026 Per Watt Rate Table

Here is where the Indian rooftop market sits in 2026. Treat these as industry-observed ranges. Module prices move with polysilicon rates, cell availability, freight, duty structure, and order size, so they shift every quarter.

| Technology | Typical wattage | Non-DCR ₹/watt | DCR ₹/watt | 2026 direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Polycrystalline | 250 to 340 Wp | ₹15 to ₹19 | ₹18 to ₹23 | Clearance stock only, effectively exiting |
| Mono PERC | 400 to 550 Wp | ₹18 to ₹24 | ₹21 to ₹29 | Stable, losing volume share to TOPCon |
| TOPCon (N-type) | 545 to 620 Wp | ₹21 to ₹27 | ₹25 to ₹33 | Softening as lines convert, now the volume product |
| Bifacial glass-glass | 550 to 700 Wp | ₹24 to ₹32 | ₹28 to ₹38 | Stable, still niche on flat RCC roofs |
| HJT | 600 Wp and above | ₹30 to ₹38 | Limited supply | Premium, thin Indian availability |

Two readings matter. The DCR column is the interesting one in 2026, because that 15 to 30 percent spread is the largest lever on your module bill and the one policy is actively changing. And polycrystalline should not appear on a new residential quote. If it does, the installer is clearing old stock. The lower rate is offset by needing 20 to 25 percent more roof area for the same capacity.

## Wattage Tiers and Where the Per Watt Rate Bottoms Out

Within one technology, larger modules generally price lower per watt. Glass, frame, junction box, and handling cost roughly the same per panel regardless of output, so those fixed costs spread across more watts.

| Wattage tier | Typical non-DCR ₹/watt | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 330 to 400 Wp | ₹20 to ₹26 | Small roofs, shaded patches, awkward shapes |
| 450 to 545 Wp | ₹18 to ₹24 | Standard residential, still widely stocked |
| 550 to 620 Wp | ₹18 to ₹23 | The 2026 residential default, mostly TOPCon |

The 2026 shift is that the volume sweet spot moved up a tier, from 540 to 550 Wp to 550 to 620 Wp, because that is where converted TOPCon lines are running. Buying at the volume tier gets you the best rate and the shortest lead time. Smaller modules still earn their higher rate on roofs with a water tank, stairwell block, or neighbouring wall, where they fill leftover clear area. ## The DCR Spread and the New Give It Up Option

This is the most consequential 2026 change for a residential buyer, and the one most quotes still do not explain.

DCR stands for Domestic Content Requirement. A DCR module needs both the cell and the module manufactured in India. A module assembled in India from imported cells is not DCR. Until this year, that requirement was effectively binding on anyone inside PM Surya Ghar.

In 2026, MNRE formalised a **Give It Up** option. A consumer can stay inside the PM Surya Ghar framework, keep the portal registration and net metering process, and decline the central subsidy. Declining the subsidy releases the DCR obligation, so the installer can supply non-DCR modules.

That turns the DCR premium from a rule into a priced choice. Here is the arithmetic on a 3 kW system.

| Path | Module rate | 3 kW module cost | Central subsidy | Net position on these two lines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DCR, subsidy claimed | ₹25 to ₹31 per watt | ₹75,000 to ₹93,000 | Up to ₹78,000 | Strongly favourable |
| Non-DCR, Give It Up | ₹19 to ₹24 per watt | ₹57,000 to ₹72,000 | ₹0 | Saves ₹10,000 to ₹20,000, forfeits up to ₹78,000 |

The central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar reaches ₹78,000 and is capped at the 3 kW band, so there is no additional central amount for capacity above 3 kW. Exact slab amounts by capacity are in the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy amount guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-amount/), and state top-ups are covered in [solar subsidy across all states](/blog/solar-subsidy-all-states/).

For a subsidy-eligible home the answer has not changed. Take the subsidy, buy DCR. Saving ₹15,000 to lose ₹78,000 is not a close call.

The option is genuinely useful in three narrower cases. Buyers above the 3 kW band, where marginal capacity earns no extra central subsidy anyway, so the DCR premium on the incremental kW buys nothing. Buyers who cannot wait out DCR module lead times, which stretch when domestic cell allocation gets tight. And buyers who want net metering and a compliant grid connection without the subsidy paperwork and inspection cycle.

That last case is more common than installers admit. Some homeowners value a four-week install over an eight-week one enough to pay for it. Defensible, just not cheap.

## ALMM Phase III and What Compliance Costs You

ALMM listing is not a quality badge you can shop around. For any subsidised or government-linked project, a non-listed module simply cannot be used, so its lower price is not a real comparison point.

Phase III continues extending the framework's reach up the supply chain, past finished modules toward cells. Three price effects follow.

Certification cost lands in the rate. Factory inspection, BIS certification, and periodic audits show up as roughly ₹1 to ₹2 per watt over a non-listed grey import.

Compliance narrows the supplier pool for any given specification. Less competition on DCR TOPCon specifically is why that cell in the rate table is the stickiest in the market.

And listings change. A model listed when you got the quote may not be listed when the DISCOM inspects. Verify the exact model number before you pay an advance, not after.

> **The one verification worth doing yourself.** Take the make, model, and wattage from your quote and check them against the current ALMM listing. Every claim rejection we hear about traces back to a module that was substituted at delivery or a model that was never listed under the exact number written on the quote.

## Technology Choice at 2026 Prices

The technology question changed this year because the premium changed.

**Mono PERC** at ₹18 to ₹24 per watt non-DCR still delivers 20 to 21 percent efficiency and the widest availability in the country. On a large open terrace where roof area costs you nothing, it remains the best rupees-per-unit choice.

**TOPCon** at ₹21 to ₹27 per watt now carries only a ₹2 to ₹4 per watt premium, for 22 to 23 percent efficiency, a better temperature coefficient, and slower degradation. The temperature point matters more in India than the spec sheet implies. Module temperature routinely crosses 60 degrees on an Indian afternoon and output falls with it. A better coefficient recovers part of that loss every clear day for 25 years. On a 5 kW system the premium is roughly ₹10,000 to ₹20,000, and where roof area binds it also fits more kW in the same footprint.

**Bifacial glass-glass** at ₹24 to ₹32 per watt earns its premium only where the rear face can see reflected light. On a flush-mounted flat RCC roof the rear gain collapses toward zero and the extra ₹6 per watt buys a heavier module and nothing else. On elevated structures, carports, and tin sheds it is the right call.

Our read for 2026: on a typical Indian home rooftop under 5 kW, DCR TOPCon in the 550 to 620 Wp class is now the default and mono PERC is the budget alternative. That is a reversal from two years ago, driven by manufacturing volume rather than any marketing claim.

## Should You Wait for 2027 Prices?

The long-run direction is down. Indian cell capacity keeps ramping, and that pressure lands on the DCR premium specifically. Expect that spread to keep narrowing rather than the base rate to crash. Short-run forecasting is worthless, because polysilicon rates, DCR cell allocation, freight, and duty structure all move quarterly and not in the same direction.

Here is the arithmetic that settles it. A 3 kW system generates roughly 360 to 400 units a month. At ₹8 per unit that is ₹2,800 to ₹3,200 saved monthly, so waiting six months costs about ₹18,000. To break even, the module rate would need to fall roughly ₹6 per watt. Movements that large inside a year are rare.

The exception is a buyer already waiting on DCR allocation for a specific project. If your installer says the DCR TOPCon you specified is eight weeks out, the real decision is whether to switch specification, not whether to wait for a price move.

## What to Verify Before You Sign a 2026 Quote

The list changed this year because of Give It Up and Phase III.

1. **Exact make, model, and wattage** in writing, so you can check the ALMM listing yourself against the current list.
2. **DCR status with certificate**, if you are claiming subsidy. A verbal assurance is worth nothing at inspection.
3. **Whether the quote assumes subsidy or Give It Up.** These produce very different module rates, and some installers quote the cheaper non-DCR number without saying which path it implies.
4. **Technology stated explicitly.** "Mono" on a quote can mean mono PERC or TOPCon, and the two are ₹2 to ₹4 per watt apart.
5. **Product warranty and performance warranty as separate numbers.**
6. **Rate inclusive or exclusive of GST**, and who pays freight to your city.
7. **Quote validity date**, because module rates move each quarter.

Also confirm your installer is empanelled on the PM Surya Ghar portal before you commit, regardless of how good the panel rate looks. [Choosing an empanelled vendor](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) walks through that check.

## The Bottom Line

Solar panel price in India in 2026 did not move much in nominal terms. What moved was everything determining which number applies to you: TOPCon became the volume technology and narrowed its premium, domestic cell capacity started compressing the DCR spread, ALMM Phase III raised the compliance floor, and the Give It Up option turned the DCR requirement into a priced decision instead of a rule.

Three things to do now:

- Get quotes on **DCR TOPCon in the 550 to 620 Wp class** as your baseline, with mono PERC priced as the alternative, not the other way round.
