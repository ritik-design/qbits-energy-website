Every French solar proposal written before 5 June 2026 contained two revenue lines that no longer exist. The prime à l'autoconsommation was abolished for new installations under the revised S21 arrêté, and surplus buyback for new applications fell to 1.1 centimes per kWh excluding tax. A great many templates, spreadsheets and imported platforms still carry both. The result is quotes promising an upfront bonus the customer will not receive and export revenue roughly twenty times larger than what will actually arrive. This guide compares the platforms French installers should shortlist now that the value case has moved entirely into self-consumption.

## The Two Lines That Have To Come Out Of Your Template

> **What most French quotes still show.** An upfront prime reducing the net cost, and an annual export revenue figure calculated at the old buyback rate. Neither applies to a new installation. The first is a discount the customer will not receive, and the second overstates export income by roughly a factor of twenty.

The awkward part is that this is not a slow drift into obsolescence. It happened on a single date, and quotes issued the week before were correct. Any installer with a template that was accurate in May is now issuing documents that are wrong in a way a customer can verify with one search.

The replacement structure is simpler than what it replaces:

| Line | Old proposal | Post-reform proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront bonus | Prime à l'autoconsommation | Removed entirely |
| Export revenue | Meaningful annual figure | A few tens of euros, stated plainly |
| Avoided import | One line among several | The entire value case |
| Storage | Optional add-on page | Priced option in the main comparison |
| Recommended size | Roof capacity | Daytime load curve, or load plus storage |

The document gets shorter and more honest. It also gets harder to write with a tool that cannot simulate self-consumption hourly, because the central claim is no longer "here is your generation" but "here is the share of it you will actually use."

## Show The Export Line, Small As It Is

There is a temptation to drop the export revenue line entirely on the grounds that a EUR 50 figure looks unserious next to a five-figure installation cost.

Include it anyway, for three reasons.

It pre-empts the question. A customer who does not see an export line will ask what happens to surplus, and the answer is better given in writing than improvised on a doorstep.

It makes your argument for you. A line reading "4,680 kWh exported, worth EUR 51" is the clearest possible demonstration that surplus is not where the money is, which is exactly the point you need the customer to accept before they will consider a smaller array or a battery.

It protects you against the competitor who inflates it. If a rival quote shows meaningful export revenue, a customer holding your itemised figure can see which of you is working from current rules.

## The Objection You Will Get, And How To Answer It

Because pre-reform connections keep their tariffs for twenty years, France now has a large installed base whose owners quote genuinely excellent paybacks. Neighbours, forums and any article written before June describe economics that were real and are no longer available.

This objection will arrive in nearly every French sales conversation for the next several years. It is worth addressing in the proposal itself rather than leaving it to the salesperson, because a written explanation is more credible than a verbal one and because the alternative is losing jobs to whoever quotes the old numbers.

The framing that works is factual and brief. Connection requests validated by Enedis before 5 June 2026 retain the previous arrangements for twenty years. Yours will not, and any quote that shows otherwise is either out of date or optimistic. Then move directly to what does still work, which is displacing retail import.

## What Still Belongs In The Document

**A priced storage option.** Not as an appendix. Under current rates a battery converts near-worthless surplus into full-retail avoided import, so it belongs in the main comparison with its own payback.

**An hourly self-consumption figure with its basis stated.** This is now the most load-bearing number in the proposal. Say what load profile it came from and show what happens if it lands ten points lower.

**NF C 15-712-1, NF C 15-100 and Consuel.** Naming the standards and the attestation requirement distinguishes a planned installation from a price.

**Site-specific yield.** France spans a wide latitude range, and the gap between Lille and Marseille changes the self-consumption balance rather than just the annual total.

## Worked Example: Three Ways To Quote The Same Lyon Household

An illustrative calculation, not a specific customer project. Inputs: household consuming 4,000 kWh a year, usable roof supporting 6 kWp, roughly 7,200 kWh annual generation at that size, 35% self-consumption without storage rising to about 70% with a battery.

| | A: 6 kWp, old template | B: 6 kWp, current rates | C: 6 kWp plus storage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-consumed | 2,520 kWh | 2,520 kWh | about 5,040 kWh |
| Exported | 4,680 kWh | 4,680 kWh | about 2,160 kWh |
| Export revenue shown | Overstated | about EUR 51 | about EUR 24 |
| Upfront bonus shown | Prime applied | None | None |
| Value driver | Bonus plus export | Avoided import | Avoided import, doubled |
| Is the quote accurate? | No | Yes | Yes |

Column A is what a stale template produces and it is simply wrong. Column B is honest and will look worse than column A to a customer comparing quotes, which is the commercial problem the reform created for careful installers.

Column C is the answer to that problem. Doubling self-consumption roughly doubles the avoided-import benefit, which is the only benefit that still exists. Whether the battery cost justifies it depends on the household's evening demand and on hardware pricing, and that is a calculation to run rather than a claim to make. But an installer who can only offer columns A and B is choosing between dishonesty and a weak quote, and column C is how you avoid that choice.
