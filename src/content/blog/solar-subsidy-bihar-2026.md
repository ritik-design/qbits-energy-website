## Bihar's State Top-Up: What Was Approved in 2026

In August 2026 the Bihar Cabinet approved state assistance for residential rooftop solar under PM Surya Ghar, paid on top of the central amount. As reported by BioEnergy Times (13 August 2026) and Energetica India (14 August 2026):

- **₹10,000 per kW, capped at ₹20,000 per consumer**, paid by direct benefit transfer
- **Combined support**: ₹40,000 for 1 kW, ₹80,000 for 2 kW, up to ₹98,000 for 3 kW or more
- **First come, first served**, with the scheme capped at five lakh consumers and approved for one year
- **Estimated outlay**: ₹1,000 crore

News reports say the Chief Minister launched it as the Mukhyamantri Solar Protsahan Yojana on 22 September 2026 and transferred the state assistance to 25,000 beneficiaries who had already installed systems under PM Surya Ghar. We found no official government release with the full eligibility terms, so confirm with your DISCOM or BREDA (Bihar Renewable Energy Development Agency, the state nodal agency) before you assume your system qualifies.

Table: Central and Bihar state support by system size (as reported, October 2026)

| System Size | Central | Bihar state top-up | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | ₹10,000 | ₹40,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | ₹20,000 | ₹80,000 |
| 3 kW and above | ₹78,000 | ₹20,000 | Up to ₹98,000 |

## Last Date and Scheme Validity

MNRE's guidelines run PM Surya Ghar through FY 2026-27, and the national portal shows the scheme closing on 31 March 2027. As of mid-October 2026, reports say the Centre is considering an extension and a successor phase, but nothing has been notified, so treat 31 March 2027 as the working deadline. The Bihar top-up is reported as approved for one year from August 2026. The central subsidy is released after commissioning, so installation alone before the date is not enough.

## Bihar's Power Situation: The Grid Reality

Bihar's electricity supply has improved. [Reporting in 2026](https://patnapress.com/bihar-power-consumption-infrastructure-growth-2026/) quotes the state energy department as putting average supply at about 23 to 24 hours a day in urban areas and 22 to 23 hours in rural areas, against far lower figures in 2005. Those are state-reported averages, and outages remain local: a feeder, a village or a time of year can be much worse than the average.

This changes the solar decision. An on-grid inverter's mandatory [anti-islanding](/glossary/anti-islanding/) function shuts the system down the moment grid power fails, which is exactly when a household with long outages needs backup most:

Table: On-grid, hybrid and DG generator compared during grid power and outages

| System Type | During Grid Power | During an Outage | Monthly Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **On-Grid Solar** | Runs; reduces bill | Shuts down (anti-islanding) | Bill savings only |
| **Hybrid Solar + Battery** | Runs; reduces bill | Continues from battery for selected loads | Bill savings plus lower or no generator cost |
| **Existing DG Generator** | Off (grid available) | Running; fuel cost depends on your hours and load | Fuel cost only |

For a Bihar family that currently runs a diesel generator through long outages, a hybrid system can cut both the generator cost and the grid bill. For a family with short, rare outages, an on-grid system usually gives the faster payback.

Both operate through the PM Surya Ghar national portal. District lists below follow the public Wikipedia entries and SBPDCL's own statement that it serves 17 districts; your bill is the authority.

> [!WARNING] Wrong DISCOM entity means resubmission
> Select the correct entity on the portal. Entering the wrong consumer number under the wrong DISCOM can fail validation and force a fresh submission. Patna and Bhagalpur are common mix-ups: both are SBPDCL.

## NBPDCL and SBPDCL Application Process: Step by Step

We have not attached day counts to the stages below because no DISCOM-level timelines are published.

### The Bihar PM Surya Ghar 7-Step Application Framework

<!-- steps -->
1. **Identify your DISCOM**: NBPDCL or SBPDCL based on your district and, above all, your bill. Both are listed on the portal under Bihar.
2. **Register on national portal**: Select Bihar and your specific DISCOM entity. Enter your consumer number exactly as it appears on the bill.
3. **Complete Aadhaar eKYC**: Enter Aadhaar number, receive OTP on your Aadhaar-linked mobile.
4. **Upload documents**: Latest electricity bill, Aadhaar, PAN, bank passbook, property proof. Rural applicants without formal title documents should ask their division what alternative proof it accepts.
5. **DISCOM feasibility and approval**: The DISCOM assesses your roof, sanctioned load and the local network. Wait for approval before installing.
6. **Select empanelled vendor**: Choose a vendor from the portal's empanelled list and compare at least two quotes before confirming. Vendor density is highest in Patna; rural districts may have fewer choices.
7. **Installation, meter replacement, inspection and subsidy**: After installation the DISCOM inspects, installs the net meter and commissions the system; the central subsidy follows verification on the portal. In July 2026 BERC waived the application and meter testing fees for domestic rooftop consumers.

## Choosing the Right System for Bihar

The choice between on-grid and hybrid depends on your location's grid reliability, which you can measure.

**Recommend on-grid if:**
- You are in a city or town with short, infrequent outages
- Your primary goal is the shortest payback on the PM Surya Ghar subsidy
- You are comfortable with no backup power during outages

**Recommend hybrid if:**
- You experience long or frequent outages, which you can confirm from a log of a few weeks
- You currently spend a meaningful amount monthly on DG fuel or inverter battery replacement
- You need uninterrupted power for medical equipment, a refrigerator, or study lighting

## Bihar-Specific Inverter Requirements

Bihar's electrical environment places specific demands on inverters:

- **Voltage range handling**: Voltage in some district feeders can swing widely. Compare each datasheet's grid-voltage range; for example, the QB 1.5/2.0/2.7/3.0/3.3/3.6/4.0KTLS datasheet lists a 90 to 290 Vac grid range.
- **Monsoon protection**: Bihar's monsoon (June to September) is intense and some districts flood. Check the ingress protection rating on the datasheet of the exact model you are considering, and mount outdoors with that in mind.
- **Temperature range**: Bihar summers are hot. Compare the datasheet's operating temperature range and derating behaviour; the QB 1.5 to 4.0KTLS datasheet lists an operating range of -25 to +60 °C.
- **Battery compatibility**: For hybrid systems, confirm the inverter supports LFP battery chemistry and has configurable charge and discharge parameters for the specific battery type.

## Common Mistakes Bihar Solar Applicants Make

- **Choosing on-grid when you have long outages**: An on-grid system shuts down during an outage, so you lose all solar benefit exactly when backup matters most.
- **NBPDCL vs SBPDCL confusion**: Selecting the wrong DISCOM entity causes consumer number validation failure. Check your bill header.
- **Battery without proper warranty**: Battery warranty varies widely in the market. Require the warranty terms in writing with the battery manufacturer's name on the certificate.
- **Overestimating net metering income**: During outages on-grid solar cannot export, and the year-end surplus rate is under review by BERC, so do not build your payback on export income.
- **Ignoring the free 125 units**: A household that uses 125 units or fewer a month has little bill to cut; a payback calculation must start from the units you actually pay for.

## Bihar Solar ROI: Two Illustrative Cases

Both cases below are assumed households, not real customers. The inputs, formula and result are shown so you can replace them with your own bill and quote. Generation uses the Patna reference site (1,438 kWh per kWp a year) as a proxy for both.

> [!FORMULA]
> Payback (years) = (gross cost - central subsidy - state top-up) ÷ annual saving

**Case 1: 3 kW on-grid, Patna (SBPDCL), 250 units a month.** The household pays for 125 units a month after the state's free 125 (assumption: the support is applied to net billed units; confirm with SBPDCL). A 3 kW system models at about 360 units a month, more than the household uses, so the whole billable amount disappears.

Table: Illustrative 3 kW on-grid household in Patna: inputs, savings and payback

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly consumption (assumed) | 250 units |
| Units billed after the free 125 | 125 units |
| Monthly energy bill at ₹7.42 (BERC FY 2026-27) | about ₹928 |
| Annual saving (₹928 × 12; export surplus not valued) | about ₹11,130 |
| Gross cost (assumed) | ₹1,70,000 |
| Net cost after central subsidy ₹78,000 | ₹92,000 |
| Net cost after central + ₹20,000 state | ₹72,000 |
| **Payback, central only** | **about 8.3 years** |
| **Payback, central + state top-up** | **about 6.5 years** |

**Case 2: 3 kW hybrid with 5 kWh battery, Muzaffarpur (NBPDCL).** The household uses 250 units a month, has long outages, and spends an assumed ₹3,000 a month on a diesel generator, which we assume the hybrid system displaces. This is an assumption to test, not a promise: the generator saving depends on the battery size and the loads you back up.
