That matters if your modules are the newer 600 to 700 Wp kind. Havells prints a 600 V maximum DC voltage and a -30 C cold end. It also prints short-circuit current, surge-protector type, noise and optional arc-fault protection. Treat the rest as questions for both brands, not a ranking.

## Which four datasheet rows matter most for a home?

Four datasheet rows decide most home fits. **Tracker current** is how much current each solar input can take, and a bigger module needs a bigger number. **Maximum DC voltage** and the **DC window** limit how many modules you can put in one string. **IP rating** is dust and water protection, and IP66 is higher than IP65. **Communication** tells you how the inverter reports to your phone. The rest are for your installer. If you want the reasoning behind each row, the [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) walks through them.

## Worked checks: do the modules fit?

These checks are **illustrative**. They use two assumed modules, and your own module datasheet replaces them.

**Module A (illustrative, 700 Wp class):** Voc 48.6 V, Vmp 40.3 V, Isc 18.3 A, Imp 17.4 A, a temperature coefficient of -0.26% per C applied to both voltages, a cold design temperature of 5 C and a hot cell temperature of 65 C. The [Qbits vs GoodWe](/blog/qbits-vs-goodwe/) and [Qbits vs Solis](/blog/qbits-vs-solis/) pages use the same figures. Per module, that gives 51.1 V cold (48.6 V x 1.052) and 36.1 V hot (40.3 V x 0.896).

**Module B (545 Wp class, from Havells's own module sheet):** the Havells ENVIRO-PMHC-545 sheet (file created 6 Aug 2024) prints 545 Wp, Vmp 41.98 V, Imp 12.99 A, Voc 49.97 V, Isc 13.74 A and a Voc coefficient of -0.2917% per C. We apply that coefficient to both voltages. Cold Voc is 52.9 V and hot Vmp is 37.1 V per module. We use it for two reasons. A Havells inverter is likely to be offered with a Havells module in a kit. And it shows the case where the 15 A tracker is not a problem.

**5 kW single-phase, current check (one string per tracker).**

| Module | Imp (A) | Qbits QB-5KTLD, 20 A | Havells 5000 D-NG, 15 A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, 700 Wp class | 17.4 | Fits | Over the printed 15 A by 2.4 A |
| B, 545 Wp | 12.99 | Fits | Fits |

For module A the Havells sheet does not support a single string on one tracker. The short-circuit figure is fine (18.3 A against 22.5 A). But the printed maximum input MPPT current is 15 A, and we do not know what the unit does above it. Ask Havells what happens at 17.4 A before you order. For module B both fit on current, and Havells's 600 V gives it one more module per string, as shown next.

**5 kW single-phase, voltage check (one string).**

| Modules in the string | Cold Voc, A / B | Qbits, 550 V max | Havells, 600 V max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 511 V / 529 V | Fits | Fits |
| 11 | 562 V / 582 V | Over 550 V | Fits |
| 12 | 614 V / 635 V | Over | Over 600 V |

A 10-module string of module A is 7.0 kWp. That is under the 7500 W Qbits prints and the 7500 Wp Havells recommends. Two other limits apply.

Havells prints "3750 W for a single MPPT", so 7.0 kWp on one tracker is above that row, and the sheet does not say what happens above it. Qbits prints no per-tracker power row. Suppose you split the array into two strings of five module-A units (3.5 kWp each, 181 V hot, 256 V cold). Each tracker then sits under Havells's 3750 W, but the current is still above its 15 A, and the hot voltage is under Havells's 210 V full-power lower bound. Qbits's window starts at 80 V, so 181 V is inside it, but its sheet prints no full-power range.

**10 kW three-phase, two strings of 10 module-A units (14.0 kWp).** Cold voltage is 511 V and hot voltage is 361 V, so both sit inside the 1100 V maximum and both windows. The current again splits the two sheets: 17.4 A is within Qbits's 20 A and over Havells's 15 A. The hot voltage of 361 V is also below Havells's 420 V full-power lower bound, so ask Havells whether the unit reaches 10 kW at that voltage. Twelve modules per string would clear 420 V (433 V hot). But 24 modules of 700 Wp is 16.8 kWp, over the 15 kW PV ceiling both brands print.

**5 kW backup on a 48 V battery.** The [three-phase hybrid comparison](/blog/best-three-phase-hybrid-inverter-homes-shops/) works the battery method in full, and we reuse it. A 5 kW load at 93% conversion draws about 5.4 kW from the battery, which is 112 A at 48 V and 122 A late in discharge at 44 V.

| Model | Backup rating against 5 kW | Battery current limit against 112 A and 122 A |

| Qbits QBH-5KS48P | 5 kW rated, no margin | 120 A passes 112 A, fails 122 A |

This is arithmetic on printed figures, not a sizing approval. The Qbits 5 kW unit clears the 112 A case with a small margin. The Havells figures, as printed, do not support a sustained 5 kW load. ## What we could not compare

- **Reliability in the field.** Neither sheet gives failure rates, returns or uptime, and we have none.
- **Service in your city.** Havells publishes a customer-care route and a network count, and Qbits has a partner directory. We used neither, and a sheet cannot show how fast a fault is attended where you live.
- **Price.** Qbits quotes per model and quantity. We hold no Havells quote and print no price for either brand.
- **Warranty.** Both brands give terms in writing for the exact model, and the terms differ by model and date. This page compares no warranty term for either brand.
- **Certificates and registrations.** Both sheets list standards and both carry certification marks in places. A listed standard does not prove that your unit is registered or certified for your DISCOM (the state power distribution company). Collect current certificates and registration numbers for the exact model from each brand, and verify them yourself.
- **Net-metering acceptance.** The DISCOM decides whether a model is accepted for net metering. [What DISCOMs check on an inverter](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) describes the review, and each brand should give you its approved-model position in writing.
- **Software and monitoring.** We neither compared apps, portals or API access nor tested either brand's monitoring.
- **Other classes.** We did not tabulate Havells's 25 kW and larger units or Qbits's matching classes. The [Qbits vs GoodWe](/blog/qbits-vs-goodwe/) page uses the same method for those.

## A checklist to take to Qbits and Havells

1. **Which unit exactly?** Get the model code, the sheet version or date and the country version. For Havells, confirm whether you are quoted the 5000 D-NG, the older 5000 D or the kit's "Enviro Plus 5000".
2. **Does the tracker current suit your module?** Compare your module's working current (Imp) and short-circuit current (Isc) with the printed tracker current and short-circuit rating. Try your string in the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/) first.
3. **Is the voltage safe on your coldest morning?** Ask for the maximum DC voltage and the full-power voltage range, then check your string at the lowest local temperature.
4. **Which certificates exist?** Ask for current test certificates and registration numbers for that exact model, with issue dates.
5. **Is it on the DISCOM list?** Ask whether the model appears on your DISCOM's accepted list and get the reply in writing.
6. **Who installs it?** Ask who installs and commissions the system and what that installer's scope covers. Havells's GREEHA brochure marks the mounting structure and earthing as the installer's or vendor's scope.
7. **Who attends a fault?** Ask who comes in your city, whom to call and how a replacement is routed, and get that on the quote or by email.
8. **What does the warranty say?** Request the written warranty for the exact model, covering the base term, remedy, claim process and exclusions.
9. **Which rows are missing?** Ask Qbits for the rows its sheet leaves out: short-circuit current, AFCI, noise, zero-export setting, hybrid switch-over time, battery compatibility and communication. Ask Havells for the 5000 HY's own battery and backup rows and the meaning of its "3750 W for a single MPPT" row.
10. **How do the other brands read?** If Solis or GoodWe is also on your list, [Qbits vs Solis](/blog/qbits-vs-solis/) and [Qbits vs GoodWe](/blog/qbits-vs-goodwe/) read them on the same rows. The [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) explains which lines to check on any sheet.
