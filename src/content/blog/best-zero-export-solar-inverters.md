The **best zero-export solar inverter in India is not the model with a “zero export” checkbox**. It is the exact inverter, meter or current transformer, controller, firmware, wiring, and commissioned settings that satisfy the written connection condition for the property.

Among current public sources reviewed on 26 September 2026, **Microtek GTI 5KW-M12 has the clearest India-facing exact-product claim** because its technical table explicitly lists “Zero Export or Adjustable Power Limit Export (Optional).” **Solis S5-GR3P5K-LV has the most complete public control documentation**: its linked exact-model manual describes zero injection, supported smart-meter routes, a zero-watt setpoint, average and per-phase modes, and fail-safe behaviour. Havells Enviro GTi 4KT-G3 states a feed-in limitation function at family level but does not publish the same control detail.

Qbits publishes this article and sells competing on-grid inverters. Qbits is not ranked because its current public QB-5KTLS evidence does not establish export limiting or the required control hardware. We did not test any system, verify a local approval, or inspect the meter, CT, firmware, settings, field response, warranty, stock, price, or service outcome.

## How the shortlist was built

A product qualified only when:

1. a current manufacturer page or PDF names the exact model or explicitly includes it in a named family;
2. that source states zero export, feed-in limitation, or export power control;
3. phase and rated output are identifiable;
4. the published wording is quoted within its actual scope;
5. linked exact-model manuals are checked for meter or CT scope, setpoints, operating modes, and fail-safe behaviour;
6. missing firmware, accuracy, response, India applicability, and approval evidence remains visible.

Dealer pages, marketplaces, search snippets, and unsupported claims about a brand's whole range were excluded. The three products are not ranked as electrical equivalents because their capacities and phases differ. The verdicts distinguish an India-facing product claim from a more detailed global control manual rather than forcing one universal winner.

## Most complete public control documentation: Solis S5-GR3P5K-LV

Solis' current global page names S5-GR3P(5-10)K-LV and lists 5K, 6K, and 10K variants. It describes the family as three phase and states “Supports export power control” plus 24-hour load-consumption monitoring.

The page links a July 2024 manual that explicitly covers S5-GR3P5K-LV and S5-GR3P6K-LV. That manual says zero injection can be achieved through the internal Export Power Management function. It identifies two optional DTSD1352-C three-phase smart-meter routes:

- a direct-insert meter with a maximum input current of 80 A;
- an external-CT meter supplied with 150 A/5 A CTs.

The manual allows the meter on the grid or load side for export control. It publishes an allowed-export setting from 00000 W to 29900 W and says grid-side or load-side meter modes default to 0 W backflow. It also documents an average three-phase mode and a per-phase mode based on the lowest-loaded phase.

Fail-safe behaviour is selectable, not automatic proof of a compliant installation. With fail-safe on, loss of the CT or meter stops generation and raises an alarm. With it off, the inverter holds the last output and can return at full unlimited power after a restart. The quotation and commissioning record must therefore state the chosen mode and prove it matches the approved condition.

This is stronger technical evidence than a feature label. It is still a global page with a European manual, not proof of current India stock, India-specific certificate scope, accessory inclusion, control accuracy, response time, or DISCOM acceptance.

**Conditional verdict:** take S5-GR3P5K-LV to technical review when a three-phase 5 kW design fits. Require the India-applicable manual, exact meter or CT order code, approved mode, settings record, and local acceptance evidence.

## A zero-export inverter is not a zero-export system

The control loop needs to know what is happening at the agreed grid-connection point. A typical design therefore includes more than an inverter:

| System element | Evidence to request | Commissioning question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measurement point | Approved single-line diagram | Does the sensor see the full net flow between site and utility? |
| Meter or CT | Exact make, model, ratio, orientation, and inverter compatibility | Is it installed on the correct conductor and direction? |
| Phase mapping | Terminal schedule and meter configuration | Does every measured phase correspond to the controller's phase? |
| Communication | Cable, protocol, addressing, termination, and firmware requirements | Is live data stable at the inverter or controller? |
| Export setting | Approved setpoint and access control | Is the configured value the value authorised for this project? |
| Failure behaviour | Manufacturer manual | What happens after meter, CT, or communication loss? |
| Verification | Test method, instruments, load steps, timestamps, and readings | Does observed grid flow stay within the written acceptance basis? |

A menu screenshot proves only that a setting was visible. It does not prove the sensor is installed at the right boundary, phases are mapped correctly, the controller receives valid data, or the plant responds as approved.

## Export limiting, zero export, and no grid connection are different

**Export limiting** can mean any cap on export. A 3 kW export cap is not zero export.

**Zero export** targets no intentional injection at the specified measurement boundary. The equipment manual and approval documents must define how that target is controlled and verified.

**Off-grid operation** means the system is not synchronised with the utility in the same way. A grid-connected zero-export system remains a grid-connected generator with metering, protection, isolation, and approval questions.

Zero export also does not create backup. The compared products are on-grid inverters. When the grid is absent, ordinary anti-islanding behaviour still applies. Backup needs a separately approved hybrid or off-grid architecture.

## The regulatory answer is address-specific

There is no state-by-state rule table in this article because the reviewed evidence does not support one stable national summary for every homeowner, consumer category, and DISCOM.

The [MNRE residential CFA operational guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) were issued on 13 June 2024. They place physical inspection with the DISCOM and keep metering and related processes subject to state regulation and the relevant DISCOM route.

The guidelines also address non-metered grid-connected systems that do not feed into the grid. Under this scheme, such systems are eligible only subject to approval by the respective State Electricity Regulatory Commission. The DISCOM must inspect the installation, verify the reverse-power-relay protection, and record suitable remarks. This scheme clause does not turn a manufacturer's zero-export feature into blanket connection approval or replace other current state requirements.

Before selecting hardware, ask the serving DISCOM in writing:

- whether export is permitted for this consumer category and address;
- whether zero export or another cap is a connection condition;
- where export is measured;
- whether phase-wise or aggregate control applies;
- which meter, CT, controller, and protection arrangement is accepted;
- what response, tolerance, averaging, and failure behaviour are required;
- what drawings, certificates, tests, seals, and settings records must be submitted;
- whether the arrangement affects net metering, gross metering, or scheme eligibility.

Do not rely on a search snippet, an old circular, another consumer's approval, or a state-wide statement copied from a sales page. The written current project condition is the governing input.

## Size from daytime load, not available roof

A zero-export plant cannot assume surplus generation will earn export credit. When onsite demand falls below available PV output, the controller must reduce generation or send energy to supported storage within the approved operating logic.

That makes time-aligned load data important. Annual consumption alone can hide a low midday base load. Ask for interval data or a defensible load profile that shows weekdays, weekends, seasonal changes, and large loads that switch on or off.

Model at least:

- PV energy available in each interval;
- site load at the same time;
- permitted export setpoint;
- curtailment when PV exceeds load plus permitted export;
- battery charging only if an exact supported storage design exists;
- grid outages and controller unavailability where relevant to the approved logic.

Do not present curtailed energy as bill savings. It was available from the array but not delivered to a paid or useful destination.

## How to test the installed control

The final procedure must come from the exact manufacturer documents and the approved connection condition. A practical evidence record should still identify:

1. inverter, controller, meter or CT models, serial numbers, and firmware;
2. approved single-line diagram and measurement boundary;
3. CT orientation, ratio, and phase mapping;
4. active control mode and authorised setpoint;
5. starting site load and PV output;
6. controlled load changes used during the test;
7. time-aligned readings at the inverter, controller, and agreed grid point;
8. observed behaviour after a measurement or communication interruption, when safe and authorised to test;
9. final settings backup, access control, seals, and handover record;
10. signatures or acceptance evidence required by the responsible parties.

Do not invent a universal response time or tolerance. Compare the observed result with the exact written acceptance basis for the project.

Require every bidder to state:

- exact inverter model, phase, rated output, hardware revision, and firmware;

- meter, CT, controller, logger, communication, and cable models;

- curtailment estimate and the assumptions behind it;
- protection, testing, commissioning, settings backup, and handover scope;
- current DISCOM and regulatory evidence for the address;
- complete written warranty, service responsibility, price, and exclusions.
