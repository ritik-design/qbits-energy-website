Choosing the right inverter capacity is one of the most consequential decisions in any solar system design. The capacity (measured in kilowatts) determines how much DC power from your panels gets converted to usable AC electricity, and indirectly influences daily generation, panel array configuration, electrical connection requirements, PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility, ROI timeline, and 25-year economic outcome.

This guide compares the three most popular residential and small commercial inverter sizes in India - 3 kW, 5 kW, and 10 kW - across every dimension that matters: typical home suitability, daily and annual generation, panel configuration, cost, electrical connection requirements, subsidy eligibility, ROI economics, and the mistakes that cost lakhs over the system's life.

## Quick Decision Reference

| Property type | Recommended inverter | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 BHK / small apartment | **3 kW** | Matches consumption, maximum subsidy efficiency |
| 2-3 BHK home (low consumption) | 3 kW | Sufficient for typical load |

Use this as the starting point, then validate against actual consumption data and future load growth plans below.

### Where 3 kW makes sense

- Monthly consumption around 250-400 units
- Single-phase electrical connection (no DISCOM upgrade needed)
- Low future load growth expected
- Maximum subsidy efficiency on a per-watt basis (₹78,000 subsidy on a 3 kW system is the best subsidy density of the three tiers)
- Apartment installations where roof area is limited

### Where 3 kW falls short

- Households planning to add EV charging in the next 5 years
- Families with consumption above 400 units/month
- Homes where roof area can support 5 kW+ DC array
- Buyers prioritising absolute rupee savings over subsidy density

### Why 5 kW dominates

The 5 kW tier wins in the residential Indian market for four converging reasons:

1. **Optimal cost-per-watt economics** - inverter and panel cost per installed watt is typically lowest in the 5 kW range due to scale efficiency at the supplier level
2. **Single-phase compatibility** - fits the vast majority of existing Indian single-phase residential connections without DISCOM upgrade
3. **Consumption match** - aligns with typical 3-4 BHK consumption of 500-800 units/month, with headroom for future growth
4. **Generation density** - 20-25 daily units covers most daytime household consumption, with surplus exported for net metering credits

### Where 5 kW falls short

- Very small homes (1-2 BHK low consumption) where 3 kW is sufficient and more subsidy-efficient
- Large villas with 1,000+ units/month consumption that justify 10 kW
- Three-phase load profiles requiring three-phase inverter

## Performance: Premium vs Budget Across Capacity Tiers

### Efficiency at rated capacity

- **Premium inverters**: 96-98% across the load range (20-100% rated power)
- **Budget inverters**: 92-94% across the same range
- **Annual generation difference**: 150-200 extra units per kW of capacity from premium efficiency

For a 5 kW system, that 4-6 percentage point efficiency difference compounds to **750-1,000 extra units per year** - roughly ₹6,000-8,000 of additional annual savings at typical residential tariffs.

### Temperature derating in Indian climate

- **Quality inverters**: maintain full rated capacity to 45 deg C ambient (often higher)
- **Budget inverters**: start derating significantly at 40 deg C, losing capacity exactly when generation is highest (peak summer afternoon)

In Indian peak summer where ambient temperatures regularly hit 42-48 deg C, the derating gap between premium and budget inverters can cost 5-10% of daily generation - cumulative across the season.

### Weather protection

- **Premium**: IP66 per IEC 60529 for any outdoor mounting in Indian conditions (monsoon, coastal salt, dust storm)
- **Budget**: IP65 common, often insufficient for outdoor mounting in monsoon belt without sheltered installation

## DC Oversizing: The Free Generation Multiplier

Modern Indian inverters support 130-200% DC oversizing - meaning the connected panel DC capacity can exceed the inverter's AC rating by 30-100%. The economics:

| Inverter AC rating | Panel DC capacity range | Generation uplift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 kW AC | 3.5-4.5 kW DC | 8-12% extra annual generation |
| 5 kW AC | 6-7.5 kW DC | 8-12% extra annual generation |
| 10 kW AC | 12-15 kW DC | 8-12% extra annual generation |

Why oversizing works: most generation hours during the day produce well below the inverter's rated capacity. Extra panels capture more morning, evening, and partly cloudy day generation that a perfectly matched inverter would miss. Only during midday peak does the inverter clip excess DC power - and clipping losses are typically smaller than the overall generation gain.

The economic case for 130-150% DC oversizing is strong in most Indian locations. Beyond 150%, clipping losses begin to eat into the gains. Confirm your specific inverter's maximum DC input rating before specifying the panel array.

## High-Wattage Panel Compatibility

The 2025-2026 panel market is dominated by 550-650W modules, with 700-750W variants now common. Inverter selection must support these higher-wattage panels:

- **Wider voltage windows** - 600-1100V max input for modern strings
- **Robust MPPT capabilities** - multiple independent MPPTs handle different orientations or shading patterns
- **Strong DC oversizing support** - 130-200% headroom for future panel upgrades

Older inverter platforms designed for 250-330W panels often cannot handle modern high-wattage panel string configurations. Always verify inverter datasheet compatibility against the specific panel SKU before procurement.

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy Interaction

| System size | Central subsidy |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 1 kW | ₹30,000 |
| Above 1 kW and up to 2 kW | ₹60,000 |
| **Above 2 kW** | **₹78,000 (maximum)** |

The central subsidy caps at the 3 kW tier. Choosing 5 kW or 10 kW does not increase the subsidy beyond ₹78,000 - but the additional generation still accrues net metering credits, which over 25 years often exceeds the marginal subsidy difference. State-level subsidies layered on top of PM Surya Ghar vary materially by state and tier.

## Common Capacity Selection Mistakes

1. **Undersizing to minimise upfront costs** - saving ₹20,000-30,000 by choosing a smaller inverter typically forfeits ₹2,00,000+ of generation over 25 years. Once the panel array layout is fixed, upgrading the inverter later is expensive and disruptive.

2. **Overlooking DC oversizing capability** - selecting an inverter that does not support 130%+ DC oversizing means you cannot capture the 8-12% generation uplift that oversizing provides.

3. **Ignoring future load growth** - electric vehicle charging adds 8-15 units/day of consumption. New appliances, increased AC usage, growing family - all push consumption upward over the system's 25-year life. Size for current consumption plus 15-25% headroom.

4. **Choosing based solely on inverter unit price** - TCO including warranty, efficiency, monitoring, and service network matters far more than the upfront price difference between premium and budget inverters.

5. **Missing the single-phase to three-phase transition cost** - choosing 10 kW for a single-phase home requires DISCOM connection upgrade costing ₹15,000-50,000 and taking 30-60 days. Factor this into the project economics, not just the inverter line item.

6. **Subsidy-only optimisation** - choosing 3 kW because it has the best subsidy density forfeits net metering revenue from the larger generation that 5 kW would have produced over 25 years.

7. **Ignoring inverter warranty differential** - a 12-year full replacement warranty vs a 5-year repair warranty changes the entire economic model. Premium warranty eliminates mid-life replacement costs (₹40,000-80,000 for a residential inverter) during the failure-prone period.

## How to Size: The Practical Method

Apply these four steps before defaulting to the popular 5 kW choice:

### Step 1: Calculate actual consumption

Pull 12 months of electricity bills. Average daily consumption in units (kWh) gives baseline load.

### Step 2: Project 5-year consumption growth

Add expected load growth: electric vehicle charging (8-15 units/day per car), additional AC units (4-6 units/day per 1.5 ton AC), growing family, lifestyle changes. Add 15-25% headroom to baseline.

### Step 3: Match generation to consumption

Target inverter generation that covers 80-100% of projected consumption. Net metering credits cover the rest. Over-sizing beyond 100% of consumption wastes capital because surplus exports earn lower compensation than self-consumed solar.

### Step 4: Validate against roof area and electrical connection

Confirm the panel array (DC kW) fits the available roof area without shading. Confirm the inverter capacity matches single-phase vs three-phase reality. Only then commit to the capacity tier.
