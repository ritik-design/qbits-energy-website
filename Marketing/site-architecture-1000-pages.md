### Worked example — `/glossary/mppt`

**Definition:** Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) is an electronic technique inside a solar inverter that continuously adjusts the operating voltage and current of a solar panel string to extract the maximum possible power, regardless of changing sunlight, temperature, or partial shading. Modern MPPT controllers achieve 99%+ tracking efficiency. Inverters with multiple independent MPPTs (e.g., dual MPPT, quad MPPT) allow strings of different orientation or shading to operate independently — important on Indian rooftops with chimneys, water tanks, or multi-facet roofs.

**Why it matters to a buyer:** An inverter with dual MPPT generates 8–15% more units on real-world Indian rooftops than a single-MPPT unit at the same kW rating. Over 25 years, that compounds to ₹1.5–3 lakh in extra generation.

**Related terms:** [PWM](/glossary/pwm) · [String](/glossary/string) · [Inverter Efficiency](/glossary/inverter-efficiency) · [DC Link Voltage](/glossary/dc-link-voltage)

**Related Qbits product:** Every Qbits hybrid inverter uses dual independent MPPTs — see [QH5](/products/hybrid/5-kw-qh5).

**FAQ:**
- Q: How many MPPTs do I need for a typical Indian home rooftop? — A: Two, if your roof has any chimney, parapet, or different orientations.
- Q: Does more MPPT = more cost? — A: Slightly, but the payback is under 18 months on most rooftops.
