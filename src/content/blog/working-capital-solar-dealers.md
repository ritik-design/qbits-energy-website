**Working capital for a solar dealer is the cash needed between paying for stock and collecting from customers.** It is not the dealership registration amount, annual turnover, or expected profit. Calculate the cash gap across actual dates before choosing any finance product.

A dealer can show profit on an invoice and still run short of cash. Stock may sit in the warehouse. A customer may pay late. Freight and taxes may fall due before the sale proceeds arrive. Finance can bridge a measured timing gap, but it cannot make slow stock or doubtful receivables disappear.

> **Short version:** list each cash outflow and receipt by date, run a slower-sales case, and find the largest negative balance. Compare funding only after you know that number. Do not assume Qbits credit, stock finance, returns, margins, or sales.

This guide is for education and preparation. It does not recommend a lender or finance product, and it is not financial, tax, accounting, or legal advice.

## Separate launch investment from ongoing working capital

Launch investment answers, `What cash must we commit to start?` Working capital answers, `What cash remains tied up while the operating cycle repeats?`

The two overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

| Cash need | Typical timing question | Correct owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration, deposit, premises, demo unit, and opening setup | What must be paid before launch? | [Solar dealership investment guide](/blog/solar-dealership-investment-india/) |
| Inventory, freight, tax timing, customer credit, and operating costs | How much cash is tied up before collection? | This working-capital guide |
| Manufacturer payment terms and approved supplier limit | When is an eligible invoice due, and what exposure counts? | [Distributor credit and territory guide](/blog/solar-distributor-credit-territory-terms/) |
| Margin, rebate, and contribution | What is earned after the stated costs and triggers? | [Solar dealer margin guide](/blog/solar-dealer-margin-india/) |

Do not use annual sales as the working-capital number. Turnover measures activity over a period. Working capital is a balance and timing problem. A fast stock cycle with customer advances can use less cash than a smaller business carrying ageing inventory and long receivables.

## Map the stock cash cycle before asking for finance

Start with one real replenishment cycle. Use the exact products and dates in the proposed order, not an average copied from another dealership.

1. Record the purchase order date and any advance.
2. Record the supplier invoice, tax, freight, insurance, and unloading dates.
3. Record when the stock becomes saleable.
4. Estimate the sale date for each model using supportable demand, not a target.
5. Record the customer advance, invoice, and expected collection dates separately.
6. Add operating payments due before collection, including payroll, rent, delivery, and service logistics.
7. Add confirmed supplier credit only under its current written rules.
8. Repeat the timeline with slower stock movement and delayed collections.

Use a stock sheet that exposes ageing and cash, not only unit counts:

| Stock line | Quantity | Landed cash outlay | Cash paid date | Expected sale date | Expected collection date | Written return or rotation right | Downside issue |

Leave the return column blank unless a current agreement creates that right. A verbal assurance is not a stock exit plan.

## Calculate the peak gap across dates

The calculation must run as a timeline. A month-end snapshot can hide a shortage that occurs earlier in the month.

For each date, calculate:

`Net cash movement = cash receipts on that date - cash payments on that date`

`Closing available cash = opening available cash + cumulative net cash movement`

The lowest closing balance shows the peak operating shortfall in that scenario. Keep the contingency reserve separate so the business can see what caused the underlying gap.

Use this blank schedule:

| Date | Stock and supplier payments | Tax, freight, and other payments | Operating payments | Customer receipts | Confirmed finance draw | Closing available cash |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Use one consistent tax basis. Ask your accountant how recoverable tax, input credit, and the business's accounting policy should appear. Do not improve the model by counting a requested facility, expected rebate, disputed receivable, or hoped-for return as available cash.

### Run three cases, not one forecast

A single sales case tells you whether the plan works if the plan works. Finance risk sits in the cases where it does not.

| Scenario | Change these inputs | Decision it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating case | Supported stock movement and collection dates | Day-to-day funding need |
| Slow case | Later sales, later collections, unchanged payment obligations | Whether the limit survives a weaker cycle |
| Exit case | No new sales, old stock remains, facility reduces or ends | Whether the business can settle obligations without an assumed buyback |

Choose the delays from your own customer and stock records. This guide does not supply a default sales rate, collection period, or reserve percentage because those inputs would create false precision.

## Compare finance by the gap it can actually fund

The cheapest-looking facility may not cover the moment when cash is needed. Compare scope, timing, control, and exit terms before cost.

| Funding route | What it may address | Questions to answer in writing | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Own business cash | Stock, operating payments, and reserves selected by the owner | How much remains available after other obligations? | Ties up cash that may be needed elsewhere |
| Customer advance | A specific customer order | Is it refundable, restricted, or linked to delivery milestones? | Does not fund unsold stock unless the contract permits that use |
| Supplier credit | Eligible supplier orders or invoices | What counts towards exposure, when is payment due, and when can credit be suspended? | It is a commercial term, not free capital or profit |
| Cash credit or overdraft | Revolving operating needs under the approved facility | How is drawing power set, what security and reporting apply, and how can the limit change? | Sanctioned limit and usable headroom may differ |
| Term working-capital loan | A defined borrowing amount under a repayment schedule | What are the total cost, instalments, security, covenants, and prepayment terms? | Fixed repayments may continue after stock slows |
| Receivables finance or TReDS | Eligible confirmed customer receivables | Must the seller and buyer participate, which invoices qualify, and when is payment final? | Does not finance unsold inventory |

The [Reserve Bank of India's TReDS FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=132), dated 1 January 2020 and still available when checked on 26 September 2026, describes TReDS as an electronic platform for financing MSME trade receivables through multiple financiers. The FAQ says only MSMEs participate as sellers and explains that the process uses a confirmed invoice or bill obligation. It also says transactions processed through TReDS are without recourse to the MSME seller.

That does not mean every dealer invoice qualifies. Participation, buyer acceptance, platform rules, pricing, and transaction eligibility still need confirmation. TReDS should not appear in an inventory model until the actual receivable can use it.

## Compare the total borrowing terms, not only the stated rate

For an MSME term loan, ask for the Key Facts Statement before accepting the facility. The [Reserve Bank of India's 15 April 2024 circular](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12663&Mode=0) applies its harmonised KFS requirements to new retail and MSME term loans sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024 by the regulated entities it covers.

The circular defines the Annual Percentage Rate as the annual cost of credit including interest and other charges associated with the facility. It also states that third-party charges recovered through the regulated entity, such as insurance or legal charges, form part of the APR and must be disclosed separately. Fees not mentioned in the KFS cannot be charged during the loan term without the borrower's explicit consent.

Use the KFS to compare term-loan offers on the same amount and tenure. Then reconcile it with the full agreement, repayment schedule, security documents, and sanction conditions.

Do not assume that the term-loan KFS circular governs a cash-credit or overdraft facility in the same way. For a revolving facility, request the applicable sanction letter, interest and charge schedule, drawing-power method, stock-statement requirements, review conditions, default clauses, and closure process.

## Prepare a finance file that reconciles

A finance provider may request different documents based on the borrower, facility, security, and risk review. Prepare a clean file, then obtain the provider's exact current list.

The working file can include:

- legal identity, ownership, KYC, and business-address records;
- GST and Udyam records where applicable;
- filed tax returns and signed financial statements for the periods requested;
- current bank statements and existing facility statements;
- stock by model, quantity, landed cost, and age;
- customer receivables and supplier payables by age;
- purchase orders, customer orders, invoices, and collection evidence;
- the proposed stock cycle and downside cash-flow model;
- details of current loans, guarantees, security, and contingent obligations; and
- written supplier terms, including payment, returns, credit notes, and termination treatment.

Use the same reporting date across working capital, bank debt, cash-credit limits, receivables, and stock. Reconcile totals to the underlying statements. Do not count an undrawn limit twice or describe a requested facility as sanctioned.

## What most stock-finance plans get wrong

The biggest error is financing the sales target instead of the cash cycle. A target does not establish when a unit will sell, what the customer will pay, or when the money will arrive.

Other common errors are just as costly:

- valuing stock at expected selling price instead of the cash basis used in the model;
- buying a broad product mix without demand by model or customer type;
- treating available supplier credit as permanent;
- assuming slow stock can be returned, rotated, or bought back;
- counting a back-end rebate before its target and payment conditions are met;
- overlooking freight, insurance, damage, redelivery, and warranty logistics;
- using invoice dates as collection dates;
- using receivables finance for an invoice that has not been accepted or is not eligible; and
- matching short-lived stock needs with repayments the business cannot support after sales slow.

Finance increases the consequences of a weak stock decision. It does not correct one.

## What the Qbits application does and does not establish

As checked on 26 September 2026, the Qbits partner form asks applicants for working capital, total bank-loan amount, cash-credit limits, other capital, and the bank to which assets are hypothecated. It also asks about turnover, warehouse capacity, team, experience, and proposed sales commitment.

These are application fields. They do not establish a public Qbits minimum, finance product, credit limit, payment period, stock commitment, margin, territory, approval rule, or sales outcome. The public page also does not state that Qbits arranges working-capital finance.

Prepare supportable figures from one date. If the application progresses, request the proposed stock, payment, freight, return, price-change, warranty-logistics, and exit terms in writing. Then rerun the cash model with those terms.

If your dealer or distributor plan is documented and finance-ready, submit the [official Qbits partner application](/become-our-partner/) using the role that matches your business. Application is the start of review, not a finance sanction or permission to order stock.
