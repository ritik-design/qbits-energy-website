India's solar inverter market is easy to describe and hard to measure. Every grid-connected system needs an inverter, and capacity additions are well documented. What is not well documented is the market in rupees or in brand shares, where published numbers differ by a factor of two or more. This report keeps to figures that name a source and a date. Where a number cannot be sourced, it says so instead of filling the gap. Data points were checked between 12 and 14 October 2026.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - Published 2025 market-size estimates range from USD 386.7 million (IMARC) to USD 0.86 billion (Market Research Future). Treat the spread as the finding.
> - Capacity is better measured than revenue: India added about 44.6 GW of solar in FY2026 (JMK Research) and 35.2 GW in January to September 2026, of which 9.8 GW was rooftop.
> - Mercom India reported inverter shipments to Indian solar projects rose 40.8% in 2025, with central inverters at 51% of shipments.
> - Gujarat (6,882 MW) and Maharashtra (5,442 MW) led rooftop capacity in March 2026 (MNRE data via Down To Earth). The top ten states hold close to 86% of the total.
> - MNRE publishes no ALMM list for inverters. A proposal for domestic-manufacturing rules for inverters and batteries is reported as under consideration.

Mercom India tracks the inverter side directly. It reported that [inverter shipments to solar projects in India increased 40.8% in 2025](https://www.mercomindia.com/solar-inverter-suppliers-leading-indias-market-in-2025) compared with 2024, driven by record installations and a strong pipeline for commissioning in the first half of 2026, and that central inverters accounted for 51% of total shipments. Mercom's 2024 leaderboard coverage put string inverters at 51.3% and central inverters at 48.7%, so the mix swung back towards central units in 2025 as large ground-mount plants came online.

Two cautions apply. First, those are shares of shipments to solar projects, a measure that is weighted towards utility-scale plants where central inverters are common. It is not a residential split. Second, rooftop figures differ by source and cut-off date: MNRE data compiled by Climate Compatible Futures showed 25.7 GW of cumulative rooftop capacity at March 2026, while JMK's September 2026 figure is 33.4 GW, a different date and a different compilation.

## Segment definitions: residential, C&I and utility

The three segments buy different products for different reasons, which is why a single national "market share" is of limited use to a buyer.

Table: Inverter segments, typical product and what drives the purchase

| Segment | Typical inverter | What drives the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential rooftop | Single-phase or small three-phase string inverter, or a hybrid inverter where backup is wanted | PM Surya Ghar eligibility, installer recommendation, warranty and local service |
| Commercial and industrial rooftop | Three-phase string inverters, often several per site | Tariff savings, datasheet specifications, monitoring, references |
| Utility-scale | Central inverters and large string inverters | Tenders, bankability, supplier balance sheet |

The two market reports that publish segment shares disagree, which is itself informative. TechSci estimated for 2025 that string inverters held about 43% of the market, on-grid systems about 72%, three-phase models about 67% and utilities about 51%. Market Research Future estimated string inverters at 45.6%, three-phase at 71.8% and utility-scale at 65.4%. Both agree on the direction: three-phase and on-grid products dominate, because utility and commercial plants dominate revenue. Residential is the fastest-growing application in Market Research Future's forecast, at 16.0% a year from 2026 to 2035.

For a household, the relevant point is that the inverter is usually chosen by the installer. The procurement checks that matter to you are covered in the [on-grid inverter](/on-grid-inverter/) and [hybrid inverter](/hybrid-inverter/) hubs.

PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is the main driver of residential rooftop demand. Reports in October 2026 describe a scheme with an outlay of INR 750 billion, a target of 10 million households and a 30 GW rooftop target, with the current scheme ending on 31 March 2027 ([TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/markets/india-may-mandate-local-inverters-batteries-for-rooftop-pv), 12 October 2026, citing Mint). ## Five checks that matter for the Indian grid and climate

A brand that does well in Europe or China still has to cope with Indian grid conditions, heat and service distances. These are the checks procurement teams actually use. They are a checklist, not a score.

> [!CHECKLIST] Five checks for Indian grid and climate
> - **Grid compatibility.** Indian feeders see voltage sags, brownouts and frequency excursions. The allowed AC range depends on the model, the DISCOM's connection conditions and the inverter's grid-code setting, so read it from the exact model's datasheet and confirm the anti-islanding test.
> - **Climate tolerance.** Check the ingress protection rating for the install location, and read the temperature derating curve to see what output the inverter keeps in summer rooftop heat. The datasheet, not the brochure, states both.
> - **Compliance posture.** BIS registration under the Solar Photovoltaics goods order lists IS 16221 (Part 2) for safety and IS 16169 for islanding prevention. [Saur Energy reported](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/mnre-extends-self-certification-for-200-kw-with-solar-inverters-till-dec-31-2026-12151390) that MNRE extended self-certification for inverters above 200 kW to 31 December 2026 or until further orders. Check the current notice before relying on any date. Our [BIS glossary entry](/glossary/bis-certification/) explains the registration route.
> - **Service reach.** Ask for the written warranty and service terms for the exact model, and for a service partner who covers your pincode. A warranty is only as useful as the nearest person who can act on it.
> - **Monitoring.** Check whether fault alerts reach the installer without a dashboard login, and who owns the monitoring data. See our guide to [solar inverter monitoring systems](/blog/solar-inverter-monitoring-systems-in-india/).

## ALMM and inverter compliance are separate checks

MNRE's ALMM covers solar modules (List-I) and cells (List-II). MNRE publishes no inverter list. List-II was notified to be effective from 1 June 2026, so a module on List-I must use cells from a List-II manufacturer ([Energetica India, 10 December 2024](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/almm-list-ii-for-solar-pv-cells-to-be-effective-from-june-1-2026)). MNRE has since issued clarifications and exemptions for some project types, so check the current position on [MNRE's site](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/).

For inverter buyers this means two parallel checks: the module and cell requirement against the ALMM lists, and the inverter against its own certificates and the DISCOM's accepted-make list. A seller who answers an inverter question with an ALMM claim is answering the wrong question. Our [ALMM scope guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) walks through the difference.

The direction of policy is worth watching. TaiyangNews reported on 12 October 2026 that the Renewable Energy Secretary said proposals for locally made inverters and batteries in the next phase of PM Surya Ghar remain under consideration. Nothing has been notified.

## Trends: hybrid systems, storage and monitoring

Hybrid inverters, battery storage and remote monitoring all feature in installer conversations, but only some of it can be quantified from public sources.

**Hybrid and storage.** No public series tracks hybrid inverter shipments in India. The constraint on storage is supply: TaiyangNews, citing Wood Mackenzie, reported domestic battery-cell capacity of about 2 GWh in 2026 against a tender pipeline of roughly 260 GWh. A hybrid inverter without a locally available battery is a partial system, so the battery decision matters as much as the inverter. Our [on-grid versus hybrid ROI analysis](/blog/on-grid-vs-hybrid-solar-inverters-roi/) compares costs under different outage patterns.

**Monitoring.** Remote monitoring is moving from an extra to an expected feature, because installers servicing many sites cannot visit each one. The useful test is whether the system tells the installer what is wrong, not only that something is.

**EV charging.** Some commercial and society projects combine rooftop solar with EV charging. We found no sourced India share for this, so we make no estimate. It is a design consideration for inverter sizing and export control.

## Challenges that limit growth

Table: Challenges that limit rooftop solar growth, what each means for a project and how to mitigate

| Challenge | What it means for a project | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DISCOM approval and net metering delays | Subsidy and savings start later than the installation date | Confirm the DISCOM process and expected timeline before signing; see [how to apply for net metering](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) |

The [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) processes remain the hardest constraint to solve at project level, because they depend on grid infrastructure and administration rather than the installer. Where a feeder cannot take exports, a [net metering](/glossary/net-metering/) alternative or an export-limited design is a conversation with the DISCOM, not a decision for the inverter vendor.

And treat any single market-size figure with no named source, however confident it sounds, as an unverified claim rather than a statistic.

## What the data means for buyers and installers

- **Do not anchor on a market-size number.** The estimates differ by a factor of more than two, and none is measured sales.
- **Use shipment and capacity data for direction, not for brand choice.** Mercom's ranking is weighted to large plants. A home buyer should compare exact models.
- **Treat rooftop concentration as a service question.** If your state is outside the main rooftop states, ask harder questions about local service.
- **Separate the module check from the inverter check.** ALMM answers the first. Certificates and DISCOM acceptance answer the second.
- **Expect rules to move.** The local-content proposal for inverters is under consideration, not law.

Qbits Energy publishes datasheets for its on-grid and hybrid models on the [datasheet page](/download-datasheets/). For warranty and service terms, obtain the current written terms for the exact model and sale, since the public datasheets do not set a universal duration, remedy or service commitment.
