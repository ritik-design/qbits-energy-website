## What a Solar Takeoff Includes

A complete solar takeoff is not a module count. It is the full procurement and labour list the project office needs to order materials, schedule crew, and price the bid. A useful takeoff lists every category below.

- Modules. Brand, model, wattage, count, spare allowance.
- Inverters. String or microinverter, count, MPPT inputs used, AC output rating.
- Racking. Rail length in meters, clamp count, end clamp count, splice count, L-feet or hooks.
- [DC cables](/glossary/dc-cable/). Conductor size, total length, MC4 pair count.
- [AC cables](/glossary/ac-cable/). Conductor size, run length from inverter to combiner and combiner to meter.
- [Conduit](/glossary/conduit/). Type, diameter, total run length, sweeps, junction boxes.
- Breakers and fuses. AC main breaker, DC string fuses, combiner fuses.
- [Combiner boxes and disconnects](https://heavengreenenergy.com/products/acdb-dcdb/). Quantity, ampere rating, weatherproof rating.
- [Mounting hardware](https://heavengreenenergy.com/products/mounting-structures/). Bolts, washers, flashings, grounding lugs, grounding conductor length.
- [Balance of system](https://heavengreenenergy.com/products/balance-of-system/). Surge arrestors, monitoring gateway, sensors, signage.
- Labour estimate. Module mounting hours, wiring hours, inverter commissioning hours.

Miss any of those lines and the bid is wrong. The procurement team back-orders parts on install day, the crew waits, and the margin erodes.

## Common Takeoff Mistakes

The bids that lose money rarely lose on module pricing. They lose on the lines the estimator forgot. The five most common misses.

1. Missing labour. The BOQ lists every part and zero hours. The crew shows up on day one and the margin is gone by lunchtime.
2. Forgetting AC cable to the meter. The DC side is detailed and the AC run to the service panel is left blank. On a long warehouse roof that single line can swing the bid by tens of thousands of rupees.
3. Wrong mounting count. End clamps are not mid clamps. Splices are not free. Counting one clamp per module instead of one per module plus end clamps per row understates the hardware by ten to fifteen percent.
4. Missing spare parts. Modules break in transit. MC4 connectors get crushed. A spare allowance of two to three percent protects the install schedule.
5. No contingency. Cable offcuts, breakage, weather delays. A two to three percent contingency line keeps the project office honest with the buyer.

Auto-takeoff does not eliminate judgment. It does eliminate the lines that get skipped because the estimator was tired.
