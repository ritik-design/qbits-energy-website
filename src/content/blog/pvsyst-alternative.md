## Why Engineers Look for a PVsyst Alternative

PVsyst is the gold standard for bankable yield reports. Lenders and IPPs trust the methodology, and the project files that drop out of it have funded gigawatts of solar globally. Most engineers shopping for an alternative are not unhappy with the math. They are unhappy with the operational surface around the math, and with how isolated PVsyst is from the rest of the team's workflow.

### Desktop install, Windows-only

PVsyst is a desktop application that runs only on Windows. Mac and Linux users either run a virtual machine, dual-boot, or skip the tool entirely. New engineers have to install, license, and update a local copy on every laptop. Multi-seat teams cannot share active project sessions. The browser-based [solar design platform](https://surgepv.com/solar-designing) model has overtaken this on every other dimension.

### No proposal builder, no financial model

PVsyst stops at the yield report. There is no proposal builder, no CRM, no e-signature, no real cashflow model tied to local tariffs. Teams maintain a separate set of tools for every step downstream of the simulation. Each handoff is a copy-paste step, and each copy-paste is a chance for the proposal to drift from the model.

### Per-seat licensing that compounds for teams

PVsyst's Standard licence is roughly €500 per user per year, and growing teams accumulate licences linearly. Add a Mac that is incompatible, a contractor who needs a second seat, and a junior engineer who only runs occasional reports, and the licence drawer fills up fast. SurgePV's per-seat ladder is flatter and gets cheaper at team size.

### UX age and learning curve

PVsyst's UX has roots in the late 1990s. It rewards experience and it punishes new engineers. Onboarding a junior into bankable simulation can take weeks. Browser-based tools with modern UX cut that to days.
