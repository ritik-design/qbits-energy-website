The inverter is one line in a solar project's cost, and financing usually covers the whole system. The right financing strategy transforms these capital-intensive purchases into manageable, ROI-positive investments aligned with business or household cash flow profiles. The wrong financing approach either ties up working capital that should fuel growth or pays unnecessary interest costs that erode 25-year ROI.

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - Six main financing routes exist: upfront cash, EMI loans, [CAPEX vs OPEX](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/solar-ppa-vs-capex-vs-opex-india/)/PPA, leasing, government subsidies, and IREDA/bank-specific products.
> - We quote only lender rates we could read: SBI Surya Ghar 5.75% up to ₹2 lakh and 7.90% above (as on 15 December 2025); HDFC Personal Loan from 9.99%; IREDA lends to business and institutional projects, not households.
> - [PM Surya Ghar](https://quickestimate.co/blog/pm-surya-ghar-emi-options) provides up to ₹78,000 for residential systems and can be combined with EMI financing for a cash-flow-neutral monthly outcome.
> - Section 32 depreciation at 40% WDV (confirm with your accountant) gives business buyers a year-one tax shield that can favour CAPEX over OPEX/PPA; compare the two on your own numbers.
> - A longer model-specific written warranty may help with some lenders, but lenders differ: ask for any rate or tenor concession in writing and do not assume one.
> - Subsidy, financing and tax benefits each have their own conditions; add them up for your own case rather than assuming a combined percentage.

## Understanding Inverter Financing in 2026

India's installed solar capacity was 171.05 GW on 30 September 2026 (MNRE data reported by Saur Energy, 8 October 2026), and JMK Research reports about 44 GW of solar added in FY2026. The market has developed specialised financing mechanisms for solar equipment procurement, addressing working capital challenges for EPCs, distributors, and end-customers.

Five forces shape the 2026 financing landscape:

- **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** with up to ₹78,000 central subsidy driving residential adoption
- **IREDA lending** to business, industrial and institutional renewable projects
- **Bank product specialisation** for solar across major public and private banks
- **NBFC growth** in solar-specific financing with faster processing
- **Corporate sustainability mandates** driving CAPEX commitments from large businesses

For any buyer profile, financing options are now more accessible than at any previous point in the Indian solar market.

## Financing Option 1: Upfront Cash Purchase

The simplest and historically most common approach for cash-rich buyers.

### Advantages

- **Eliminates interest charges** entirely
- **Simplifies accounting** with single asset capitalisation
- **Qualifies for [accelerated depreciation](/glossary/accelerated-depreciation/)** at 40% WDV in year one (Section 32)
- **Tax shield offsets opportunity cost** of capital deployed
- **No ongoing payment obligations** to track
- **Negotiating leverage** with EPCs for better pricing on cash deals

### Best for

- Established businesses with strong cash flow and tax liability
- Residential buyers with available capital and tax-advantaged accounts
- Distributors managing working capital cycles
- Buyers prioritising simplicity over leverage

### Considerations

For business buyers with significant tax liability, the after-tax effective cost of upfront purchase is often lower than financed alternatives once the Section 32 depreciation tax shield is applied. Run the after-tax comparison before defaulting to financing.

> [!EXAMPLE] Year-one tax shield on a ₹40 lakh purchase
> At 40% WDV in year one, the depreciation is ₹16 lakh. At a 30% corporate tax rate that is ₹4.8 lakh of immediate tax savings on a ₹40 lakh inverter investment (the figures used in the Section 32 answer in the FAQ below). This is why upfront cash can beat a loan for a business with tax liability.

## Financing Option 3: CAPEX vs OPEX Models

Strategic choice for commercial and industrial buyers between owning the solar asset and consuming the electricity as a service.

Table: CAPEX versus OPEX/PPA for commercial and industrial solar, pros and cons

| Model | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[CAPEX](/glossary/capex/)** (own the system) | Full ownership, asset control, depreciation benefits, lower 10-year TCO | Capital intensive upfront, project management overhead |
| **[OPEX](/glossary/opex/)/PPA** (third-party owns) | Pay-as-you-go, off-balance-sheet, bundled maintenance, no capital required | Higher lifetime cost, no depreciation benefit, no asset ownership |

### Decision drivers

- **Tax liability**: businesses with high tax liability favour CAPEX for Section 32 depreciation
- **Capital availability**: businesses constrained on capital prefer OPEX
- **Operational ownership**: businesses wanting full control prefer CAPEX
- **Balance sheet management**: businesses preferring off-balance-sheet leverage OPEX

> [!IMPORTANT] The condition that flips CAPEX against OPEX
> Tax liability is a major driver, but there is no universal threshold. Compare CAPEX (with the depreciation tax shield) against the PPA tariff over the same period on your own numbers. A business short of capital may still prefer OPEX/PPA.

## Financing Option 4: Leasing Structures

Less common but useful for specific buyer profiles.

Table: Operating lease versus finance lease structures for solar equipment

| Lease type | Structure | End-of-term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operating lease** | Monthly rental payments, asset on lessor's balance sheet | Asset returned to lessor |
| **Finance lease** | Higher monthly payments, asset on lessee's balance sheet | Ownership transfers to lessee |

Typical terms:

- 5-10 year tenor
- Minimal upfront capital
- Tax treatment varies (operating lease deductible as rental; finance lease subject to depreciation rules)

### Best for

- Businesses preferring off-balance-sheet structures
- Buyers without sufficient capital for upfront purchase
- Specific accounting or tax planning requirements

## Required Documentation

Standard documentation across most loan applications:

### For business borrowers

- Business registration certificate (proprietorship, partnership, LLP, or company)
- GST returns for last 2 years
- 2-3 years of audited income tax returns
- Bank statements (6-12 months)
- Inverter quotations from approved manufacturers
- Client contracts demonstrating project pipeline (for EPCs)
- Land/premises documents
- Identity proof of directors/partners

### For residential borrowers

- Identity and address proof (Aadhaar, PAN)
- Income proof (salary slips for last 6 months, or ITR for last 2 years)
- Bank statements (6 months)
- PM Surya Ghar registration certificate (if applicable)
- Quotation from empanelled installer
- Roof ownership / society NOC for installations

### For IREDA-specific loans

- Additional technical documentation: system design, panel specifications, EPC capability statement
- Detailed project DPR for larger installations
- Performance guarantees from EPC

## Closing

Financing strategy is not separate from product selection - they compound. A model-specific written warranty does not just protect operations; with some lenders it may help financing terms, so ask the lender to confirm. Subsidies do not just reduce upfront price; they accelerate payback. Section 32 depreciation does not just save tax; it changes the right answer between CAPEX and OPEX. Build your financial structure around your specific project profile, integrate the subsidies and tax benefits available to your buyer category, and treat financing as a strategic lever rather than a back-office function. The framework above is the same one Indian solar buyers use to convert capital-intensive procurement into manageable, ROI-positive investments across every system size from residential to utility-scale.
