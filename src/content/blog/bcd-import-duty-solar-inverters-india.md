  - q: "What is the Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on solar inverters imported into India?"
    a: "As of 2026, the Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on solar inverters (HS code 8504) imported into India is 20%. This applies to string inverters, central inverters, and microinverters imported from outside India, including from China, Germany, Taiwan, and all other countries. BCD is levied on the assessed customs value (CIF + landing charges). In addition to BCD, IGST (5% or 18%, depending on how the inverter is classified and supplied), social welfare surcharge, and other levies apply. This policy significantly increases the landed cost of a fully imported solar system compared to systems using domestically manufactured components."

> [!KEYTAKEAWAYS]
> - BCD on imported solar inverters (HS code 8504) is 20% as of 2026; modules attract 40% and cells 25%, on top of IGST at 5-18% and a 10% Social Welfare Surcharge on the BCD amount.
> - Inverters manufactured in India, regardless of the parent company's ownership, attract zero BCD and only domestic GST, a structural cost gap of roughly 20-25% versus imported units.
> - Chinese OEMs such as Sungrow, Growatt, Huawei, and Deye are exposed to the full 20% BCD on units imported directly, unless they scale India manufacturing.
> - On a 10 kW imported inverter with ₹40,000 CIF value, total duties add up to roughly ₹17,584, pushing the landed cost to ₹57,584 before freight.

Understanding these policies is not just about compliance. It is about margin protection, project eligibility, and competitive positioning in a market where 90%+ of residential installations are now linked to subsidy and net metering programmes.

## BCD on Solar Inverters: What the Rates Actually Are

The [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) and BCD are two separate policy instruments that work in parallel. Understanding both is essential.

### Customs Duty Structure for Solar Equipment (2026)

Table: Customs duty structure for solar equipment, 2026 (effective total computed with the SWS and IGST method below)

| Product | HS Code | BCD Rate | IGST | Effective Total Duty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solar inverter (string/central) | 8504 | **20%** | 5%–18% | ~28–44% |
| Solar PV module | 8541 | **40%** | 5% | ~51% |
| Solar PV cell | 8541 | **25%** | 5% | ~34% |
| Mounting structure (aluminium) | 7610/7604 | 10%–15% | 18% | ~31–38% |
| DC cable | 8544 | 10% | 18% | ~31% |
| Battery (LFP/lithium) | 8507 | 10%–15% | 18% | ~31–38% |

*Rates subject to change with Union Budget notifications. Verify current rates at [CBIC](https://www.cbic.gov.in/) before procurement.*

The 20% BCD on inverters is significant but lower than the 40% on modules, reflecting the government's intent to protect domestic module manufacturing most aggressively, while inverter manufacturing is at an earlier stage of domestic development.

### How Total Customs Incidence Is Calculated

The Import Duty Impact Calculator applies the following formula:

> [!FORMULA] Total customs incidence on an imported inverter
> **Step 1:** BCD = CIF Value × BCD Rate
>
> **Step 2:** Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) = BCD × 10%
>
> **Step 3:** IGST base = CIF + BCD + SWS
>
> **Step 4:** [IGST](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) = IGST base × IGST Rate
>
> **Step 5:** Total incidence = BCD + SWS + IGST

**Example:** 10 kW imported inverter, CIF value ₹40,000

Table: Worked duty calculation for a 10 kW imported inverter with a CIF value of ₹40,000

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BCD (20%) | ₹40,000 × 20% | ₹8,000 |
| SWS (10% of BCD) | ₹8,000 × 10% | ₹800 |
| IGST base | ₹40,000 + ₹8,000 + ₹800 | ₹48,800 |
| IGST (18%) | ₹48,800 × 18% | ₹8,784 |
| **Total duties** | | **₹17,584** |
| **Landed cost** | ₹40,000 + ₹17,584 | **₹57,584** |

The same inverter manufactured in India and sold domestically would attract only IGST at 5–18% on the ex-works price, depending on classification, no BCD, no SWS. The landed cost advantage of domestic manufacture is approximately 20–25% before factoring in logistics.

## The Import Duty Impact Calculator

### The Import Duty Impact Calculator

Apply this five-step framework to any inverter procurement decision involving an imported product:

<!-- steps -->
1. **Determine CIF value**: get the supplier's CIF India price (Cost, Insurance, Freight to Indian port). Do not use ex-works prices without adding freight, insurance, and port handling.
2. **Apply BCD**: multiply CIF by 20% for inverters, 40% for modules, 25% for cells.
3. **Calculate SWS**: multiply BCD by 10% (Social Welfare Surcharge is levied on BCD).
4. **Calculate IGST**: sum CIF + BCD + SWS, then multiply by the applicable IGST rate (5% or 18% depending on product classification).
5. **Compare with a domestically made alternative**: the domestic product's true cost is ex-works price + domestic freight + GST input credit. The net difference reveals the true import premium.

Pairing this framework with a broader [solar design and estimation toolkit](https://surgepv.com/tools/) lets EPCs model landed cost alongside system design and string sizing in the same workflow, rather than treating customs math as a separate spreadsheet exercise.

> [!EXAMPLE] Duty bill for an EPC doing 50 residential jobs a month
> For an EPC doing 50 residential installations per month using a 5 kW imported inverter at ₹25,000 CIF:
>
> - BCD: ₹5,000
> - SWS: ₹500
> - IGST (18%): ₹5,490
> - Landed cost: ₹35,990 vs ₹25,000 ex-works
>
> At 50 units/month, the monthly import duty spend = 50 × ₹10,990 = **₹5,49,500 per month** in duties alone. Switching to a domestically manufactured alternative avoids this import duty, directly improving EPC margins.

## How BCD Affects Chinese OEM Inverters in India

### Growatt

Growatt is a major mid-market player with aggressive pricing in India. Their core strategy has been direct import, making their landed pricing sensitive to BCD fluctuations. Growatt has been working on localisation to reduce duty exposure, but as of mid-2026, most Growatt models in the Indian market are imported. EPCs using Growatt on PM Surya Ghar projects must verify each model's BIS registration and current certificates, and confirm DISCOM requirements, before procurement.

### Made-in-India Brands: Qbits, Luminous, Microtek, and Others

Brand nationality does not prove the country of origin, import route, customs treatment or GST outcome of a particular inverter. Those facts depend on the goods, tariff classification, importer, invoice and current law. Obtain the country-of-origin and customs documents for the exact SKU, and use a qualified tax or customs adviser for the transaction.

MNRE's current ALMM page publishes PV module and cell lists, not an inverter list. Domestic manufacture therefore should not be presented as a structural inverter-ALMM advantage.

## ALMM and Inverters: What to Check for PM Surya Ghar

The schemes below apply the ALMM requirement to solar modules (List-I) and cells (List-II). MNRE publishes no ALMM list for inverters, so the inverter checks further down are about the exact model's own documents.

- PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (residential subsidy programme)
- Central government-funded solar projects (SECI, CPSU schemes)
- State government solar schemes that adopt ALMM as a module eligibility criterion

### Inverter Verification Checklist for EPCs

Before confirming any inverter purchase for a PM Surya Ghar project:

> [!CHECKLIST] Inverter verification for a PM Surya Ghar project
> - **Step 1:** Ask the seller for the exact model's current BIS registration and test certificates. For modules, download the current ALMM list from [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/).
> - **Step 2:** Match the exact model number, wattage, and firmware version on the certificates to the invoice.
> - **Step 3:** Verify the manufacturer name on the certificates matches the invoice (brand name vs legal entity name sometimes differ).
> - **Step 4:** Check the certificate validity date, because certificates have an expiry and renewal cycle.
> - **Step 5:** Keep the certificates and, for modules, the downloaded ALMM list version and date as project documentation.

## Policy Risk: What EPCs Must Watch for in 2026 and Beyond

BCD rates are determined by Union Budget notifications and can change annually. EPCs with forward order books need to track three policy signals:

- **BCD revision upward**: if the government increases BCD on inverters from 20% to 30% (as has been discussed for a phased approach similar to modules), the landed cost of imported inverters jumps further. Domestic alternatives become even more attractive.
- **ALMM list expansion**: MNRE periodically adds new module and cell manufacturers, and has not published an inverter list. EPCs should watch for any change in the scope of the list and for new listed module brands.
- **PLI scheme for inverters**: the Production-Linked Incentive scheme for advanced chemistry cells and certain electronics components may extend to solar inverters, encouraging more domestic manufacturing. EPCs who build relationships with PLI-beneficiary manufacturers gain supply chain stability.
- **BIS mandatory certification expansion**: currently BIS certification is required for certain inverter categories; expansion of mandatory BIS coverage would further restrict non-certified imported products.

## Common EPC Mistakes in Import Duty Management

- **Using ex-works prices in project BOMs**: ex-works pricing from a Chinese supplier is meaningless without adding the full duty incidence. BOMs submitted to clients should reflect landed costs, [quotation software that itemises landed cost line by line](https://quickestimate.co/features/quotation-system/) rather than ex-works pricing prevents this discrepancy from reaching the client.

- **Choosing imported inverters to save ₹5,000/unit without checking certificates and DISCOM requirements**: the subsidy is ₹30,000–₹78,000 per system. No unit-level saving justifies risking the homeowner's subsidy through a missed document check.
- **Not building BCD escalation risk into multi-year EPC contracts**: if BCD increases from 20% to 30% mid-contract, the EPC absorbs the margin hit unless the contract has a material cost escalation clause.
