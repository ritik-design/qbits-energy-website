## The 5 kW Inverter Fit Test — 4 Questions Before You Buy

This is the named framework for this post. Run through the four questions in order. Your answers narrow the field to one or two products.

### The 5 kW Inverter Fit Test

1. **How many hours of unscheduled power cuts do you face on a peak-summer weekday?** — If the answer is fewer than one hour per day, an on-grid inverter gives you the fastest payback and full PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility. If the answer is two hours or more — or if the cuts are unscheduled and unpredictable — you need a hybrid inverter. An on-grid inverter shuts down the moment the grid disconnects due to [anti-islanding](/glossary/anti-islanding/) safety requirements; it generates zero electricity during those hours regardless of how bright the sun is.

2. **Do you plan to add battery storage within the next five years?** — If yes, buy a hybrid inverter now even if you do not plan to install the battery immediately. Adding battery capability later requires replacing the entire inverter on an on-grid unit, which costs ₹45,000–₹70,000. A hybrid inverter with the battery port unused costs ₹20,000–₹35,000 more at purchase, but eliminates the rewiring and replacement cost later. If your answer is definitively no — you live in a metro with reliable grid power and no plans to change — an on-grid unit is the financially correct choice.

3. **Is the installer quoting an ALMM-listed model?** — This is a non-negotiable gate. PM Surya Ghar subsidy is only released after your DISCOM verifies that the installed inverter is on the [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/). A non-ALMM inverter means you forfeit ₹66,000–₹84,000 in central subsidy on a 5 kW system. Always ask the installer for the ALMM certificate number for the specific model being quoted — not just the brand name, because ALMM listing is model-specific, not brand-wide.

4. **What is the effective warranty cost per year?** — Take the inverter purchase price, divide by the warranty years, and compare brands on warranty cost per year rather than sticker price. A ₹50,000 inverter with a 12-year warranty costs ₹4,167 per year of coverage. A ₹35,000 inverter with a 5-year warranty costs ₹7,000 per year. The cheaper unit costs 68% more per year of protection — and does not cover the years when the inverter is most likely to need service (years six through ten of a typical 180–270 V Indian grid operating environment).

Applying this test honestly to your situation produces one of two answers: on-grid if your grid is reliable and you want the fastest payback, or hybrid if power cuts are frequent or battery future-proofing matters. Both answers are correct; the mistake is choosing the wrong one and discovering the error after installation.

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy on a 5 kW System: The Complete Economics

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy structure is often misunderstood in the context of a 5 kW system. Many homeowners assume the subsidy caps at 3 kW and nothing above that is covered. That is incorrect.

The central subsidy scale for residential systems is:

- **First 1 kW**: ₹30,000 (flat)
- **Next 2 kW (i.e., 1–3 kW)**: ₹18,000 per kW = ₹36,000
- **Above 3 kW up to 10 kW**: ₹9,000 per kW

For a 5 kW system: ₹30,000 + ₹36,000 + (2 kW × ₹9,000) = **₹84,000** maximum central subsidy.

## Technical Specifications That Actually Matter for a 5 kW Inverter

Most online comparisons list efficiency and price, then stop. For an Indian residential installation, five additional technical parameters determine whether a 5 kW inverter performs reliably for twelve years or requires service calls within three.

- **MPPT count** — Single-MPPT inverters work well for roofs where all panels face the same direction with no shading. Dual-MPPT inverters are essential for east-west split roofs, L-shaped terraces, or any installation where one string is partially shaded at different times of day. For a 5 kW inverter with 12–14 panels, dual-MPPT allows the two panel groups to be tracked independently, recovering 8–15% more generation from mixed-orientation rooftops. The [MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) glossary entry explains the tracking mechanism in detail.

- **Maximum DC input voltage** — Most 5 kW string inverters accept 600 V DC maximum. Premium models accept 800 V DC, which allows longer string configurations with fewer parallel strings, reducing DC cable losses and combiner costs. For most Indian residential rooftops of 30–40 panels, 600 V DC is adequate. Verify the string voltage at minimum temperature (December in North India) does not exceed the rated maximum, or the inverter will shut down on cold mornings.

- **Input voltage range (MPPT tracking range)** — A wide MPPT tracking range (80–550 V is typical for a 5 kW unit) ensures the inverter begins tracking as early as possible after sunrise and continues tracking until close to sunset, maximising daily generation. Inverters with a narrow tracking range start and stop generating later and earlier, losing 10–20 minutes of peak generation per day.

- **Rated efficiency and weighted efficiency** — The peak efficiency figure (often 98.x%) applies only at rated power under ideal conditions. The weighted efficiency (CEC or European weighted average) is the realistic number — it accounts for operation at partial load, which is where most residential inverters spend the majority of their operating hours. Always compare weighted efficiency, not peak efficiency.

- **Warranty depth and type** — As covered in the Fit Test, a 12-year full replacement warranty is materially different from a 5-year prorated warranty. The distinction matters most in years six through ten, when capacitor and cooling fan wear typically begin to surface in inverters operating in 40–45 °C ambient environments. See the [how to evaluate solar inverter reliability](/blog/how-to-evaluate-solar-inverter-reliability/) post for the full reliability framework.

## Five Mistakes Indian Buyers Make When Choosing a 5 kW Inverter

Even well-researched buyers make avoidable errors at the 5 kW tier. These five mistakes account for the majority of service complaints and subsidy rejections received by Indian solar installers.

**Mistake 1: Buying on-grid in a power-cut zone.** The anti-islanding protection in every grid-tied inverter disconnects the system the instant the grid fails. A 5 kW on-grid inverter in Lucknow is completely non-functional during the four to six hours of summer load shedding that are typical in many UP districts — precisely the hours when your AC would be running at peak. The inverter is not broken; it is doing exactly what it is supposed to do. But it is useless to you.

**Mistake 2: Failing to verify ALMM listing for the specific model.** Installers sometimes quote a brand that has ALMM listing on some models but not the exact unit being installed. The subsidy application will be rejected at the DISCOM verification stage. This mistake costs the homeowner ₹66,000–₹84,000 in forfeited subsidy and often requires a partial system replacement. Always ask for the ALMM certificate number for the exact model number in the quote. The [how to choose a solar inverter for home in India](/blog/how-to-choose-solar-inverter-for-home-india/) guide covers the full verification process.

**Mistake 3: Buying a 5 kW inverter with a single MPPT for an L-shaped or east-west roof.** Single-MPPT inverters optimise for the single best operating point across all panels simultaneously. If half your panels face east and half face west, the inverter compromises between the two string voltages all day, losing 10–15% of potential generation. A dual-MPPT inverter tracks each string independently and captures full generation from both orientations. On a 5 kW system generating 20 units per day, that 15% loss is 3 units per day — ₹9,000–₹12,000 per year at current tariffs.

**Mistake 4: Choosing by efficiency alone.** The difference between a 97.6% and a 98% efficient inverter on a 5 kW system amounts to approximately 0.4 units per day — roughly ₹1,000–₹1,500 per year. The difference between a 5-year and a 12-year warranty, if the inverter fails in year eight, is ₹50,000–₹70,000 in replacement cost. Warranty depth has a ten to fifty times larger financial impact than efficiency at the margin for most residential buyers.

**Mistake 5: Not confirming the installer is an empanelled PM Surya Ghar vendor.** The subsidy application must be submitted through the PM Surya Ghar portal by the registered installer. An unregistered installer — regardless of competitive pricing — cannot complete the application on your behalf. Before signing any agreement, ask the installer for their registration ID on the PM Surya Ghar portal and verify it at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/). This single check prevents the most common subsidy disbursement failure.
