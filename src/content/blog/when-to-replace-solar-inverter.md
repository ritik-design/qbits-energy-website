**Repair a solar inverter when a qualified diagnosis identifies a repairable fault, suitable parts and an authorised repair path are available, and the comparable repair scope fits the owner's downtime and risk limits.** Replace it when those conditions fail or when a compatible replacement has the better documented outcome. Age alone is not the decision.

Do not open the enclosure, bypass protection or keep resetting a safety-related fault to collect more evidence. Start with records that can be gathered externally, then use qualified service and the exact model documents.

## What should be recorded before requesting service?

**Record the exact model, serial number, displayed message, event time and operating conditions before the first service conversation.** Add clear external photos, monitoring screenshots, recent generation history and any work performed before the fault. This gives the technician a usable history without asking the owner to make electrical measurements.

Create a case file containing:

| Record | Where to find it | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model and serial number | Equipment label and invoice | Identifies the correct manual, policy and parts |
| Fault text or code | Display or monitoring portal | Preserves the original symptom |
| Date, time and recurrence | Event log | Shows whether the issue is isolated or repeating |
| Grid and weather context | Owner notes and monitoring | Helps distinguish site conditions from an internal fault |
| Recent changes | Installer or service records | Flags wiring, firmware or equipment changes |
| Warranty and service documents | Handover pack | Identifies the correct claim route |

Use the [solar inverter error-code guide](/blog/solar-inverter-error-codes-guide/) to organise the information, not to guess which component has failed. Codes and remedies are model-specific.

## Which findings require a safety-first response?

**Smoke, burning smell, visible damage, water entry, abnormal heating or repeated protection operation needs a safety-first response.** Keep people away, follow the approved site shutdown or emergency procedure if it can be performed safely, and contact qualified service. Do not remove covers or defeat an isolator or protective device.

A red light or code does not always mean the inverter itself needs replacement. The cause may sit in the array, AC supply, communications, protection, wiring or another connected device. That is why a diagnosis should identify the failed function and evidence, not just quote a new unit.

If the system is connected to the grid or includes a battery, the isolation and stored-energy conditions may be different from a simple appliance. Use the exact manuals and site single-line diagram.

## What should a qualified diagnosis contain?

**A useful diagnosis names the observed fault, tests performed, measurements taken, likely cause and recommended corrective action.** It should also state what was not tested and whether the system is safe to remain isolated or operate under any restriction. “Inverter faulty” is not enough for a repair-or-replace decision.

Ask for a written service note with:

- Exact inverter, firmware and connected equipment identified.
- Fault history and monitoring evidence reviewed.
- External array, grid, protection and communication causes checked as applicable.
- Measurements and test equipment recorded.
- Repairable assembly or non-repairable condition identified.
- Parts, authorisation and expected service path confirmed.
- Safety status and next action stated.

If a supplier recommends replacement without a model-specific diagnosis, ask which evidence rules out repair and whether the quote includes removal, installation and recommissioning.

## How should warranty and service coverage change the decision?

**Check the controlling product warranty, installer agreement and service terms before accepting paid work.** The headline warranty period does not establish the remedy or who pays diagnosis, labour, freight, removal and reinstallation. Start a documented claim if the diagnosed condition may be covered.

The [solar inverter warranty guide](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty/) lists the terms and evidence to retain. Keep ticket numbers, submitted files, written decisions and promised dates. Do not authorise an outside repair until you understand whether it changes any remaining coverage.

Installer workmanship and manufacturer product responsibility may be separate. A wiring or protection defect can sit outside a product warranty even when the inverter reports the symptom.

## When is repair the better-supported option?

**Repair is better supported when the fault is identified, the manufacturer or authorised service route supports the repair, required parts are available, and the written quote has acceptable scope, warranty and downtime.** The decision should use current evidence for the exact model rather than a universal age or price threshold.

Check these conditions:

- The cause has been separated from array, grid and installation problems.
- The proposed repair follows an authorised procedure.
- Parts are new or their status and warranty are disclosed.
- The repair quote includes labour, travel, freight and taxes as applicable.
- The repair warranty and exclusions are written.
- The expected service and recommissioning sequence is stated.
- Repeating faults and previous repair history have been considered.

A low repair quote is not comparable if it excludes the board, freight or second site visit that the diagnosis already indicates may be needed.

## When is replacement the better-supported option?

**Replacement is better supported when the unit is not safely or authoritatively repairable, required parts are unavailable, the same fault remains unresolved, or a complete compatible replacement case is stronger on documented cost and downtime.** Replacement may also be necessary when project requirements have changed and the old model cannot meet them.

Do not infer failure from age alone. Confirm why the existing unit cannot return to acceptable service. Useful replacement evidence includes a written non-repairable finding, unavailable-parts confirmation, repeated service records, or a like-for-like comparison showing that repair cannot meet the owner's defined requirements.

If the owner now needs backup, additional capacity or a different plant-control function, treat that as a new design decision. It should not be presented as proof that the old unit has failed.

## What compatibility checks are required before replacement?

**A replacement must match the existing PV array, AC connection, protection, communications and any battery system.** Verify the exact module and string data against the proposed inverter's voltage and current limits under design conditions. Then check phase, output rating, grid settings, mounting, connectors and monitoring requirements.

The replacement review should cover:

| Interface | Required check |
| --- | --- |
| PV array | String voltage, operating window, current and tracker assignment |
| AC connection | Phase, voltage, output, protection and approved connection |
| Battery | Voltage, current, chemistry, BMS and model compatibility |
| Mechanical | Location, clearances, enclosure limits and mounting |
| Communications | Logger, portal, meter, plant controller and account transfer |
| Compliance | Documents required by the current project and DISCOM process |

Use the [inverter datasheet guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) and current manuals. Do not assume a newer inverter with the same kilowatt label is a drop-in replacement.

## How should repair and replacement costs be compared?

**Compare equivalent scopes and keep uncertain events as labelled scenarios.** Repair cost may include diagnosis, parts, labour, travel, freight and recommissioning. Replacement may include equipment, design checks, removal, mounting or cable changes, monitoring setup, approvals, commissioning and disposal of the old unit.

Use this worksheet:

| Cost or condition | Repair | Replacement | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnosis already paid |  |  | Invoice |
| Equipment or parts |  |  | Dated quote |
| Labour, travel and freight |  |  | Quote scope |
| Design and compatibility review |  |  | EPC scope |
| Installation and recommissioning |  |  | Quote scope |
| Expected downtime |  |  | Service schedule or scenario |
| Warranty after work |  |  | Written terms |
| Exclusions and unresolved risk |  |  | Service note and contract |

The [solar inverter TCO worksheet](/blog/inverter-tco/) explains how to keep downtime and replacement assumptions visible. Do not invent a failure probability or claim that one option always saves money.

## Will replacement affect approvals or net metering?

**Ask the relevant DISCOM or authorised project representative before changing a grid-connected model.** Notification, document, test and approval requirements can differ by state, connection agreement and project type. Obtain the applicable requirement in writing and include it in the replacement scope.

The module-focused ALMM lists should not be used as an inverter registry. For the inverter, request the exact certificates, registrations and grid-interface evidence required by the project and current connection process. Match every document to the proposed model and revision.

## What should be handed over after the work?

**The owner should receive a closed service record for a repair or a complete recommissioning pack for a replacement.** The record should make the next fault easier to diagnose and preserve evidence for warranty, insurance and future service. It also establishes what changed and which party remains responsible.

For a repair, retain the diagnosis, parts used, work performed, test results, repair warranty and updated ticket. For a replacement, also retain the old and new serial numbers, final design changes, settings, commissioning record, monitoring access, product policy and any DISCOM communication.

Contact a listed [authorised service partner](/authorized-service-partners/) or the responsible installer for a model-specific diagnosis. Product, electrical, warranty and approval owners should review the decision before work begins.
