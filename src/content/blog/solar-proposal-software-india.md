That error comes from Excel, and from proposal tools built for markets where incentives are percentages. ## What An Indian Solar Proposal Has To Get Right

Four things decide whether an Indian residential proposal survives contact with the DISCOM and the customer.

**The subsidy slab, applied as a slab.** ₹30,000 per kW to 2 kW, then ₹18,000 per kW to 3 kW, hard cap ₹78,000, residential only. A 5 kW system receives the same ₹78,000 as a 3 kW system. Any tool that multiplies system size by a single rate produces a wrong number. SurgePV's [PM Surya Ghar guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/india/guides/pm-surya-ghar) sets out the current structure.

**ALMM enforcement at design time, not procurement time.** ALMM List I covers modules and List II covers inverters, and both refresh quarterly. A component can be delisted between your quote and your installation, and using a non-ALMM product disqualifies the installation from central financial assistance entirely. Filtering the component database against the live list is the only reliable defence, and SurgePV's [ALMM compliance guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/india/guides/almm-compliance) covers how the lists work.

**IS and CEA standards.** IS 14086 governs modules on performance and safety, IS 16221 governs inverters including anti-islanding, and the CEA Technical Standards for Connectivity of Distributed Generation Resources set earthing and maximum system voltage. Maximum DC voltage is commonly capped at 600 V for residential in many states, with commercial reaching 1,000 V. A design that breaches the state DC voltage limit fails inspection regardless of how good the proposal looks.

**DISCOM-specific net metering.** Export compensation typically runs ₹3 to ₹5.50 per unit against APPC. Settlement matters as much as the rate: monthly settlement wastes summer surplus, while annual carry-forward banks it. Two identical systems in two states can differ by more than a year on payback purely on this. See SurgePV's [net metering regulations guide](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/india/guides/net-metering-regulations) for the state picture.

## The Subsidy Error That Costs Indian Installers Deals

> **What most Indian installers get wrong.** They quote the subsidy as a rate, not a slab. On a 5 kW residential system, applying ₹30,000 per kW gives ₹1.5 Lakh. The customer is actually entitled to ₹78,000. That is a ₹72,000 hole in the proposal, and it surfaces after the customer has already made the decision.

Here is the arithmetic on three common system sizes. This is an illustrative calculation using the published MNRE slab structure.

| System size | Flat-rate quote at ₹30,000 per kW | Correct PM Surya Ghar entitlement | Overstatement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | ₹60,000 | None |
| 3 kW | ₹90,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹12,000 |
| 5 kW | ₹1,50,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹72,000 |
| 8 kW | ₹2,40,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹1,62,000 |

The 2 kW case is why the error persists. Small systems quote correctly under both methods, so an installer can run the flat-rate template for months without noticing, then apply it to a 5 kW enquiry and produce a proposal that is off by ₹72,000.

Two consequences follow. The obvious one is the refund conversation and the cancelled job. The less obvious one is that the installer quoting correctly looks expensive next to the installer quoting incorrectly, and loses the deal on a comparison that was never valid. Automatic slab application is not a convenience feature in this market. It is the difference between competing on price and competing on a fiction.
