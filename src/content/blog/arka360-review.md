## What Arka 360 Is and Where It Came From

Arka 360 is a browser-based platform for designing solar systems, running shading and generation analysis, and producing customer-facing proposals. It is built by Arka Energy, headquartered in Bengaluru, with the company founded in 2017 according to its own company profile.

The product covers the sequence an installer actually runs. You start from an address, build a 3D model of the roof, place modules, run a shading study, produce a generation report, and generate a sales proposal. CAD export and a CRM add-on sit alongside that on every published India plan.

The origin story matters commercially. Most solar design software sold in India was designed for North American or European buyers first. The financial model assumes net metering credited against a retail bill. The compliance layer assumes NEC. The module database is weighted toward brands an Indian EPC will never quote. Arka 360 did not start from that position, and Indian users notice the difference in the first hour.

## What Arka 360 Genuinely Does Well

These are real strengths, and skipping past them would make this review useless.

**It was built for Indian workflows, not translated into them.** This is the single biggest thing Arka 360 has going for it. When a product is designed around a market from the start, the defaults are right. Nobody has to explain to the tool that the customer is comparing against a DISCOM slab tariff rather than a US utility rate schedule. Competitors that entered India later carry assumptions that have to be worked around, and working around assumptions is where designer hours disappear.

**Browser-based with no installation.** A designer needs a login and a laptop. That sounds minor until you have onboarded a junior engineer in a Tier 2 city on a desktop application with a licence dongle and a version-mismatch problem. Cloud delivery removes an entire class of operational friction, and it is why cloud tools have taken share from installed software across the category.

**Residential design and proposal output are solid.** The core loop works. You get a 3D roof model, a shading report, a generation estimate, and a proposal a homeowner can read. For the volume of work that dominates Indian rooftop, that is the whole job. Tools that are more capable are not automatically better here, because a 5 kW system on a clean RCC roof does not reward extra depth.

**Open pricing.** Arka publishes numbers. Most competitors in this space make you sit through a discovery call to learn what a seat costs.

**It understands the Indian market context.** Micro-inverter design on the Basic plan, CAD export on every plan, and a CRM add-on bundled rather than sold as a separate product line all reflect how small Indian installers actually operate, with one or two people doing design, quoting, and follow-up.

## Where Arka 360's Fit Runs Out

We are only going to state limits we are confident about. There are plenty of things we have seen asserted about Arka 360 that we could not verify, and those are not in this review.

**Depth of bankable reporting.** Arka 360's Premium plan includes a PVsyst export. That is a useful feature and a clear signal about intended workflow: for lender-grade yield modelling, the expectation is that you finish the job in PVsyst. For residential and small commercial sales that is completely fine, because no homeowner asks for a P90. For a C&I project going to a lender or an IPP, it means two tools, two licences, and a reconciliation step between them. If your pipeline is moving in that direction, price the second licence into the comparison.

**Breadth of multi-country compliance.** Arka runs distinct regional editions with separate pricing pages and separate feature sets. That is a sensible way to serve one market properly, and it is the same trade Aurora made in the United States. The cost shows up for a team quoting across borders. An Indian EPC that starts winning work in the Gulf or East Africa is not simply changing a country dropdown. Platforms that carry NEC, IEC, AS/NZS, and IS code libraries plus multiple tariff structures in a single licence handle that case with less friction. This is a difference in product strategy, not a defect.

**Plan-level project caps at the entry tier.** The Lite plan is capped at 360 residential projects. For a solo designer that is generous. For a growing team it is a ceiling you will hit, and the step up to Basic is a 52% increase in annual cost. Worth modelling before you anchor on the ₹46,000 number.

> **What most buyers get wrong.** They compare Arka 360 against a US platform on design features and conclude it is the cheaper option. The real question is different: what does your next two years of pipeline look like? If it is Indian residential, Arka's fit is strong and the price is fair. If it includes bankable C&I or a second country, you are comparing one licence against two.

## An Illustrative Example: A Coimbatore EPC Choosing Between Them

This is a worked illustration, not a real customer. Take an EPC with four designers doing roughly 70% residential rooftop under PM Surya Ghar and 30% small commercial in Tamil Nadu.

On the Arka 360 side, Premium at ₹1,00,000 a year plus 18% GST is ₹1,18,000. If that licenses the whole account, it is the cheapest credible option on the table and the decision is straightforward. If it is per seat, four designers land near ₹4,72,000, roughly $5,600 at 2026 rates, which is close to SurgePV's five-seat arithmetic of $1,299 times four, or $5,196. That single contract question moves the answer, which is why it is the first thing to ask.

Now change one variable. The EPC wins a 900 kW captive rooftop that a bank will finance. That project needs a yield report the lender accepts. On the Arka path, the design is exported to PVsyst and the bankable run happens there, adding a licence and a reconciliation step. On the SurgePV path, the P90 comes out of the same project file that produced the layout.

Change one more. The same EPC opens a branch in Sri Lanka. Regional editions mean a second setup and a second commercial conversation. A single multi-country licence does not.

The point is not that one tool wins. It is that the decision is driven by pipeline shape, not by the design canvas, and most buyers evaluate the canvas.

## Who Should Buy Arka 360

- Solo designers and small Indian residential installers, where Lite at ₹46,000 a year is very hard to beat on value.
- Teams whose work sits inside one region and does not need lender-grade yield output.
- Installers who want design, proposals, and basic CRM from one vendor without integration work.
- Buyers who value published pricing and want to avoid a procurement cycle.
- Anyone whose bottleneck is quoting speed on straightforward rooftop jobs.

## Who Should Not Buy Arka 360

- EPCs whose pipeline includes bankable C&I, PPA, or IPP work, where the PVsyst step becomes a permanent second workflow.
- Teams quoting across several countries from one office, where regional editions create duplicate setup.
- Engineering-led firms that need detailed stringing, SLDs, and yield depth as standard rather than as a top-tier upgrade.
- Buyers who need white-label proposals in multiple languages as a core requirement.

## Verdict

Arka 360 is a good product that deserves its standing with Indian installers. Building for a market from the inside is genuinely harder than localising a foreign product, and the difference is visible in how quickly an Indian designer becomes productive in it. At ₹46,000 to ₹1,00,000 a year, the pricing is fair for what it delivers, and publishing those numbers openly is a point in its favour.

The limits are about scope rather than quality. The PVsyst export on Premium tells you where bankable yield modelling is expected to happen, which means a second tool for project-finance work. Regional editions mean a team selling in more than one country manages more than one setup. Neither is a flaw. Both are the consequence of a deliberate decision to serve one market properly, and that decision is why the product is good at what it is good at.

So the recommendation splits cleanly. If you are an Indian residential installer or a small EPC working inside one region, buy Arka 360 and stop shopping. If your pipeline includes bankable commercial work, or you expect to quote outside India within two years, SurgePV covers both in a single licence at $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year, and that is where the arithmetic turns.

Three things to do next:

- Ask Arka whether your plan is licensed per seat or per account, because that answer changes the comparison more than any feature does.
- Write down the share of your next 24 months of pipeline that will need a lender-accepted yield report.
- Rebuild three of your recent projects in both tools and compare the finished proposal, not the design screen.
