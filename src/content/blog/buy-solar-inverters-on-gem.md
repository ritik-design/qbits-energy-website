On a GeM solar inverter bid you do not write the specification or the document list. The department's bid page and its Additional Terms and Conditions (ATC) do. GeM is the Government e-Marketplace, and an EPC or supplier sourcing inverters for it has three jobs.

1. Separate what GeM itself requires from what this buyer added.
2. Get the brand owner (the OEM) to authorise you in writing for that bid.
3. Tie every specification line to a datasheet page before you quote.

This post is for the supplier side of a GeM bid. It is not the PM Surya Ghar vendor route, which the [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) covers. It is also not the private-sector RFQ (request for quotation) you send to inverter bidders; for that, use the [solar inverter RFQ template](/blog/solar-inverter-rfq-template-epc/). Once you win and place your own order with the brand, the [bulk inverter supply checklist](/blog/inverter-procurement-india/) takes over.

## Which GeM bids contain a solar inverter?

The two bids we read show two common shapes. A third shape, the custom or BoQ (bill of quantities) bid, appears in GeM's own bid text. GeM says it should not replace a regular category item.

| Shape | Example read | What the bidder supplies | Who may bid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate contract for inverters alone | CREDA, bid GEM/2026/B/7935715, 21 Aug 2026. Sixteen schedules from 1 kW single-phase to 100 kW three-phase | Inverters, delivered to field offices, quantity not fixed | The ATC says only the OEM or its authorised representative, dealer or distributor |
| Turnkey on-grid plant | CTTC, bid GEM/2025/B/6138725, 15 Apr 2025. Plants of 100, 150 and 250 kWp | Design, supply, installation, testing and commissioning, with the inverter inside the scope | The notice invites OEMs or authorised agents only |

In both, the inverter is judged through documents about the OEM, not only about you. That is the point most suppliers miss, and the rest of this post follows from it.

## How do GeM rules and the buyer's ATC differ in a bid?

A GeM bid page has two sources of rules. The first is GeM's own bid-detail block and standard text. The second is the buyer's ATC, which the buyer adds after approval from its competent authority. GeM's text on the bid says the buyer alone is responsible for the effect of the ATC. Objections go to the buyer, not to GeM.

The same GeM text lists clauses that, if the buyer includes them, make the bid and the resulting contract null and void. Several matter to an inverter bidder:

- mandating a specific brand, make, model or manufacturer, except single-bid or proprietary article certificate buying;
- making physical documents a pre-qualification condition (other than EMD and the signed integrity pact), or seeking samples at bid stage, unless the buyer's own ministry or department policy allows trial or samples;
- referring to conditions published on an external site or to external documents;
- asking for a tender or bid participation fee;
- adding ATC clauses that contradict the bid-detail section, including specifications, EMD, ePBG, and the Make in India and MSE preference sections.

If you disagree with a clause, GeM gives sellers a representation window in the seller dashboard. According to the archived seller FAQ, the time you have depends on bid length:

- one day for bids of 3 to 5 days;
- two days for bids of 6 to 9 days;
- four days for bids of 10 days or more.

The buyer is to reply 24 hours before the bid closes. Confirm current timings on the live page, because we could only read the 2024 copy.

### The CREDA inverter bid, layer by layer

This is a closed bid, used as a worked example. The left column shows what each layer set.

| Topic | GeM bid-detail page | Buyer's ATC and bid document |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Documents from seller | Experience criteria, past performance, bidder turnover, a certificate requested in the ATC, OEM Authorization Certificate, up to four ATC documents, compliance of BoQ specification with supporting document | A 21-item checklist: turnover and net worth certificates, ITRs, test reports, vendor registration with CREDA, manufacturer authorisation, datasheets for each category, service centre details |
| Money tests | Minimum average annual turnover of Rs 33 lakh over 3 years; EMD Rs 2,00,000 | Aggregate turnover of at least Rs 1 crore in the inverter field over 3 years; positive net worth of Rs 25 lakh |
| Experience | 3 years of past experience for same or similar items | At least 300 kW of on-grid inverters supplied to government bodies, in any three of the last five financial years, on a prescribed certificate format. Private sales are not counted |
| Who qualifies | Startup relaxation: No | OEM and authorised dealers or distributors only; no consortium; no start-ups |
| Evidence of product | Compliance of BoQ specification | Valid test reports for each category from an MNRE-approved or NABL lab, in the name of the bidder or OEM. A sample-fee receipt is accepted at bid stage; the valid certificate is due within 90 days of agreement |
| Origin | Not set in the bid-detail pages we read | Inverter, charge controller and other BOS to be of Indian origin, with an undertaking on indigenous components |
| After-sales | Not set in the bid-detail pages we read | Five-year onsite warranty; GPRS monitoring with SIM and data charges for 60 months in the bidder's scope; an authorised service centre, preferably at Raipur |

The ATC is where the inverter risk sits. A bidder who read only the GeM page would have missed three things: the test-report-per-category rule, the five-year onsite warranty and the 60 months of monitoring data.

### Where the two layers disagreed in this bid

In the copy we read, the GeM bid-detail page shows the bid ending on 9 September 2026 at 17:00. The notice inside the ATC says online submission closes on 11 September 2026 at 17:00, with samples inspected on 10 September. Both cannot be the deadline.

Treat the earlier time as your deadline, because the portal accepts or rejects the upload. Then ask the buyer in writing which date stands. That is our advice, not a rule from GeM.

The ATC also asks for hard copies and samples, and says addenda will be published on the buyer's website. GeM's standard text restricts physical documents, samples (unless buyer policy allows them) and references to external documents. We are not saying the CREDA clauses are invalid. The buyer's policy may permit them, and its ATC states that only the documents uploaded on GeM are evaluated. We are saying a bidder should read these clauses against GeM's list and use the representation window if the buyer's position is unclear. Until the buyer withdraws a clause, comply with it.

## What OEM authorisation does a GeM inverter bid need?

Suppliers often ask the brand for "an authorisation letter" and receive one that does not fit. The bids and GeM's FAQ show three separate things.

| Document | Issued by | What it shows | Where it is asked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Catalogue authorisation on GeM | OEM, through GeM's OEM Panel and reseller mapping | That you may list or pair with the OEM's product in a category | GeM seller FAQ. Resellers must upload authorisation where OEMs list the product |
| Bid-level OEM Authorization Certificate | The OEM, for one bid | That this OEM authorises this seller for this tender | Listed under documents required from seller in both bids read |
| Manufacturer Authorization Form (MAF), with test report consent and warranty undertaking | The OEM, in the form the ATC describes | Authority to supply, permission to use the OEM's test reports, and written warranty backing | CREDA ATC |

The archived GeM seller FAQ sets two rules. In certain categories (Quadrant 1 and Quadrant 2) only OEMs or their authorised resellers may transact. A seller who is both must say which they are for each product.

A brand owner that does not sell directly in India can have its Indian representative register as a "Deemed OEM". That needs an authorisation letter on the OEM's letterhead. We could not confirm which quadrant the On Grid Solar Inverter category sits in, so check it on the category page.

### What to ask the OEM to put in the letter

This list is ours, built from the clauses above. Send it to the brand as a table so nothing is left to a standard template.

| Ask | Why the bid needs it |
| --- | --- |
| Bid number and buyer name | Ties the letter to the bid being evaluated, so a generic letter does not invite a clarification query (our recommendation) |
| Exact model designations against each schedule line | The ATC wants test reports and datasheets for each category, so the letter must match the lines you quote |
| Validity date that covers the bid and the contract | The worked example below shows the gap a short validity leaves |
| Consent to use named test reports, with report numbers and the lab | CREDA asks for consent from the owner of the test reports |
| The warranty form the ATC specifies, attached as the OEM's written terms for the exact model | CREDA wants a five-year onsite undertaking; do not bid on a brochure. See the [warranty and service checklist](/blog/inverter-warranty-service-checklist-bulk-buyers/) |
| OEM turnover certificate, if the bid has an OEM turnover test | The CTTC bid sets a separate OEM average turnover criterion |
| Local content certificate with percentage and locations, if you want purchase preference | CTTC requires it from the OEM, and Class 1 status in that bid needed at least 50% local content |
| Service support statement for the buyer's state | CREDA wants a service centre in Chhattisgarh |

A deemed OEM may not fit a clause that asks for proof of being the manufacturer in the manufacturer's name. Raise that in the pre-bid queries.

### Worked example: authorisation validity against bid validity

This is illustrative arithmetic on CREDA's dates. The GeM page shows an offer validity of 180 days from the bid end date, which is 9 September 2026 on that page.

- 9 September 2026 plus 180 days is 8 March 2027.
- The rate contract itself is for 12 months, and the ATC says approved rates run for one year from the award date.

So an authorisation letter valid to 31 December 2026 would lapse about ten weeks before the offer validity ends. It would lapse well before the rate contract does. Ask for a validity that runs to the end of the contract, or a written commitment to renew.

Pre-bid timing matters too. In this bid, pre-bid queries closed on 27 August 2026, six days after issue. The OEM letter, test report consent and local content certificate all need the brand's internal sign-off. Send the request on the day you download the bid, not the day before it closes.

## Match each specification line to a datasheet page

For a quote that survives technical evaluation, put every parameter in a table with a page reference. These rows come from the CREDA specification. For the 50 kW class, the [50 kW inverter comparison](/blog/best-fifty-kw-solar-inverter-india/) lists the datasheet fields to line up across brands.

| ATC parameter (CREDA) | What to find in the datasheet | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum efficiency above 95% and weighted efficiency above 94%, measured per IS/IEC 61683 | Peak efficiency and the weighted figure, with the test method | Many datasheets give peak efficiency only. A missing weighted figure needs a written statement, not a guess |
| Operating temperature from minus 15 to plus 60 degrees C | Operating range and the output derating curve | A unit can operate across the range and still reduce output above a stated temperature. State which |
| IP65 | Ingress rating of the exact model | Ratings are per model, not per brand |
| Power factor 0.95 lag to 0.95 lead | Adjustable power factor range | Confirm it holds at rated active power |
| CEA protection limits: trips at 110% and 80% voltage, 50.5 Hz and 47.5 Hz, 60 second reconnection, islanding cease within 2 seconds, DC injection under 0.5% | Default settings and adjustable range | The numbers are the same CEA clauses used in the RFQ template; ask for test report references |
| Standards: IS 16221 (Part 2) with IEC 62109-2, IS 16169 with IEC 62116, IS 17980 with IEC 62891, IEC 61727 | Certificate and lab report for the exact model | A family datasheet does not show which sizes a test report covers |
| GPRS monitoring with SIM and data charges for 60 months | Communication options and the platform | Put the 60-month data cost in your price. A plain Wi-Fi unit may not meet this line |

For reference, Qbits datasheets publish IP66, the MPPT voltage window, maximum DC voltage and communication options per family. One example is the [6 to 17 kW three-phase TLC family](/our-products/QB-6-17KTLC/). A published figure that beats the bid still has to appear on the datasheet of the exact model, and the buyer decides compliance. The [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) shows how to check the model string against the document.

### The BIS line

India's Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025 was notified in the Gazette on 27 January 2025. According to the PIB release of 29 January 2025, it covers inverters used in solar PV applications. Products must conform to the latest Indian Standards and carry the BIS Standard Mark under a BIS licence. The order applies to manufacturers, importers, distributors, retailers and sellers, so a GeM reseller is inside its scope. It came into effect 180 days from publication.

The CREDA ATC makes the same order a mandatory compliance standard and cites it by Gazette number. We did not read the gazette text itself.

The route for inverters above 200 kW is handled separately and has been extended by MNRE notices. Read the [inverter regulations guide](/blog/solar-inverter-regulations-india-2026-bis-iec-compliance/) and the BIS certification entry for status before you bid. Ask the OEM for the licence number or certificate reference rather than a statement of compliance.

## Seller onboarding and preference rules

According to the archived GeM seller FAQ, a primary seller registration needs:

- the constitution type and name;
- the Aadhaar number or personal PAN of the authorised signatory;
- the organisation's address, bank details and an active email;
- CIN, PAN, DIPP, UAM or ITR details, as the constitution requires.

Aadhaar is no longer mandatory for this step according to that FAQ. Start-ups registered under DIPP can get relaxation on experience and turnover and an EMD (earnest money deposit) exemption on GeM generally. The CREDA ATC, however, did not allow start-ups to bid. Always read the ATC before assuming a relaxation applies.

Preference rules can favour the OEM. In the CTTC bid, MSE (micro and small enterprise) purchase preference was open only to the manufacturer or OEM of the offered product on GeM. Resellers of another OEM's product were not eligible. Class 1 local supplier preference needed an OEM certificate of local content.

The same bid says that if the lowest bidder (L-1) is not an MSE, an MSE seller within the buyer's price band (15% in that bid) is given a chance to match L-1. That seller can be awarded part of the quantity. A reseller therefore cannot assume the lowest price wins the whole order.

## Bid-week plan

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Day of download | Save the GeM bid page, the ATC and every corrigendum. Build a clause sheet, with columns for GeM layer, ATC layer and datasheet page |
| Same day | Send the OEM your authorisation, test report consent, local content and warranty request |
| Before the query cut-off | Send written queries on date mismatches, physical documents, samples, the test-report-per-category rule and the deemed-OEM question |
| Mid window | Check the seller dashboard for corrigenda; GeM's corrigendum text says the new terms supersede the earlier ATC |
| Two days before close | Upload the full set and confirm EMD or bid-securing declaration, MSE and startup documents, if you are relying on them |
| After close | Keep the filed set; the OEM letter and the datasheet pages are your record if the buyer raises a clarification during evaluation |

## The document pack to copy

**Before you commit to bid**

- [ ] GeM bid page, ATC and all corrigenda saved and compared
- [ ] Clause sheet marked GeM layer or ATC layer
- [ ] Date mismatches and unclear clauses raised in writing
- [ ] OEM confirmed it will issue authorisation for this bid

**From the OEM**

- [ ] Authorisation naming the bid, buyer and exact models
- [ ] Validity covering the offer period and the contract
- [ ] Test reports with report numbers, lab name and consent to use them
- [ ] Written warranty terms for the exact model, in the form the ATC asks
- [ ] OEM turnover certificate and local content certificate if the bid needs them
- [ ] Datasheet and installation manual for each quoted model, with issue date

**From you**

- [ ] Turnover, net worth, ITR and experience certificates in the buyer's format
- [ ] Self-declaration on blacklisting and conflict of interest, where asked
- [ ] Service centre details for the buyer's state
- [ ] A price sheet that includes any monitoring data charges, freight and taxes the ATC puts in your scope
