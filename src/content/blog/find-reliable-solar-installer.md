A poorly chosen installer can void your manufacturer warranty, disqualify you from the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, and reduce your annual generation by 15–25% through bad wiring, wrong tilt angles, and missed earthing connections. ## Why Installer Quality Is the Biggest Risk in a Rooftop Solar Purchase

The Indian solar industry has seen explosive growth since PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana was announced. With millions of households applying for subsidies, thousands of new installation companies have entered the market, many without trained staff, proper certifications, or any intention of honouring warranty commitments.

Independent quality assessments by industry bodies have documented the consequences. Systems installed by unverified contractors show measurable underperformance from the first year: loose DC connections cause resistance heating and arc faults; incorrect string configurations clip generation during peak sun hours; missing surge protection devices allow voltage spikes to damage inverter circuits; and poor earthing creates shock hazards during monsoon season.

> **15–25%.** The generation shortfall documented in poorly installed residential solar systems in India compared to correctly specified and installed systems of the same capacity. *Source - [Bridge to India Solar Market Report](https://bridgetoindia.com/), 2025.*

The financial implication is direct. On a 3 kW system producing an average of 4 units per day, a 20% shortfall means 292 fewer units per year, roughly ₹2,000–₹2,500 in lost generation at ₹7–8 per unit, every single year for 25 years. That is ₹50,000–₹60,000 of compounding loss from a single bad installation decision.

Beyond generation loss, a poorly installed system can void the manufacturer warranty on your inverter. Most inverter manufacturers in India, including Qbits, specify that warranty claims require installation by a trained and authorised technician using manufacturer-approved wiring methods. An installation that does not meet these standards is not covered, even if the inverter itself is defective.

For homeowners pursuing the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the stakes are even higher. The [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) commissioning inspection can fail if the installer used non-ALMM equipment or filed paperwork incorrectly, costing you the central government subsidy of up to ₹78,000. According to the [MNRE PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), over 1 crore applications had been registered by mid-2026, making installer quality a national-scale consumer protection concern.

## The 8-Step Installer Verification Checklist

This proprietary framework provides a repeatable, evidence-based method to evaluate any solar installation company before you hand over a deposit. Each step produces a pass, a conditional pass, or a disqualification result. A reliable installer passes all eight.

### The 8-Step Installer Verification Checklist

1. **PM Surya Ghar Empanelment Check**: Verify the installer's registration number on the MNRE portal. A current empanelment means they have met DISCOM financial and technical criteria and signed pricing discipline agreements.
2. **ALMM Equipment Verification**: Confirm every panel and inverter model in the quotation appears on the current MNRE ALMM list. Non-ALMM equipment disqualifies your subsidy automatically.
3. **Three Local References**: Request references from three installations within 10 km, all completed within 18 months. Visit at least one in person and speak with the homeowner.
4. **DISCOM Commissioning Track Record**: Ask for DISCOM application reference numbers from their three most recent net metering approvals in your district. Verify those numbers are real by calling your DISCOM helpline.
5. **Licensed Electrician Verification**: Ask to see the wireman or supervisor licence of the electrician who will supervise your installation, issued by the state Electrical Licensing Board under CEA regulations.
6. **Itemised Quotation Review**: Reject any bundled lump-sum quote. Require a model-specific bill of materials listing every component with its brand, model, grade, and unit price.
7. **Post-Installation Monitoring Setup**: Confirm the installer will configure your inverter's monitoring system and that you will receive a live generation alert before they leave the site.
8. **AMC Offer and Terms**: Require a written Annual Maintenance Contract specifying visit frequency, fault response time (target: 48–72 hours), scope of work, and price.

Apply this checklist to every installer you shortlist. Any installer who refuses to provide information for any of these eight steps should be disqualified without negotiation. ## Step 1: Verify PM Surya Ghar Empanelment on the MNRE Portal

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana requires that all installation work under the scheme be performed by a [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/)-empanelled vendor. This is not optional. If your installer is not on the empanelled list, your subsidy application will be rejected at the commissioning stage, regardless of how well the installation itself was done.

The verification process takes five minutes:

- Visit [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and navigate to the registered vendor section.
- Select your state and DISCOM from the dropdown menus.
- Search for the installer by name or registration number.
- Confirm the registration is current (not expired) and covers your district.

Ask the installer to share their DISCOM registration number at your first meeting, before any site visit or quotation. A legitimate empanelled installer will provide this immediately. An installer who claims to be empanelled but cannot produce a registration number, or asks you to "trust them" on this point, should be disqualified.

For a detailed explanation of what empanelment means and what documents an empanelled installer must provide, the guide on [empanelled vendors for PM Surya Ghar](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) covers the full requirements including DISCOM agreement terms and pricing norm compliance.

| Empanelment Status | What It Means for You |
| --- | --- |
| **Empanelled, current** | Subsidy-eligible; DISCOM has vetted the company |
| **Empanelled, expired** | Treat as non-empanelled; subsidy risk high |
| **Not empanelled** | Cannot trigger subsidy; avoid for PM Surya Ghar |
| **Cannot verify** | Installer is being dishonest; disqualify immediately |

## Step 2: Verify ALMM-Listed Equipment in the Quotation

The [ALMM list](/glossary/almm-list/) (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) is the MNRE's registry of solar panels and inverters that meet Indian quality standards and are approved for use in subsidy-linked installations. Every model of panel and every model of inverter in your quotation must appear on the current ALMM list.

This matters for two reasons. First, the [DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) commissioning inspector will record the model numbers of installed equipment during their site visit. If any component is not ALMM-listed, the inspection fails and your subsidy cannot be processed. Second, ALMM listing is a strong proxy for genuine BIS certification and manufacturer accountability, uncertified assemblers cannot obtain ALMM approval.

How to check:

- Visit the MNRE ALMM portal at [mnre.gov.in/almm-list](https://mnre.gov.in/almm-list/).
- The list is divided into Module and Inverter sections. Check both.
- Download the current PDF and search for the exact brand and model name from the installer's quotation.
- Confirm the ALMM Phase III listing, since Phase I and Phase II listings have different compliance requirements for 2026 onwards.

For the background on how Phase III tightened quality requirements and what it means for equipment selection, read the [ALMM Phase III guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/). Reliable installers welcome this check, they work with ALMM-listed brands because it protects both their business and their customers.

## Step 3: Visit Three Local References in Person

References that you cannot visit in person are nearly useless. An installer can fabricate phone numbers or arrange for friends to impersonate satisfied customers. What cannot be faked is the physical appearance of an installed system that you inspect yourself.

When you visit a reference installation:

- **Observe cable management**: DC cables from the rooftop panels should run in UV-resistant conduits, not loosely tied with cable ties or draped over the roof structure. Exposed cables degrade within three monsoon seasons.
- **Check mounting structure alignment**: Panels should be level and uniformly tilted. Uneven mounting causes unequal shading within strings and measurable yield loss.
- **Look at the [earthing](/glossary/earthing/) connection**: The inverter body and the mounting structure should both show visible earthing conductors connected to a dedicated earth pit or the building's earthing system. Missing earthing is a safety hazard and a warranty violation.
- **Ask the homeowner about after-sales experience**: Have they had any faults? How quickly did the installer respond? Did the monitoring system work from day one?

- **Distance matters**: Request references within 10 km of your property. An installer who has only done work in distant districts may not understand your local DISCOM's commissioning requirements.
- **Recency matters**: References older than 24 months may reflect work from a team that has since changed. Ask for installations from the past 18 months.
- **Volume matters**: An installer who can only provide one reference has a thin track record. Three references in 18 months in your area is the minimum threshold for confidence.

## Step 4: Check the DISCOM Commissioning Track Record

An installer may have completed many systems that generate power but were never formally commissioned, meaning no net metering approval was obtained and the owner is either exporting to the grid without measurement or, in zero-export configurations, simply consuming self-generated power without subsidy. This is common with lower-cost contractors who collect payment before completing the paperwork.

A reliable installer has a track record of successful [commissioning](/glossary/commissioning/), meaning their systems are formally approved by the DISCOM, the bidirectional meter is installed, and the subsidy payment was received by the homeowner.

How to verify:

- Ask the installer for the DISCOM application reference numbers from their three most recent commissioning approvals in your district.
- Call your DISCOM's residential solar helpline (most states publish this on their website) and read out those reference numbers.
- Confirm that those applications exist in the DISCOM system and that commissioning was approved.

If the installer cannot provide reference numbers, or the DISCOM cannot confirm the numbers exist, the installer is either very new to your district or has been completing systems informally. Neither is acceptable for a PM Surya Ghar application.

For the complete net metering approval process and timelines by state, the [net metering India guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) explains every step from application to bidirectional meter installation. Understanding this process yourself makes it much easier to evaluate whether an installer's claimed track record is credible.

## Step 5: Verify Licensed Electricians on the Installation Team

Under the [Central Electricity Authority (CEA)](/glossary/cea/) Measures Relating to Safety and Electric Supply Regulations, all electrical work on a grid-connected solar installation (including DC wiring, AC connection, and earthing) must be performed or directly supervised by an individual holding a valid electrical wireman or electrical supervisor licence issued by the state government's Electrical Licensing Board.

This requirement exists because incorrect electrical work on a solar system creates real risks: arc faults from loose DC connections have caused roof fires in residential solar installations; improper neutral connections have damaged grid equipment during power cuts; and missing surge protection has allowed lightning-induced voltage spikes to destroy inverters and household appliances.

> **CEA Regulation 3(1).** *"No person shall operate and maintain a medium voltage electrical installation unless he holds a certificate of competency or a permit as specified in these regulations."* Source - [Central Electricity Authority, Government of India](https://cea.nic.in/).

What to ask:

- Request the licence number and expiry date of the wireman or supervisor who will lead your installation.
- Verify the licence at your state's Electrical Licensing Board portal (most states have online verification).
- Confirm that the licence category covers solar PV installation and AC grid connection work.

An installer who employs helpers or general labourers for electrical work without a licensed supervisor is operating outside the law and outside every inverter manufacturer's warranty terms. Do not accept assurances that "the licence is coming" or that it "is with the office."

## Step 6: Review the Quotation Structure - Itemised vs Bundled

The structure of the quotation tells you more about an installer's integrity than almost anything else. A bundled quotation ("3 kW on-grid system for ₹1,80,000 all-inclusive") gives the installer the latitude to substitute any component for a cheaper alternative after you have signed and paid a deposit.

The substitutions that damage your system typically happen in three areas:

- **Inverter brand and model**: The empanelled installer quotes a reputable ALMM-listed inverter but installs a local assembler unit with no certification and no manufacturer warranty.
- **DC cable grade**: Specified as 4 mm² DC-rated solar cable but installed as 2.5 mm² standard electrical cable, causing resistance heating and fire risk.
- **Earthing quality**: Proper copper earthing conductors and earth pit construction replaced by a connection to the building's common earth without a dedicated system earth.

An itemised quotation removes this latitude. Professional installers increasingly generate these from dedicated [solar quotation software](https://quickestimate.co/features/quotation-system/) rather than a spreadsheet, which reduces the chance of a line item being quietly dropped. It must include:

- **Panel brand and model** with ALMM certificate number
- **Inverter brand and model** with ALMM certificate number and warranty period
- **[Mounting structure](/glossary/mounting-structure/)**: material grade (galvanised iron or aluminium), load rating
- **[DC cable](/glossary/dc-cable/)**: brand, cross-section area (mm²), insulation type (double-insulated, UV-resistant)
- **[AC cable](/glossary/ac-cable/)**: grade and cross-section
- **[Surge protection device (SPD)](/glossary/spd/)**: DC-side and AC-side separately
- **Net metering application charge**: explicitly listed, not hidden in installation fee
- **Labour charge**: separately stated
- **GST**: 12% on goods, 18% on services, correctly computed

If the installer cannot provide model-level specifications for every component, they are either not planning to use quality components or they do not have enough technical knowledge to select them correctly.

## Step 7: Confirm Post-Installation Monitoring Capability

A solar system that produces no alerts is not necessarily performing correctly, it may simply have a monitoring system that is not configured or not connected. Generation problems that go undetected for months represent lost kWh that can never be recovered.

A reliable installer will:

- Configure your inverter's monitoring portal and log you in before leaving the site.
- Confirm that the inverter is connected to your home Wi-Fi or a SIM-based 4G module.
- Send you a test alert (at minimum, a generation summary for the first day) to confirm the data pipeline is working.
- Explain how to read your daily and monthly yield, and what a normal generation curve looks like for your location.

The [solar inverter monitoring systems](/blog/solar-inverter-monitoring-systems-in-india/) landscape in India ranges from basic app-based dashboards to AI-driven platforms that send WhatsApp alerts with daily generation summaries and fault notifications. The best installers configure monitoring as a standard part of commissioning, not as an optional add-on.

Ask the installer specifically:

- Which monitoring platform does the inverter use?
- Will I receive alerts for faults and for days with unusually low generation?
- Can I see generation data going back more than 12 months?
- Will the monitoring subscription expire, and at what cost?

## Step 8: Evaluate the AMC Offer and Terms

A solar system requires regular maintenance to sustain its 25-year performance trajectory. Dust accumulation on panels alone reduces generation by 5–15% in northern and central Indian cities between monsoon seasons, according to field data published by [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/). Loose AC and DC connections develop over thermal cycling and require periodic re-torquing. Inverter firmware updates are issued by manufacturers to improve grid compatibility and fault handling.

An Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) formalises this ongoing relationship. The absence of a written AMC offer from an installer is a strong signal that they intend to collect payment and move on, leaving you to find support from whoever answers the phone when something eventually goes wrong.

A well-structured AMC must specify:

- **Visit frequency**: minimum two scheduled inspections per year (pre-summer and post-monsoon)
- **Panel cleaning**: included in scheduled visits, with dry or wet method noted
- **Connection check**: torque verification of all DC and AC terminals at each visit
- **Earthing continuity test**: measured with a clamp meter and recorded at each visit
- **Firmware updates**: included as issued by the inverter manufacturer
- **Fault response time**: 48–72 hours from fault report to on-site resolution (not just acknowledgment)
- **Exclusions**: explicitly listed (typically: panel cracking from physical impact, rodent damage to cables, grid surge events)
- **Price escalation**: fixed for the first three years or capped at a published index

For a 3–5 kW residential system, expect AMC pricing between ₹3,000 and ₹6,000 per year from a reputable installer. A price significantly below this range usually means the installer has no capacity to honour response time commitments. A price significantly above this range warrants a detailed line-by-line justification.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Choosing a Solar Installer

Even careful buyers make predictable errors. Recognising these patterns saves significant money and frustration.

- **Choosing on price alone**: The cheapest quote for a 3 kW system in India is almost always cheap because something critical has been removed: ALMM-listed equipment, proper earthing, a licensed electrician, or a written AMC. The subsidy-adjusted benchmark cost is your floor, not a negotiating target.
- **Skipping the reference visit**: Speaking to a reference over the phone is insufficient. Physical inspection of the installation reveals quality issues that no homeowner would think to mention in a phone call: exposed cables, missing conduits, uneven panel alignment.
- **Accepting verbal warranties**: Every commitment from an installer (warranty period, AMC terms, commissioning timeline, DISCOM paperwork responsibility) must be in writing and signed before you pay any amount. Verbal assurances are unenforceable.
- **Not reading the [solar inverter warranty](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty/)**: The inverter is the most failure-prone component in a solar system. Understand what the manufacturer's warranty actually covers, what voids it, and whether the installer is an authorised service partner for the inverter brand they are installing.
- **Ignoring the AMC gap**: A 5-year manufacturer warranty on your inverter covers component replacement but not the labour cost of diagnosis, cable replacement, or re-commissioning after a fault. An AMC bridges this gap.
- **Not asking who does the DISCOM paperwork**: Net metering approval requires multiple document submissions, DISCOM site inspections, and follow-up communication. Some installers hand this responsibility back to the homeowner after installation. Confirm in writing that DISCOM commissioning is fully managed by the installer as part of the agreed scope.
