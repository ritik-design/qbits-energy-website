## What is a TOPCon cell

TOPCon, Tunnel Oxide Passivated Contact, is a next-generation crystalline silicon solar cell architecture that uses an ultra-thin tunnel oxide layer (1 to 2 nanometres of silicon dioxide) plus a thin doped polysilicon layer at the rear surface. The combined structure provides excellent electrical passivation of the rear contact while still allowing current to tunnel through to the metallisation.

TOPCon delivers 24 to 25 percent cell efficiency, with module efficiency landing at 22 to 23.5 percent. Compared to PERC, TOPCon offers 1 to 2 percentage points higher efficiency, a better temperature coefficient, lower LeTID and a higher bifaciality factor (80 to 85 percent against 70 to 80 for PERC).

From 2024 onward, TOPCon has become the dominant cell technology for new module manufacturing capacity globally and in India. Major Indian manufacturers have transitioned or are transitioning from PERC to TOPCon in their new gigawatt-scale plants.

## TOPCon explained simply

A standard PERC cell has a passivated rear with metal contacts that touch the silicon directly. TOPCon adds a layer of ultra-thin glass on the back, then a thin layer of doped silicon, then the metal contact. Current still flows through (electrons tunnel through the thin oxide), but electron recombination at the metal contact is reduced.

The result is higher voltage and current per cell, leading to 1 to 2 percent more conversion efficiency at the same module footprint.

## Why TOPCon matters

Efficiency leadership. 24 to 25 percent cell efficiency is the new commodity benchmark.

Module-area savings. More watts per square metre for the same rooftop or land area.

Better temperature performance. Indian summer sites lose less yield to heat.

Lower degradation. Annual losses of 0.40 to 0.50 percent against 0.45 to 0.55 for PERC.

Bifacial leadership. 80 to 85 percent bifaciality factor improves yield gain.

Industry transition. Major capacity additions in 2024 to 2026 are TOPCon-first.

## How TOPCon works

1. Light absorbed in n-type silicon substrate.
2. P-N junction (front emitter, n-base) separates carriers.
3. Electrons collect at front contact through standard silver paste fingers.
4. Holes travel toward rear, encounter the tunnel oxide.
5. Tunnel oxide is thin enough (1 to 2 nm) for quantum tunnelling.
6. Doped polysilicon layer beneath collects holes.
7. Metal contact on polysilicon completes the circuit.

The tunnel oxide passivates the silicon-metal interface electronically, reducing recombination by an order of magnitude compared to direct metal contact.

## Technical deep dive

### Cell structure

| Layer | Material | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Front contact | Silver paste | Electron collection |
| Anti-reflective coating | SiNx | Reduce front-side reflection |
| Emitter | p-type silicon | Junction formation |
| Base | n-type silicon | Light absorption |
| Tunnel oxide | SiO2, 1 to 2 nm | Electrical passivation |
| Doped polysilicon | n+ poly | Hole collection |
| Rear contact | Aluminium or silver paste | Conduction to external circuit |

### Degradation

| Mechanism | TOPCon |
|---|---|
| Initial LID | Less than 0.5% in first 100 hours |
| LeTID | Largely eliminated (n-type base) |
| Annual degradation | 0.40 to 0.50%/year |
| 25-year retention | 85 to 88% |

### Bifaciality

TOPCon bifaciality factor: 80 to 85 percent. Combined with rear-side power generation, total annual yield gain over monofacial PERC can reach 25 to 30 percent.

Highest mainstream efficiency.

Lower temperature loss in hot climates.

Compatible with existing module manufacturing equipment with modest retooling.

## Limitations

Manufacturing complexity adds 5 to 10 percent cost.

n-type silicon supply chain is still scaling.

Polysilicon deposition is sensitive to process control.

Boron diffusion challenges in emitter formation.

## Common problems

Process variability between manufacturers. Quality matters more for TOPCon than for mature PERC.

PID risk for systems with grounded inverter without PID-resistant materials.

Hot spots from cracked cells (same as any silicon cell).

Soiling losses similar to PERC, 5 to 25 percent annually.

Junction box reliability.

## Common mistakes

Buying TOPCon from unproven manufacturers. Quality consistency is key.

Mixing TOPCon and PERC in the same string.

Underestimating the bifacial gain in plant design.

Ignoring temperature coefficient differences when modelling yield.

Treating TOPCon as a marketing label without verifying actual cell architecture.
