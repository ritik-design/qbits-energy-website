**What changed for a Gujarat solar dealer: on 22 October 2026 the Government of Gujarat added a state top-up for new homes of up to 2.18 kWp DC, on top of the national PM Surya Ghar scheme.** A household in that band pays only 5% or 10% of a benchmark cost. SURYA Gujarat, the state's older rooftop scheme, is a 2019 order and is not what this change is built on.

For a solar dealership or installer in Gujarat, three things now matter. Who is allowed to sell, what the household pays, and which DISCOM (the power distribution company) decides. This guide is for a Gujarat dealer, installer or electrical contractor deciding whether and how to start. Every rule below comes from a government document, with its date. Anything we could not open is listed at the end. Nothing here is an income promise.

Disclosure: Qbits Energy sells solar inverters and publishes this guide. Its office is in Surat, Gujarat. This guide describes no Qbits dealer terms and makes no claim about Qbits presence or service in any Gujarat district.

> **Short version, as of 30 October 2026**
> - SURYA Gujarat is the state GR (government resolution) of 5 August 2019, not a 2024 scheme. It gave 40% state subsidy up to 3 kW and 20% above 3 kW up to 10 kW. A household took either the state subsidy or the central one, never both.
> - The new GR of 22 October 2026 asks a household to pay 5% (SC/ST) or 10% (others) of a benchmark cost, for a system up to 2.18 kWp DC commissioned on or after that date. The state covers the balance after the central subsidy.
> - A Gujarat vendor registers with the DISCOM that covers its corporate office, under a GUVNL letter of 1 August 2023. One Rs 2.5 lakh PBG (a performance security issued by a bank) covers the whole state.
> - A dealer who only sells equipment has no registration in any of these documents. The registered vendor is the firm that installs and maintains the plant.
> - Gujarat had 22.7% of all PM Surya Ghar installations to 19 March 2026, but its share of new installations fell from 41.7% in 2024 to 14.9% in early 2026.

## What was SURYA Gujarat?

SURYA Gujarat stands for Surya Urja Rooftop Yojana-Gujarat. The 2019 GR states its terms plainly.

| Term | What the GR says |
| --- | --- |
| Who | Residential consumers of all DISCOMs. Industrial, commercial and government consumers are excluded |
| Subsidy | 40% for systems up to 3 kW, 20% above 3 kW up to 10 kW |
| Central subsidy | Either the state subsidy or the MNRE subsidy, but not both |
| Capacity | 1 kW DC or more, regardless of sanctioned load; subsidy limited to 10 kW |
| Surplus power | Bought by the DISCOM at Rs 2.25 per unit |
| Modules | Cells and modules of non-Indian origin are not eligible |
| Delivery | GUVNL is the nodal agency; DISCOMs release the subsidy after certifying commissioning |
| Target | 2 lakh consumers in 2019-20 and 8 lakh cumulatively by the end of FY 2021-22 |

Two details matter for a dealer. First, the either-or rule is why a Gujarat customer in 2020 to 2023 chose between the state and the central subsidy. Second, vendors were empanelled by tender. A GUVNL clarification of 2023 says MGVCL ran the tender for all Gujarat DISCOMs. It covered design, supply, installation, testing and commissioning, 700 MW for urban and 300 MW for rural homes, with five years of comprehensive maintenance. The portal lists 2,293 installers across four scheme labels (SG2019-20, SG2020-21, SG2022-23 Urban and SG2022-23 Rural).

The 2019 GR says the scheme runs to FY 2021-22. The 2023 GUVNL clarification says the GR's operative period is up to FY 2024-25. We did not find the extension order. Either way, the portal's last new-application window closed on 8 December 2023. The clarification itself names the national portal as the route open to any home in India.

For the homeowner view of this scheme, our [SURYA Gujarat guide](/blog/surya-gujarat-scheme-guide/) covers subsidy slabs and the household application. Note that it was last checked in September 2026, before the 22 October top-up below.

## What does the 22 October 2026 state top-up pay?

The 22 October 2026 GR (EPCD/BUD/e-file/20/2025/2435/B1) gives extra state support, over and above PM Surya Ghar, for new residential rooftop systems of up to 2.18 kWp. This is the real change for a dealer.

| Condition | What the GR says | What it means at the counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household pays | 5% of the benchmark cost for SC/ST, 10% for others | A low upfront figure, because the household's share is a percentage of the benchmark, not of your quote |
| Benchmark cost | Rs 50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 45,000 for the additional kW | The calculation base, taken from the MNRE office memorandum of 7 June 2024 as the GR prints it. We opened that memorandum on 31 October 2026 and it gives the same two rates, from 13 February 2024 |
| State subsidy | The balance after the central subsidy and the household's share | Moves with the central subsidy rate |
| Capacity | Up to 2.18 kWp installed DC; above that, no state subsidy at all | The cap is on DC module capacity, not on inverter size |
| Start | Systems commissioned on or after 22 October 2026 | Earlier jobs do not qualify |
| Payment | DISCOMs and Torrent Power pay the household by DBT; GUVNL is nodal | The household is paid, not the vendor |
| Add-ons | No added rooftop capacity for three years from installation | An upsell to a bigger system is closed for that customer |
| Existing systems | A home with an earlier system can add capacity only up to a combined 2.18 kWp | Check what is already on the roof |
| Duration | Only for as long as PM Surya Ghar runs | Tied to the national end date |

The GR also says that all other provisions of the 5 August 2025 GR continue. Its preamble describes that earlier GR as a waiver of Rs 2,950 in meter, meter-testing, meter-box and connectivity charges for new residential systems up to 6 kW, and for the common facilities of group housing societies and welfare associations.

### Worked example (illustrative arithmetic)

Assumptions: the central subsidy rate is Rs 30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp, as set out in the MNRE guidelines text (PDF uploaded July 2025), which our [stock planning guide](/blog/solar-inverter-stock-planning-new-dealers/) quotes. The household is not SC/ST and pays 10%. These are our sums, not figures from the GR.

| System | Benchmark cost | Central subsidy | Household pays (10%) | State top-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kWp | Rs 50,000 | Rs 30,000 | Rs 5,000 | Rs 15,000 |
| 2 kWp | Rs 1,00,000 | Rs 60,000 | Rs 10,000 | Rs 30,000 |

For an SC/ST household the same 2 kWp system gives Rs 5,000 from the household and Rs 35,000 from the state. We have not computed 2.18 kWp, because the GR does not say how the central subsidy is counted on the last 0.18 kW.

### Where can a dealer slip on the 2.18 kWp rule?

- **Module wattage sets eligibility.** The cap is DC. Four 545 Wp modules make 2.18 kWp. Four 550 Wp modules make 2.20 kWp, which is over the cap, and the GR says the household is then ineligible. This is simple arithmetic, not a reason the GR gives. Check the installed DC total on the final design, not the inverter nameplate.
- **A quote above the benchmark.** The GR states the household's share as a percentage of the benchmark. It does not say who pays if the quote is higher. Ask the DISCOM in writing before pricing any job.
- **The disbursal procedure.** The GR says DBT is paid "in accordance with the applicable procedure". We found no published procedure, portal screen or timeline for it.
- **The GR says nothing about vendors.** It names no registration, empanelment or documents for installers.

## Who can sell rooftop solar in Gujarat, and who must register?

PM Surya Ghar subsidy is claimed through a registered vendor, per the MNRE documents summarised in our vendor registration guide. In Gujarat that registration follows GUVNL's letter of 1 August 2023 to the four DISCOMs and Torrent Power. The letter applies the MNRE procedure of 17 May 2023.

1. The vendor applies, with the declaration and documents MNRE requires, to the DISCOM in whose area its corporate office stands.
2. That DISCOM collects the PBG for all DISCOMs, including Torrent Power. The letter says one PBG of Rs 2.5 lakh covers the entire state.
3. It registers the vendor for the whole of Gujarat and shares the list with the other DISCOMs for upload to their national-portal accounts.
4. A vendor whose corporate office is in Torrent Power's Ahmedabad or Surat area applies to UGVCL or DGVCL respectively.
5. A vendor with its office outside Gujarat applies by region: north to UGVCL, west to PGVCL, south to DGVCL, east and north-east to MGVCL.

The letter is dated 2023, and We did not find a newer Gujarat procedure. Confirm that it still applies before you pay for a PBG. Our [vendor registration guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vendor-registration-installers/) covers the MNRE side, the state and national routes and the PBG checks.

### Should you be a dealer, an installer or an EPC?

| Role | What the documents show | Registration found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dealer, or distributor of inverters and components | Sells to installers. No subsidy duty of its own in the documents read | None in the documents we read |
| Installer, or vendor, for homes | Surveys, installs, tests, commissions, signs the joint inspection report and runs the plant for five years (SURYA Gujarat portal) | DISCOM registration under the 2023 letter, with a PBG |
| EPC for factories and shops | Outside the home subsidy, under GERC's captive and gross-metering rules | Electrical contractor licence. PM Surya Ghar covers residential consumers, so its vendor registration is not the route for a factory job |

A dealer who wants to install in its own name becomes a vendor. That brings the PBG and the five-year maintenance duty. A dealer who stays a dealer needs installer customers who are registered and trusted by the household. A dealer's own paperwork, GST and Udyam, is in our [GST and Udyam guide](/blog/gst-udyam-solar-inverter-dealer/).

We found no GEDA document that empanels vendors. In the documents we read, GEDA (or "an agency designated by the Government of Gujarat") issues the registration and commissioning certificates for the project under GERC's rules. The portal's important-documents page also describes a registration form for the "Unified Single Window Rooftop Solar PV Portal of GEDA". If you have heard of a separate GEDA empanelment for PM Surya Ghar work, ask GEDA to show you the order.

## Which DISCOM serves your customer, and how does a household apply?

Four GUVNL subsidiaries distribute power in most of the state. The copy list of the 2026 GR shows where each is run from.

| DISCOM | Managing director's office (from the GR copy list) |
| --- | --- |
| UGVCL, Uttar Gujarat Vij Company | Mehsana |
| MGVCL, Madhya Gujarat Vij Company | Vadodara |
| DGVCL, Dakshin Gujarat Vij Company | Surat |
| PGVCL, Paschim Gujarat Vij Company | Rajkot |

Torrent Power, a private distribution licensee, appears in the same GR with an Ahmedabad address. The GUVNL letter treats its Ahmedabad and Surat areas separately. So a customer in Surat or Ahmedabad may be served by Torrent Power rather than a GUVNL company. The household's bill names the licensee. Use the bill, not the district map.

The household route is short. Apply on the national portal with the consumer number, choose a registered vendor, install, then let the DISCOM inspect. The national portal waives technical feasibility and takes the net-metering agreement into the application, per a PIB release of 24 March 2026. GERC's Fifth Amendment says the same in state text. There is no separate written connectivity agreement for PM Surya Ghar systems, and the agreement starts on the commissioning date.

## Which GERC net-metering rules touch a dealer's jobs?

These come from the GERC net-metering regulations of 2016 as amended. The Third Amendment is dated 31 May 2022, the Fourth 4 September 2024 (gazette of 6 September 2024) and the Fifth 3 November 2025. We read the Fourth and Fifth in gazette text, and re-opened both on GERC's own site on 31 October 2026. The first table is residential, the second is for commercial and industrial jobs.

| Rule | What the regulation says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | Net metering from 1 kW up to 1,000 kW. Gross metering above 10 kW up to 1,000 kW | Third Amendment, reg 6.2 |
| Homes | Allowed regardless of sanctioned load | Third Amendment, reg 9.1.1 |
| Surplus | Rs 2.25 a unit for the first 5 years for self-owned and SURYA Gujarat systems, then 75% of a GUVNL bidding average | Third Amendment, reg 9.1.3 |
| Banking | No banking charge on solar power consumed by homes | Third Amendment, reg 9.1.5 |
| Up to 10 kW | Complete applications are deemed accepted without a technical feasibility study | Fourth Amendment, reg 7 |
| Delay | Compensation of not less than Rs 500 a day if the licensee delays without cause | Fourth Amendment, reg 7 |
| Strengthening | Included in the licensee's revenue requirement up to 6 kW; above 6 kW recovered per kW at LT from the applicant, and per kVA at HT, for state DISCOMs | Fourth Amendment, reg 5.1 |
| Completion papers | CEI charging approval, meter and CTPT test certificates, IEC test reports for modules, inverters and cables, anti-islanding, and a no-volt relay above 10 kW | Fourth Amendment, reg 7 |
| Third-party sale | A developer may install on a home and sell power under lease or PPA | Third Amendment, reg 9.1.2 |

For a dealer the useful line is the completion paperwork. The inverter's IEC test reports are on the list, so the people selling the inverter will be asked for them. Capacity limits for shops, factories and captive users, and how the states differ, are in our [net-metering limits guide](/blog/net-metering-capacity-limits-commercial-by-state/). For a worked Surat factory case, see [solar for diamond polishing units in Surat](/blog/solar-for-diamond-polishing-units-surat/).

### What did the Fifth Amendment change about single-phase limits?

The Fifth Amendment does two things. It adds the agreement waiver above. It also replaces regulation 8(2), which used to set the voltage level by connected load (up to 6 kW at 240 V single phase), with a pointer to the supply voltage levels in the GERC Supply Code as amended from time to time.

So the old 6 kW single-phase figure is no longer in the net-metering regulation. We re-read the amendment in its gazette text on 31 October 2026. We also read GERC's consolidated Supply Code, which is the 2015 code with four amendments, the latest in force from 23 September 2024. GERC's code page showed no later amendment that day. Its clause 3.2 gives 230 V single phase for installations up to and including 6 kW of connected load, with motive power load other than irrigation pump not above 2 HP. Above 6 kW it gives 400 V three phase. Clause 3.3 lets a licensee supply a different voltage and phase with the Commission's approval. So ask the DISCOM which supply level applies to the connection before you size a single-phase job in Gujarat.

## Does a Gujarat rooftop solar job need a licensed contractor?

The central rule is regulation 31 of the CEA (Central Electricity Authority) safety regulations 2023. Installation work must be done by a contractor licensed by the State Government, under a certificate-of-competency holder. Our [contractor licence guide](/blog/electrical-contractor-licence-solar-installers/) explains the rule and could not verify Gujarat. We have now read one more Gujarat page.

The Licensing Board page of the Chief Electrical Inspector (CEICED) says the board issues wireman permits, supervisor permits and electrical contractor licences. It says supervisor exams are usually held twice a year, in May to June and October to November, at Ahmedabad, Vadodara, Surat, Rajkot and Bhavnagar. A supervisor permit holder who wants a contractor licence needs no other supervisor. The board's only office is in Gandhinagar.

Two limits apply. The page's footer shows a last update of 13 April 2016, and it refers to the Indian Electricity Rules 1956, not the 2023 regulations. It also never mentions solar. We read an archived copy dated September 2025. Ask the Licensing Board in writing whether a rooftop solar job needs a licensed contractor, which licence class applies, and where the current form is.

## Which business model fits these rules?

- **Supplying registered installers.** You stock equipment and sell to vendors. You need no subsidy registration, but your products must pass the installer's paperwork, including IEC reports.
- **Registered residential vendor.** You survey, install, commission and maintain for five years, and hold the PBG. The top-up changes the price conversation with a household, not your duties.
- **Commercial and industrial EPC.** This is outside the home top-up. The GERC captive and gross-metering rules decide the economics, and a factory's tariff is a separate matter.
- **Third-party sale.** The regulation allows a developer to put a system on a home under a lease or power-sale agreement. The Surya Gujarat portal also lists lease and PPA as ways to go solar. We have not examined how DISCOMs treat these in practice.

Three risks sit across all four. The national scheme's current end date is 31 March 2027, and we found no official extension on 30 October 2026. The 2026 top-up lasts only while the national scheme does. And a household that takes the top-up cannot add capacity for three years.

## Which documents should a Gujarat dealer keep ready?

- Company or firm registration, PAN, GST and bank details.
- Electrical contractor licence, supervisor and wireman permits (Gujarat Licensing Board).
- The DISCOM's registration application, declaration and the Rs 2.5 lakh PBG.
- Names and training records of the technical staff declared.
- A five-year maintenance plan you can actually staff.
- Per job: consumer number and licensee name, proof of roof possession, the design with DC total, module and inverter datasheets, IEC test reports, CEI approval where the DISCOM asks, meter and CTPT certificates.
- For the top-up: commissioning date on or after 22 October 2026, installed DC at or below 2.18 kWp, and the household's category (SC/ST or other).
