Gujarat households searching for the Surya Gujarat scheme want one number: how much subsidy they will get. The honest answer is that the number you can rely on today comes from the central scheme, not from the state programme that gave this search its volume.

Surya Gujarat, formally the Surya Urja Rooftop Yojana, is Gujarat's own rooftop solar programme. It runs through **Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Limited (GUVNL)** and its distribution companies, and it predates the national scheme. Much of the material still circulating about it, including the per-kW state top-up figure repeated across solar websites, traces back to orders issued years ago.

This guide separates three things that get blended together. First, what Surya Gujarat is and where it sits now. Second, the central PM Surya Ghar assistance that actually pays out, with slab amounts and the order behind them. Third, the Gujarat layer: your licensee, the state metering rules, the vendor requirement, the documents, and the timing written into the regulations. Every figure carries its issuing body and year, and where a widely repeated number has no current order behind it, this guide says so.

> **TL;DR**
> - Surya Gujarat is a GUVNL state programme; the money that pays out in 2026 comes from PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana.
> - Central assistance is ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 per kWp for the third kW, capping at ₹78,000, per MNRE Order No. 318/17/2024-Grid Connected Rooftop dated 16 March 2024.
> - State top-ups are allowed but must be disbursed through the National Portal, per clause (i) of the same MNRE guidelines. A vendor promise is not evidence.
> - The ₹10,000 per kW, ₹20,000 maximum GEDA figure still quoted online was reported in 2016, according to PV Tech (2016).
> - Residential surplus is bought at the Average Pooled Power Purchase Cost fixed for the commissioning year across 25 years, per GERC Notification No. 5/2016 dated 18 June 2016.
> - Rooftop capacity on one distribution transformer is capped at 65% of its peak capacity, per the same GERC notification.
> - Gujarat holds 8,146.70 MW of grid-connected rooftop solar, the most of any state, out of 32,595.64 MW nationally as on 31 August 2026, according to MNRE (2026).

**Short version.** Surya Gujarat is Gujarat's state rooftop solar programme, run by GUVNL through UGVCL, MGVCL, PGVCL, DGVCL and Torrent Power. In 2026 the subsidy that actually disburses is central: ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000, per MNRE (2024). Any Gujarat state top-up must come through the National Portal, backed by a current order.

## What Surya Gujarat actually is

Surya Gujarat is a single-window rooftop photovoltaic portal operated by GUVNL, supported by GIZ and AHAsolar. It handles vendor empanelment, consumer applications and tracking. It is a delivery channel and an empanelment register, not a separate cheque.

The programme left Gujarat with real stock. Gujarat had 8,146.70 MW of grid-connected rooftop solar as on 31 August 2026, the highest of any state, out of a national 32,595.64 MW, according to MNRE (2026). That is almost exactly a quarter of India's rooftop fleet. Read the heading carefully: it covers all grid-connected rooftop including commercial and industrial, not the scheme alone.

One correction to a common claim. Gujarat leads on cumulative stock, not on the latest year's additions. Maharashtra added the most rooftop capacity in FY2026 at 2,144 MW against Gujarat's 1,777 MW, according to JMK Research (2026). Having the most installed is not the same as installing the most now.

Two dates on the portal matter. Its most recent new-application alert covers 23 November 2023 to 8 December 2023, for PGVCL and MGVCL rural tenders plus Torrent Surat urban. Its discovered rates are labelled 2020-21. Both signal a channel in maintenance mode, not an open state window.

## Does the state scheme stack on top of PM Surya Ghar?

Stacking is permitted by design, and the mechanism is specified. Clause (i) of the MNRE Operational Guidelines (2024) lets state governments supplement central assistance with an additional residential subsidy. The same clause requires any state component to be disbursed through the National Portal.

That gives you a clean test. A genuine state top-up appears inside your portal application and pays through the same channel. It does not arrive as a line on a sales quotation.

Note also what got absorbed. The Rooftop Solar Phase II Programme, running since 2019, stands subsumed within PM Surya Ghar, and Phase II installations are not covered under the newer scheme, per MNRE (2024). Our [PM Surya Ghar guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) covers the central scheme end to end.

## The subsidy structure, with slabs and dates

The slabs are set in the MNRE Operational Guidelines issued as Order No. 318/17/2024-Grid Connected Rooftop dated 16 March 2024. Rates are per kWp of installed capacity, and the third kW is paid at a lower rate.

| Capacity | Central assistance rate | Gujarat applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 2 kW | ₹30,000 per kWp | Yes |
| Third kW | ₹18,000 per kWp | Yes |
| Above 3 kW | No further assistance for a single household | Yes |
| Special category states | ₹33,000 and ₹19,800 per kWp | No |
| Group housing society common facilities | ₹18,000 per kWp, up to the lower of 20 x 3 kW or 100 kW | Yes |

Gujarat is not on the special category list, per MNRE (2024). That list covers Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, the North East including Sikkim, and the island union territories. A Gujarat household uses the standard rates.

One detail decides which year's rates apply. Subsidy rates are notified per calendar year and apply to beneficiaries who registered on the National Portal during that year, per MNRE Office Memorandum dated 10 June 2022. Your registration date, not your commissioning date, anchors the slab. Our [Gujarat subsidy overview](/blog/solar-subsidy-gujarat-2026/) tracks the state picture.

## Who is eligible, and who is excluded

Eligible installations are grid-connected residential systems on a roof, terrace, balcony or elevated structure, including building-integrated photovoltaics, per the MNRE Operational Guidelines (2024). Group and virtual net metering installations qualify if the discom approves the metering arrangement.

The exclusions are specific rather than general:

- **Elevated structures below 8 feet** of ground clearance at the lowest point.
- **Non-DCR modules** in any form. The Domestic Content Requirement means domestically manufactured modules using domestically manufactured cells.
- **Rooftop Solar Phase II installations**, which are not covered under PM Surya Ghar.
- **Group housing society connections** claiming the common-facility rate, unless dedicated to common facilities only. That capacity ceiling also counts plants residents already installed.

GERC Notification No. 5/2016 adds a floor and a ceiling: not less than 1 kW, not more than 1 MW. After its First Amendment (Notification No. 2 of 2017), residential consumers were freed from the 50% of sanctioned load cap that still binds other categories. Our [discom glossary entry](/glossary/discom/) defines the licensee terms.

## How to apply through the portal and your discom

The route has two halves: a national application and a state-level connection process. Do them in order.

1. Register on the National Portal for PM Surya Ghar with your consumer number and the licensee named on your electricity bill.
2. Select a vendor registered or empanelled with that discom. The portal publishes the list.
3. Have the vendor survey the site and propose a capacity that fits your roof and your consumption.
4. Complete the net metering agreement, now part of the portal application, per PIB (2026).
5. Install, then have the discom carry out its physical inspection.
6. Upload documents, let the e-token activate, then redeem it in your portal profile.

The e-token step is the one most people misread. The token is generated when you submit the application. It activates only after documents are uploaded and the discom inspection is complete, and you must redeem it before assistance is released, per MNRE (2024). If you financed the system, assistance goes to the loan account first, with any remainder to your bank account.

MNRE has also removed three friction points, according to PIB and MNRE (2026). The technical feasibility requirement is waived. Automatic load increase up to 10 kW is allowed. And a collateral-free bank loan at repo rate plus 50 basis points, presently 5.75% per annum over 10 years, is available. ## Why the registered vendor requirement matters

This is not a formality, and the obligations attach to the vendor rather than to you. MNRE Office Memorandum F. No. 318/6/2022-GCRT dated 10 June 2022 requires vendors installing residential rooftop solar to register or empanel with the beneficiary's discom. Know these conditions before you pay a deposit.

| Requirement | Detail, per MNRE (2022) |
| --- | --- |
| Performance bank guarantee | ₹2,50,000, valid at least five years |
| Empanelment validity | One year initially, renewed annually |
| Discom list publication | On the discom website, updated every month |
| Maintenance obligation | Comprehensive maintenance for at least five years |
| Technical manpower | At least three trained technical staff declared |
| Penalty for misleading information | Blacklisting and forfeiture of the guarantee |

Vendor discipline is active, not theoretical. MNRE deactivated 66 registered PM Surya Ghar vendors for a month over backlogs of pending applications, as reported by SaurEnergy (2026). It also advised vendors not to hold applications beyond 60 days without valid reason. A deactivated vendor disappears from the consumer selection page. Check your vendor's live status before signing, using our [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/).

## Documents and timeline, stage by stage

The GERC 2016 regulations prescribe a duration per activity rather than one headline number. These are regulatory targets, not observed averages, and PM Surya Ghar has since removed some steps.

| Stage | Prescribed duration, per GERC (2016) | Key documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GEDA registration certificate | 5 days from a complete application | Application, consumer details |
| Chief Electrical Inspector approval | 10 days | Single line diagram, earthing diagram, wiring diagram |
| Discom letter for feasibility report | 5 days | Proof of legal possession of roof, co-owner NOC, CEI approval, load details |
| Field technical feasibility report | 10 days from the head office letter | Feeder and transformer data, no dues certificate, no pending dispute certificate |
| In-principle consent after the report | 5 days | Connectivity charge demand, or a strengthening estimate |
| Connectivity agreement | Within 15 days of consent, or 30 days if strengthening is needed | Signed agreement, charges paid |
| Project completion window | 6 months from the letter | Installation records |
| Completion documents | 5 days from the intimation letter | Ownership proof, CEI charging approval, meter and CTPT test certificates, IEC test reports for modules, inverter, cables, junction box and meter |
| Meter installation | 7 days | Solar meter plus bidirectional meter |
| GEDA commissioning certificate | 5 days from intimation | Joint site visit |

Two timings sit outside that table. Central assistance is processed within 15 days of approval, per MNRE (2024). The installer undertakes operation and maintenance for five years after commissioning, according to the SURYA Gujarat portal (GUVNL, 2026).

Connectivity charges are fixed by system size, per GERC (2016). They are ₹1,500 from 1 kW up to 6 kW, ₹10,000 above 6 kW up to 100 kW, and ₹50,000 above 100 kW up to 1 MW. Our [documents checklist](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-documents-required/) covers the national side.

## Net metering and settlement under the GERC order

For residential consumers, GERC Notification No. 5/2016 dated 18 June 2016 works like this. If the discom supplies more than your system generates in a billing period, you are invoiced for net consumption at your prevailing tariff. If injection exceeds consumption, the discom buys the excess at the **Average Pooled Power Purchase Cost (APPC)** set by the Commission for your commissioning year. That rate then holds for the 25-year system life.

Three features are easy to miss:

- **Banking runs for one billing cycle only.** There is no open-ended annual carry-forward in the 2016 text.
- **Generation is set off against units consumed, not against load or demand.** Fixed and demand charges remain payable.
- **Your commissioning year fixes your export rate for 25 years.** Commission a year later and you inherit a different APPC.

The Gujarat Solar Power Policy 2021 set the residential and MSME surplus rate at ₹2.25 per unit, as reported by SaurEnergy (2020), and removed the 50% sanctioned load ceiling. The framework is moving again. GERC has published draft Grid Interactive Distributed Renewable Energy Sources Regulations, 2026 to replace the 2016 regulations. The draft adds net billing, group net metering and virtual net metering, and a public hearing was held on 18 June 2026, as reported by SaurEnergy (2026). Until it is notified, confirm your commissioning-year rate with your licensee. Our [net metering guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) and the [APPC glossary entry](/glossary/average-pooled-cost/) explain the mechanics.

## Why Gujarat applications get rejected or stall

Most refusals are documentary or technical. Very few are discretionary.

1. **Transformer headroom.** Cumulative rooftop capacity at one distribution transformer cannot exceed 65% of its peak capacity, per GERC (2016). If the feeder is full, you wait or pay for augmentation.
2. **Non-DCR modules.** Their use in any form makes the installation ineligible, per MNRE (2024). Ask for module make, model and DCR status in writing.
3. **Missing CEI approval.** The single line, earthing and wiring diagrams need Chief Electrical Inspector sign-off first.
4. **No dues or pending dispute.** The feasibility checklist requires both certificates, per GERC (2016).
5. **Missed document deadlines.** Completion documents filed later than 5 days after the intimation lead to cancellation and forfeiture of charges paid, per GERC (2016).
6. **Project overrun.** After the 6 month window, a 2 month notice is issued; silence then means cancellation within 30 days.
7. **Bank account mismatch.** Proof that the account is held by the consumer is required before release, per MNRE (2024).
8. **Vendor status.** A deactivated vendor cannot accept new applications, as reported by SaurEnergy (2026).

Nobody can promise you an outcome or a particular slab, and rules vary by discom. Our [rejection reasons guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-rejection-reasons/) walks through the appeal path.

## What Surya Gujarat does not cover

This is the section most sales conversations skip. Every point comes from the published regulations.

**The discom carries no responsibility for your subsidy.** The net metering interconnection agreement is explicit, per GERC Notification No. 5/2016. The distribution licensee shall not be liable for delivery or realisation by the consumer of any fiscal or other incentive provided by the central or state government.

**Network costs are yours.** The consumer bears all setup costs including metering and interconnection, per GERC (2016). That extends to modifications, service line upgrades and transformer upgrades where required. Strengthening work takes 45 days after you pay the estimate.

**Your bill does not go to nothing.** Generation is set off against units, not load or demand, so fixed and demand charges still appear. The scheme does not promise a zero electricity bill.

**The state top-up figure circulating online is dated.** A GEDA subsidy of ₹10,000 per kW of installed PV, capped at ₹20,000 per consumer, was reported by PV Tech (2016). It was released after commissioning, against an initial target of 100,000 consumers. That is a 2016 report, not evidence of a 2026 entitlement, and no current GEDA order restating it could be located.

**The state portal's own rates are old.** Its discovered rates page is labelled 2020-21, weak ground for arguing a 2026 quotation down.

## The Bottom Line

Surya Gujarat gave the state an early lead and a working single-window portal. The money in 2026 comes from PM Surya Ghar, and any state layer must arrive through the National Portal with an order behind it.

- **Read your bill, not a district map.** Identify your licensee (UGVCL, MGVCL, PGVCL, DGVCL or Torrent Power) and apply against that name on the National Portal.
