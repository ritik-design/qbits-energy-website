## The Six Categories, And Which Ones Have To Talk

| Category | What it does | How tightly it must integrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and simulation | Layout, shading, yield, BS 7671 compliance | Core. Everything downstream derives from it |
| Proposal and quoting | Customer document, SEG modelling, payback, e-signature | Must share the design record |
| MCS and compliance | Certification evidence, G99 documentation | Should be produced from the design, not rebuilt |
| CRM and pipeline | Leads, follow-up, conversion | API connection is sufficient |
| Project execution | Scheduling, install, handover pack | Should receive the design |
| Monitoring | Performance, service, warranty | Standalone, connects to the installed base |

> **The UK-specific insight.** In most markets the compliance row is a formality that follows the work. Here it is load-bearing, because MCS certification is what gives the customer access to SEG. A stack that treats it as an afterthought pushes a recurring manual rebuild into the back office and calls it admin.

The practical test when evaluating any platform is simple. Ask what happens to the MCS evidence when a design changes during the sale. If the answer involves someone remembering to update a separate document, you have found the recurring cost.

## What Is Genuinely UK-Specific

**MCS certification and its evidence trail.** Covered above, and the single strongest argument for choosing tools by their output format rather than their feature list.

**SEG rates are supplier-set.** There is no national export tariff. The value of an exported kilowatt-hour depends on which supplier the household is with, so an averaged assumption is wrong for most customers. This has a useful corollary: for a household on a poor SEG rate, recommending a switch can be worth more than any hardware upgrade you could sell them. Being able to model that quickly is both good advice and a credibility builder.

**BS 7671 and G99.** The electrical installation standard and the grid connection requirement respectively. A design produced against NEC defaults will need rework.

**Roof stock.** British housing is old, complex and heavily chimneyed, which makes shading analysis less optional than in markets with simpler roofs. Our [solar shading analysis software](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software/) guide covers why hourly module-level modelling matters on obstructed roofs.

## Where UK Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Underbought: MCS-ready output.** The reliable false economy. A cheaper design tool plus manual documentation appears to save money and consumes back-office hours indefinitely.

**Underbought: the design-to-proposal link.** A capable design tool plus a quoting spreadsheet is cheaper until a quote goes out with a superseded layout.

**Underbought: hourly shading.** On British roofs with multiple chimneys and dormers, annual-average shading figures are actively misleading, and they lead to string layouts that make the problem worse than it needed to be.

**Overbought: all-in-one suites bought early.** A small installer needs accurate designs, credible proposals and clean certification evidence. Project execution modules can wait.

**Overbought: seats for viewers.** Sales staff and site crews need finished documents, which is not the same as a licence.

| Task | With a disconnected stack | With an integrated core |

| Design the system | Once | Once |
| Retype the design into a quote | Every job | Not required |
| Confirm the customer's SEG supplier and rate | Manual lookup | In the model |
| Rebuild MCS evidence from the design | Every job | Produced from the record |

Four of those six rows disappear. None of them is glamorous, none appears in a feature comparison, and together they are usually the difference between a back office that scales with volume and one that becomes the bottleneck at about fifteen jobs a month.

The row worth singling out is the fifth. Design changes during a sale are normal, and in a disconnected stack every change means finding and updating each downstream document. That is where the version drift originates, and version drift between the quoted system and the certified one is a genuine MCS problem rather than a tidiness problem.
