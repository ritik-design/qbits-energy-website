**Short version.** Get two exact model numbers intended for the same job, put their datasheets side by side against your design, then compare written India warranty terms and two installed quotes on identical scope. That process decides it. The brand names do not.

## Why the brand level question does not work

Both Sungrow and Growatt sell multiple inverter families in India, spanning residential and commercial capacities, on-grid and hybrid architectures, single and three phase.

That means a statement like "Sungrow is premium and Growatt is mid market" compares positioning rather than products, and positioning tells you nothing about whether a specific model has the MPPT count your roof needs or supports the battery you intend to buy.

It also means the comparison changes entirely depending on what you are buying. Two models can be close on one project and not comparable at all on the next.

So the useful question is never which brand. It is which of these two specific models better fits this specific design, and are both quotes covering the same work. The [Qbits vs Sungrow vs Growatt comparison](/blog/qbits-vs-sungrow-vs-growatt/) adds a third brand to the same exercise, and the [Growatt review](/blog/growatt-solar-inverter-review-india/) and [Sungrow review](/blog/sungrow-solar-inverter-review-india/) cover each line separately.

### Reading the single-phase table

The two datasheets are close on almost every row: rated output, tracker count, one string per tracker, 16 A per tracker, THD and power factor. The rows that do differ are these.

**Maximum DC voltage and window.** The Sungrow accepts up to 600 V at the input and lists a 40 to 560 V MPPT window. The Growatt accepts up to 550 V with a 40 to 550 V window. A longer string on a cold morning is where the extra 50 V of headroom matters. Check your module's open-circuit voltage, corrected for the coldest expected temperature, against the maximum, and its maximum-power voltage on a hot day against the lower window edge. The [MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) entry explains both checks.

**Enclosure.** The Growatt datasheet lists IP66, and the Sungrow lists IP65. The difference is the protection against powerful water jets. For a covered wall mount it may not matter, and for an exposed or coastal roof it is one more reason to check the corrosion protection for the exact model.

**Efficiency.** The Growatt lists 98.4% maximum and 97.5% European efficiency, and the Sungrow lists 97.9% and 97.3%. The gap in weighted efficiency is two tenths of a percentage point. It is small beside the effect of string design, shading and clipping.

**Grid voltage window.** The Growatt lists 160 to 300 V and the Sungrow lists 154 to 276 V. Neither tells you what your DISCOM's connection conditions allow, and the setting applied at commissioning is what counts.

## The worksheet that settles it

Use the same sheet for both quotes. Fill the right-hand column from the datasheet and the written terms, and leave a cell blank if the document does not state it.

| Requirement | Sungrow quote | Growatt quote | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Model and type | Exact SKU | Exact SKU | On-grid, hybrid or off-grid function |
| Phase and rated AC output | Datasheet value | Datasheet value | Matches sanctioned connection and load |
| PV input | MPPT count, voltage window, current per tracker | Same fields | Against your proposed array and string plan |
| Battery support, if required | Approved pack list and backup rating | Same fields | Model and firmware combinations, in writing |
| Monitoring | Logger supplied, platform, account ownership | Same fields | Network requirement and who owns the data |
| Warranty | Written India terms, remedy, cost liability | Same fields | Not the headline duration alone |
| Service | Claim route for your district | Same fields | Named location, not a national figure |
| Price | Itemised installed quote | Itemised installed quote | Identical inclusions, tax and installation scope |

For a roof with more than one orientation, count independent MPP trackers rather than DC connectors. The [single versus dual MPPT guide](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/) explains why that distinction matters, and the [sizing guide](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) covers the string checks that constrain both candidates equally. Ask each installer to document string allocation and current per tracker against the model they are proposing. If only one of them does, that tells you something useful about the quotes as well as the inverters.

When a battery is involved, match nominal and operating voltage across the full state-of-charge range, maximum charge and discharge current in both directions, continuous backup output, surge capability with its duration, and transfer behaviour. A CAN or RS485 connector on both devices does not establish approved battery-management operation. Those are interface standards describing wiring and signalling, not proof that two products exchange meaningful instructions. The [hybrid buying guide](/blog/best-hybrid-solar-inverter-india-2026/) sets out the common evaluation framework.

## Monitoring

Sungrow's residential datasheets describe one-click access to the iSolarCloud platform, ten-second real-time data refresh, remote firmware updates on the India RS-L datasheet and, on the global RS datasheet, online IV-curve scan. Growatt lists its own monitoring platform with ShineWiFi-X, Shine4G-X, ShineLan-X and ShineLink-X accessories. Rather than comparing interfaces, settle the practical questions for whichever model you pick:

1. Does the model need a separate logger, dongle or SIM to report at all?
2. Who owns the plant account, and how is it transferred if you change installers?
3. Is per-tracker data visible, or only plant totals?
4. Which faults raise an alert, and does it reach you or only the dealer?
5. How long is history retained?

Two practical points. The clock can start before you switch the inverter on, so put the production or delivery date and the installation date on the handover record. And a coastal site changes the Sungrow answer completely, because the distance clause can reduce or void cover. Read it before the specification sheet.

## Service in India

Both manufacturers list an India route for claims and neither list tells you who visits your site. Growatt's contact page lists Growatt New Projects Pvt Ltd in Hyderabad with a toll-free line and a service email. Sungrow's India product page shows no India phone, email or address and links to a distributor finder and a home-support page. Ask each authorised seller:

- Which legal entity accepts a claim, and through which channel?
- Which named service partner covers my pincode, and do they hold the replacement board today?
- Who bears removal, transport and refit costs under the remedy?
- How long does the maker commit to supplying spares after the warranty term ends?

The answers matter more than any national centre count. The [warranty claim guide](/blog/solar-inverter-warranty-claim/) covers the evidence a claim needs.

## ALMM and certification

MNRE's current [ALMM page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/) publishes PV module and cell lists, not an inverter list, so it cannot rank inverter brands. The India Sungrow datasheet prints IS 16169 and IS 16221 (BIS) in its compliance line, while the Growatt MIN X2 datasheet lists IEC 62116 and IEC 61727 and no Indian standard numbers, and the Growatt MID X2 datasheet lists BIS. Treat a printed line as a pointer. Ask each seller for the BIS registration and certificates for the exact model, and confirm the scheme and DISCOM requirements separately. See the [certification guide](/blog/solar-inverter-certifications/).

## Which to choose when

These are starting points. Your string design, warranty documents and local service route override them.

- **Long strings or a cold-climate site.** The SG5.0RS-L lists 600 V maximum DC against the MIN 5000TL-X2's 550 V. Check your own cold-morning open-circuit voltage first.
- **Exposed or coastal roof.** The Growatt single-phase and three-phase models above list IP66 and the Sungrow models list IP65. For a coastal site also read Sungrow's distance clause and the corrosion rating, and ask Growatt for its written position on the same site.
- **Battery backup.** Decide the battery class first. Low-voltage (Growatt SPH) and high-voltage (Sungrow SH, Growatt MOD XH) systems are different projects.
- **Commercial three-phase.** The 20 kW pair is evenly matched on the datasheet. Decide on string layout, service route for the site and the warranty remedy.
- **A fixed warranty period you need in writing.** Growatt's datasheets state a 5 or 10 year line. Sungrow's term sits in its warranty terms, and the India document should be requested from the seller.

## Making two quotes comparable

Most brand comparisons are decided by a price difference that is not real, because the two offers cover different work. Before comparing totals, confirm both quotes state the same array: identical module model, count and layout. Confirm both assume the same mounting structure and cable runs, since a longer AC run changes both cost and voltage rise. Confirm both include or both exclude the DISCOM application, meter charges, protection equipment, monitoring hardware and commissioning. Then check what each one excludes. Only once the scope matches does the remaining difference say anything about the inverters. This page quotes no prices because a figure from another market or month would mislead.
