## Battery compatibility comes first

Most best lists rank hybrids by efficiency, MPPT count or app polish. Those matter, but a hybrid exists to charge and use a battery. If the inverter cannot manage your battery correctly, the battery charges slowly, wears early or does not deliver backup at all.

### Three battery chemistry tiers you will meet

- **Lithium iron phosphate (LFP).** The usual choice for new installs. Typical cycle ratings are in the thousands at 80 percent depth of discharge. For example, Victron states [2,500 cycles to 80 percent depth of discharge at 25 degrees C for its LFP batteries](https://www.victronenergy.com/blog/2019/02/26/lithium-superpack-batteries-an-all-in-one-solution/), against 600 cycles at 50 percent for its AGM battery. Other makers publish different figures, so read each battery's own datasheet. LFP normally talks to the inverter through a BMS.
- **Other lithium chemistries (NMC and similar).** Higher energy density, different voltage curves and different thermal behaviour. Many hybrid inverters list them under "Li-ion", so check the exact chemistry the supplier has approved, not just the word lithium.
- **Lead-acid (VRLA, AGM, tubular).** Lower upfront cost per kWh, shallower usable depth of discharge, and no data link needed. Several of the five datasheets list lead-acid, but one lists lithium-ion only and GoodWe's sheet notes that lead-acid is not available in all markets, so check the variant.

### CAN, RS485 and voltage-only charging

A [BMS](/glossary/bms/) tells the inverter the state of charge, cell temperature and allowed charge and discharge current. Three arrangements are common:

- **CAN bus.** A fast protocol used by many lithium battery makers. GoodWe's ES G2 datasheet lists CAN for communication with the BMS.
- **RS485 (Modbus or a vendor protocol).** Widely supported, but the message set differs between makers. RS485 on both devices does not prove they understand each other.
- **Voltage-only ("dumb") mode.** The inverter charges to a set voltage profile and has no cell-level data. It works for lead-acid. With lithium it removes a layer of supervision.

CAN or RS485 labels on both products do not prove they exchange the same messages. A safe pairing needs six checks for the exact inverter and battery:

> [!CHECKLIST] Six checks before pairing an inverter and battery
> - The battery's full operating voltage stays inside the inverter's battery window.
> - Charge and discharge current limits agree in both directions.
> - The chemistry and charging profile are supported.
> - The BMS protocol is implemented on both products.
> - Firmware versions and communication cable pinout match.
> - The battery make and model are approved in writing by the responsible supplier.

If the approved pairing is only verbal, treat compatibility as unresolved. For a battery-brand view, see the [battery brands guide](/blog/best-solar-battery-brands-india-2026/).

## Battery match matrix: backup hours, charge rate and depth of discharge

Three questions, asked in order, turn a pile of datasheets into a battery decision.

### Variable 1: backup hours and the battery size they need

List the circuits you want during an outage, add their running watts, and decide how many hours you need. Then:

> [!FORMULA] Backup hours
> Backup hours = battery kWh x usable depth of discharge (DoD) x inverter efficiency / load kW

> [!FORMULA] Required battery size
> Required battery kWh = load kW x outage hours / (DoD x inverter efficiency)

The worked tables below assume 80 percent usable DoD for LFP and 90 percent battery-to-AC efficiency, so the combined factor is 0.8 x 0.9 = 0.72. Both are planning assumptions. Datasheets print peak efficiency, which real use rarely reaches, so a lower working figure is the cautious choice.

Table: Backup hours by critical load and battery size, at 80 percent usable DoD and 90 percent efficiency

| Critical load | Battery (nominal) | Usable x efficiency | Backup hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 kW | 5 kWh | 5 x 0.72 = 3.6 kWh | 3.6 / 1.5 = 2.4 h |
| 2.0 kW | 10 kWh | 10 x 0.72 = 7.2 kWh | 7.2 / 2.0 = 3.6 h |
| 2.5 kW | 10 kWh | 10 x 0.72 = 7.2 kWh | 7.2 / 2.5 = 2.9 h |
| 2.0 kW | 15 kWh | 15 x 0.72 = 10.8 kWh | 10.8 / 2.0 = 5.4 h |
| 3.0 kW | 15 kWh | 15 x 0.72 = 10.8 kWh | 10.8 / 3.0 = 3.6 h |

Read the other way, a 2 kW load for a 4-hour outage needs 2 x 4 / 0.72 = 11.1 kWh of nominal battery. The inverter's kW rating is a separate limit. It caps the load, not the hours.

### Variable 2: charge rate against the solar window

A battery that cannot refill between outages gives less backup each time. Recharge time is:

> [!FORMULA] Recharge time
> Recharge hours = energy to replace (kWh) / (average charging power kW x charge efficiency)

Two limits set the charging power: the inverter's battery current, and the solar surplus after daytime loads. Take a 10 kWh battery used down to 80 percent DoD, so 8 kWh must be replaced, and assume 95 percent charge efficiency:

- With 4 kW of average charging power: 8 / (4 x 0.95) = 8 / 3.8 = 2.1 hours.
- With an inverter limited to 30 A at 48 V, which is 1.44 kW: 8 / (1.44 x 0.95) = 8 / 1.368 = 5.8 hours.

Current alone is not comparable across models. At 48 V, 120 A is 5.76 kW. At a 200 V high-voltage battery, 50 A is already 10 kW, which is why Sungrow prints a separate 6,600 W maximum charge power for its 50 A sheet. Always compare current at the battery voltage, and compare it with the PV surplus you can actually expect in the monsoon, not summer noon.

### Variable 3: depth of discharge and cycle life

[Depth of discharge](/glossary/battery-dod/) is the share of the battery's rated capacity drawn each cycle. Ranges vary by maker, and the maker's datasheet is the only figure that binds the warranty. As a typical planning range, LFP is often rated for 80 to 90 percent usable DoD, and lead-acid for around 50 percent. Victron's own comparison above uses 80 percent for LFP and 50 percent for AGM. Deeper discharge shortens life.

To size with a DoD buffer, divide the energy you need by the DoD. If you need 8 kWh usable from an 80 percent DoD battery, specify 8 / 0.8 = 10 kWh nominal. The [C-rate glossary](/glossary/c-rate/) explains how charge and discharge speed also affects life. For the full sizing method with a worksheet, use the [battery sizing guide](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/).

## PM Surya Ghar with a hybrid inverter

The central scheme pays on solar module capacity, not on the inverter or the battery. In the [MNRE operational guidelines for the residential component](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) (guideline text read in October 2026; later clarifications may apply):

- Assistance is 30,000 rupees per kWp for the first 2 kWp and 18,000 rupees per kWp for the third, with no additional assistance beyond 3 kWp. Special-category states have higher rates.
- The guideline says an installation "may include additional technology components such as small wind hybrids, battery storage", but the assistance is calculated on module capacity only. A battery therefore adds cost but no central subsidy.
- Assistance does not depend on inverter size. It follows the rated DC capacity of the modules.
- Off-grid installations are not eligible. For systems that connect to the grid without exporting, such as battery hybrid systems, the guideline says eligibility is "subject to approval by the respective Electricity Regulatory Commissions".
- Modules must be manufactured in India, and modules and inverters must meet the standards MNRE prescribes.

Rules change by order and by state, so verify at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) and with your DISCOM. The [PM Surya Ghar guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) walks through the application. Never treat a slab as a guaranteed amount for a given household.

## Common mistakes when buying a hybrid inverter

**Choosing the inverter before the battery.** If your preferred battery is not on the inverter's approved list, you either change the battery or return the inverter. Decide chemistry, brand and capacity in parallel with the inverter.

**Treating the inverter kW as battery capacity.** A 5 kW hybrid does not contain 5 kWh of storage. With a 5 kWh battery and a 2 kW load, the planning figure is 5 x 0.72 / 2 = 1.8 hours, not the 2.5 hours that 5 / 2 suggests and certainly not five hours.

**Ignoring charge limits in the monsoon.** Cloudy months cut the solar surplus, so a large bank with a modest charge limit can refill only partly between outages. Size the charge path for the weaker season.

**Assuming "LFP compatible" means every LFP battery.** BMS protocols are not fully standardised, and each maker approves specific battery models. Ask for the approved pairing by model number.

**Buying on peak efficiency.** The difference across the five sheets is 97.5 to 97.7 percent. That gap is smaller than the effect of choosing the wrong backup rating.

**Verifying subsidy treatment after signing.** Confirm with the DISCOM, before the deposit, how a hybrid system will be treated, using the guideline wording above.

## Check the hybrid is the right size

Before calling an installer, run a three-step check.

**Step 1: load audit.** List what must run during a cut and multiply watts by hours. For a 3 BHK with these circuits:

- 4 ceiling fans x 75 W x 8 h = 2,400 Wh
- 8 LED lights x 10 W x 5 h = 400 Wh
- 1 refrigerator x 150 W x 24 h x 50% compressor duty = 1,800 Wh
- Phones and Wi-Fi router = 200 Wh
- **Total: 4,800 Wh (4.8 kWh)**

**Step 2: battery.** Divide by usable DoD and efficiency: 4.8 / 0.72 = 6.7 kWh nominal. Round up to the next module size your battery supplier offers.

**Step 3: inverter.** The inverter must carry the load that runs together, not the daily total. Running watts add up to about 300 (fans) + 80 (lights) + 150 (refrigerator) + 50 (router and chargers) = 580 W, plus the refrigerator compressor's starting surge, which is on its nameplate. Even a 3 kW inverter has large headroom here, so a bigger model is justified by what you may add later, such as a water pump or air conditioner, and not by this list.

Then confirm string compatibility with the [string sizing calculator](/string-sizing-calculator/) before the DC wiring is finalised.

## For installers: sizing PV and battery together

Array and battery should be sized together, not in sequence. On a new install, sizing the battery first and the array second tends to produce a battery that the array cannot refill after a long outage, or an array that exports at a low rate while the battery sits full. Check three numbers together: the peak sun the array delivers in your worst month, the inverter's maximum charge power at the battery voltage, and the load that must still run in the evening. The Qbits QBH models list 1 or 2 MPPTs and per-tracker current limits by model, so check the exact column for the string layout.

## What most best lists get wrong

Efficiency is easy to rank because every datasheet prints a percentage. It is rarely the binding constraint in a hybrid purchase. An inverter with the highest peak efficiency can still be wrong if its backup output is too low or the battery pairing is unsupported. Peak efficiency also says nothing about field reliability or service quality.

The same applies to warranty years. A headline term does not tell you the remedy, exclusions, registration deadline, labour cover or who handles the claim. Get the current written terms for the exact model.
