## What The Solar Labs Actually Is Today

The Solar Labs was an India-headquartered software company building cloud tools for solar installers: preliminary sales quotations, optimised system design, and customer-facing proposals. That description still fits the product. What changed is the label on the box.

The vendor's own help centre carries a legacy article titled "Hands-on with The Solar Labs" that walks through the Design Studio, while every current marketing page, plan list and support surface uses ARKA 360. Meanwhile a direct check of thesolarlabs.com in August 2026 returns unrelated content, not a redirect and not a product page. On that evidence the name reads as a legacy label rather than a live brand, even where it still lingers in help documentation and third-party directories. The software itself is being actively sold under the current name, so this is a naming change to verify with the vendor, not a sign that the product has gone away.

We are stating this plainly rather than reviewing a ghost, because it changes how you should shop. Any feature list, screenshot or price you find attached to The Solar Labs name is undated at best. Verify everything against the current product before you budget against it.

> **What most buyers get wrong here.** They read a two-year-old review of The Solar Labs, build a shortlist from it, and then discover the plan structure, the regional editions and the export options are all different. Shortlist the product, not the brand name it used to carry.

## Genuine Strengths

These are real advantages, not politeness before a sales pitch.

**It was built for Indian workflows, not translated into them.** This is the single biggest thing the product has going for it, and it is easy to underrate if you have never watched a team fight a US tool. Most solar design software sold in India was scoped for North American or European buyers first. The financial model assumes generation credited against a retail utility bill. The compliance layer assumes NEC. The module catalogue is weighted toward brands an Indian EPC will never quote. Starting from an Indian premise instead means the defaults are closer to right in the first hour, and Indian installers notice that immediately.

**Browser-based, with no install friction.** A designer needs a laptop and a login. There is no workstation spec to argue about, no licence dongle, and no version drift between the office machine and the site engineer's laptop. For a growing EPC hiring junior designers, day-one productivity is a real cost line, and cloud delivery cuts it.

**Design carries through to a proposal in one tool.** The platform runs the actual sequence an installer runs: 3D roof model, shading study, generation report, then a customer-facing techno-commercial proposal. That last step is where a lot of engineering-first software gives up and hands you a spreadsheet. Removing the rebuild between design and quote is worth more to a residential team than another decimal place of yield accuracy.

**Shading and 3D modelling depth for the price band.** Shading simulation across the year with heat map output, terrain handling, and CAD export sitting on every published India plan is a reasonable feature load at ₹46,000 a year. Against tools costing three times that, the gap in core residential design output is smaller than the price gap suggests.

**Open pricing.** Publishing a plan table lets a buyer self-qualify without a sales call. In a category where "contact us" is the norm, that is a genuine courtesy and it earns trust.

## Limitations We Are Confident Of

**Bankable yield reporting is not the centre of gravity.** The clearest evidence is the product's own plan structure: PVsyst export appears on the Premium tier. That is a sensible design decision, and it also tells you where lender-grade modelling is expected to happen. If your pipeline includes project-finance C&I or PPA deals, expect a second licence and a second workflow for the yield report. That is not a criticism of the software's honesty, it is a scoping fact you should budget for.

**Multi-country work means multiple editions.** The vendor publishes distinct regional editions and separate price lists. For a team selling in one market that is clean and probably cheaper. For a team quoting across India plus the Gulf, Africa or Southeast Asia, it means configuration effort repeats per region rather than living in one compliance layer.

**Brand and continuity risk.** A rename is not a fault, but it is a diligence item. If you are signing an annual plan on the strength of reviews written under an older name, ask directly about product roadmap continuity, data portability and what happens to legacy accounts. Get the answer in writing before you commit.

We are not going to claim the product lacks any specific feature we could not test. Several capabilities we would want to assess, including depth of DISCOM tariff coverage and how ALMM filtering is handled, were not verifiable from published material, so we have left them as open questions for your own trial rather than asserting an absence.

## Who Should Buy It

Buy it if most of these describe you.

- You are an Indian residential or small commercial installer, and rooftop work is the bulk of your pipeline.
- Your bottleneck is turning enquiries into signed proposals fast, not producing lender-grade yield studies.
- You sell inside one market and have no near-term plan to quote across borders.
- Your budget sits in the ₹46,000 to ₹1,00,000 per year band and predictable published pricing matters more than negotiated enterprise terms.
- You want new designers productive in days, without a workstation build or a training programme.

At that profile the product is a sound, honestly priced choice, and arguing against it would be a pitch rather than a review.

## Who Should Not Buy It

- **Teams whose revenue depends on bankable yield reports.** If a lender, an IPP or a PPA counterparty expects a formal yield study as standard output, you are buying a second licence anyway. Price the pair, not the plan.
- **Teams quoting across several countries from one office.** Regional editions duplicate work that a single multi-market compliance layer would absorb.
- **Utility-scale and large ground-mount developers.** The product's demonstrated depth is rooftop residential and commercial. Large-array layout optimisation is a different discipline.
- **Buyers who need documented compliance breadth as a procurement condition.** If your tender or client requires named IS code clearances, ALMM-filtered catalogues and DISCOM tariff sourcing to be evidenced from the tool, verify each one in trial rather than assuming.

## Verdict

The Solar Labs earned its reputation honestly. Building solar design software from an Indian premise, when nearly every competitor was retrofitting a US product, was the right call and Indian installers were right to reward it. As ARKA 360 it remains a credible, openly priced, browser-based platform that does residential rooftop work well and carries it through to a proposal without a rebuild step. If that is your business, buy it and stop reading comparison tables.

Our recommendation tilts to SurgePV for a narrower reason than "it is better." It is a scope argument. The compliance layer runs deeper on the India requirements that increasingly appear in tenders and subsidy paperwork, the design-to-proposal path and the bankable yield output live in the same licence rather than across two, and the multi-market coverage means a team exporting beyond India does not maintain parallel configurations. At $1,299 to $1,899 per user per year that is a higher line item than a ₹46,000 Lite plan, and it should be, because it is absorbing work you would otherwise pay for twice.

Three things to do next:

- Confirm current plan inclusions and pricing directly with the vendor, because material published under The Solar Labs name is out of date.
- Rebuild three of your own recent projects in each shortlisted tool and compare the finished proposal, not the feature list.
- Decide whether bankable yield or a second country will be in scope within two years, because that answer settles the shortlist by itself.
