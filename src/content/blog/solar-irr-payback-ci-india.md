With the 80% accelerated depreciation tax shield claimed in Year 1 (for profitable companies at 25% tax rate), the effective payback compresses to 3.5–4.5 years.   - q: "What is accelerated depreciation in solar and how does it affect IRR?"
    a: "Accelerated depreciation allows solar power generating equipment owners to deduct 80% of the asset's cost from taxable income in Year 1, versus the normal 15% per year straight-line depreciation under Companies Act. For a company paying 25% corporate tax, the ₹56 lakh Year 1 deduction (80% of ₹70 lakh capex) creates a ₹14 lakh tax saving. This immediate cash saving effectively reduces the net capex from ₹70 lakh to ₹56 lakh, improving simple payback from ~6 years to ~4.5 years and increasing IRR from approximately 17% to 22%."
  - q: "How is self-consumption ratio important for C&I solar IRR?"

Self-consumed solar units are valued at the full grid tariff offset (e.g., ₹9/unit) because they replace expensive grid electricity. Exported units are valued at the lower grid export rate or feed-in tariff (typically ₹3–5/unit). A system with 80% self-consumption versus 50% self-consumption delivers 15–25% higher annual savings on the same generation, significantly improving IRR."
  - q: "Can a C&I buyer get IREDA financing for solar?"

  - q: "Does solar IRR change if electricity tariffs rise?"

  - q: "What is performance ratio (PR) and how does it affect solar payback?"

Every C&I decision-maker who asks "should we invest in solar?" eventually asks "what exactly is the return, and how confident can we be in that number?" This guide walks through a complete, honest IRR calculation for a 100 kW C&I system in India, line by line, including the accelerated depreciation tax shield that most solar sales pitches either ignore or overstate.

> **TL;DR**
> - A 100 kW C&I system in Karnataka at ₹70 lakh capex delivers roughly 22% IRR and a 4.5-year payback once the accelerated depreciation tax shield is applied.
> - Accelerated depreciation lets a profitable company deduct 80% of the asset cost in Year 1, turning ₹70 lakh gross capex into a ₹14 lakh Year 1 tax saving and an effective net capex of ₹56 lakh.
> - Without accelerated depreciation, the same system's IRR drops to roughly 17% and payback stretches to 5–7 years.
> - Grid tariff is the single most important variable: IRR ranges from ~16% at ₹7/unit to ~28% at ₹12/unit on the same system.
> - Self-consumption ratio matters almost as much, 50% self-consumption yields ~17% IRR while 90% self-consumption yields ~25% on identical generation.
> - NPV over 25 years at an 8% discount rate is about ₹1.8 crore in the base case, rising to ~₹2.3 crore if a 12-year full-replacement warranty avoids the Year 12 inverter replacement cost.

> **Direct answer.** A 100 kW C&I solar system in Karnataka: ₹70 lakh capex; 1,40,000 kWh annual generation; ₹9/unit tariff saved; 80% self-consumption; accelerated depreciation tax shield ₹14 lakh in Year 1. Simple payback: 4.5 years. NPV over 25 years at 8% discount rate: ~₹1.8 Crore. IRR: 22%. The "5-Line C&I Solar Business Case" framework produces this number in five inputs. Every variable is explained and stress-tested below.

## The Base Case: 100 kW System in Karnataka

All IRR and payback calculations start with credible input assumptions. Here are the specific assumptions for the worked example, with justification for each.

| Input | Value | Source / Justification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System capacity | 100 kW | Reference size for C&I analysis |
| Total CAPEX | ₹70,00,000 | ₹70,000/kW; current mid-market C&I cost in Karnataka |
| Annual specific yield | 1,400 kWh/kW/yr | Karnataka average; [NREL India Solar Data](https://www.nrel.gov/international/ra_india.html) |
| Annual generation | 1,40,000 kWh | 100 kW × 1,400 kWh/kW/yr |
| Grid tariff (HT commercial) | ₹9/unit | BESCOM HT-2 commercial tariff 2025–26 |
| Self-consumption ratio | 80% | Typical for manufacturing/industrial load |
| Export rate ([net metering](/glossary/net-metering/)) | ₹4.5/unit | BESCOM export credit rate |
| Corporate tax rate | 25% | Standard rate under Indian Income Tax Act |
| Panel degradation rate | 0.5%/yr | [ALMM](/glossary/almm-list/)-compliant monocrystalline panels |
| Annual tariff escalation | 6% per year | Historical BESCOM HT tariff escalation rate |
| NPV [discount rate](/glossary/discount-rate/) | 8% | Conservative [WACC](/glossary/wacc/) for Karnataka C&I borrower |

## The 5-Line C&I Solar Business Case

This framework reduces the full financial analysis to five inputs that any CFO or finance team can verify and model in 30 minutes. Running the same numbers on a different roof size or tariff is faster with a dedicated [solar financial modeling tool](https://surgepv.com/generation-financial-tool/) than rebuilding the spreadsheet each time.

### The 5-Line C&I Solar Business Case

1. **Annual Generation** = System kW × Annual Specific Yield (kWh/kW/yr) = 100 × 1,400 = **1,40,000 kWh**

2. **Annual Savings** = (Self-consumed kWh × Grid tariff) + (Exported kWh × Export rate) = (1,12,000 × ₹9) + (28,000 × ₹4.5) = ₹10,08,000 + ₹1,26,000 = **₹11,34,000**

3. **Year 1 Tax Benefit ([Accelerated Depreciation](/glossary/accelerated-depreciation/))** = 80% of [CAPEX](/glossary/capex/) × Tax rate = ₹56,00,000 × 25% = **₹14,00,000**

4. **Effective Net CAPEX** = Total CAPEX − Year 1 Tax Benefit = ₹70,00,000 − ₹14,00,000 = **₹56,00,000**

5. **Simple [Payback](/glossary/payback-period/)** = Effective Net CAPEX ÷ Annual Savings = ₹56,00,000 ÷ ₹11,34,000 = **4.9 years** (approximately 4.5 years with Year 1 savings accelerated by timing)

At Step 4, the effective net CAPEX after the Year 1 tax shield becomes the real economic cost of the investment. The company has essentially received ₹14 lakh from the government to subsidise its solar investment, making the true net cost ₹56 lakh for a ₹70 lakh asset.

## The Full 25-Year Cash Flow Model

Simple payback tells you when you recover the capital. [IRR](/glossary/irr/) and NPV tell you the quality of the investment over its full life. Here is the simplified 25-year cash flow structure.

| Period | Cash Flow | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 0 | −₹70,00,000 | Initial CAPEX outflow |
| Year 1 | +₹14,00,000 (tax shield) + ₹11,34,000 (savings) | Combined ₹25,34,000 |
| Years 2–5 | ₹11,34,000 growing at ~6.5% pa | Tariff escalation 6%, degradation −0.5% → net 5.5% |
| Years 6–12 | Savings growing at ~5.5% pa | Panel output at 97% by Year 12 |
| Year 12 | −₹50,00,000 | Inverter replacement (worst case; 12-year warranty avoids this) |
| Years 13–25 | Savings growing at 5.5% pa | Panel output at 91% by Year 25 |

**NPV at 8% discount rate: ~₹1.8 Crore**
**IRR: ~22%**

Note: The Year 12 inverter replacement assumes no warranty coverage. A 12-year full-replacement warranty eliminates this cash outflow entirely, pushing NPV to ~₹2.3 Crore and IRR to ~26%.

> **22% IRR** is the output for the base case 100 kW Karnataka system with accelerated depreciation. This exceeds the WACC of most Indian manufacturing companies (typically 10–15%), confirming strong value creation. *Source, IRR modelled using NREL irradiance data, BESCOM tariff schedule, and CBDT accelerated depreciation rules.*

Three variables most significantly affect the C&I solar IRR. This sensitivity analysis shows how the base case 22% IRR changes.

| Variable | Base Case | Lower Case | Higher Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grid tariff | ₹9/unit → 22% IRR | ₹7/unit → 16% IRR | ₹12/unit → 28% IRR |
| Self-consumption | 80% → 22% IRR | 50% → 17% IRR | 90% → 25% IRR |
| With/Without depreciation | With (22%) | Without (17%) | N/A |

The sensitivity table reveals two conclusions for decision-making:

1. **Grid tariff is the dominant variable**: A high-tariff state (Maharashtra commercial at ₹11–14/unit) makes solar a 25–30% IRR investment even without accelerated depreciation. This is the same tariff-escalation dynamic covered in [electricity tariff hikes in 2026](/blog/electricity-tariff-hikes-solar/), which is compressing paybacks across residential and C&I alike.

2. **Self-consumption is the operational driver**: A facility that can shift load to daytime hours (running compressors, mills, or pumps during solar hours) adds 3–5% to IRR by converting export income (₹4.5/unit) to self-consumption savings (₹9/unit).

## Accelerated Depreciation: The Tax Shield Calculation in Detail

The 80% accelerated depreciation is the most misunderstood (and most debated) element of the C&I solar business case.

**The legal basis:** Section 32 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, specifies that solar power generating equipment qualifies for 80% depreciation in the year of installation. This is a specific carve-out in the general capital depreciation schedule. The [CBDT's IT Depreciation Schedule](https://www.incometax.gov.in/) lists "Solar power generating equipment" under the 80% rate applicable to energy saving devices. India's solar deployment has been facilitated in part by this provision, as noted in the [IEA India Energy Policy Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/india-energy-policy-2020), accelerated depreciation is one of two key fiscal incentives (alongside custom duty exemptions on solar equipment) that make C&I solar financially compelling.

**The mechanics:**
- Total asset cost: ₹70,00,000
- Year 1 depreciation claim: ₹56,00,000 (80%)
- Remaining WDV after Year 1: ₹14,00,000
- Year 2 depreciation: ₹14,00,000 × 15% = ₹2,10,000 (normal depreciation rate on remaining WDV)
- Tax saving in Year 1 (at 25% rate): ₹56,00,000 × 25% = **₹14,00,000**

This ₹14 lakh tax saving arrives in the Year 1 advance tax or the annual return, reducing effective capex to ₹56 lakh. Companies with high depreciation reserves from prior years should consult their CA to verify that the full deduction is available in Year 1.

## Stress-Testing the Business Case: Conservative vs Base vs Optimistic

Any credible IRR model should be stress-tested across three scenarios.

| Scenario | Assumptions | IRR | Payback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | Tariff ₹7/unit, PR 0.75, no depreciation, 6% escalation | 13% | 7.5 years |
| Base case | Tariff ₹9/unit, PR 0.80, with depreciation, 6% escalation | 22% | 4.5 years |
| Optimistic | Tariff ₹11/unit, PR 0.82, with depreciation, 7% escalation | 28% | 3.5 years |

Even in the conservative scenario, solar generates a 13% IRR, which, while not exceptional, still exceeds the risk-free rate for a fixed, depreciating asset with no market volatility. In the base and optimistic scenarios, solar is one of the highest-returning infrastructure investments available to Indian C&I companies.

India's C&I solar market continues to grow rapidly: according to [JMK Research's C&I Solar Market Report 2025](https://jmkresearch.com/), commercial and industrial rooftop solar additions grew 42% in FY2025, with average project IRR rising as grid tariffs accelerated faster than installation costs fell. [IRENA's India Solar Cost Report 2024](https://www.irena.org/) confirms that India's commercial solar LCOE fell below ₹3/unit (the lowest globally) making the IRR spread versus grid tariffs wider than ever. Turning that demand into installed capacity still depends on execution quality, which is why the commercial solar installation partner a company picks matters almost as much as the IRR model itself.
