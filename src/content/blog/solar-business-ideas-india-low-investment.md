**The solar business ideas that need the least money are the ones where you sell your skill, not stock.** Panel cleaning, maintenance, sub-contracted installation and customer referrals all start with tools, training and a few months of running costs. Selling products, from solar lights to inverters, ties cash up in stock and in what customers owe you. Government registration adds paperwork. For installers on the PM Surya Ghar portal it also adds a performance bank guarantee (PBG).

We could not find a named, dated primary source that publishes a typical start-up cost for any of these models. Several listicles quote ranges. We do not repeat them, because none traces to a source you can check. This guide ranks the models by **cash at risk** instead, then gives you a worksheet to put your own numbers in.

This is general information as of **30 October 2026**, not tax, legal or financial advice, and it makes no income promise. Whether a solar business pays depends on your market, your customers and your control of cash.

> **Short version**
> - Service-led models (cleaning, maintenance, sub-contracted installation) need the least stock cash. Their risk is wages and slow payment.
> - Stock-led models (retail, dealership) need more cash, and the biggest drain is usually customer credit, not the shelves.
> - Rural pump work under PM-KUSUM, the central farm-solar scheme, is tendered. A newcomer's realistic route is working beside a selected bidder, not bidding alone.
> - Check GST, Udyam, training and electrical licensing before you spend. Each answer depends on what you actually sell.

## Eight low-investment solar business ideas, ranked by cash at risk

These eight solar business ideas are ranked qualitatively by where the cash goes, not by what a particular business will earn.

| Model | What you sell | Where cash gets stuck | What to verify first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panel cleaning and basic maintenance | Visits, on a contract or a one-off | Tools, safety gear, a vehicle, wages before the first payment | Roof-access safety, customer contract wording, who is liable for damage |
| Operation and maintenance (O&M) of small plants | Monitoring, inspection, fault visits | Test instruments, trained staff, response vehicle | Who may open an inverter or touch AC wiring on your team |
| Installation as a sub-contractor | Labour and site supervision for another installer | Crew wages while the main contractor pays late | Written scope, payment dates, who handles the paperwork with the DISCOM (power distribution company) |
| Referral and lead generation for installers | Introductions to customers | Almost none, but also no control over the sale | A written commission agreement, honest promises to the customer |
| Retail of small solar products | Lanterns, lamps, water heaters, chargers | Stock and display, plus any credit you extend | GST treatment of each product line |
| Own installation business | Complete rooftop systems | Material purchases, delayed customer and subsidy payments, any PBG | State electrical licensing, PM Surya Ghar registration if you want that work |
| Dealership of inverters, panels or batteries | Branded equipment to installers and shops | Opening stock, receivables from installers, demo units | The written programme terms, not a headline investment figure |
| Rural solar pump work | Installation and service for pump projects | Vehicle, spares, a five-year maintenance duty on tendered work | Who the selected bidders are in your district |

Making panels or inverters is left out on purpose. It needs plant, machinery and approvals that put it outside a low-investment start.

### Cleaning and basic maintenance

This is the lowest-stock entry. You carry no inventory, and customers pay for visits. The risk sits in two places: safety on roofs, and the pull to do work beyond your skill. Cleaning a panel is not the same as opening an inverter. A customer who loses generation to your mistake will not renew.

### O&M, and installation as a sub-contractor

Both are skill businesses, so training matters more than capital. The main cash risk in sub-contracting is simple. You pay your crew every week, and the main installer pays you in stages. Agree the payment dates and the scope in writing before the first site. Our guide to [working capital for solar dealers](/blog/working-capital-solar-dealers/) shows how to map that gap, and the same method works for a crew.

### Referral and lead generation

Introducing customers to an established installer needs almost no capital, so first-time starters like it. Its weakness is control. You do not choose the installer's quality, and a customer who has a bad experience remembers who recommended the firm. Get the commission terms in writing, and never promise a customer a subsidy amount you cannot confirm.

### Retail of small solar products

Solar lanterns, lamps and water heaters sit in the 5% GST group (see the GST section below). Retail needs a shop or a stall, stock and a billing system. The cash decision is how much credit you give. The worked example later in this guide shows why.

### Your own installation business

You buy materials, pay a crew and wait for customers and DISCOMs. PM Surya Ghar work comes through a registered-vendor route that carries a bank guarantee. MNRE's April 2024 [draft guidelines](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3716e1b8c6cd17b771da77391355749f3/uploads/2024/04/202404162127034309.pdf) list Rs 2.5 lakh for state registration and Rs 25 lakh for national registration. That is a draft, so confirm the live amount with the registering authority. Our [PM Surya Ghar vendor registration guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vendor-registration-installers/) covers the documents, the declaration and the checks to run before a bank issues anything.

### Dealership of branded equipment

A dealership is the most stock-heavy model on this list. Opening stock, demo units and receivables from installers all draw on cash. Do not trust any programme that quotes a single investment number. Read [how much investment a solar dealership needs](/blog/solar-dealership-investment-india/) for the worksheet. If you are weighing a franchise against a dealership, [solar franchise vs dealership](/blog/solar-franchise-vs-dealership/) sets out the differences.

To resell inverters through Qbits, the entry point is the [partner application](/become-our-partner/). Sending it starts a review of your business. The application sets no commercial terms, and none are described in this guide.

### Rural pump work

The PM-KUSUM guidelines on the [national portal](https://pmkusum.mnre.gov.in/assets/pdf/KUSUMguidelines.pdf) describe centralised tendering for standalone solar pumps, run through central public sector undertakings. In that text, only makers of solar water pumps, controllers or solar panels may take part in the bidding. Selected bidders must give a five-year annual maintenance contract (AMC) from installation. In each state, the State Implementation Agency handles the work.

A newcomer usually enters through installation, service or a local dealer role beside a selected bidder, not by bidding directly. These guidelines date from the scheme's launch and have been amended since. Check the portal for the current text and for the scheme's present status before you plan around it.

## How much cash does a low-investment solar business need? A worksheet

A start-up needs enough cash to last until customers have paid enough to cover the next month. That figure matters more than the headline cost. Use this formula for every model on your shortlist.

**Cash needed = setup costs + opening stock + (monthly running cost x months until steady collections) + customer-credit float + contingency**

Two illustrative examples follow. **All figures are assumed for illustration. Replace each one with a written quote from your own supplier, landlord or bank.**

### Example 1: a two-person maintenance start (illustrative)

| Line | Assumption | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly running cost | Technician Rs 25,000, helper Rs 15,000, fuel, phone and software Rs 10,000, other Rs 10,000 | Rs 60,000 |
| Months until steady collections | Three months of building customers and getting paid | 3 |
| Running-cost cash | Rs 60,000 x 3 | Rs 1,80,000 |
| Tools and safety gear | Assumed one-time purchase | Rs 50,000 |
| Subtotal | | Rs 2,30,000 |
| Contingency | 20% of subtotal | Rs 46,000 |
| **Cash needed** | | **Rs 2,76,000** |

There is no stock and no customer-credit line, because customers pay per visit. If you sell annual contracts and let customers pay in instalments, you would add a float line.

### Example 2: a small solar-product shop (illustrative)

| Line | Assumption | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening stock | Assumed | Rs 3,00,000 |
| Display and signage | Assumed | Rs 50,000 |
| Monthly running cost | Rs 40,000 x 3 months | Rs 1,20,000 |
| Customer-credit float | Rs 2,00,000 of monthly credit sales x 1.5 months (45-day credit) | Rs 3,00,000 |
| Subtotal | | Rs 7,70,000 |
| Contingency | 20% of subtotal | Rs 1,54,000 |
| **Cash needed** | | **Rs 9,24,000** |

The float is valued at sale price here for simplicity, which slightly overstates it. The lesson holds anyway. At 45-day credit the float equals the whole opening stock. At 30-day credit it falls to Rs 2,00,000 (Rs 2,00,000 x 1), which cuts the subtotal by Rs 1,00,000. **How much credit you extend can matter more than how much stock you buy.**

For a dealership, add the lines the programme's written terms create: deposits, demo units, freight and the credit your installers will ask for. The dealership investment guide linked above walks through them.

## Checks that depend on what you sell

### What GST rate applies to solar products?

Solar goods moved to 5% GST from 22 September 2025, but each product still needs checking. The GST Council's [press release on its 56th meeting](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau_0.pdf), dated 3 September 2025, lists a cut from 12% to 5% on renewable energy devices and parts for their manufacture. The list includes solar power-based devices, solar power generators, solar lanterns and lamps, solar cookers, and solar water heaters and systems. The same release says the changes take effect from 22 September 2025.

The rate for a given product still depends on how it is classified. Solar inverters are a known trap, because the heading for static converters is treated differently from panels. Read [GST on solar inverters](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) before you price an inverter line, and ask your supplier in writing which notification entry they apply.

### Udyam and GST registration

Udyam registration (the government's registration for micro, small and medium enterprises, MSMEs) and GST registration are separate. A small trader can be eligible for one and not required to hold the other. Neither registration appoints you as anyone's dealer. Our [GST and Udyam guide for dealers](/blog/gst-udyam-solar-inverter-dealer/) gives the thresholds as of 26 September 2026 and says what each registration does and does not give you.

### What does the NISE solar training programme require?

The National Institute of Solar Energy (NISE) runs the Suryamitra Skill Development Programme for MNRE. On [its page](https://suryamitra.nise.res.in/), checked on 30 October 2026, NISE describes a 600-hour residential course. It says the course also prepares candidates to become solar entrepreneurs. The page names ITI or Diploma holders (electrical, electronics, civil, mechanical, fitter, instrumentation, welder) as the intended participants. It states that people with a degree in any discipline or higher are strictly not eligible. Certificates are issued by the Skill Council for Green Jobs after assessment. The page does not state a course fee.

Two practical points follow. If you hold a degree, this particular course is not open to you, so budget for another way to build technical competence. If you want PM Surya Ghar vendor registration later, note that the 2022 declaration in our guide asks for at least three people trained in rooftop solar work.

### Do you need an electrical licence for a solar business?

Rules on who may carry out electrical work differ by state. We have not verified the licensing position for any single state in this guide. The state-by-state position, with the rows we could not verify, is in our [electrical contractor licence guide for solar installers](/blog/electrical-contractor-licence-solar-installers/). The PM Surya Ghar vendor declaration asks a firm to confirm it can meet electrical-safety and other legal requirements. Before you take wiring or grid-connection work, ask your state electrical licensing authority and the local DISCOM what they require. Ask specifically about the person who signs the work.

## Before you spend anything

1. Name your first ten customers, or the installer who will take your referrals. A plan without a named buyer is a guess.
2. Write down who pays whom, and on which day, for every project. Stage payments are where small firms run out of cash.
3. Compute the worksheet above with real quotes, and add a second line for the case where collections arrive a month late.
4. Check the registration and licence for the work you actually intend to do, not for solar in general.
5. Never promise a customer a subsidy figure, a generation figure or a payback period you cannot document.
6. Treat any "franchise" or "dealership" fee as one item on the sheet, and read the programme's written terms before paying.
