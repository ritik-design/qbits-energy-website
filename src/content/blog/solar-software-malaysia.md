A technically excellent design for a customer who cannot obtain quota is not a project, it is unpaid work. That single fact should shape how a Malaysian business organises its stack: qualification before design, quota status tracked as a hard condition, and a design tool whose drawing output is good enough for the PE stamp that every grid-connected job requires. Teams that design first and check quota later are systematically donating engineering hours.

## What Is Genuinely Malaysia-Specific

**Quota as a binding constraint.** Covered above. The practical implication for software is that quota status should be a field in your CRM with a hard gate attached, not a note in someone's email.

**The 75 percent of Maximum Demand cap.** Unusual and easily mishandled. Because it is calculated from the customer's Maximum Demand rather than being a fixed ceiling, the tool needs that figure as an input. A platform that offers a single configurable capacity limit cannot represent this properly, and a design produced without it may be inadmissible.

**1:1 net billing.** Genuinely favourable, and worth understanding as a design simplification. Where neighbouring markets credit at a generation rate well below retail and force careful load-curve matching, Malaysian export offsets import at parity. That means annual consumption is a reasonable sizing basis and the pressure toward storage is lower. It is one of the few markets in this series where the older, simpler design logic still holds.

**The PE-stamped SLD.** A BEM-registered engineer has to stamp the single line diagram on all grid-connected work. This sets a quality bar on your design tool's drawing output that a sales-oriented diagram will not meet.

**MS IEC 62109 and 2-second disconnection** on TNB supply loss.

**GITA and timelines.** A 100 percent investment tax allowance over 3 years subject to MIDA approval, and a typical NEM timeline of 4 to 6 months that commercial buyers plan around.

## Where Malaysian Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Underbought: qualification tooling.** The highest-return fix available, and it is usually process rather than software. Tracking quota status and Maximum Demand as hard fields prevents wasted design effort.

**Underbought: SLD output quality.** If your engineer redraws the single line diagram before stamping it, your design tool is producing a sales picture rather than an engineering document.

**Underbought: GITA cashflow modelling on commercial work.** Presented properly it changes decisions, and it is frequently reduced to a footnote.

**Overbought: heavy self-consumption and battery modelling.** Genuinely useful in most markets in this series and less critical under 1:1 net billing. A team buying an expensive platform primarily for its storage optimisation may be paying for a capability the Malaysian mechanism does not reward.

**Overbought: seats for viewers.** Universal.

## Worked Example: Why The Non-Domestic Cap Is Not A Number

An illustrative comparison, not a specific customer project. Two commercial customers with identical roof area, different Maximum Demand.

| | Customer A | Customer B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usable roof capacity | 600 kWp | 600 kWp |
| Maximum Demand | 400 kW | 150 kW |
| NEM ceiling at 75% of MD | 300 kWp | 112.5 kWp |
| Roof-based design would propose | 600 kWp | 600 kWp |
| Permissible under NEM | 300 kWp | 112.5 kWp |
| Overdesign if roof-led | 2x | Over 5x |

Same building footprint, and the permissible system differs by a factor of nearly three between them. A design tool working from roof area alone will produce two identical proposals and both will be inadmissible, one dramatically so.

This is the clearest argument for putting the Maximum Demand figure into qualification rather than treating it as a compliance detail. It is not a constraint that trims a design at the margin, it frequently determines the entire scale of the project, and it is knowable before any engineering work happens.
