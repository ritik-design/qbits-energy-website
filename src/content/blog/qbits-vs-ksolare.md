**In a Qbits vs KSolare comparison, the biggest finding is KSolare's own paperwork: its site carries three different datasheets for one 10 kW three-phase unit.** KSolare prints more protection rows, a -30 C cold end, short-circuit current per tracker and a public set of lab test reports. Qbits prints a higher maximum DC voltage on three-phase units (1100 V against 1000 V), a higher PV power ceiling on the larger units and a lower weight at 30 kW. A dealer should settle which KSolare sheet applies before quoting, then check module current, roof layout and the paperwork customers ask for.

**Disclosure.** Qbits publishes this article and sells the Qbits models in it. KSolare is a competitor, so a dealer should read our summary with that in mind. Both brands went through one method: the brand's own current datasheet, the version or date printed on it, and the date we read it. The KSolare files came from the links on ksolare.com and the Qbits files from the public datasheet downloads on qbitsenergy.com, all on 30 October 2026. Every KSolare figure is KSolare's own claim, not a result of ours. We tested no unit and hold no field data on either brand. Both brands' sheets are image files, so we read them visually and quote the figures as printed.

**What "two Indian brands" means here.** Both brands sell inverters to Indian installers, and the Qbits office is in Surat, Gujarat. KSolare's own brochure and About page say it was established in 2012, is located in Technology Park, Pune, has in-house production in Pune and runs a PCB design centre. That is KSolare's statement, and we did not visit any site. We make no claim about where any Qbits unit is made, and we cannot confirm where any KSolare unit is made. If origin matters to your customers, ask each brand in writing for the factory address and the production site for the exact model.

This article is for dealers and installers deciding what to stock, quote or add as a second brand. It reads documents and tests no hardware.

Four things to know about the KSolare documents before reading the tables.

- **KSolare's pages sit on a second web address.** The ksolare.com home page links its product pages, datasheets and test reports to biovisionpe.com/ksolare. The downloads page on ksolare.com carries no datasheets. We cannot tell from the pages how the two addresses are related. We took the documents as KSolare's because ksolare.com links to them and the sheets carry the KSolare name and logo. Confirm this with KSolare when you ask for the sheet that matches your unit.
- **One class, several sheets.** At 10 kW the three sheets disagree on trackers, tracker current and voltage window (table below). The Mega Plus sheet prints two different 50 kW columns and does not say which model code is which.

We compare only classes where both brands have a datasheet we opened. The KSolare hybrid page we opened links no datasheet, so we compare no hybrid.

The Qbits product record lists further families we left out, such as the QB-80-125KTLC. ## What KSolare does well

These strengths come from KSolare's own pages and documents, opened on 30 October 2026.

- **Test reports on the public pages.** KSolare links six lab test reports. The ones we opened name KSolare Energy Pvt Ltd as customer and cover IS 16169 and, in some, IS 16221 Part 2. They are: a single-phase series up to 6.2 kW (URS Products and Testing, dated 17 April 2025); a single-phase one-tracker series up to 6.5 kW (URS, 4 June 2024); and a Hi Physix Laboratory report dated 16 December 2025. Two older Hi Physix reports follow: a single-phase report dated 16 June 2022 and a three-phase series up to 22 kW (16 June 2022). A sixth file is a scan of Hi Physix reports dated 18 June 2022. We did not establish the model coverage of the December 2025 report or the scan. None of the summaries we read names a model above 22 kW. These are lab test reports, not registration certificates, and we read no registration number. Even so, this is more open than Qbits on one point: Qbits's datasheets page says certificates are requested separately for the exact model.
- **A public service route.** KSolare's After Sales page lists an IVR line, auto-generated tickets sent to the customer and the dealer, and remote monitoring. It also lists a site visit by a local service engineer and a replacement stated as within 48 hours by the local distributor or KSolare. These are KSolare's statements and we did not test the route or the timing. A public warranty-claims page also sets out the claim procedure. We compare no warranty terms.
- **More protection rows.** The Mega Plus sheet prints short-circuit current per tracker, type II surge protection, ground-fault monitoring, AC short-circuit protection and optional AFCI. The Qbits sheets print "Yes" for surge protection with no type, and no short-circuit current or AFCI row.
- **A colder printed range.** Three of the four KSolare sheets print -30 to +60 C; the Pro Plus sheet prints -25 to +60 C. The Qbits on-grid sheets print -25 to +60 C.
- **A 22 A tracker on a single tracker 5 kW unit.** That is more tracker current than the 20 A on both Qbits 5 kW sheets, which matters for 700 Wp class modules (see the worked check).
- **A public hotline and factory description.** The brochure describes a Pune factory test sequence (HV test, a six-hour heat run, automated test, IP65 water test) and a 24 hour hotline. We did not verify any of it.

Qbits publishes model datasheets on its [datasheets page](/download-datasheets/). That page says installation manuals, written warranty and certificates are requested separately for the exact model, which is less open than KSolare's public test reports and claims page.

## A worked check: do the strings fit?

This check is **illustrative**. It uses a 700 Wp class module with assumed values. Use your own module's datasheet.

**Assumed module (illustrative):** Voc 48.6 V, Vmp 40.3 V, Isc 18.3 A and Imp 17.4 A, with -0.26% per C on both voltages, a 5 C cold design temperature and a 65 C hot cell. The other comparison posts here use the same assumptions.

**5 kW single-phase, a 10-module string (7.0 kWp).** Cold open-circuit voltage is 10 x 48.6 V x (1 + 0.0026 x 20) = 511 V, under 550 V on all three units. Hot operating voltage is 10 x 40.3 V x (1 - 0.0026 x 40) = 361 V, inside every printed window. Current: 17.4 A at Imp is under the 20 A on both Qbits models and under the 22 A on the KSolare unit. Isc of 18.3 A is under KSolare's printed 26 A short-circuit rating (Qbits prints none). The DC power row is where they differ: 7.0 kWp is under the 7500 W Qbits row and over the 5 kW "max peak DC input power" on the KSolare sheet by 40%. The KSolare brochure's "50% overloading" line would cover it, but the sheet does not say so.

**10 kW three-phase, one string per tracker.** A single 700 Wp class string at 17.4 A fits the Qbits 20 A trackers and the 22 A Pro Plus tracker, but not a 16 A tracker. So two of the three KSolare 10 kW sheets (the Pro Model and the 3 to 15 kW sheet) rule out this module on tracker current, and the third does not.

**50 kW, six strings of 16 modules (67.2 kWp).** Cold open-circuit voltage is 16 x 48.6 V x 1.052 = 818 V, under 1000 V on both brands. Hot operating voltage is 16 x 40.3 V x 0.896 = 578 V, inside every printed MPPT window. Two strings read 34.8 A at Imp.

| Unit | Trackers as printed | Strings the trackers carry at 17.4 A | Six strings fit on current? | 67.2 kWp against the printed PV power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qbits QB-50KTLC-Plus | 40 / 40 / 20 / 20 A | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 | Yes, with no spare tracker | Under the 75 kW row |
| KSolare Mega Plus, first 50 kW column | 4 x 40 A | 2 per tracker = 8 | Yes, two spare string places | Over the 50 kW row |
| KSolare Mega Plus, second 50 kW column | 4 x 40 A | 2 per tracker (3 strings printed) = 8 | Yes | Over the 65 kW row |

Three limits apply. Qbits prints no short-circuit rating, so the Isc check is open for that unit. The KSolare 50 kW columns print different PV power rows, and the brochure speaks of 50% overloading. The DC power result therefore depends on which KSolare figure applies to your model. Finally, we read the printed Qbits row as two 40 A and two 20 A trackers but not their order, so get that order in writing. Run your own layout through the same arithmetic before you quote.

## What this means for a dealer deciding between them

Datasheets will not settle a stocking decision, but they do show how much work each brand leaves to you.

- **Stock mix.** Tracker count and tracker current decide which models can be quoted for 600 to 700 Wp modules. Compare the tracker rows above against the modules you actually sell before you count models.
- **Quote hygiene.** If a brand has several sheets for one capacity, put the model code and sheet version into each quote. Then a later dispute is about a named document.
- **Evidence you can pass on.** If your customers ask for test reports, ask each brand which reports exist for the exact model, who issued them and when. KSolare publishes some on its pages; for Qbits, ask for the documents for the exact model.
- **Claims route.** Both brands tell you who handles a fault only in their own words. Ask each how a claim moves from site to brand and back, as in the [dealer service claim process](/blog/dealer-inverter-service-claim-process/).
- **Agreement terms.** Territory, credit, returns and termination are written in the dealer agreement, not on a datasheet. The [dealer agreement checklist](/blog/solar-dealer-agreement-checklist/) lists what to review with an adviser before you sign with any brand.

If Qbits is one of the brands on your list, the [partner application](/become-our-partner/) collects your business details and starts a review. The application sets no terms and this guide describes none, so rely only on what you receive in writing.

## What we could not compare

- **Reliability in the field.** Neither datasheet reports failure rates, returns or uptime, and we hold none for either brand.
- **Service in your district.** KSolare states a service route and a 48 hour replacement, and the Qbits office is in Surat, Gujarat. We tried neither route, and a datasheet cannot show how fast a fault is attended.
- **Price.** Qbits quotes per model and quantity. We hold no KSolare quote and print no price for either brand.
- **Warranty.** Terms differ by model and by document, and KSolare's own sheets, brochure and claims page do not print a single figure. This page compares no warranty term for either brand. Ask each for the base term, remedy and exclusions in writing for the exact model.
- **Certificates and registrations.** Each datasheet lists standards or none. A listed standard, or a published lab test report, does not show that a given unit is registered or certified for your DISCOM (the state power distribution company). Get current certificates and registration numbers for the exact model from each brand, and verify them yourself.
- **Net-metering acceptance.** The DISCOM decides whether a model is accepted. [What DISCOMs check on an inverter](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) describes that review, and a dealer should get each brand's approved-model position on paper before promising a customer anything.
- **Hybrid, off-grid and micro inverters.** KSolare's hybrid page links no datasheet, so there is nothing to set against the Qbits hybrid sheets.
- **Software and monitoring.** We did not compare apps, portals or API access. KSolare says its remote monitoring uses an Indian-based server, which we did not check.

## A checklist for dealers to take to Qbits and KSolare

1. **Which unit and sheet?** Get the model code, the sheet version and date, and the sheet that matches the nameplate of the unit you will receive.
2. **What are the tracker limits?** Ask for current per terminal, short-circuit current and the per-string limit.
3. **Is the PV power limit hard?** Ask whether the printed maximum PV power can be exceeded and what the unit does above it.
4. **Which reports and certificates exist?** Ask for them for the exact model, with the issuing body and the date.
5. **Is the model on the DISCOM list?** Ask whether it appears on your DISCOM's accepted list and get the answer in writing.
6. **Where is it made?** Ask for the production site and its address for the exact model.
7. **Who handles a fault?** Ask who attends in your district, how a replacement is routed and who bears each cost.
8. **Where are the spares?** Ask which spares sit in India and which come only on order.
9. **What is the warranty?** Request the base term, remedy, claim process and exclusions for the exact model in writing.
10. **Which rows are missing?** Ask Qbits for the rows its sheets leave out: short-circuit current, AFCI (arc-fault protection), surge type and the terminal map. Ask KSolare which of its sheets applies and which standards each model meets.
11. **How do the other brands read?** The [datasheet reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) shows how to read each line of any sheet before you rely on it. The same method is applied to other brands in [Qbits vs Solis](/blog/qbits-vs-solis/) and [Qbits vs GoodWe](/blog/qbits-vs-goodwe/).
