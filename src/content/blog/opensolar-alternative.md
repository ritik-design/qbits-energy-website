## Why Installers Look for an OpenSolar Alternative

OpenSolar built a strong residential community by leading with a free tier. The product is genuinely useful for simple residential designs, and the salesforce angle is well executed. Most installers shopping for an alternative are not unhappy with the residential workflow. They are unhappy with what happens when projects get more complex, when C&I lands in the pipeline, or when the add-on invoice arrives.

### The hidden cost of 'free'

OpenSolar publicly describes its model as free to installers, funded through hardware and finance partners, with some capability available as paid extensions. None of this is hidden, and it is a legitimate way to run a software business. The practical point for a buyer is that the licence fee is not the whole cost picture. Teams that started on free are worth encouraging to total up extensions plus manual workaround time and compare that against a flat per-seat licence, rather than assuming zero.

### C&I shading and engineering depth

OpenSolar's shading model is fine for straightforward residential rooftops with simple obstructions, which is the work it is positioned around. In our own testing it gets thin faster than engineering-first tools on C&I rooftops with multiple arrays, complex tilt and orientation, or inter-row [shading analysis](/glossary/shading-analysis/) on ground-mount. Teams that need bankable simulation for lender-funded projects typically move to a tool with full 8,760-hour module-level granularity, the same depth covered in our [commercial solar design software guide](/blog/commercial-solar-design-software/). SurgePV ships this [8,760-hour shade analysis](https://surgepv.com/shadow-analysis) engine on every paid plan.

### Hardware integrations and lock-in

OpenSolar's hardware integrations are useful, and the vendor's publicly described model is funded by hardware and finance partners rather than by installer seats. Nothing stops you specifying whatever you like, but teams who want hardware neutrality enforced by their tooling, plus a clean BOQ export, sometimes prefer a platform with no supplier integrations behind the equipment list. Installers who prefer to shop inverters on spec rather than platform routing can pull datasheets straight from a manufacturer's [full product catalog](/our-products/), including the [C&I-scale packages](/c-i-solution/) for projects that outgrow simple residential add-ons.

### Proposal output and brand control

OpenSolar's proposal templates are good, and buyers report that advanced branding, multi-language support, and white-labelled web proposals sit in paid extensions rather than the free core. Packaging changes, so confirm what is included before you plan around it. SurgePV includes a [branded solar proposal](https://surgepv.com/solar-proposals) builder with e-signature, web sharing, and nine languages on every paid plan, a category covered in more depth in our [solar proposal software guide](/blog/solar-proposal-software/). Teams that also want a dedicated [quotation and follow-up workflow](https://quickestimate.co/features/quotation-system/) often pair this with QuickEstimate on the sales side.
