> **Quick answers**
>
> - There is no scheme that gives residential solar panels away at zero cost.
> - PM Surya Ghar provides central financial assistance against a published slab structure.
> - MNRE guidelines state ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp, ₹18,000 for the third, capped at ₹78,000.
> - Assistance does not increase above the cap, so oversizing is funded entirely by you.
> - Zero upfront offers are finance products. Compare total cost, ownership and exit terms.
> - The scheme never asks for an OTP or an advance fee through a social media link.

**Short version.** The scheme is real, the free panels are not. Expect to fund the balance of the contract after assistance, verify the vendor and the application through official channels only, and treat any offer that needs your OTP as fraudulent.

## Why the phrase persists

"Free solar panel scheme" is a search phrase, not a government programme name. It persists because assistance schemes get compressed into a headline as they travel through social media, and because some advertisers deliberately blur the line.

What exists is PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, a central scheme providing financial assistance toward eligible residential rooftop installations. "Muft bijli" refers to the free electricity the system is expected to generate over time, not to free equipment.

That distinction is the whole article. Everything else follows from it.

## What the guidelines actually provide

The [MNRE residential CFA guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) set out the standard residential slab.

| System capacity | Central financial assistance, standard slab |
| --- | --- |
| First 2 kWp | ₹30,000 per kWp |
| Additional 1 kWp, taking the system to 3 kWp | ₹18,000 |
| 3 kWp and above | Capped at ₹78,000 |

Special category states have different amounts in the guidelines. These are published slabs rather than an entitlement, and an individual application is still subject to eligibility, vendor, equipment and DISCOM conditions.

Two implications are frequently missed.

First, the cap means assistance stops increasing above 3 kWp under the standard slab. A 5 kWp system receives the same central assistance as a 3 kWp one, so the additional capacity is funded entirely by you. Size the system for your consumption and your state's settlement rules, not to chase assistance that is not there.

Second, assistance is calculated from eligible installed solar module capacity. Adding a battery does not increase it.

## Reading a quote properly

A quote should separate four figures: the gross contract amount, the eligible central assistance, any independently verified state support, and the amount you must fund.

Take an illustrative example. A vendor presents a ₹150,000 installed quote for an eligible 3 kWp system under the standard slab. Subtracting an assumed approved ₹78,000 leaves ₹72,000 to fund, before any finance cost.

That arithmetic is illustrative. It is not a current market price, and the ₹78,000 is assumed rather than approved. The actual project may differ on both numbers, which is exactly why a quote presenting a single "after subsidy price" without showing the components is doing you no favours.

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Itemised proposal | Shows modules, inverter, structure, wiring, labour and exclusions separately |
| Official scheme application | Identifies the applicant and the current status |
| Vendor registration record | Confirms the installer through the official route |
| Module and cell documents | Supports the applicable domestic content check |
| Final invoice and commissioning record | Establishes installed equipment and contract amount |

The [vendor verification guide](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) explains how to check those records, and the [full scheme guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) covers eligibility and the application steps.

## What assistance does not cover

Applicants routinely budget for the panel and inverter and then meet costs nobody mentioned.

Structural work for an unsuitable roof, cable runs longer than standard, a connection upgrade from single to three phase, DISCOM application and meter charges, and any civil work are commonly outside a headline quote. So is ongoing cleaning and maintenance.

Ask for exclusions in writing. A quote that lists what is included but not what is excluded leaves the gap for you to discover after the contract is signed.

## Zero upfront offers are finance, not subsidy

A no upfront payment offer can be legitimate. It is a financing arrangement and should be assessed as one.

Establish who owns the equipment during and after the term, the total amount repayable across the full period rather than the monthly figure, what happens if you default or sell the property, who is responsible for service and repairs, what tariff and generation assumptions underlie any savings projection, and whether you can exit early and on what terms.

A lease or long term payment obligation attached to your roof is a significant commitment. The monthly number is the least informative part of it.

## Telling a real offer from a harvesting scam

The scheme is genuine. A substantial volume of advertising invoking it is not, and the tells are consistent.

- **Requests for an OTP.** No legitimate application needs your one time password. This is account takeover, not registration.
- **Advance processing or registration fees** payable to an individual account before any site visit.
- **Application through a social media link or an unofficial form** rather than the official portal.
- **Guaranteed approval**, guaranteed amounts, or a promised timeline. No installer controls the DISCOM decision.
- **Urgency**, such as a scheme closing in days, or a limited number of slots.
- **A vendor certificate sent as an image** rather than verifiable through the official route.
- **No written itemised quote**, or refusal to identify the exact inverter and module models.

Begin at the [official PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) for current government information, and verify the vendor through the official route. Do not send an OTP or bank details to any form promising instant approval.

## State support sits on top, and varies

Central assistance is only one layer. Some states operate their own support for residential rooftop solar, administered separately and on their own conditions, and some do not.

Because these change by order and differ widely, treat any state figure quoted in a sales conversation as unverified until you have seen it on an official state or DISCOM source. A vendor combining a central slab with an assumed state amount to produce an attractive net price is presenting two assumptions as one certainty.

Ask which specific state scheme is being claimed, under which order, and where it is published. If that cannot be answered, price the project on central assistance alone and treat anything further as an upside rather than a plan.

## After approval, keep the paperwork

Assistance is disbursed against a completed and verified installation, so the records created at commissioning are what close the process.

Keep the sanctioned application, the final itemised invoice, the commissioning record, the equipment serial numbers, and the DISCOM connection approval together. If disbursement is delayed, those documents are what an escalation rests on, and reassembling them months later is considerably harder than filing them at the time.

## What to do next

Confirm your DISCOM and sanctioned connection, get at least two itemised quotes that separate contract value from assumed assistance, verify each vendor through the official channel, and check the equipment documents for the exact models proposed.

Then decide on the arithmetic in front of you rather than on a headline. A properly quoted system with a clear balance to fund is a better outcome than an advertised free one.
