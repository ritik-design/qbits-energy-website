## The Six Categories, And Which Ones Have To Talk

| Category | What it does | How tightly it must integrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and simulation | Layout, shading, yield, single-line diagrams | Core. Everything downstream derives from it |
| Proposal and quoting | Customer document, subsidy, payback, e-signature | Must share the design record, not re-enter it |
| CRM and pipeline | Leads, follow-up, conversion tracking | API connection is sufficient |
| Procurement and inventory | Ordering against the approved bill of materials | Should receive the design, not a retyped list |
| Project execution | Site scheduling, installation progress, handover | Should receive the design |
| Monitoring | Post-commissioning performance and service | Standalone, connects to the installed base |

> **The distinction that saves money.** Integration is worth paying for where one system's output is another's input. It is not worth paying for where two systems merely need to exchange a record occasionally. Design and proposals sit firmly in the first case. CRM and monitoring sit in the second, which is why buying an all-in-one suite to get a mediocre CRM bundled with a good design tool is usually a bad trade.

The category most Indian teams underinvest in is not on the sales side at all. It is the handoff from approved proposal into procurement and execution. That is where a module specified as ALMM-listed becomes a different module at the warehouse, where the inverter ordered does not match the one in the single-line diagram, and where the as-built quietly stops matching the document the customer signed.

## What Is Genuinely India-Specific

Four requirements decide whether a global platform is usable here, and most global platforms carry none of them.

**ALMM compliance.** Subsidised residential work and a great deal of government-linked project work require modules from the approved list. A module database that does not know which entries are ALMM-listed pushes that check into a spreadsheet, and spreadsheets go stale. The failure mode is not theoretical: specifying an off-list module produces a rejection rather than a query.

**PM Surya Ghar subsidy bands.** The central subsidy, reaching ₹78,000 for 3 kW systems and layered with state top-ups, is banded rather than proportional. A tool applying a flat percentage will misquote it, and it will misquote it in the direction that loses the customer's trust when the actual disbursement arrives.

**DISCOM-level net metering.** India does not have one net metering rule, it has a rule per state utility. Sizing limits, settlement mechanics and export treatment all vary. A national assumption is wrong somewhere in the country almost by definition, and a team quoting across states needs the library rather than the average.

**IS code and CEA safety requirements.** Clearances, earthing, protection and documentation have to match Indian codes, and a design produced against IEC or NEC defaults will need rework at approval.

A platform that handles none of these is not cheaper, it has simply moved the work to your engineers and made it invisible on the invoice. Our [best solar design software India](/blog/solar-design-software-india/) guide covers which platforms carry these natively.

## Where Indian Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Overbought: all-in-one suites purchased before the pipeline justifies them.** A three-person residential installer does not need procurement modules and project execution tracking. They need accurate designs and credible proposals, produced fast. The suite gets bought, twenty percent of it gets used, and the annual renewal becomes an argument.

**Overbought: seats.** Licences are frequently bought for everyone who might occasionally look at a design. In most teams two or three people actually produce designs and proposals, and the rest need to view a finished document, which is not the same product.

**Underbought: the design-to-proposal link.** This is the reliable false economy. A capable design tool plus a separate quoting spreadsheet appears cheaper than an integrated platform, and it is, until you count the quotes that went out with a superseded layout or a stale tariff.

**Underbought: shading analysis that runs hourly.** Annual-average shading figures are close to useless for a customer conversation and actively misleading on obstructed roofs. Our [solar shading analysis software](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software/) guide covers why module-level hourly modelling matters.

**Underbought: anything that carries the approved design downstream.** Covered above, and worth repeating because it is the least visible and most expensive gap in the stack.

A subsidy quoted at the wrong band, a module that turns out not to be ALMM-listed, a tariff from the wrong DISCOM: each of these is cheap to prevent at design stage and expensive at every later stage.
