**Qbits can be a reasonable inverter to shortlist, but the public evidence does not support an unconditional “yes.”** A good choice is an exact model whose electrical limits fit the project and whose seller supplies the warranty, certificate, monitoring, compatibility and service documents that the public product sheet does not settle.

**Disclosure:** Qbits publishes this article and sells the products being evaluated. This is a document audit, not an independent long-term test or a customer-review study. We do not use Qbits' own marketing claims as proof of field reliability, service quality or customer outcomes.

> **The short version**
>
> - Current Qbits documents provide useful model-level electrical data for on-grid and hybrid families.
> - The QB-5KTLD sheet publishes two MPPTs, 5 kW rated output, IP66 and separate maximum and European efficiency figures.
> - The QBH-5KS48P sheet publishes a 48 V battery class, 5 kW rated off-grid output, IP66 and lead-acid or lithium support.
> - Those specifications are manufacturer statements. They are not independent reliability results.
> - Public material reviewed here does not establish complete warranty terms, exact battery compatibility, current exact-model certificate files, service outcomes or monitoring-account terms.
> - Download the exact datasheet, then close those gaps in writing before paying.

## What “good” means in this review

“Good” is not a single specification. It means the exact inverter is suitable, documented and supportable for one real project.

We use six checks:

1. **Correct operating type:** on-grid for a grid-connected design without claimed backup, or hybrid for a defined battery-backed design.
2. **Electrical fit:** the proposed module strings remain inside the inverter's voltage and current limits across expected site conditions.
3. **Useful output:** the rated output, phase and any backup output match the connection and essential loads.
4. **Complete documents:** the model code matches across the quote, datasheet, nameplate, invoice, certificate and warranty.
5. **Operable monitoring:** included hardware, app access, plant ownership and alerts are agreed before commissioning.
6. **Named support:** the buyer knows who commissions the unit and who handles a fault or warranty claim in the installation pincode.

This method deliberately excludes popularity, sales claims and brochure superlatives. A large installation count would not prove that the quoted model suits your array. An efficiency percentage would not prove a low field-failure rate. A service-network number would not prove response time at your address.

## What the public documents do not establish

The evidence gaps are the most important part of an honest Qbits inverter review.

The reviewed public material does not establish:

- comparative failure rates or expected service life;
- independently measured energy yield at Indian homes;
- verified customer satisfaction or service-response performance;
- complete warranty terms for the exact model and sale;
- current exact-model certificate files and their validity;
- compatibility with every module, battery, meter, logger or export-control device;
- what monitoring equipment is included in a particular quote;
- stock, price, installation quality or service availability at a given pincode.

This does not prove a product is poor. It means those questions remain open. A careful buyer turns each unknown into a document request rather than filling it with a positive or negative assumption.

## Check the on-grid electrical fit

For the QB-5KTLD example, the published 80 V to 550 V MPPT range, 550 V maximum DC input and 20 A per-MPPT current are design boundaries. They are not instructions to connect any number of panels.

Ask the installer to show, for each MPPT:

- the exact module make and model;
- modules in series and strings in parallel;
- cold-weather open-circuit voltage;
- hot-weather operating voltage;
- operating current and short-circuit current;
- the roof orientation and shading assigned to that MPPT;
- the DC isolator, surge protection, earthing and cable selection.

A 7.5 kW published maximum PV input on a 5 kW model also does not mean every 7.5 kW array is safe or productive. Current, voltage, roof direction, clipping, local conditions and design rules all matter. The [guide to reading solar inverter datasheets](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) explains how to compare these fields without treating one headline number as the answer.

An on-grid inverter should not be sold as a power-cut backup solution unless a separate documented backup architecture provides that function. If your main requirement is to run lights, fans, refrigeration or another load during outages, settle the backup design first.

## Check the hybrid battery and backup design

The QBH-5KS48P document states a 48 V battery class and names lead-acid or lithium. That is a starting point, not approval for every 48 V battery.

For a lithium proposal, obtain written confirmation of:

- exact battery brand and model;
- usable voltage range and current limits;
- Battery Management System (BMS) communication protocol;
- required firmware versions and communications cable;
- charge and discharge settings;
- what happens if BMS communication is lost;
- which company supports the inverter-battery pairing;
- whether either warranty excludes the proposed combination.

For lead-acid batteries, ask for the bank configuration, capacity, charge settings, ventilation requirements and maintenance responsibility.

The same discipline applies to backup performance. The QBH document publishes 5,000 W rated off-grid output for this model. That does not promise that every household load will start or run together. The installer must produce an essential-load schedule with running watts, motor-starting demand, allowed simultaneous loads and expected battery runtime.

The datasheet wording reviewed for the QBH family describes switching within **10 seconds**. Do not let that become “instant” or “milliseconds” in a sales conversation. If an uninterrupted supply is critical for a computer, medical device, server or control system, obtain the exact transfer behaviour and design a suitable power-quality solution around the real requirement.

## Get the warranty before comparing years

The reviewed Qbits datasheets use the phrase **“Expandable Warranty.”** That phrase does not establish the base term or what an extension covers.

Before payment, request one document tied to the exact model and invoice that states:

1. base warranty duration and start date;
2. extension duration, price, registration deadline and eligibility;
3. issuing legal entity and claim contact;
4. repair, replacement or refund remedy;
5. labour, travel, removal, reinstallation and freight responsibility;
6. turnaround process and any temporary replacement policy;
7. exclusions for installation, grid conditions, surge, water, pests, corrosion or communications;
8. required installer, commissioning and maintenance records;
9. whether replacement equipment receives a new or remaining term.

Do not rely on a verbal period or a number copied from a general catalogue. The [installation and product warranty guide](/blog/solar-installation-vs-product-warranty/) separates the responsible party, remedy, exclusions and claim route to record for competing quotations.

## Verify certificates and project approval separately

The reviewed QB-5KTLD family datasheet prints standards and a BIS registration number. That is not the same as having the current certificate file in hand.

Ask for the applicable document, then check:

- the certificate or registration number;
- issuing body and current validity;
- applicant or manufacturer name;
- exact model and suffix coverage;
- referenced standard and edition;
- whether the supplied nameplate matches the document.

Certification also does not automatically settle subsidy or interconnection eligibility. The registered-vendor route, module requirements, technical-feasibility approval, metering and DISCOM rules can be separate. Confirm the current project requirements with the relevant authority and installer before procurement.

## Confirm monitoring ownership, not just Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi on a datasheet does not tell you whether the logger is included, which app is used or who controls the plant account.

Put these items in the quote:

- logger, dongle, meter and current transformers included;
- internet method and any recurring charge;
- installer and owner account permissions;
- transfer process if the installer closes or changes;
- live power, daily energy, history and fault alerts available;
- data retention and export options;
- who can change remote settings or firmware;
- support route when the inverter produces energy but the app stops updating.

Have the owner account created on the buyer's email or mobile number during commissioning. Record the serial numbers and keep the setup details with the handover pack.

## Test the local service route before purchase

A service page can identify a route, but it cannot prove the result of a future case. Use the [Qbits authorised service-partner page](/authorized-service-partners/) to find a starting point, then verify the local scope directly.

Ask the seller or service contact:

- Do you handle this exact model in my pincode?
- Who performs commissioning and retains the record?
- What evidence is required to open a case?
- Which faults can be diagnosed remotely?
- Are labour, travel and freight chargeable?
- Where is the unit sent if a workshop repair is needed?
- Who owns the case when the installer and manufacturer disagree?

Keep the answers in writing. This is more useful than a nationwide network number because it assigns responsibility for your installation.

## Who should shortlist Qbits, and who should pause

**Shortlist Qbits** when an exact current model fits the array or backup design, the quote identifies every required accessory, and the seller provides the governing warranty, certificate and local support route in writing.

**Pause** when the proposal shows only a brand and kW number, uses an on-grid model to promise backup, assumes any 48 V battery will work, offers a warranty period without terms, or presents a logo as proof of exact-model certification.

Also pause if the seller refuses to show the string calculation or essential-load schedule. Those are not optional details. They are the design evidence that connects an inverter specification to your roof and appliances.
