**Solar technician training for the inverter side of a plant needs four things: a written list of skills, a supervised ladder from watching to doing, a sign-off by a licensed supervisor, and a record sheet per person.** This guide gives a dealer all four, tied to the national qualification for solar installers. It is a training plan, not a licence. A technician who passes your sign-off is still not a permit holder. The work must still run under the licensed contractor, supervisor and permit structure that your state sets.

This is general information as of **30 October 2026**, not legal or safety advice. Every number below that comes from a document names the document. Numbers we could not trace to a document are left out, and the last section lists them.

> **Short version**
> - The external benchmark is the Skill Council for Green Jobs (SCGJ) qualification pack **SGJ/Q0101, Solar PV Installer (Suryamitra)**, at level 4 of the National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF). Its model curriculum is 420 hours, including 60 hours on the job. The Suryamitra course page of the National Institute of Solar Energy (NISE) describes 600 hours. The two figures differ, and we did not find a source that reconciles them.
> - On our reading of the central safety regulations, a person who works on or assists with electrical work has to be designated. Designation needs a certificate of competency or a work permit. Plan training so that unpermitted trainees watch and assist under supervision. They must not become the hands on a live circuit.
> - Torque, isolation order, test method, test voltage and pass values come from the inverter brand's manual or the project specification. Teach people where to find them. Do not teach them numbers from memory or from a blog.
> - Brands may or may not run installer training. Ask each one in writing. Never assume.

## What the national qualification sets as the benchmark

You do not have to invent a syllabus. India already has one, and it gives you a defensible yardstick for what an installer should be able to do.

We read two SCGJ documents on 30 October 2026. One is the model curriculum for SGJ/Q0101 version 3.0, hosted on the National Qualification Register. The other is SCGJ's job-roles list for solar energy, which lists version 4.0 of the same pack. We also re-read NISE's [Suryamitra Skill Development Programme page](https://suryamitra.nise.res.in/).

| Role | Code | NSQF level | Training hours stated | Source and date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solar PV Installer (Suryamitra) | SGJ/Q0101 | 4 | 420 (version 3.0 curriculum: 360 hours of mandatory modules, including a 60-hour employability module, plus 60 hours of on-the-job training) | Model curriculum v3.0; SCGJ job-roles list, v4.0 |
| Solar PV Installer, Electrical | SGJ/Q0102 | 4 | 390 | SCGJ job-roles list, v4.0 |
| Solar PV Installation Helper | SGJ/Q0111 | 2 | 270 | SCGJ job-roles list, v4.0 |
| Rooftop Solar Grid Junior Engineer | SGJ/Q0106 | 5 | 540 | SCGJ job-roles list, v4.0 |

Four things in those documents matter to a dealer:

- **The versions differ.** The model curriculum we read says it was last reviewed on 27 May 2021 and is valid up to 26 May 2024. The job-roles list names version 4.0 of the pack and shows validity to 30 May 2027. We did not open the version 4.0 curriculum, so the module hours below are from version 3.0 only.
- **Entry rules differ by version.** Version 3.0 lists several routes, including 10th pass plus an Industrial Training Institute (ITI) course, a three-year diploma, or 12th pass with science. The minimum job entry age is 16. The 2025 list gives 12th pass (science) or equivalents and a minimum age of 18 for the installer role. Read the current document before you advertise a vacancy against it.
- **NISE describes a different course.** The NISE page calls its programme "a 600 hours residential skill development program following the Qualification Pack of SCG/Q0101". SCGJ's own documents write the code as SGJ/Q0101, so the NISE spelling looks like a typo, but we cannot confirm that. The page restricts entry to ITI or diploma holders in listed trades and says degree holders are "strictly not eligible". It states no fee and no NSQF level.
- **Certificates come from SCGJ.** NISE says certificates are issued by the Skill Council for Green Jobs after assessment. A dealer's internal sign-off is not that certificate, and should never be described as one.

The curriculum has eleven technical modules of 15 or 30 hours each, and the ones that matter for inverter work are these.

| 6. Drawings and material handling | 30 | Read drawings and the bill of materials, check what arrives on site against it |
| 7. Installing electrical components | 30 | Install inverter, junction boxes, meters, cables and conduits; DC wiring; earthing and grounding; label and secure cables |

The equipment list for the testing module names a clamp meter, multimeter, megger, earth tester, phase sequence meter and pyranometer. It also names a 1 kWp PV system for practice, which is a useful hint for a small dealer. A training rig can be one small array and one inverter that is never connected to a customer.

## Who may do what while a technician is still training

Training does not change who is allowed to do electrical work. Two parts of the Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2023, set the frame. We read them from the gazette of 8 June 2023, through an archived copy because the live CEA link did not open.

| Provision | What it says (paraphrased) | What it means for training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulation 31(1) | Installation work on a consumer's premises must be done by a licensed electrical contractor, under direct supervision of a certificate-of-competency holder, by a person holding a permit | The [licence guide](/blog/electrical-contractor-licence-solar-installers/) covers the three layers and eight state texts. Your trainee is not a layer |
| Regulation 3(1) and (3) | A contractor must designate the people who carry out work on electrical lines and apparatus, keep a register, and may designate only someone who holds a certificate of competency or work permit and is entered in the register | A trainee without a permit cannot be on that register |
| Regulation 21(3) | No person may operate or maintain an electrical plant, line or apparatus, "and no person shall assist such person on such work", unless designated and following the Schedule I precautions | Assisting is covered by the wording, not only doing |

**Our reading, not a ruling.** These provisions are written for electrical plant, lines and apparatus in general, and the text never says "solar". How your state reads them for a rooftop plant is a question for your licensing board or inspectorate. The licence guide gives you the seven written questions to ask. Until you have a written answer, design the training plan cautiously. Unpermitted trainees observe, carry, label, mount structures, prepare cables and record readings that a permit holder takes. They do not make or break live connections.

As our [vendor registration guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vendor-registration-installers/) reads it, the PM Surya Ghar vendor declaration asks a firm to confirm at least three people trained for rooftop installation work. We could not re-open the MNRE page on 30 October 2026, so treat the figure as "check the current declaration". It shows why a written training record is useful beyond your own quality control. It is evidence you can put in front of an authority, a customer or an inspector.

## The competency matrix

The competency matrix is the core training document. For each skill, the technician must show a behaviour, and the supervisor must see evidence. Where a source states a method, we name it. Where none does, the "source of the method" column says so, and the answer comes from the brand's manual or your project specification.

| # | Skill | The technician can | Evidence the supervisor keeps | Source of the method | QP module |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Site survey | Record roof or ground condition, shading, inverter location, cable route, earthing options, power distribution company (DISCOM) and meter details | Completed survey form with photos | Your own form; no national form | 5 |
| 2 | Reading drawings and the bill of materials (BOM) | Match single line diagram, layout and BOM to what arrives; flag a mismatch before installing | BOM check signed against delivery | Project drawings | 6 |
| 3 | Inverter mounting | Mount to the manual's orientation, clearances and fixing | Photo with clearances against the manual page | Brand manual for the exact model | 4, 7 |
| 4 | DC wiring and connectors | Pair connectors correctly, label strings, check polarity | String label sheet, photos of each pair | Brand manual (for example, one maker's manual says a DC cable must always have the same polarity as its connector, and wrongly assembled connectors must be reassembled) | 7 |
| 5 | AC wiring and terminations | Strip, crimp, terminate and torque to the manual, in the right phase order | Torque record per terminal | Brand manual torque table | 7 |
| 6 | Earthing, bonding and surge protection device (SPD) | Bond the inverter frame with the specified conductor, check SPD type and indicator, record earth continuity | Earth bond photo, instrument reading, SPD model | CEA reg 121 (see the [CEIG guide](/blog/ceig-inspection-solar-plant-inverter-checks/)); design | 7 |
| 7 | String check before connection | Measure string open-circuit voltage and check it is within the inverter's maximum input voltage; check polarity | String voltage and polarity table | Brand manual; project design | 8 |

| 10 | Commissioning sequence | Follow the approved sequence, start up, read AC and DC values per string, record anomalies | Signed commissioning sheet with values | Brand manual; project test plan; see the [commissioning checklist](/blog/solar-inverter-commissioning-in-india/) | 8 |
| 11 | Settings and grid profile | Apply the approved grid profile and protection settings, record them, never widen limits to stop a trip | Settings export or photos with date and firmware | Approved schedule; brand manual | 8 |
| 12 | Grid-sync and anti-islanding checks | Describe what the function does, take part in the approved verification and record it | Witnessed record or certificate reference | Approved procedure; see the [anti-islanding guide](/blog/anti-islanding-protection-solar-inverters/) | 8 |
| 13 | Monitoring set-up | Bind the right inverter to the right site, set time zone, check alarms, transfer admin access to the owner | Screenshot, account owner, logger and SIM details | Platform and brand instructions | 8, 11 |
| 14 | Fault finding with alarm codes | Read the code, time and state, place the fault in the right domain, apply the safe first move | Fault log with code, time, state, action | Brand manual defines the code; see the [troubleshooting guide](/blog/solar-inverter-troubleshooting/) | 9 |
| 15 | Handover pack | Assemble and explain the pack, start-up and shutdown, who to call | Indexed pack, owner acknowledgement | Commissioning guide's handover index | 11 |
| 16 | Safety and PPE | Choose, check and wear the right PPE; work at height safely; give first aid; stop work when a limit is hit | PPE issue log, induction record | CEA reg 21(2); curriculum module 10 | 10 |

Two cautions apply to the whole table. First, rows 7 to 12 involve live circuits, so they are for permit holders. A trainee's part is to watch, hold the record sheet and read back values. Second, a competency is not "can recite". It is "can do it correctly, on the real model, with the right document open".

## Torque and terminations: why you teach the table, not the number

Loose and over-tightened terminations are an easy thing to train badly, because every trainee remembers one number and uses it everywhere. The manuals say otherwise. In the SMA Sunny Tripower CORE1 operating manual (STP 50-40 and STP 50-41, document STP50-4x-BE-en-23, version 2.3, which we downloaded on 30 October 2026), the torque table lists a different value for each fastener:

| Fastener in that manual | Torque stated |
| --- | --- |
| SUNCLIX DC connector swivel nut | 2 Nm |
| Swivel nut for the M32 cable gland | 5 Nm |
| Enclosure lid screws | 6 Nm |
| Screws for attaching feet or profile rails | 16 Nm |
| AC terminal screws, 35 mm² to 95 mm² conductor | 20 Nm |
| AC terminal screws, 120 mm² conductor | 30 Nm |
| Swivel nut for the M63 cable gland | 33 Nm |

The range runs from 2 Nm to 33 Nm in one product. So the training exercise is simple. Put the manual and a torque screwdriver or wrench in the trainee's hands. Ask them to find and tighten five named fasteners, and to write the value and the page number on the record. These figures belong to that SMA model. They are not values for any other inverter.

## Isolation, PPE and lock-out basics

Three sources agree on the shape of safe isolation, and none of them is a substitute for your brand's manual.

**The regulation.** CEA regulation 21(1) says that before any conductor or apparatus is handled, precautions must be taken, by earthing or other suitable means, to discharge it and to prevent it from being accidentally or inadvertently charged while people work on it. Schedule I, Part-II covers shutdown work or testing on supply lines and apparatus. Its sequence transfers well as a principle: identify the hazards (it names electrocution, flash over, falls, falling objects, tool failure and fire), brief the whole gang, obtain a permit-to-work, isolate from all sources, earth, and then "positively confirm by suitable means" that the equipment is totally dead. It also says a lone worker shall never be allowed to work on electrical equipment or while testing. The supervisor must not leave the spot while work at height or testing is in progress. Part-II is written for supply lines and apparatus, so it is not a rooftop-PV procedure word for word.

**A maker's manual.** Section 9 of the SMA manual above is titled "Disconnecting the Inverter from Voltage Sources" and says to follow the prescribed sequence. In that product the order is:

1. Disconnect the AC miniature circuit breaker and secure it against reconnection.
2. Turn the DC load-break switch to O, and secure it with a padlock if the country requires it.
3. Wait until the LEDs go out.
4. Use a current clamp to confirm no current in the DC cables.
5. Release the DC connectors using insulated gloves and tools.
6. Confirm no voltage at the DC inputs and on the AC terminal block.

The manual also warns that measuring devices must have a DC input range of 1000 V or higher. That is specific to that product. Your brand's manual may differ in order, wait time and meter rating, and it is the document that controls.

**The DC fact every trainee must hold.** Modules produce voltage whenever light falls on them, so switching off the AC side does not make the DC side safe. Our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/solar-inverter-troubleshooting/) states the rule that follows: never unplug a DC connector under load.

### PPE and lock-out

CEA regulation 21(2) lists what a person working on electrical equipment must be provided with. The list is rubber gloves and safety footwear suitable for the working voltage, safety belts for working at height, a non-conductive ladder, earthing devices of the appropriate class, a helmet, a line tester or voltage detector, hand tools, and protection from arc flash. All must meet "the relevant standards" and be kept in sound condition. The curriculum's equipment lists for the practical modules add safety goggles, ear plugs, a reflective jacket and a nose mask.

Neither document uses the words "lock-out tag-out" for rooftop PV. The SMA manual's "secure against reconnection" and padlock instruction is the nearest maker wording we opened. A practical firm-level version uses a personal padlock and a tag on the AC breaker and the DC switch. One key is held by the person doing the work, and only that person removes the lock. That is our suggestion for training discipline, not a quoted standard.

## The 5-day onboarding plan

The 5-day onboarding plan is a suggested shape, not a standard. Adjust it to your jobs, but keep the principle: no live work in week one.

| Day | Focus | The new technician does | Supervisor checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Safety and orientation | Induction, PPE fitting and check, hazard briefing, first aid basics, what a permit holder does and what a trainee does not | Signed induction record; PPE issue log |
| 2 | Documents | Open a real manual and datasheet for one model; find the torque table, clearances, isolation procedure, alarm list; read a single line diagram | Can find five named items in the manual in ten minutes |
| 3 | Mounting and mechanical | Mount an inverter on the training rig to the manual's clearances; route and label cables; prepare a cable end | Clearance photo; label sheet |
| 4 | Terminations and records | Terminate and torque on a de-energised rig; fill the torque record; complete a string label sheet | Torque record matches the manual page |
| 5 | Watch a commissioning | Shadow a licensed team's commissioning from outside the live zone; hold the record sheet; write the day's settings and values | Record sheet complete; ten-question test (below) |

## The 30-day onboarding plan

| Week | Stage | Scope | Gate at the end of the week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Observe | The 5-day plan above | Test passed; induction signed |
| 2 | Assist | Carry, mount structures, prepare and label cables, and keep the job file on two or three real installs, always beside a permit holder | Supervisor's notes on each job; no unsafe-act reports |
| 3 | Do under supervision | Terminate and torque on de-energised circuits, with every termination checked by the supervisor; take part in pre-energisation inspection | Torque and photo records complete on every job |
| 4 | Support commissioning and handover | Record all values during commissioning, assemble the handover pack, run a fault-finding drill from alarm logs | Day-30 review: matrix scored, written test repeated, decision on next stage |

Our suggestion for a minimum evidence base before a day-30 review: at least three real commissionings observed and recorded, and one handover pack assembled and checked. That count is ours. No document we read sets one.

An outside course is a different scale. SGJ/Q0101 runs 420 hours, and the NISE Suryamitra course is described as 600 hours and residential. Neither is a four-week programme. An in-house plan builds on a course or an electrician's licensed background. It does not replace them.

## The sign-off checklist

Use four levels per skill, and have the licensed supervisor initial and date each.

| Level | Meaning | Who may do the task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Not yet seen | Nobody |
| 1 | Observed | Trainee watches |
| 2 | Assisted | Trainee helps, a permit holder does the work |
| 3 | Performed under supervision | Trainee does it with the supervisor present and checking, within the limits of permits and your state's reading |
| 4 | Independent with checks | Trainee does it and a supervisor reviews the record afterwards |

Before moving a person to level 3 or 4 on any row of the matrix, confirm:

- [ ] They found the right manual page and quoted the value, not a remembered figure.
- [ ] They isolated and proved dead in the order the manual gives, and the supervisor watched.
- [ ] Their record shows the instrument, its calibration date, the reading and where the acceptance value came from.
- [ ] They have used the PPE the task needs and can say what to do if a limit is hit.
- [ ] They know the stop and escalate list below and can give an example of each item.
- [ ] They passed the written test with no wrong answer on a safety item.

The sign-off is your firm's internal record. It is not a state certificate of competency, a permit, or an SCGJ certificate, and the technician's card or letter should say so.

## The stop and escalate list

Teach this as a hard rule. If any of these happens, the technician stops, makes the area safe without touching live parts, and calls the supervisor.

| Situation | Why it stops work | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smoke, burning smell, arcing, scorch marks, or water inside an enclosure | Active fault or hazard | [Troubleshooting guide](/blog/solar-inverter-troubleshooting/) |
| Ground fault alarm or low insulation reading | One maker's manual warns not to connect PV strings with ground faults to the inverter, and that parts of the system may still be live | SMA manual, safety section |
| String open-circuit voltage above the inverter's maximum input voltage | The same manual says that if it exceeds the limit, no strings are connected and the design is checked | SMA manual |
| A damaged or loose DC connector, or exposed conductor | The manual treats it as a danger to life | SMA manual, section 9 |
| The instrument is not rated for the circuit | An overvoltage can damage the meter and make its enclosure live | SMA manual, section 9 |
| The drawing and the installed system disagree | Resolve or record the deviation before energisation | [Commissioning guide](/blog/solar-inverter-commissioning-in-india/) |
| Anyone asks to widen voltage, frequency or protection limits to stop a trip | Record the event and investigate; do not change the limits | Commissioning guide |
| Unsure which grid profile applies | The manual says to contact the grid operator if the country data set or directives are unclear | SMA manual |
| Working alone, or the supervisor has left during testing or work at height | Schedule I, Part-II forbids lone working and says the supervisor must stay at the spot | CEA Schedule I, Part-II |
| A requirement from the DISCOM or inspector is unclear | Ask the authority in writing | [CEIG guide](/blog/ceig-inspection-solar-plant-inverter-checks/) |

## Ten test questions, with answers

Run these on day 5 and again at day 30. Where an answer depends on a manual, the answer names the principle, and the trainee must also show the page.

1. **The AC breaker is off. Is the DC side safe?** No. Modules produce voltage whenever light falls on them, so DC is live until isolated at the DC switch and proved dead.
2. **What order do you isolate an inverter in?** The order in the brand's manual. In the SMA manual we read, it is AC breaker off and secured, DC switch to O, wait, check there is no current in the DC cables, then prove no voltage at DC and AC. Other brands may differ.
3. **Where do you find the torque for an AC terminal?** In the manual for that exact model. In the SMA manual it is 20 Nm for 35 to 95 mm² conductors and 30 Nm for 120 mm². It is never a single number for the whole inverter.
4. **A string measures a higher open-circuit voltage than the inverter's maximum input voltage. What do you do?** Do not connect it, stop, and tell the supervisor. The SMA manual says the open-circuit voltage on the coldest day must never exceed the maximum input voltage.
5. **A DC connector has the wrong polarity on its cable. What do you do?** Reassemble it so the cable and the connector have the same polarity, as that manual says, and never force a mismatched pair together.
6. **Who may carry out installation work on a customer's premises?** An electrical contractor licensed by the State Government, under the direct supervision of a certificate-of-competency holder, by a person holding a permit (regulation 31(1)).
7. **The inverter shows a ground-fault alarm at first start-up. What do you do?** Stop. Do not keep resetting. Do not touch the frame or substructure. Escalate to the supervisor.
8. **A customer asks you to raise the grid voltage trip limit to stop nuisance trips. What do you say?** No. Record the event and the time, then investigate the grid, wiring and settings against the approved schedule.
9. **Name six items in a handover pack.** For example: equipment schedule with serial numbers, as-built single line diagram, signed commissioning report, settings record, monitoring access transferred to the owner, warranty registration, and start-up and shutdown instructions.
10. **What must you record next to a test reading, besides the number?** The circuit tested, the instrument and its calibration status, the method, and where the acceptance value came from.

## How to use the inverter brand's manuals and training

The manuals are the best free training material you have, and the brand is the only party that can tell you what its product requires.

**What to do with a manual.** Download the manual for the exact model and version. Put its isolation section, torque table, clearance diagram, alarm list and commissioning section in the trainee's folder. Note the version and date on your record sheet. If a new firmware or manual revision arrives, retrain on what changed.

**What to ask the brand, in writing.** The questions below decide what you can teach and what you must send to the brand. Ask every brand on your list. Do not assume a brand runs training or offers certification. Some may not, and the answer for each brand is whatever it gives you in writing, with a date and a name.

| Ask the brand | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which manual and version applies to the model and firmware we will install | You train from the right document |
| Do you run installer training, and if so what does it cover, how long is it, in which language, and is it online or on site | Avoid assuming it exists |
| Is there a certificate or record of attendance, and what does it say it does and does not authorise | Prevents your staff overstating it |
| What does the brand's commissioning checklist ask for, and can we use it as our record | Aligns your sheet with the brand's |
| Who can we call for a commissioning question, in which hours, and through what channel | Defines your escalation route |
| What evidence will you ask for if a unit is later claimed | Shapes what your technicians record, see the [dealer service-claim guide](/blog/dealer-inverter-service-claim-process/) |
| Is there a charge for training or support | Budget |

If Qbits is one of the brands you assess, put the same questions to it and rely only on what you receive in writing. This guide makes no statement about what training or technical support Qbits provides.
