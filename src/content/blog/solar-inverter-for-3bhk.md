## Step-by-Step Load Calculation for a 3 BHK Home

Rather than relying on rules of thumb, the correct approach is to calculate the sanctioned load, derive the peak simultaneous demand, apply a diversity factor, and size the inverter with headroom for future additions. The following five-step process mirrors the methodology used by DISCOM-empanelled solar installers.

The [solar inverter sizing guide](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) on this blog covers the general principles; this section adapts them specifically for a 3 BHK scenario.

**Step 1: List every electrical load with its wattage.**
Use the appliance table in §1 above as a template. Pull the rated wattage from the appliance nameplate or the ISI/BEE energy label. For inverter ACs, use the rated cooling capacity in watts, typically 900–1,500 W for a 1-ton unit and 1,200–2,000 W for a 1.5-ton unit.

**Step 2: Identify which loads run simultaneously during daytime.**
Not every appliance runs at once. A realistic simultaneous load for a 3 BHK occupied by a family with children at school during the day might be: both ACs off, refrigerator running, one fan per room (3 rooms), TV and laptop, totalling roughly 1,200 W. On a weekend afternoon with both ACs on and geyser running briefly: ~6,000 W.

**Step 3: Apply a diversity factor of 0.7–0.8.**
Installers use a diversity factor to account for the statistical improbability that every load peaks simultaneously. For a residential 3 BHK, the industry standard is 0.75. Applied to the 6,200 W theoretical peak: 6,200 × 0.75 = **4,650 W effective design load**.

**Step 4: Add 20% headroom for future loads.**
AC compressor surge current, new appliances, and degradation in panel output over 10 years all erode the initial headroom. Adding 20%: 4,650 × 1.2 = **5,580 W**: confirming a 5 kW inverter as the right size.

**Step 5: Confirm with daily energy consumption.**
At 5 kW system output and an average of 4.5 peak sun hours per day in Delhi ([peak sun hours](/glossary/peak-sun-hours/) vary from 3.5 in Kolkata to 5.5 in Jodhpur), the system generates: 5 × 4.5 = **22.5 kWh/day** or approximately **675 kWh/month**: comfortably covering the 17–18 kWh daily consumption with surplus for grid export.

## The 3BHK Inverter Decision Framework: On-Grid or Hybrid?

This is the question every 3 BHK homeowner faces after settling on 5 kW: should you spend ₹2.8–3.5 Lakh on a 5 kW on-grid system, or ₹4.2–5 Lakh on a 5 kW hybrid system with a 5–10 kWh battery? The answer is not a matter of preference, it follows logically from three measurable inputs.

### The 3BHK Inverter Decision Framework

1. **Daily power cut duration**: measure the average cut duration in your area over the past three months. Fewer than 2 hours per day: on-grid wins on payback. More than 4 hours per day: hybrid is justified. Between 2–4 hours: run the payback calculation in Step 2 below before deciding.

2. **Critical load requirement**: identify which loads must run through a power cut. Ceiling fans, LED lights, refrigerator, and Wi-Fi are "essential loads" at roughly 600–800 W. Both ACs running through a cut require 3–4 kW of battery capacity minimum. A 5 kWh LFP battery at 80% DoD delivers 4 kWh, sufficient for 4–5 hours of essential loads or 1.5–2 hours of both ACs. For the full methodology behind sizing that battery bank correctly, see [how to size a battery for a hybrid solar inverter](/blog/battery-sizing-hybrid-solar/); SurgePV's [battery and energy storage sizing hub](https://surgepv.com/hub/energy-storage/battery-sizing/) covers the underlying engineering in more depth for readers comparing chemistries and bank configurations.

3. **Payback delta**: the hybrid premium (typically ₹1.2–1.5 Lakh more than an equivalent on-grid system) needs to be recovered through avoided backup costs (DG fuel, UPS batteries) or avoided electricity charges during cuts. If your current DG or UPS cost exceeds ₹1,500/month, the hybrid payback delta closes in 6–8 years. If you have no power cut and no backup cost, the on-grid system pays back 2–3 years faster.

Apply all three factors sequentially. Families in Bangalore, Chennai, Pune, and most Delhi NCR locations will almost always land on on-grid. Families in Lucknow, Patna, Bhubaneswar, and rural Maharashtra will nearly always find hybrid compelling.

## PM Surya Ghar Subsidy for a 3 BHK: What You Actually Get

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is the largest residential rooftop solar subsidy programme India has ever launched, and a 3 BHK with a 5 kW system qualifies for a meaningful portion of it. Understanding the exact subsidy structure prevents the common mistake of assuming the entire 5 kW attracts the higher subsidy rate.

> **₹78,000.** The maximum central subsidy available under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for a 3 kW residential rooftop system - ₹30,000/kW for the first 2 kW (₹60,000) plus ₹18,000 for the third kW. This is the ceiling on central government support regardless of how large a system you install. *Source - [PM Surya Ghar Portal, MNRE](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/), 2026.*

Here is how the subsidy structure applies to a 3 BHK installing a 5 kW system:

| System Slab | Rate | Subsidy Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 2 kW | ₹30,000 per kW | ₹60,000 |
| 2 kW to 3 kW | ₹18,000 per kW | ₹18,000 |
| Above 3 kW (the remaining 2 kW) | ₹0 (no central subsidy) | ₹0 |
| **Total central subsidy on a 5 kW system** | - | **₹78,000** |

For many families, the practical implication is that the incremental cost of going from 3 kW to 5 kW (typically ₹80,000–1 Lakh) receives no central subsidy. However, that extra 2 kW adds roughly 150–200 kWh per month of generation, translating to ₹1,200–1,800 in monthly bill savings at ₹8/unit. The additional investment pays back in 4–5 years even without subsidy.

Several states add their own subsidy on top of the central amount. Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Maharashtra have historically offered ₹10,000–30,000 per kW on top of MNRE support. Check the [PM Surya Ghar complete guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/) for the latest state-specific amounts and application steps.

The subsidy is credited directly to the homeowner's bank account after the DISCOM ([DISCOM](/glossary/discom/) stands for Distribution Company, the utility managing your connection) verifies the installed system against the empanelled vendor's submission. The process typically takes 30–60 days post-commissioning.

**Pros of On-Grid**
- ✓ Lowest cost per kW of all solar configurations
- ✓ Eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy in full
- ✓ Net metering earns credit for every exported unit
- ✓ No battery degradation or replacement cost over the 25-year panel life

**Cons of On-Grid**
- ✗ Zero power during grid outages, mandated by anti-islanding protection
- ✗ Cannot be retrofitted to battery backup without replacing the inverter or adding a separate AC-coupled unit
- ✗ Poor fit for areas with daily or long power cuts

The [hybrid inverter](/glossary/hybrid-inverter/) option adds complexity but is the right engineering answer when grid reliability is low. For 3 BHK homes that want battery capability later without replacing the inverter now, a hybrid inverter installed without a battery is a valid intermediate strategy, you pay for the hybrid unit upfront but defer the battery cost until the grid situation demands it.

## Roof Space Requirement for a 5 kW Solar System on a 3 BHK

One of the most common anxieties among 3 BHK homeowners is whether the rooftop has enough shadow-free area. The calculation is straightforward once you know the panel dimensions.

- **Standard 545 Wp monocrystalline panel dimensions**: 2.28 m × 1.13 m = 2.58 m² per panel
- **Panels required for 5 kW**: 5,000 Wp ÷ 545 Wp = **9.2 panels → round up to 10 panels**
- **Total panel area**: 10 × 2.58 m² = **25.8 m² = ~278 sq ft**
- **Add inter-row clearance for maintenance and shading prevention (15%)**: 278 × 1.15 = **320 sq ft**
- **Add edge setback from parapet wall (typically 1 m on all sides for a 3 BHK terrace)**: adds approximately 30–40 sq ft of perimeter exclusion
- **Practical roof requirement**: **340–360 sq ft of usable, shadow-free roof area**

Most 3 BHK homes in India have a flat terrace of 600–900 sq ft. Shadow-free area depends on the water tank position, parapet wall height, and staircase head. A quick [shading analysis](/glossary/shading-analysis/) using a solar pathfinder or a free tool like PVGIS confirms available area before purchase. For terraces with complex shading from adjacent buildings or overhead tanks, a professional site survey and land feasibility assessment quantifies usable roof area more precisely than a manual measurement.

If shadow-free area is limited to 250–300 sq ft (for instance, due to a large overhead tank or adjacent building shadow), consider using higher-efficiency panels (≥ 22% module efficiency) at 580–605 Wp each, which can achieve 5 kW with 9 panels and save 15–20 sq ft.

- **Roof orientation**: South-facing is optimal; east or west-facing loses 10–15% annual generation; north-facing loses 20–25% and is generally not viable.
- **Tilt angle**: 10–15° is standard for flat roofs in India; matches latitude for most peninsular cities.
- **Panel type**: Monocrystalline PERC or TOPCon, both ALMM-listed variants are suitable; TOPCon offers marginally better low-light performance for cloudy months in coastal cities.
- **Mounting structure**: Hot-dip galvanised GI is standard; aluminium frames weigh less but cost slightly more. Ensure the terrace slab is rated for 15–20 kg/m² additional load, most RCC rooftops in India exceed this capacity by a large margin.

## Common Mistakes 3 BHK Homeowners Make When Buying Solar

Buying a solar inverter is a decision that stays with the home for 12–25 years. Several costly mistakes appear repeatedly in post-purchase reviews, installer feedback, and DISCOM complaint registers.

**Undersizing the inverter to save ₹10,000–15,000 upfront.** A 3 kW inverter for a 3 BHK with two ACs means the system curtails generation whenever the load exceeds 3 kW, which happens daily during summer afternoons. The "savings" disappear within 18 months in lost generation. The [3 kW vs 5 kW vs 10 kW inverter comparison](/blog/3kw-vs-5kw-vs-10kw-solar-inverters/) demonstrates this clearly with year-on-year generation data.

**Choosing an inverter without ALMM listing.** If your inverter is not on the MNRE ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) list, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy application will be rejected by the DISCOM. Always verify the model is ALMM-listed before purchase. The [ALMM list guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains how to check.

**Ignoring the warranty fine print.** A "10-year warranty" that covers only defects in manufacturing and excludes weather damage, surge events, and component depreciation is worth very little in practice. Ask the vendor specifically: "Is this a full unit replacement warranty or a parts-only warranty?" A 12-year full replacement warranty means the manufacturer ships a new unit to your door within 72 hours of a confirmed fault, no labour charge, no argument.

**Not accounting for shading from new construction.** A neighbouring building under construction today may cast a shadow on your south-facing roof within two years. Install panels with east-west bias or choose a dual-[MPPT](/glossary/mppt/) inverter that handles partial shading without the entire string dropping in output.

**Skipping the net metering application.** An on-grid inverter without a net meter is technically off-grid, it cannot feed surplus power to the grid, so the system shuts down when household consumption falls below generation. Net metering is mandatory for an on-grid system to function correctly and for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy to remain valid. The [net metering application guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) covers the step-by-step process for each DISCOM.

**Accepting a non-empanelled vendor.** PM Surya Ghar subsidy requires the installation to be performed by a DISCOM-empanelled vendor. Installing through an un-empanelled contractor (even with ALMM-listed equipment) voids the subsidy claim. Verifying installer credentials is one of the [eight verification steps for finding a reliable solar installer](/blog/find-reliable-solar-installer/) that every buyer should complete before signing. Check the national portal before signing any contract, and run the final quotation against the [12-point solar quotation checklist](/blog/solar-quotation-checklist/) to confirm every line item is accounted for.
