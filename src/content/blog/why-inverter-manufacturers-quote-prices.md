Qbits publishes inverter specifications, but it does not publish a per-unit price list. An EPC buyer needs a dated written quotation. It must fix the exact model, quantity, included hardware, delivery basis, tax treatment, documents, warranty reference, exclusions, and validity before two prices are comparable.

That does not make quote-based pricing automatically better. A public list reduces friction and gives buyers a quick budget reference. A vague quotation can be worse than a price list. The quotation earns its value only when it defines exactly what the buyer will receive.

> **Short version**
> - Start with an exact model, not a capacity label such as "5 kW inverter".
> - State the quantity, destination, required accessories, and supply boundary.
> - Ask for tax and freight treatment as separate lines.
> - Request the current written warranty document instead of relying on a headline term.
> - Mark any missing comparison field as **clarification required**.
> - Compare the final figure only after both quotations describe the same scope.

## What a public price list can and cannot tell an EPC

**A price list is useful for orientation, while a quotation should control a purchase decision.** The two documents solve different problems.

| Document | What it does well | What it usually cannot settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public price list | Fast budget reference, visible catalogue, simple model-to-price lookup | Project quantity, destination, freight basis, accessories, document scope, current validity |
| Dated quotation | Fixes a defined supply scope for a named buyer and project | Broader market benchmark unless the buyer obtains comparable alternatives |
| Purchase order | Records what the buyer is ordering | Missing or ambiguous terms inherited from the quotation |
| Written warranty document | Defines the controlling warranty terms | Price, delivery scope, or technical suitability |

The practical trade-off is transparency versus specificity. Publishing a list would make the first budgeting step easier. Using quotations lets each enquiry be tied to a model and scope, but it also places more responsibility on the buyer to demand a complete document.

## Why one capacity label does not define one product

**A request for a "5 kW inverter" is not precise enough to price or procure.** Capacity alone does not identify topology, phase, MPPT arrangement, DC limits, communication hardware, or the exact model.

Two products can share a capacity label and still serve different designs. An on-grid model and a hybrid model do not have the same power path. Single-phase and three-phase units are not substitutes. Even within one family, tracker count or communication options may vary by model.

Begin with the [current Qbits datasheet library](/download-datasheets/) and record the exact model identifier. If it is not yet selected, send the design inputs and ask the seller to identify the proposed model. The [datasheet-reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) shows how to carry that identifier into a BOQ and technical submission.

Do not accept a quotation that leaves the model open for later substitution. If a seller proposes an alternative, require a revised model-specific quotation and recheck the design.

## The scope fields that make a quotation usable

**A useful inverter quotation fixes the commercial boundary around the exact product.** These fields prevent most false comparisons.

| Field | What the quotation should identify | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Exact model and quantity | Prevents capacity-only substitution |
| Included hardware | Communication device, connectors, mounting items, or other named accessories | Separates included items from later additions |
| Supply boundary | Supply only, or any additional stated work | Stops one price from carrying work that another excludes |
| Delivery | Destination and whether freight or transit cover is included | Makes the delivered scope visible |
| Tax treatment | HSN treatment, applicable tax line, and whether the total includes it | Keeps tax assumptions out of the base-price comparison |
| Documents | Datasheet, test or compliance documents requested by the project, and invoice documents | Links the commercial offer to the technical submission |
| Commissioning support | Included, excluded, remote, or site-based, if offered | Prevents an undefined support expectation |
| Warranty reference | Current written document name or version supplied with the quote | Avoids treating a headline period as the full policy |
| Exclusions | Every item the seller is not supplying | Exposes scope gaps before the order |
| Validity | Last date on which the quotation can be accepted | Keeps an old number from being reused as a current offer |

Not every project needs every optional item. The point is to mark each field as included, excluded, not applicable, or clarification required. Blank space is not a commercial term.

## A copy-ready request for quotation

**Send a structured request instead of asking only for the "best price".** A model-first request gives the sales team enough context to return a useful document.

Copy this into your enquiry and replace the brackets:

```text
Subject: Solar inverter quotation request for [project name or city]

Buyer organisation: [name]
Role: EPC / Installer
Project city and delivery destination: [city, state, postcode]
Application: [on-grid or hybrid]
Connection phase: [single phase or three phase]
Exact inverter model: [model identifier, if selected]
Quantity: [units]
System size: [kW or kWp, with the basis stated]
Required communication or accessories: [list or none]
Supply boundary: [supply only, or define additional required scope]
Required documents: [list the project documents]
Required delivery date: [date, for availability confirmation]

Please show separately:
1. Unit and extended price for the stated model and quantity
2. Included accessories
3. Freight and delivery basis
4. HSN and tax treatment
5. Commissioning or technical support included, if any
6. Current written warranty document or version
7. Exclusions
8. Quotation validity
9. Any assumptions or proposed substitutions
```

The request asks the seller to confirm availability against a required date. It does not assume or promise a lead time.

## When to mark clarification required

**Use `clarification required` whenever a buyer would have to guess.** Common triggers include:

- A capacity is shown without an exact model identifier.
- Quantity is missing or expressed only as a project capacity.
- Accessories are described as "standard" without a list.
- Freight is included without a destination or delivery basis.
- Tax is shown as extra without the applicable line treatment.
- A warranty is mentioned without the controlling written document.
- Commissioning support is implied but not defined.
- A validity period is missing.
- A substitute model can be supplied without written buyer approval.
- The total depends on a condition that the quotation does not explain.

Send all clarification questions together and ask for a revised quotation. A chain of verbal answers is harder to review and easier to lose than one corrected document.

## What a quotation still does not prove

**A quotation records an offer. It does not prove technical fit, current approval, product reliability, or warranty coverage beyond the referenced documents.** Keep these checks separate.

Engineering should verify the exact model against the string design and project requirements. Procurement should confirm that every required document is current and applies to that model. The contract owner should review the controlling warranty, exclusions, acceptance terms, and remedies.

Price should be compared only after those gates pass.
