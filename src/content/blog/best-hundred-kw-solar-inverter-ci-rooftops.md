**The best 100 kW solar inverter for a C&I rooftop is the one whose trackers can carry your roof zones and whose tracker current can carry your module's strings.** At 100 kW the rated power is the same on every datasheet. The sheets differ on tracker count (8 to 12), the current each tracker accepts (20 A to 45 A), whether the unit can export above 100 kW, how much of the safety and monitoring package is standard, and the 84 to 93 kg someone has to lift onto the roof.

One tracker holds only one orientation, so a 100 kW unit that serves several roof sections is limited by its tracker count. At 600 Wp a module draws about 14.5 A per string, and at 700 Wp about 17.4 A. The tracker's current therefore decides whether it takes one string or two, and the datasheets disagree on that.

## Which ten checks decide a 100 kW inverter purchase?

Ten checks decide it. Work through them for each shortlisted model before comparing price.

1. **Tracker count against roof zones.** Strings on one tracker share a single operating voltage, which is why every orientation and shaded zone needs a tracker of its own. At 100 kW the printed count runs from 8 to 12.
2. **Tracker current against module current.** Test each tracker twice: strings times the module's operating current (Imp) against the maximum input current, and strings times short-circuit current (Isc) against the printed short-circuit rating. Printed tracker currents run from 20 A to 45 A, and 600 Wp and larger modules run 14 A to 18 A per string.
3. **Strings per tracker.** Most sheets cap it at two. Some also print a per-string or per-input limit, and that limit can be lower than the tracker figure. Read both.
4. **DC to AC ratio and clipping.** Look for a printed maximum DC power or recommended array size, then model the ratio for your site. The [DC/AC ratio guide](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/) covers the method. At 100 kW the printed ceilings we found are 150 kW and 154 kW, and four sheets print none.
5. **DC window against string length.** Open-circuit voltage on the coldest morning must stay under the maximum. Operating voltage on a hot afternoon must stay above the bottom of the MPPT window.
6. **AC output, 415 V cable and breaker.** Size from the sheet's printed maximum output current, not from kW divided by voltage, and check which kVA the unit can reach. The [AC cable sizing post](/blog/solar-ac-cable-sizing-inverter-to-acdb/) shows the method.
7. **Anti-islanding and grid-code settings.** Look for the anti-islanding row and the grid standards the sheet lists. Which settings your DISCOM requires on the exact model is a question for the DISCOM, and the [DISCOM approval post](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) covers what to ask.
8. **Monitoring and communication for C&I.** Note what is standard, what is optional, and whether the sheet names a protocol. Owners and O&M contractors want data from the inverter, and a SCADA integrator needs a documented interface.
9. **Enclosure, cooling and weight for a roof.** Compare IP rating, cooling type, operating range, and the 84 to 93 kg someone has to lift to a wall or frame.
10. **Service route.** Ask who attends a fault, who pays freight and labour, and how long a replacement takes. Datasheets do not say, so get it in writing for every brand.

## Worked example: a 500 kW AC rooftop as five 100 kW units

This is illustrative, not a real plant. Assumptions:

- A C&I rooftop of 500 kW AC, built as five 100 kW units, each serving its own roof zone. Why five units rather than four 125 kW or two 225 kW is argued in [the inverter-count post](/blog/how-many-inverters-for-hundred-kw-to-one-mw-rooftop/) and is not repeated here.
- Each zone carries twelve strings. Zone layouts are mixed on purpose, to test the trackers.
- An illustrative 600 Wp module (Voc 49.5 V, Vmp 41.5 V, Isc 15.4 A, Imp 14.5 A) whose voltages both shift by -0.26% per C. The 700 Wp case uses an illustrative module of Imp 17.4 A and Isc 18.3 A with the same voltages and 16-module strings of 11.2 kWp. Substitute your module's values.
- Cold design morning 5 C; hot cell temperature 65 C.

**String length.** One module reads 52.1 V open circuit at 5 C and 37.2 V operating at 65 C. An 18-module string reads 937 V cold and 669 V hot, inside every window above. The longest string the 1100 V maximum allows is 21 modules, at 1,094 V, and a 22-module string reads 1,146 V. A 16-module string of the 700 Wp case reads 833 V cold and 595 V hot.

**DC to AC ratio.** Twelve strings of 18 modules is 216 modules or 129.6 kWp per unit, a ratio of 1.30 on 100 kW, and 648 kWp across five units against 500 kW AC. The 700 Wp case is 134.4 kWp per unit, a ratio of 1.34. Both sit under the 150 kW printed on the Qbits sheet and the 154 kW on the Sungrow sheet. Clipping loss at a 1.30 ratio depends on your irradiance and temperature, so model it using the [DC/AC ratio guide](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/). On the Qbits unit, tracker current binds first: at 600 Wp the printed power ceiling would allow 13 strings (140.4 kWp) but the current rule below allows 12.

**Tracker current.** The strict rule from check 2 gives strings per tracker as the printed tracker current divided by the module's Imp, rounded down and capped at two. Two 600 Wp strings together draw 29.0 A at Imp and 30.8 A at Isc. Two 700 Wp strings draw 34.8 A and 36.6 A.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Growatt MAX100KTL3-X2LV (8 x 45 A) | 2 per tracker, 16 in total | 2 per tracker, 16 in total |
| GoodWe GW100K-GT (8 x 42 A) | 2 per tracker, 16 in total | 2 per tracker, 16 in total |
| Huawei SUN2000-100KTL-M2 (10 x 30 A) | 2 per tracker, 20 in total | 1 per tracker, 10 in total |
| Qbits QB-100KTLC-Pro (40, 40, 20, 20, 40, 40, 20, 20 A) | 2, 2, 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1; 12 in total | 2, 2, 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1; 12 in total |
| Solis S5-GC100K (10 x 32 A) | 2 per tracker, 20 in total | 1 per tracker, 10 in total |
| Sungrow SG110CX-P2 (12 x 30 A) | 2 per tracker, 24 in total | 1 per tracker, 12 in total |

The twelve strings of a zone fit every unit at 600 Wp. At 700 Wp, four units can carry twelve strings on tracker current: the Growatt, the GoodWe, the Qbits and the Sungrow. The GoodWe's 42 A trackers take two 700 Wp strings each (34.8 A at Imp, 36.6 A at Isc under its printed 52.5 A), so it holds sixteen. The Huawei and Solis hold ten, so each zone loses two strings on those units, which is 22.4 kWp per unit and 112 kWp across five units, or the plant drops to a ratio of 1.12 or needs a sixth unit.

Two more points apply. First, Solis and Sungrow print per-string figures of 16 A and 15 A, and a 17.4 A string is above both, so confirm that module with them even where the tracker figure allows it. Second, the sheets are silent on what the inverter does when operating current exceeds the rating, so ask each brand before you use a two-string tracker above its rated current. The Qbits sheet prints no Isc rating, which leaves its Isc check open.

**Zone allocation on one Qbits unit.** The unit has four trackers that take two strings and four that take one. Reading the sheet's order as stated above, every zone shape in this plant fits with no orientation mixed on a tracker.

| Zone | Layout (strings) | Trackers used on the Qbits unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1: one flat plane | 12 south | 40 A trackers 2, 2, 2, 2 and 20 A trackers 1, 1, 1, 1 |
| 2: east and west | 6 east, 6 west | Each side takes two 40 A and two 20 A trackers: 2, 2, 1, 1 |
| 3: three planes | 4 south, 4 east, 4 west | South and east take two 40 A trackers each; west takes four 20 A trackers |
| 4: main shed and annexe | 8 main, 4 annexe | Main takes the four 40 A trackers; annexe takes the four 20 A trackers |
| 5: shed and canopy | 10 shed, 2 canopy | Shed takes four 40 A trackers and two 20 A; canopy takes the other two 20 A |

The layout leaves no spare tracker in any zone, and a thirteenth string or a fourth orientation would not fit under the 600 Wp current rule.

A unit with two-string trackers all round leaves spares. At 600 Wp, the 10-tracker Huawei and Solis units use six of their ten trackers for the three-plane zone, the GoodWe uses six of eight, and the Sungrow uses six of twelve. That spare capacity is real value on a roof with an unplanned shaded corner. It does not help at 700 Wp on the Huawei and Solis, where those units run out of strings before they run out of trackers. The GoodWe's 42 A trackers still take two 700 Wp strings each, so it keeps its two spare trackers at 700 Wp.

How to use this: the layout fits because every group count is even or can be split across a 40 A and a 20 A tracker. A zone of three strings on one orientation would take one 40 A and one 20 A tracker, so list each zone's group sizes before you pick the unit. The [MPPT allocation post](/blog/mppt-allocation-multiple-factory-sheds/) works through a four-shed layout and the mismatch cost of mixing.

**The AC side.** At the printed maximum, a Qbits unit draws 160 A, so five units give five feeders of about 160 A and about 800 A at the combined ACDB when all run at their maximum. At 415 V the same 110 kVA works out to about 153 A, which is why you size from the printed maximum and your installation derating, not from kW alone. The six sheets print 144.3 A to 167.1 A, a spread of about 16%, and mostly a function of the kVA each unit can reach. If your DISCOM sanction is written to 500 kW, ask whether it counts nameplate or the maximum apparent power, because a unit that can output 110 kVA can exceed 100 kW.
