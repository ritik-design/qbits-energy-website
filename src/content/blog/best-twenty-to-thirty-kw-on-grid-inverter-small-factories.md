**The best 20-30 kW on-grid inverter for a small factory is the one whose number of trackers matches your sheds and whose tracker current carries your module's strings.** We compare the 25 kW model, the middle of that range. Rated power is identical across brands there, and the AC current agrees within a few per cent. The datasheets differ on tracker count (two on some, three on others), the current each tracker can take, the weight and the monitoring protocol.

That matters at factory scale because a 100 kW plant on three sheds is rarely one clean roof plane. Each orientation needs its own tracker, and a 25 kW unit with two trackers has less room to separate them than one with three. A 25 kW unit with three trackers weighs from under 30 kg (GoodWe's India sheet) to 38 kg on these sheets, against 18 kg for the Qbits column. Both facts are in the comparison below.

## What should a small factory check before choosing a 25 kW inverter?

Eight checks apply. Put every shortlisted model through them before price enters the discussion.

1. **Tracker count against shed orientations.** Every distinct roof plane, and every shaded zone, needs its own tracker, because one tracker applies one operating voltage to every string on it. List your planes first. The trade between two and three trackers is covered in [dual versus single MPPT](/blog/dual-mppt-vs-single-mppt/).
2. **Tracker current against module current.** Compare strings per tracker times the module's operating current (Imp) to the tracker's input rating, and times its short-circuit current (Isc) to the short-circuit rating. At 25 kW the printed tracker currents run from 27 A to 40.5 A, and modules of 600 Wp and above run 14 A to 17 A per string.
3. **DC window against string length.** On the coldest morning the open-circuit voltage has to stay below the maximum. On a hot afternoon the operating voltage has to stay above the floor of the MPPT window. Use the [string sizing worksheet](/blog/solar-string-sizing-ocp-india/) with your own module and your site's lowest temperature.
4. **Three-phase LT connection and what the DISCOM allows.** All five units output three-phase. What varies is your sanctioned load, the capacity cap for your consumer category and your state's net-metering ceiling. Read the [state-wise capacity limits](/blog/net-metering-capacity-limits-commercial-by-state/) and ask your DISCOM in writing.
5. **AC cable and breaker from the printed maximum current.** Size from the sheet's maximum output current, not from kW divided by voltage. At 25 kW the printed maximum is 37.9 A to 41.8 A across the five sheets.
6. **Monitoring and RS485 for C&I.** Check what is built in, what is an add-on, and whether the sheet names a protocol. A factory owner and an O&M contractor both want data off the inverter without a separate gateway purchase.
7. **DISCOM acceptance of the exact model.** There is no single national approved list. Acceptance is decided per DISCOM, per model and per revision, as the [DISCOM approval guide](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) explains. Treat a standards list on a datasheet as evidence you will be asked for, not as approval.
8. **Service route.** Who attends a fault at the factory, who pays freight and labour, and how long a replacement takes. No datasheet answers this. Get it in writing before you order.

Demand charges are a tariff question, not an inverter question. Solar reduces the energy units you buy, but whether it reduces the demand charge depends on your DISCOM's tariff and on when your maximum demand is recorded. Check your tariff order before you promise a client a demand-charge saving.

## Worked example: a three-shed factory, 100 kW AC

This is illustrative, not a real plant. Assumptions:

- A factory with three sheds and a three-phase LT connection. The LT bus sits near 415 V, which is inside every grid range printed above except where a sheet prints none.
- Target 100 kW AC, so four 25 kW units. A single 100 kW unit is a different class and is covered in [the rooftop inverter-count post](/blog/how-many-inverters-for-hundred-kw-to-one-mw-rooftop/).
- An illustrative 600 Wp module: Voc 49.5 V, Vmp 41.5 V, Isc 15.4 A, Imp 14.5 A, and a temperature coefficient of -0.26% per C applied to both voltages. Substitute your module's values.
- Design temperatures: 5 C on the coldest morning and 65 C cell temperature when hot.
- Shed layout: Shed A faces south and carries 80 modules (5 strings). Shed B is an east-west pair with 32 modules (2 strings) on each side. Shed C faces south but is part-shaded in the afternoon and carries 48 modules (3 strings).

**String length.** A string of 16 modules reads 833 V at the cold Voc and 595 V at the hot Vmp. That sits inside every window above, including GoodWe's 140 to 950 V. Twelve strings of 16 is 192 modules, or 115.2 kWp, a DC to AC ratio of 1.15 on 100 kW AC. The longest string the 1100 V maximum allows is 21 modules, at 1094 V cold Voc. A 22-module string reads 1146 V and is over 1100 V.

**Tracker current.** Two strings of this module read 29.0 A at Imp and 30.8 A at Isc. Under the strict rule from check 2, the table shows how many strings each tracker carries.

| Inverter | 600 Wp class: strings per tracker | 700 Wp class: strings per tracker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growatt MID 25KTL3-X | 1 and 2 | 1 and 2 |
| GoodWe GW25K-SDT-30 | 2, 2 and 2 | 2, 2 and 2 |
| Qbits QB-25KTLC | 2 and 2; Isc not checkable | 2 and 1; Isc not checkable |
| Sungrow SG25CX-P2 | 2, 2 and 2 | 1, 1 and 1 |
| Solis S5-GC25K | 2, 2 and 2 | 1, 1 and 1 |

The 700 Wp column uses an illustrative module of Imp 17.4 A and Isc 18.3 A, with the same coefficient. A pair of its strings would draw 34.8 A at Imp and 36.6 A at Isc. The Imp figure is above 27 A, 30 A and 32 A and below 40 A and 40.5 A, and the Isc figure is under GoodWe's printed 50 A.

Read the result. On the 600 Wp module, Qbits, GoodWe, Sungrow and Solis all take two strings per tracker. Growatt's 27 A tracker takes one, because 29.0 A is over its limit, and its 40.5 A tracker takes two.

On a 700 Wp class module, the 40 A Qbits tracker, the 40.5 A Growatt tracker and each of GoodWe's three 40 A trackers carry two strings, and every other tracker takes one (Sungrow's 30 A and Solis's 32 A). Qbits' second tracker, at 30 A as printed, takes one. A string limit of 2 per tracker on the Qbits sheet is therefore not the constraint at 700 Wp. The current is.

**Allocation across the four units.** With 16-module strings, one string is 9.6 kWp, so three strings give 28.8 kWp per unit, under 37.5 kW on the Qbits sheet. Each tracker holds one orientation only. The [MPPT allocation guide](/blog/mppt-allocation-multiple-factory-sheds/) covers the mismatch cost of mixing orientations.

| Unit | Tracker 1 | Tracker 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inverter 1 | Shed A, 2 strings | Shed A, 1 string |
| Inverter 2 | Shed A, 2 strings | Shed C, 1 string |
| Inverter 3 | Shed B east, 2 strings | Shed C, 1 string |
| Inverter 4 | Shed B west, 2 strings | Shed C, 1 string |

That uses all eight trackers of four two-tracker units. Shed C's three shaded strings sit on three different units, so its cabling is spread. With three-tracker units there are 12 trackers for the same 12 strings, and shed C's three strings can sit together on one unit. At 25 kW the practical difference between two and three trackers is how freely you can keep orientation groups apart.

**The AC side.** Each unit has a printed maximum current of 37.9 to 41.8 A, so four units give four feeders of 41.8 A at most, against one feeder of about 139 A for a single 100 kW unit (100 kW divided by 1.732 x 415 V at unity power factor). Four units have four breakers to size and four terminations. If one trips, 25% of the plant is offline. At the assumed 4.5 kWh per kWp per day and a three-day wait, that is 389 kWh, against 1,555 kWh for one 100 kW unit. The yield figure is an assumption, so use your own.

## Which 25 kW inverter suits which factory layout?

The picks below depend on the datasheet rows and on your roof. They are not a ranking of quality or reliability.

**Growatt MID 25KTL3-X** suits an installer who wants the highest quoted European efficiency here (98.6%), a lighter 23 kg unit than the three-tracker options, string monitoring on the sheet and a design with one large group and one smaller. It has two trackers of unequal size, IP65 and a 250 V start-up voltage.

**GoodWe GW25K-SDT-30** suits a factory with three clear orientation groups, where C&I monitoring matters (it names Modbus TCP and RTU and prints integrated string monitoring), where 700 Wp class modules need 40 A on every tracker so that each takes two strings, and the site is cold enough to use a -30 C rating. It trades that for a weight printed only as under 30 kg, a 950 V window ceiling, no maximum DC power row and no standards list. If you need two trackers rather than three, GoodWe's separate GW25K-SDT-C30 has a different current split (42 A and 22 A).

**Qbits QB-25KTLC** suits an installer who needs an IP66 unit at 18 kg with a 1100 V maximum and the highest maximum efficiency printed here, whose roof has two orientation groups per unit or whose layout allows one large and one small group. Its limits on the published sheet are two trackers, no short-circuit rating, no AFCI row, no string-monitoring row, a surge-protection type that is not stated, and RS485 as an option rather than standard. Its sheet does not establish the warranty terms or the service route. Ask for both in writing, as you would from any brand.

**Sungrow SG25CX-P2** suits a coastal or industrial-corrosive site, because it prints a C5 corrosion class, IP66, AFCI as standard and Type I+II DC surge protection. It trades that for 38 kg and a 98.4% maximum efficiency, the lowest here, and its per-string current needs checking against large modules.

**Solis S5-GC25K** suits an installer who wants the widest tracker current here (32 A each, six strings) with string monitoring and I-V curve scan in an IP66 unit. Its DC switch is optional on the sheet, its AFCI needs activation, and the sheet we read is the GBR version with no grid voltage range printed.

## What do the 25 kW datasheets leave out?

The five sheets are the brands' own papers, and some predate the unit you will receive. GoodWe's India sheet carries file token 20250814, Sungrow's is version 14 from 2023, and the Growatt file is named "2026.5 Update". Solis and Qbits print no date. Have each supplier confirm that its sheet covers the exact model and region you are quoted.

This page says nothing about price, stock, warranty length or remedy, certificate validity, DISCOM acceptance, firmware or field failure rates, and all of them weigh on the purchase. Ask every seller for the same set of documents below, and treat no tabled figure as final until the supplier confirms it for your exact model.

| Request | What it settles |
| --- | --- |
| Datasheet for the exact model code and region | The limits for the unit in the quote |
| Short-circuit current per MPPT in writing | Whether your module passes the Isc check |
| Per-string current limit in writing | Whether a 600 Wp or larger module fits one input |
| Derating curve against ambient temperature | Output on a hot roof |
| Current certificates for the model | Evidence your DISCOM may ask for |
| DISCOM acceptance in writing | That the model is accepted by the utility that will connect it |
| Written warranty and service terms | Term, remedy, freight, labour, who attends |
| RS485 register map | Whether your O&M contractor can read it |
