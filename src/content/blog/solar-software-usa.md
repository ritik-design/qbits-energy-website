## The Six Categories, And Which Ones Have To Talk

| Category | What it does | How tightly it must integrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and simulation | Layout, shading, yield, string sizing, plan sets | Core. Everything downstream derives from it |
| Proposal and quoting | Customer document, ITC, SRECs, financing, e-signature | Must share the design record |
| CRM and pipeline | Leads, follow-up, conversion tracking | API connection is sufficient |
| Permitting and interconnection | AHJ plan sets, utility applications | Should receive the design, not a retyped version |
| Project execution | Scheduling, install progress, inspection, PTO | Should receive the design |
| Monitoring | Performance, service, warranty claims | Standalone, connects to the installed base |

> **The distinction that saves money.** Integration is worth paying for where one system's output is another's input. Design and proposals sit firmly there. CRM and monitoring do not, which is why buying an all-in-one suite to get a bundled CRM alongside a good design tool is usually the wrong trade.

Permitting deserves a comment because it is the category most specific to the US. Requirements vary by authority having jurisdiction and interconnection applications vary by utility, which means a national template does not exist. What software can do is ensure the plan set going to the AHJ derives from the same record as the design and the proposal, so a revision made during value engineering does not leave a stale drawing in the permit package.

## What Is Genuinely US-Specific

**The federal ITC, modelled by section.** The 30% credit runs through 2032, under Section 25D for residential and Section 48E for commercial. Those are separate mechanisms with different rules, and treating them interchangeably misstates the customer's position. It is also a tax credit, so presenting it as a discount on the purchase price misrepresents when the customer actually sees the money and quietly assumes they have the tax liability to absorb it.

**SRECs, which are state markets rather than a national scheme.** Their existence and value depend on the state, and values move. A tool that carries them as a configurable input beats one hard-coding an assumption, and a proposal that projects twenty years of SREC revenue at today's price should say that it has.

**Time-of-use rates.** These matter more every year and they are what makes or breaks the storage case in states with aggressive rate structures. A model applying a flat cents-per-kWh figure cannot evaluate whether generation lands in expensive hours, and therefore cannot size storage sensibly.

**NEC compliance with local amendments.** Adoption is uneven across states, so the applicable code version is a jurisdiction question rather than a national one. Our [best solar design software USA](/blog/solar-design-software-usa/) guide covers which platforms handle this properly.

## Where US Teams Overbuy And Underbuy

**Overbought: seats.** This is the big one, and per-user monthly pricing makes it costly. At $159 to $259 per user per month, five unnecessary licences is a five-figure annual line. Most teams have two or three genuine producers and a larger group who need to view a finished PDF, which is not the same product.

**Overbought: add-on modules purchased on the promise of future workflow.** AI and sales add-ons are often bought during the initial sale and lightly used afterwards. They are worth buying when a specific bottleneck exists, not as insurance.

**Overbought: all-in-one suites bought before the pipeline justifies them.** A small residential installer does not need project execution modules. They need accurate designs and credible proposals, fast.

**Underbought: the design-to-proposal link.** A good design tool plus a quoting spreadsheet looks cheaper and is, until you count quotes sent with a superseded layout or a stale utility rate.

**Underbought: hourly shading at module level.** Annual-average shading numbers are close to useless in a customer conversation and misleading on obstructed roofs. Our [solar shading analysis software](/blog/solar-shading-analysis-software/) guide covers why.
