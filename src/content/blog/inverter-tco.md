Solar inverter selection should not focus solely on purchase price. **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** analysis reveals that budget inverters at ₹30,000 may cost significantly more over 12–15 years than premium options at ₹50,000 when accounting for efficiency losses, maintenance, warranty coverage, and replacement.

## Understanding TCO

TCO represents the complete financial picture from purchase through end-of-life:

- Upfront costs (purchase + installation)
- Annual operational expenses
- Efficiency losses (hidden cost)
- Warranty gap exposure
- Replacement and disposal
- Salvage value

Low-cost inverters at 95% efficiency can cost **₹80,000–₹1,20,000 more** than premium alternatives over operational life.

## Cost Categories

### Upfront

| Segment | Range |
| --- | --- |
| Residential | ₹35,000–₹75,000 |
| Commercial | ₹3,50,000–₹8,00,000 |

Includes equipment, labour, monitoring setup, surge protection, certification.

### Operational (Annual)

| Segment | Range |
| --- | --- |
| Residential | ₹2,000–₹6,000 |
| Commercial | ₹15,000–₹40,000 |

Covers maintenance, monitoring fees, preventive care, troubleshooting.

### Efficiency Losses (Hidden)

| Comparison | 12-year impact (10 kW) |
| --- | --- |
| 3% efficiency gap | ₹54,000 lost revenue |
| Premium (97–98%) | Baseline |
| Budget (degrades to 93–94%) | Lost revenue |

### Warranty Value

| Coverage | Actuarial value |
| --- | --- |
| 5-year repair | Baseline |
| 12-year full replacement | ₹15,000–₹20,000+ |

### Replacement / Upgrade

| Tier | Replacements (25 yrs) | Disposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | 2–3 | ₹2,000–5,000 each |
| Premium | 1–2 | ₹2,000–5,000 each |

## TCO Formula

```
TCO = Upfront + (Annual OpEx × Years) + Efficiency Loss + Warranty Gap + Replacement − Salvage
```
