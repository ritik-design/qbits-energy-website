## What is PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana

PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is the Government of India's flagship residential rooftop solar subsidy scheme. Launched on 13 February 2024 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, it aims to install rooftop solar at 10 million Indian homes by 2027 and to provide up to 300 kWh of free electricity per month to participating households.

The scheme provides central financial assistance directly to residential customers, ranging from ₹30,000 for a 1 kW system to ₹78,000 for systems of 3 kW and above. Implementation runs through the National Portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in), with DISCOMs and empanelled vendors handling installation and commissioning.

PM Surya Ghar replaces and expands the earlier Grid Connected Rooftop Solar Programme Phase II. The total scheme outlay is approximately ₹75,000 crore over the implementation period.

## PM Surya Ghar explained simply

The government will pay up to ₹78,000 toward the cost of a rooftop solar system at your home. You apply online, pick an approved installer, get the system installed and connected to the grid through a net meter, and the subsidy lands in your bank account afterwards. The system then offsets your monthly bill, often to zero or near zero, for the next 25 years.

For a typical Indian middle-class home with a 3 kW system, the math works out to a payback of three to four years and roughly 25 years of largely free electricity after that.

## Why PM Surya Ghar matters

Capex reduction. The subsidy cuts upfront cost by 35 to 50 percent for typical residential systems.

Scale. The 10 million household target is roughly 30 GW of distributed solar by 2027, comparable to a few years of utility-scale capacity addition.

DISCOM standardisation. The scheme forces standard processes, smart-meter rollout and net metering across states.

ALMM enforcement. Only ALMM-listed inverters and panels qualify, lifting compliance across the supply chain.

Climate contribution. 30 GW residential solar avoids approximately 50 million tonnes of CO2 annually.

Energy bill relief. Households get up to 300 kWh per month free, reducing energy poverty for lower-income segments.

## How the application process works

1. Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in with mobile number, electricity bill and Aadhaar.
2. Submit application with sanctioned load, roof area and basic site details.
3. DISCOM reviews technical feasibility and approves capacity.
4. Customer selects an empanelled vendor from the portal list.
5. Vendor installs the system using ALMM-listed inverter and panels.
6. DISCOM installs bidirectional smart meter, completes commissioning and issues commissioning report.
7. Customer uploads commissioning report and bank details on portal.
8. MNRE disburses subsidy by direct bank transfer within 30 days.

## Advantages

Substantial subsidy reducing payback to under 4 years.

Standardised application portal across states.

Mandatory ALMM compliance ensures equipment quality.

Net metering coordination built into the workflow.

Bank loan availability through partner banks at concessional rates.

Long-term warranty backed by central scheme.

## Limitations

Subsidy capped at 3 kW equivalent, even for larger systems.

Empanelled vendor list can be limited in remote districts.

Implementation pace varies by DISCOM, with some states still lagging on net metering and smart meter rollout.

Battery storage not subsidised.

Tenants not directly eligible.

Hybrid and ground-mount systems eligible only as grid-tie configurations.

## Common problems

DISCOM technical feasibility delay. The most common bottleneck in the process.

Smart meter procurement lag. Some districts wait 2 to 3 months for bidirectional meters.

Empanelled vendor unavailability in tier 3 and 4 towns.

Bank account mismatch between portal and DISCOM records.

Subsidy disbursement delay beyond the stated 30-day window.

Non-ALMM equipment installed by mistake, voiding subsidy.

Society NOC complications for individual flat owners.

## Best practices

Register on the portal first, before talking to any vendor.

Verify the vendor's empanelment on the portal, not on their brochure.

Confirm inverter and panel ALMM listing on the day of installation.

Photograph commissioning seal numbers on the smart meter.

Keep all invoices and DISCOM correspondence for at least three years.

Audit the first three bills against expected generation.

Plan installation in October to January for fastest commissioning in most states.

## Indian market context

The scheme is implemented through DISCOMs as the primary executing agency. State Renewable Energy Development Agencies (SRDAs) support coordination in some states.

Empanelled vendor lists are maintained per district. The portal updates daily.

The Ministry of Power and MNRE jointly oversee implementation. The Prime Minister's Office tracks state-wise progress monthly.

Bank loan partnerships with SBI, Canara Bank, PNB, BoB and others offer concessional financing for the customer share of capex.

PM Surya Ghar dovetails with the smart meter rollout under RDSS. Districts with smart meter coverage above 80 percent see fastest commissioning.

## Common mistakes

Engaging a non-empanelled vendor expecting subsidy. Subsidy is denied.

Buying non-ALMM equipment to save cost. Subsidy is denied.

Skipping the DISCOM technical feasibility step. Application is rejected.

Installing before the portal registration. Subsidy may be denied.

Missing the commissioning report upload. Subsidy disbursement stalls.

Ignoring smart meter installation. System runs but credits are not recorded.

Submitting wrong bank account details. Disbursement fails.

## Key takeaways

PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is India's most significant residential rooftop solar scheme to date, with central subsidy up to ₹78,000 and a target of 10 million homes by 2027. Successful execution depends on the centralised portal, empanelled vendors, ALMM-listed equipment, smart meter installation and DISCOM coordination. Typical payback under the scheme is 2.5 to 4 years.
