## What is a DISCOM?

A **DISCOM** is an electricity distribution company. It operates or administers the distribution service for a defined area and is usually the company named on the electricity bill. Its customer-facing responsibilities include the connection, meter, billing and local network process under applicable regulation.

In rooftop solar, the DISCOM is the utility contact for an eligible grid connection. The exact approval route, technical limits, meter type, export settlement and billing rules depend on the state regulator and that utility's current procedure.

## What does the DISCOM check for solar?

| Stage | Typical DISCOM role | What the applicant should keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application | Receive consumer and proposed-system details | Application ID and submitted documents |
| Feasibility/approval | Check relevant connection and network conditions | Written conditions or objections |
| Installation closeout | Review required installer and equipment records | Model list, commissioning documents and photos |
| Metering/interconnection | Arrange or approve the applicable meter and connection | Meter details and commissioning record |
| Billing | Apply the approved settlement arrangement | Bills showing import/export or other relevant readings |

Do not assume every DISCOM follows the same order or approves every roof for the same capacity. The [net-metering application guide](/blog/how-to-apply-net-metering-india/) and [approval-timeline guide](/blog/net-meter-approval-timeline/) explain the consumer workflow. For PM Surya Ghar, check the [official scheme route](/blog/mnre-rooftop-solar-scheme/) and the registered vendor separately.

## How to identify and contact the correct utility

Use the electricity bill's company name, consumer number and service address. A district can have more than one utility or a special service area; a generic state list can point to the wrong operator. Use the official customer portal or bill contact details for status, meter disputes and grievance channels.

A private or state-owned DISCOM is not inherently faster or slower for every solar application. This page does not assign universal approval times, national company counts or operator performance figures, because current comparable data is not available. For a real project, the utility's written instruction and dated application record are more useful than a national average.
