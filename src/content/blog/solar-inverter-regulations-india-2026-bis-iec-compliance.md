> **Quick answers, checked 26 September 2026**
>
> - BIS and ALMM are not competing certificates. They answer different questions.
> - India’s 2025 solar-goods order covers inverter safety and performance standards.
> - ALMM List-I covers modules. List-II covers cells. Neither is an inverter list.
> - A standard printed on a datasheet is not the current licence or registration evidence.
> - Subsidy, tender and DISCOM acceptance remain separate project checks.
> - Stock the exact model only after its documents, markings and intended project route match.

**Short version.** Before stocking a solar inverter brand, verify the exact model against the current BIS route created by the applicable Quality Control Order. Use ALMM only for the module and cell questions it actually covers. Then check the scheme, tender and DISCOM requirements for the projects that will use the stock.

## BIS vs ALMM: the decision table

| Check | What it covers | Product level | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BIS licence or registration under the applicable QCO | Conformity to named Indian Standards and use of the Standard Mark | Exact covered model or declared series scope | ALMM status, tender acceptance or grid connection |
| IEC certificate or test report | Testing against the stated international standard and edition | Exact model or tested family | Indian legal compliance unless the applicable Indian order accepts that route |
| ALMM List-I | MNRE-enlisted solar PV module models and manufacturers | Module model and manufacturing location | Inverter compliance or cell compliance by itself |
| ALMM List-II | MNRE-enlisted solar PV cell models and manufacturers | Cell model and manufacturing location | Inverter compliance or module listing by itself |
| Scheme or tender requirement | Eligibility and evidence for that programme or procurement | Bid, project, vendor and equipment schedule | Approval under another scheme or tender |
| DISCOM or project approval | Connection, metering, protection, inspection and commissioning | The specific installation | Product conformity for every unit sold elsewhere |

This separation matters for stock. A dealer can hold an inverter with a valid product document that still does not satisfy a particular tender. An EPC can also select compliant modules and cells while leaving the inverter evidence unresolved.

## What the current inverter order requires

The controlling national document is the **Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025**, issued by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) as S.O. 492(E) on 27 January 2025 for India. It replaced the 2017 order while preserving valid existing licences until their expiry.

The [official 2025 QCO notice](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/notification-for-solar-systems-devices-and-components-goods-order-2025/) says covered goods must conform to the named Indian Standards and bear the Standard Mark under a Bureau of Indian Standards licence under Scheme II. BIS is the certifying and enforcing authority.

For inverters, the order divides the equipment into two entries.

| QCO entry | Standards named in the 2025 order | Decision the standard addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power inverters for photovoltaic power systems | IS 16221 (Part 2): 2015 / IEC 62109-2: 2011; IS/IEC 61683: 1999 | Inverter safety; efficiency measurement procedure |
| Utility-interconnected photovoltaic inverters | IS 16221 (Part 2): 2015 / IEC 62109-2: 2011; IS 16169: 2019 / IEC 62116: 2014; IS 17980: 2022 / IEC 62891: 2020 | Safety; islanding-prevention testing; MPPT efficiency |

The order also says the latest notified version of an Indian Standard, including amendments, applies from the date of notification. Do not freeze a stocking policy around a screenshot of an older standard list.

## The temporary route for inverters above 200 kW

Capacity changes the evidence route. MNRE notification **S.O. 3706(E), dated 3 July 2026**, extends implementation of the 2025 order for SPV inverters above 200 kW until 31 December 2026 or further orders, whichever is earlier.

The [official July 2026 notification](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/notification-regarding-implementation-of-solar-systems-devices-and-components-goods-order-2025/) permits conditional self-certification for those units. It requires the manufacturer to hold the corresponding valid IEC certificates and test reports from accredited laboratories named by the notification.

Three boundaries are easy to miss:

1. It applies to capacity **above 200 kW**, not 200 kW and below.
2. It expires on the earlier of 31 December 2026 or a further order.
3. It is not a waiver of evidence. It specifies a different, conditional evidence route.

If stock may be delivered after the cut-off, record who must recheck the order and what happens if the route changes before dispatch or commissioning.

## What ALMM covers in September 2026

ALMM means **Approved List of Models and Manufacturers**. The [MNRE ALMM page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/), last updated 16 September 2026, states that:

- List-I specifies solar PV module models and manufacturers;
- List-II specifies solar PV cell models and manufacturers;
- the first module list was issued on 10 March 2021; and
- the first cell list was issued on 31 July 2025.

MNRE also states that only List-I module models and manufacturers are eligible for use in government projects, government-assisted projects, projects under government schemes and programmes, open-access projects and net-metering projects installed in India, including the other categories specified on that page.

There is no inverter list on the solar ALMM page. An inverter seller saying “ALMM approved” should be asked which official list, exact product and model the statement refers to. If the answer is an inverter, the wording is wrong.

The [detailed ALMM scope guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) owns the module, cell and current exception questions. This page keeps the narrower stocking decision: do not substitute an ALMM claim for the inverter’s own QCO and BIS evidence.

## Scheme enlistment is not ALMM

Another similarly named record can create confusion. MNRE issued a **Framework for enlistment of models of OEMs of solar PV modules and inverters under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana** on 21 June 2024.

The [official framework notice](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/framework-for-enlistment-of-models-of-oems-of-solar-pv-modules-and-inverters-under-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana-reg/) describes a separate scheme model-enlistment programme administered through NISE. The framework as issued calls it voluntary and says installations not using enlisted models can remain eligible for Central Financial Assistance when they comply with the scheme guidelines.

That programme is not ALMM and is not a BIS licence. Before quoting for PM Surya Ghar work, check the latest scheme notices rather than relying on the 2024 framework alone.

## Product conformity does not approve the project

Product evidence answers whether the equipment follows the specified product route. It does not approve a design, connection or subsidy file.

The [MNRE residential CFA operational guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) dated 13 June 2024 illustrate the distinction. They require vendors to meet the scheme’s technical specifications, and they place physical inspection and portal approval with the relevant DISCOM. Metering arrangements and technical feasibility follow the applicable state and DISCOM process.

For other projects, the controlling document may be a tender schedule, owner specification, State Electricity Regulatory Commission regulation, DISCOM checklist or Electrical Inspector requirement. Build a project evidence matrix rather than applying one residential-scheme checklist to every sale.

## The eight checks before stocking an inverter model

Use a separate row for every exact SKU. “Brand approved” is not a useful status.

1. **Freeze the complete model name.** Match the quote, nameplate format, datasheet and proposed invoice description.
2. **Classify the product.** Confirm whether the QCO’s power-inverter or utility-interconnected entry applies and whether another category is relevant.
3. **Obtain the current authority record.** Ask for the BIS licence or registration evidence, including its number, holder, status, standards and model scope.
4. **Verify the record independently.** Use the [BIS Compulsory Registration Scheme public search](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/), not a cropped supplier image.
5. **Match every scope field.** Check legal entity, manufacturing location where shown, exact model or declared series, standard edition, issue date, validity and limitations.
6. **Handle the above-200 kW route separately.** If applicable, obtain the IEC certificates and accredited-laboratory reports required by the current extension, then recheck the cut-off.
7. **Map the intended project class.** Record the scheme, tender, state, DISCOM and commissioning date that will govern likely use.
8. **Set a stop-stock trigger.** Define what happens when evidence expires, a model changes, a licence is suspended or the project route changes.

## What to put in the stock file

Keep a controlled file for each model and revision.

| Record | Minimum fields |
| --- | --- |
| Model master | Full model, capacity, phase, hardware revision and intended application |
| QCO and BIS evidence | Order, applicable entry, standards, licence or registration number, holder, exact scope, status and date checked |
| Test evidence | Report or certificate title, model, standard and edition, laboratory, accreditation scope, issue date and exceptions |
| Product documents | Current datasheet, installation manual, settings guide and communication accessory schedule |
| Project matrix | Scheme or tender clause, state, DISCOM, required evidence, owner and status |
| Incoming inspection | Purchase order, invoice, serial numbers, nameplate photographs, Standard Mark, accessories and quarantine record |
| Review control | Next review date, responsible person, open clarification and stop-stock condition |

Record the source URL and the date checked. A file named `BIS certificate latest.pdf` without a model, source or review date will fail exactly when the team needs it.

## Common claims that should stop the purchase

Pause the stock decision when a supplier says:

- “The whole brand is BIS approved.”
- “IEC is international, so BIS is unnecessary.”
- “The inverter is ALMM listed.”
- “Tier 1 means the inverter is certified.”
- “The same certificate covers every capacity.”
- “The tender will accept it because the DISCOM accepted another model.”
- “The certificate is being renewed, so you can order now.”

None of these statements supplies the missing evidence. Record the gap and ask for the current document or an answer from the controlling authority.

## Qbits documents and the next check

Qbits public datasheets identify model families and technical values. They do not, by themselves, establish a current BIS licence, ALMM status, tender eligibility, subsidy eligibility or DISCOM approval for the exact unit being considered. This article therefore makes no current Qbits certification or approval claim.

[Download the Qbits datasheet for the exact model](/download-datasheets/) and add it to the model register. Then obtain and verify every current certificate, licence, scheme document and project-specific approval required for the intended use before committing stock.
