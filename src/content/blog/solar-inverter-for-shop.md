## Right Inverter Size for a Small Shop: The 1.5–5 kW Range

The [solar inverter sizing](/blog/solar-inverter-sizing/) decision for a small shop has three inputs: peak load, daily energy generation target, and roof area available. Most single-phase shop connections in India carry a sanctioned load of 3–10 kW, which sets the ceiling on your solar system size under most state net metering regulations.

### 1.5–2 kW: Suitable for Basic Shops

A 1.5 kW or 2 kW on-grid inverter suits a shop that runs only lights, a ceiling fan, a small refrigerator, and a POS terminal — no AC. This is the entry point for kirana stores, small stationery shops, and tailoring units. With a 2 kWp panel array generating approximately 8–10 kWh/day in a city like Hyderabad (with 5.5 peak sun hours), you offset 240–300 kWh per month. At ₹9/unit, that saves ₹2,160–₹2,700/month on a system costing roughly ₹80,000–₹1,00,000 installed.

### 3 kW: The Sweet Spot for Most Shops

A 3 kW inverter with a 3.5–4 kWp panel array is the most popular choice for Indian small commercial installations. It covers a pharmacy with a vaccine refrigerator, a salon with 2–3 styling stations, or an electronics retailer with 2–3 display TVs and a server. At ₹1.2–1.5 Lakh installed cost (after GST ITC), this system generates 400–480 kWh/month, saving ₹3,600–₹4,320/month at ₹9/unit — a 3.5–4 year payback period.

### 5 kW: For AC-Running Shops and Higher Bills

A 5 kW single-phase on-grid inverter handles the salon or pharmacy that runs a 1.5-ton split AC through eight months of the year. In Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, and Gujarat — where commercial tariffs hit ₹10–12/unit — a 5 kW system with 5.5 kWp of panels generates 700–800 kWh/month, saving ₹7,000–₹9,600/month. At ₹2.2–2.8 Lakh installed cost (after ITC), payback drops to just 2–3 years.

## The Shop Solar ROI Framework: Four Steps to a Sound Decision

This proprietary framework collapses the commercial solar buying decision into four verifiable steps. Every shop owner can complete all four steps in under two weeks before committing to an installer.

### The Shop Solar ROI Framework

1. **Load Audit** — List every electrical appliance, its wattage, and its daily operating hours. Sum the peak simultaneous wattage. This determines your minimum inverter capacity. Add 15% headroom for inrush currents from compressors and motors. The result is your design load.

2. **Tariff-Based Sizing** — Calculate your monthly unit consumption from your DISCOM bill (units consumed ÷ 30 = daily units). Divide by your city's average [peak sun hours](/glossary/peak-sun-hours/) (4.5–6.0 for most Indian cities). The quotient is the panel capacity in kWp needed to offset 80–90% of your consumption. Match this to the nearest standard inverter size (1.5, 2, 3, 5 kW).

3. **Net Metering Application** — File the net metering application with your DISCOM before installation begins. In most states, the DISCOM issues a feasibility letter within 15–30 days. This letter specifies the maximum exportable capacity and the bi-directional meter specification. Building the system before this approval can result in rejection of the net meter application and loss of the export benefit.

4. **GST ITC Claim** — If your shop is GST-registered, preserve all tax invoices showing HSN 8502 (inverter) and HSN 8541 (solar panels) with 12% GST clearly stated. File ITC claims in GSTR-3B in the month of installation. The refundable credit reduces your effective system cost by ₹20,000–₹40,000 on a typical ₹2–3 Lakh system — a benefit that residential buyers simply cannot access.

Applying this four-step sequence in order avoids the two most expensive mistakes: buying an undersized system that fails to cover the AC load in May, and buying an oversized system that exports more than the DISCOM will accept.

## Commercial Net Metering: How It Works for a Shop

[Net metering](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) is the regulatory mechanism that makes commercial solar economically viable. Without it, your solar system generates power only when the shop is open and lights are on — surplus generation during mid-day lunch breaks is wasted. With net metering, every unit of surplus solar is exported to the grid and credited to your account, to be consumed later without paying ₹0.

The process for a commercial net metering connection in India involves these steps:

- **Step 1** — Apply to your DISCOM's online portal with a load certificate, roof ownership/consent letter, and inverter technical compliance document (BIS certificate, IEC 61727 test report).
- **Step 2** — DISCOM site inspection within 7–15 working days in most states. Inspector verifies roof access, existing meter, and proposed panel layout.
- **Step 3** — Feasibility sanction letter issued. This specifies the net-metering approved capacity, which equals the lesser of your sanctioned load or the applied capacity.
- **Step 4** — Installation completed by a DISCOM-empanelled installer. The DISCOM replaces the existing meter with a [bidirectional meter](/glossary/bidirectional-meter/) at their cost (in most states).
- **Step 5** — Commissioning inspection. DISCOM engineer witnesses the inverter energisation and confirms anti-islanding protection is active. The system goes live.

Under net metering, your DISCOM bill converts to a net-unit bill: units consumed from the grid minus units exported to the grid, multiplied by the commercial tariff. On high-generation days (summer, monsoon rains absent), you may export more than you consume, earning a credit that rolls over to the next billing cycle.

According to the [Central Electricity Regulatory Commission's net metering framework](https://www.cercind.gov.in/), commercial and industrial consumers are permitted to install rooftop solar up to their sanctioned load capacity under net metering, with surplus credits carried forward for up to 12 months.

Solar panels (HSN 8541) attract 12% GST. A 3 kW system with a ₹1.5 Lakh installed cost (pre-GST) carries approximately ₹18,000 of GST. A 5 kW system at ₹2.5 Lakh (pre-GST) carries ₹30,000 of GST.

## Why IP66 Matters for Market-Area Shops

Most shop owners look at inverter spec sheets and skip past the IP (Ingress Protection) rating — a two-digit code that determines whether the inverter survives the environment your shop operates in. For a shop installed in a market lane, IP66 is not a premium feature. It is a basic requirement.

Market environments subject inverters to conditions that a laboratory test environment never replicates:

- **Fine construction dust** from nearby renovation work settles on circuit board traces and causes short circuits over 12–18 months.
- **Monsoon splash-back** from flooded gutters reaches wall-mounted inverters positioned behind counters or on rear walls.
- **Pressure washing** — many shops and their neighbours clean shopfronts with pressurised hoses; water finds its way through cable glands and vented housings.
- **Vehicle exhaust** in busy market areas deposits carbon particulates and acidic compounds on unprotected boards.

An IP65 inverter protects against dust (first digit: 6) and against low-pressure water jets (second digit: 5). An [IP66 inverter](/blog/ip66-inverters-india-s-climate-survival-guide/) adds protection against high-pressure, high-volume water jets from any direction — a meaningful difference when a neighbour's pressure washer sends a spray across your wall.

The [IP65 versus IP66 distinction](/blog/ip65-vs-ip66-solar-inverters-weather-protection-guide/) may seem minor, but field data from inverter service teams consistently shows that board-level corrosion from moisture ingress is the leading cause of inverter failure in Tier-2 and Tier-3 market installations. Replacing a circuit board costs ₹8,000–₹15,000 and requires 3–7 days of system downtime — during which your shop absorbs full commercial tariff again. An IP66 inverter typically adds ₹2,000–₹5,000 to the purchase price but eliminates this recurring expense.

## On-Grid vs. Hybrid Inverter for a Shop: The Decision Matrix

The choice between an on-grid inverter and a hybrid inverter is primarily driven by your area's power-cut frequency and duration. The [on-grid versus hybrid](/blog/on-grid-vs-hybrid/) decision is different for a shop than for a home because the cost of lost business during an outage is real and calculable.

| | On-Grid Inverter | Hybrid Inverter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Upfront cost (3 kW)** | ₹1.2–1.5 Lakh | ₹2.0–2.5 Lakh (inverter alone) |
| **Battery additional cost** | Not applicable | ₹60,000–₹1.2 Lakh (10 kWh LFP) |
| **Power during outage** | No — shuts down | Yes — critical loads stay live |
| **Net metering compatible** | Yes | Yes (most states) |
| **Payback period** | 2–4 years | 5–7 years (with battery) |
| **Best for** | Areas with ≤ 1 hr/day outage | Areas with 2+ hrs/day outage |
| **GST ITC claimable** | Yes | Yes |

**Pros of on-grid for a shop**
- ✓ Lowest capital cost — fastest payback
- ✓ Full net-metering benefit — no surplus generation wasted
- ✓ Simpler installation and commissioning
- ✓ No battery maintenance cost over 10 years

**Cons of on-grid for a shop**
- ✗ Zero power during grid outages — POS terminal, billing system, and refrigeration all go down
- ✗ Cannot serve as a backup for perishable storage (vaccine fridge, dairy)
- ✗ Upgrading to hybrid later requires full inverter replacement in most cases

For shops in states with reliable grids (Gujarat, Maharashtra urban areas, Karnataka urban areas), an on-grid system is the correct economic choice. For shops in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, eastern Rajasthan, or parts of Odisha — where outages exceed 2–3 hours daily — a [hybrid solar inverter](/hybrid-inverter/) with a 5–10 kWh lithium iron phosphate battery provides business continuity that has measurable monetary value.

## Common Mistakes Small Shop Owners Make When Buying Solar

Even with the best intentions, shop owners regularly make the following errors that reduce system performance or increase lifetime cost. Each one is avoidable with a 10-minute check before signing the installer's contract.

- **Skipping the DISCOM net metering application pre-check** — Installing first and applying later often results in the DISCOM rejecting the net meter application because the system size exceeds the sanctioned load, or because the installer used a non-ALMM-listed inverter. Check the [ALMM list guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) before purchasing any inverter.

- **Choosing an inverter based on price alone** — Cheap inverters from unlisted manufacturers fail DISCOM commissioning inspections, voiding any chance of net metering. They also carry no service network in Tier-2 cities. The [solar inverter quality guide](/blog/solar-inverter-quality/) explains the five checks that separate durable commercial-grade units from price-sensitive alternatives.

- **Ignoring seasonal load variation** — A salon that runs minimal AC in winter and four hair dryers plus AC in summer has a 3× load swing. Size for the summer peak, not the comfortable winter average, or you will clip generation and trip the inverter during peak summer months.

- **Not verifying the installer's empanelment status** — For net metering in most states, the installing vendor must be empanelled by the DISCOM or state nodal agency. Using an un-empanelled installer means the DISCOM will not commission the net meter and your system operates at a disadvantage (no export credit).

- **Missing the GST ITC window** — ITC must be claimed in the return for the tax period in which the invoice is received. Waiting until the next financial year to file can forfeit the credit under GST's time-bound ITC rules. Flag your purchase to your accountant in the same month the inverter is delivered.

- **Failing to account for inverter placement heat** — An inverter mounted on a south-facing wall inside a glass shopfront can reach ambient temperatures of 50–55 °C in May. At these temperatures, an inverter derate function kicks in, reducing output by 10–20%. Mount the inverter on a shaded north or east interior wall, or inside a ventilated electrical cabinet, to preserve full nameplate output through summer.
