## What is a monocrystalline panel

A monocrystalline solar panel is a photovoltaic module whose individual cells are cut from a single continuous silicon crystal. The single-crystal structure means electrons move more freely through the silicon, raising conversion efficiency above polycrystalline alternatives. Modern mono modules achieve 20 to 23 percent module efficiency in mass production, with premium HJT touching 24 percent.

The single crystal is grown using the Czochralski process: molten silicon is slowly pulled and rotated to form a cylindrical ingot. The ingot is then sliced into thin wafers, processed into cells through diffusion and metallisation, interconnected and laminated into a module.

For Indian solar, mono PERC dominated installations from 2020 to 2024 and is being progressively replaced by mono TOPCon from 2024 onward, with HJT entering the premium segment. Polycrystalline has nearly disappeared from new installations.

## Monocrystalline explained simply

A solar cell is a thin slice of silicon. Monocrystalline cells are sliced from one continuous crystal, like cutting slices from a single carrot. Polycrystalline cells are cast from many small crystals, like cutting through a fruit cake with embedded grains. The clean crystal structure of mono lets electrons move with less obstruction, producing more electricity from the same sunlight.

## Why monocrystalline matters

Roof space efficiency. Higher efficiency means more watts per square metre, which is critical for Indian rooftops with limited area.

Cost parity. Mono is now cheaper per watt than poly because Indian and Chinese gigawatt-scale mono lines have lower marginal cost.

Low-light performance. Mono cells start producing earlier and end later in the day.

Standardised supply. Mono PERC, TOPCon and HJT are the industry standard.

Bifacial compatibility. Mono is the foundation for high-yield bifacial modules.

ALMM dominance. Most ALMM-listed modules are monocrystalline.

## How monocrystalline cells work

1. Photon absorption. Sunlight strikes the silicon, exciting electrons to the conduction band.
2. Electron-hole pair generation. Each absorbed photon creates one electron-hole pair.
3. P-N junction separation. An internal electric field separates electrons toward the front contact and holes toward the rear.
4. External circuit. Electrons flow through the load and back to the rear contact.
5. Module-level interconnection. Cells are wired in series within the module to add voltage.

The single-crystal structure minimises grain boundaries, which would otherwise trap electrons and reduce conversion efficiency.

## Technical deep dive

### Cell technologies inside monocrystalline modules

| Technology | Efficiency | Cost | Use case |

| Standard mono | 18 to 20% | Lowest | Legacy |
| Mono PERC | 20 to 22% | Low | Mainstream until 2024 |

| IBC | 23 to 25% | Highest | Specialty |
| Tandem perovskite/silicon | 30%+ (lab) | Pre-commercial | Emerging |

### Module efficiency vs cell efficiency

Module efficiency is lower than individual cell efficiency due to cell spacing, frame area and glass transmission. A 23 percent cell typically gives a 21 to 21.5 percent module.

### Temperature coefficient

| Parameter | Mono PERC | Mono TOPCon | Mono HJT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voc temp coefficient (%/°C) | -0.27 to -0.30 | -0.25 to -0.28 | -0.21 to -0.25 |
| Pmax temp coefficient (%/°C) | -0.34 to -0.37 | -0.30 to -0.33 | -0.24 to -0.28 |

HJT panels lose less power in heat, giving better real-world yield in Indian conditions, the same -0.35%/°C PERC coefficient measured in the field in [Case Study (10 kW Solar on a Jaipur Villa), 4-Year Payback](/blog/case-study-jaipur-villa/).

### Degradation

| Mechanism | Mono behaviour |
|---|---|
| Initial LID (light-induced degradation) | 1 to 2% in first month |
| LeTID (light + elevated temperature) | Controlled in modern mono PERC |
| Annual linear degradation | 0.4 to 0.55%/year |
| 25-year retention | 84 to 88% of original |

## Advantages of monocrystalline

Highest efficiency per square metre.

Better low-light performance.

Better temperature performance (especially HJT).

Lower lifetime degradation.

Compatible with bifacial.

Mature supply chain.

Aesthetic uniform dark appearance.

## Limitations

Slightly higher manufacturing energy than thin film.

LID and LeTID effects (now controlled).

Single-crystal structure has small unused wafer corner area.

Premium technologies (HJT, IBC) carry price premiums.

## Common problems

PID (potential-induced degradation). Older modules without PID-resistant materials lose 10 to 30 percent over 5 to 10 years. Modern modules are PID-tested.

Hot spots. Cracked cells under load cause local heating and accelerated degradation.

Snail trails. Cosmetic marks from cell hairline cracks plus moisture and humidity.

Back sheet cracking. Polymer back sheets in older modules can crack in tropical UV.

Junction box failure. Bypass diode short circuits or open circuits.

Soiling. Indian rooftops accumulate dust quickly. 5 to 25 percent yield loss in dry season.

## Best practices

Choose ALMM-listed and BIS-certified mono modules.

Verify positive power tolerance (typically 0 to +5 W).

Specify PID-resistant back sheet and EVA.

Plan annual cleaning schedule for Indian conditions.

Use proper torque on MC4 connectors.

Document module serial numbers at commissioning.

Monitor I-V curves at annual inspection.

## Common mistakes

Choosing module on cost-per-watt alone, ignoring temperature coefficient.

Skipping the ALMM verification on the day of supply.

Mixing module brands or wattages within one string.

Skipping cleaning schedule. Indian soiling losses can wipe out the LID-vs-LeTID debate.

Ignoring positive power tolerance, leading to under-sized string voltage estimates.

Buying without PID test certification for systems with grounded inverter.

## Key takeaways

Monocrystalline is now the default cell technology for Indian solar. Mono PERC dominated 2020 to 2024; mono TOPCon dominates from 2024 onward; HJT serves the premium segment. ALMM listing, BIS certification, temperature coefficient and 30-year degradation profile are the key selection criteria. Annual cleaning matters more than the choice between PERC and TOPCon at most Indian sites.
