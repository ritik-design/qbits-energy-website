## What the Surya Gujarat Scheme Actually Is in 2026

Surya Gujarat is the Gujarat government's residential rooftop solar programme, formally named Surya Urja Rooftop Yojana. It launched in 2019 with an aggressive state subsidy. The design paid 40% of the benchmark cost for systems up to 3 kW and 20% for capacity between 3 kW and 10 kW. These rates come from the original [GUVNL scheme document](https://www.svnit.ac.in/Unnat_Bharat/PI/pdf/Sustainabe%20Energy/Schemes%20by%20Government%20of%20Gujarat/Surya%20Urja%20Rooftop%20Yojana%20Gujarat/SURYA-Gujarat%20Surya%20Urja%20Rooftop%20Yojana.pdf) (2019). That structure made Gujarat the rooftop solar leader in India before the national scheme existed.

The scheme's role changed in February 2024 when PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana launched nationally. The central scheme took over the headline subsidy, and Surya Gujarat repositioned as a state top-up that layers on the central CFA rather than replacing it. The state top-up continues through FY 2026-27 with no change in headline rates, according to [SolarVipani's subsidy tracker](https://solarvipani.com/in/solar-subsidy/latest-updates) (2026).

Three agencies run the state side. **GEDA** (Gujarat Energy Development Agency) is the state nodal agency and handles subsidy disbursement and vendor empanelment. **GUVNL** (Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Limited) is the holding company that coordinates implementation through its four DISCOM subsidiaries. **GERC** (Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission) sets the net metering rules that decide how your exported units are credited.

The practical result: Gujarat applicants deal with two schemes, two registrations, and one DISCOM. The schemes are complementary, not alternatives. Claiming only the central subsidy and skipping the state registration leaves ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 on the table for a typical 3 kW home.

## Surya Gujarat Subsidy Amounts: The Full Math

The combined subsidy for a Gujarat home in 2026 has two fixed components and one variable one. The central CFA rates are fixed nationally, according to the [PM Surya Ghar national portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) (2026):

- **1 kW system:** ₹30,000
- **2 kW system:** ₹60,000 (₹30,000 per kW)
- **3 kW system and above:** ₹78,000 (₹30,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹18,000), capped at ₹78,000 for residential systems

The Surya Gujarat state top-up adds roughly ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 per kW for the first 3 kW. The slab is budget dependent and set annually, so treat ₹15,000 per kW as a working midpoint. Confirm the current figure on [geda.gujarat.gov.in](https://geda.gujarat.gov.in/) before you sign a contract. Above 3 kW, neither scheme pays more. A 5 kW system gets the same subsidy as a 3 kW system.

Worked example with 2026 Gujarat market pricing (hypothetical homeowner, typical installer quotes):

| System size | Gross cost | Central CFA | State top-up (est.) | Net cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | ₹55,000 | ₹30,000 | ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 | ₹5,000 to ₹15,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹1,10,000 | ₹60,000 | ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 | ₹10,000 to ₹30,000 |
| 3 kW | ₹1,65,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 | ₹27,000 to ₹57,000 |
| 5 kW | ₹2,75,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 | ₹1,37,000 to ₹1,67,000 |

Gross costs reflect standard tier 1 module installations in Gujarat cities. State top-up uses the ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 per kW estimate. Your figures will vary by installer and the live GEDA slab.

Two financing points sit behind this table. First, both subsidies arrive after commissioning, so you fund the gross cost upfront or through a loan. Gujarat banks and the portal's empanelled lenders offer solar loans near 7% for the balance. This keeps EMI below the monthly bill savings on most 3 kW cases. Second, structure height changes the budget. Elevated structures above 4 feet add ₹3,000 to ₹4,000 per kW to the gross cost, a figure the original scheme benchmark documents called out separately. Neither subsidies scale with structure cost, so a tall structure directly extends your payback.

The payback math is why Gujarat leads the country. At a ₹6.50 per unit blended residential tariff and about 4.8 peak sun hours, a 3 kW system saves ₹22,000 to ₹27,000 per year. Net costs under ₹57,000 put payback at roughly two to three years. Some 1 kW and 2 kW applicants recover their full cost in under 18 months. Gujarat has commissioned over 3.5 lakh residential systems under PM Surya Ghar, the highest of any state, according to [GEDA](https://geda.gujarat.gov.in/) (2026).

## Surya Gujarat Eligibility: The Fine Print

Eligibility looks simple on the portal and trips people up in the details. The core rules:

- **Residential connection only.** Your electricity connection must be a domestic category connection with one of the Gujarat DISCOMs or Torrent Power. Commercial, industrial, and agricultural connections do not qualify for this scheme.
- **Property ownership or consent.** You must own the property, or hold a registered society or association consent for rooftop rights. Tenants generally cannot apply unless the owner applies.
- **One claim per consumer number.** Each consumer number gets the subsidy once. A previous unsubsidised installation does not qualify retrospectively.
- **Aadhaar, bank, and consumer number must match.** The name on the electricity bill, the Aadhaar, and the bank account for direct benefit transfer must align. Mismatches are a top rejection cause.
- **Empanelled vendor mandatory.** The installer must be registered on the national portal for your district. Our guide on [choosing an empanelled vendor for PM Surya Ghar](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) explains how to verify registration before paying an advance.
- **ALMM listed equipment.** Modules and inverters must be on the MNRE ALMM list. A non listed inverter forfeits the entire subsidy.
- **Feasibility before installation.** Installing before your DISCOM issues feasibility approval makes the application ineligible, even if everything else is perfect.
- **Grid connected systems.** The scheme applies to on grid and hybrid systems with net metering. Pure off grid systems do not qualify.

One nuance worth stating plainly: the state top-up follows the central eligibility gate. If your PM Surya Ghar application is rejected, the Surya Gujarat claim fails with it. ## Which Gujarat DISCOM Handles Your Application

Gujarat is unusual in having five distribution utilities with distinct territories. Your DISCOM is printed on your electricity bill, and it owns feasibility, net metering, and commissioning. The subsidy money is the same everywhere; the processing culture is not.

| DISCOM | Territory | HQ | Portal notes | Typical timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UGVCL | North Gujarat (Mehsana, Gandhinagar, parts of Ahmedabad rural) | Mehsana | National portal plus UGVCL backend | 30 to 50 days |
| PGVCL | Saurashtra and Kutch (Rajkot, Jamnagar, Bhavnagar, Junagadh) | Rajkot | National portal plus PGVCL backend | 35 to 60 days |
| MGVCL | Central Gujarat (Vadodara, Anand, Bharuch, Panchmahal) | Vadodara | National portal plus MGVCL backend | 35 to 55 days |
| DGVCL | South Gujarat (Surat, Valsad, Navsari, Tapi, Vapi) | Surat | National portal plus DGVCL backend | 35 to 50 days |
| Torrent Power | Ahmedabad and Surat licensee areas | Ahmedabad | Torrent rooftop portal for DISCOM steps | 30 to 50 days |

Timelines are indicative ranges from registration to commissioning based on installer reported experience, not official SLAs. Volume districts run faster because the rooftop desks handle more applications.

Three DISCOM specific points matter. First, **Torrent Power consumers** run a split workflow. The central subsidy application goes through pmsuryaghar.gov.in, but feasibility, net metering, and commissioning go through Torrent's own rooftop solar portal. Do not assume a Torrent area application looks like a UGVCL one.

Second, **sanctioned load** interacts with system size differently across DISCOMs. If your planned system exceeds your sanctioned load, some circles ask for a load enhancement application first, which adds two to four weeks. Raise this question at the feasibility stage, not after installation.

Third, **net metering settlement** follows GERC rules statewide: exported units offset consumption, and surplus is settled at the DISCOM's notified rate. The [net metering India complete guide](/blog/net-metering-india-complete-guide/) explains the billing mechanics if your monthly statement is confusing after commissioning.

Territory boundaries catch people out in two specific places. Ahmedabad city is split between Torrent Power's licensee area and UGVCL's surrounding network. Two homes on opposite sides of a ring road can follow different portals. Surat has the same split between Torrent Power and DGVCL. If your application shows an unexpected DISCOM name, do not force it through. Check the bill, because filing under the wrong utility bounces at the first scrutiny stage and costs two to three weeks.

Rural applicants in PGVCL and UGVCL territories should also plan for longer net meter installation windows. Feeder-level transformer capacity checks are more common in agricultural belts, and inspection slots in smaller taluka towns are less frequent than in Rajkot or Mehsana city. None of this changes your subsidy amount. It only changes the calendar, so budget the longer end of the timeline range if you are outside a district headquarters.

## How to Apply for the Surya Gujarat Scheme: Step by Step

The sequence matters more than the paperwork. Most failures come from doing step 4 before step 2. For the national portal screens in detail, see [how to apply for PM Surya Ghar online](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-apply-online/). Here is the full Gujarat sequence:

1. **Check your DISCOM and consumer number.** Look at your latest electricity bill. Note the consumer number, sanctioned load, and whether your supplier is UGVCL, PGVCL, MGVCL, DGVCL, or Torrent Power.
2. **Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in.** Enter your state, DISCOM, and consumer number. Complete e-KYC with Aadhaar and your mobile number. Save the application reference number.
3. **Register for the state component.** Complete the Surya Gujarat or GEDA registration for the state top-up in parallel. The two registrations can start on the same day. The state subsidy does not follow automatically from the central application.
4. **Wait for DISCOM feasibility approval.** The DISCOM checks technical feasibility at your site or desk level, typically within 7 to 15 working days. Do not let any installer start work before this approval arrives.
5. **Select an empanelled vendor.** Pick an installer from the national portal's district list. Verify their registration ID on the portal itself. Confirm the quoted inverter and module models appear on the MNRE ALMM list.
6. **Install and apply for net metering.** The vendor installs the system and submits the work completion report. The DISCOM inspects, installs the bidirectional meter at no cost under the scheme, and issues the commissioning certificate.
7. **Upload documents on both portals.** Upload the commissioning certificate, vendor invoice, and bank details on pmsuryaghar.gov.in for the CFA. Upload the same set on the state channel for the Surya Gujarat top-up.
8. **Track disbursement.** Central CFA typically lands in 30 to 60 days as a direct bank transfer. The state top-up follows GEDA's cycle, usually another 30 to 60 days. Both go to the same Aadhaar seeded account.

Your empanelled installer should drive steps 5 through 7. Your job is to verify each gate: feasibility before installation, ALMM before payment, and uploads after commissioning.

Keep one folder with every document before you start. You will need the latest electricity bill, Aadhaar, and a cancelled cheque or passbook copy of the Aadhaar seeded account. Also keep property ownership proof or society consent, plus the vendor's invoice after installation. Photograph the system, the new bidirectional meter, and the commissioning certificate the day the DISCOM engineer visits. Half of all document-related delays we see come from applicants hunting for a paper that existed but was never scanned.

## Why Surya Gujarat Applications Get Rejected

Rejections cluster around a short list of causes. The [PM Surya Ghar rejection reasons](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-rejection-reasons/) post covers the national list; these are the ones that hit Gujarat applicants hardest:

- **Installation before feasibility.** The single most common fatal error. Eager vendors start work to close the sale, and the application dies at verification.
- **Non empanelled vendor.** A competent installer who is not registered for your district cannot complete a subsidised application. No exceptions.
- **Non ALMM equipment.** One unlisted module or inverter model forfeits both subsidies. Verify model numbers, not just brand names.
- **Aadhaar and bank mismatch.** The bank account must be Aadhaar seeded for DBT, and names must match the electricity bill. A spouse's account with a different name spelling fails.
- **Duplicate claim.** A consumer number that already received subsidy, including from the pre 2024 state scheme in some records, cannot claim again.
- **Wrong consumer category.** Applications filed on a commercial or agricultural connection are rejected at scrutiny. Convert the connection category first if the premise is genuinely residential.
- **Missing or inconsistent commissioning documents.** Invoice amounts, system capacity, and the commissioning certificate must agree. A 3.2 kW invoice against a 3 kW application triggers manual review.

> **What most Gujarat applicants get wrong:** they treat the subsidy as a discount the installer applies at billing. It is not. Both subsidies arrive after commissioning as direct bank transfers. If an installer's quote already deducts the subsidy, ask exactly how and when they recover it, and get the answer in writing.

A rejected application is not always final. Document and mismatch rejections can usually be corrected and resubmitted through the portal. Eligibility failures, like a second claim on the same consumer number, cannot.

## Myths About the Surya Gujarat Scheme, Corrected

**Myth 1: "Surya Gujarat replaced PM Surya Ghar in Gujarat."** Wrong. The central scheme is the primary subsidy everywhere in India including Gujarat. Surya Gujarat is the state layer on top. You need both registrations, and the central eligibility rules govern both payouts.

**Myth 2: "The state gives 40% subsidy, so I pay almost nothing."** The 40% and 20% structure was the original 2019 design. These rates come from the [GUVNL scheme document](https://www.svnit.ac.in/Unnat_Bharat/PI/pdf/Sustainabe%20Energy/Schemes%20by%20Government%20of%20Gujarat/Surya%20Urja%20Rooftop%20Yojana%20Gujarat/SURYA-Gujarat%20Surya%20Urja%20Rooftop%20Yojana.pdf) (2019). Since 2024 the state contribution works as a per kW top-up alongside the central CFA. Quoting the old percentages in 2026 is a red flag that a vendor is working from stale material.

**Myth 3: "Bigger systems get proportionally bigger subsidies."** No. Both subsidies cap at 3 kW. A 5 kW system gets exactly the same ₹78,000 plus state top-up as a 3 kW system. Size the system to your consumption, not to the subsidy. Above roughly 400 units a month, a 4 kW or 5 kW system still makes financial sense on savings alone. The subsidy argument ends at 3 kW.

**Myth 4: "Any Gujarat electrician can install and I claim later."** Retrospective claims do not exist. No feasibility approval and no empanelled vendor means no subsidy, full stop. This is the most expensive myth in the market: it converts a ₹78,000 benefit into zero.

The honest tradeoff to acknowledge: the scheme's process adds 60 to 120 days and paperwork in exchange for ₹1,00,000 or more of support. For most households that trade is obviously worth it. For buyers who want a system live within two weeks, a non subsidised installation is faster, and some choose it knowingly. The exception is commercial premises, which were never eligible for this scheme. They should evaluate [PM Surya Ghar versus KUSUM or open access](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-vs-kusum-yojana/) routes instead.
