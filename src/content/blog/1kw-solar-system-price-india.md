A 1 kW rooftop solar system does not have one honest nationwide price. The best official reference is the **₹50,000 per kW benchmark** in the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy's PM Surya Ghar residential guidelines. That figure has applied to the first 2 kW since 13 February 2024.

It is a policy benchmark, not a compulsory vendor rate. The same guidelines let the consumer and registered vendor agree the system and financial terms. Your quotation therefore needs to show what its number buys.

> **TL;DR**
> - MNRE's installed-system benchmark is **₹50,000 for the first 1 kW**, effective 13 February 2024.
> - Standard-category central financial assistance is **₹30,000 for an eligible 1 kWp installation**. Special-category state and UT rates differ.
> - ₹50,000 minus ₹30,000 leaves **₹20,000**, but that is benchmark arithmetic, not a guaranteed final cost.
> - Two named 550 W panel listings checked on 26 September 2026 totalled **₹20,598 to ₹23,068 for 1.10 kWp of panels only**. They did not include an inverter, structure, wiring, labour or grid work.
> - Equipment-only and installed quotes are not comparable until both show tax, delivery, structure, protection, installation and DISCOM scope.
> - Qbits publishes no inverter price. Its products remain quote-only, and Qbits does not sell panels or install rooftop systems.

**Short version.** Use ₹50,000 as the official 1 kW scheme benchmark before central assistance, not as a guaranteed market price. For an eligible 1 kWp system in a general-category state, the published CFA is ₹30,000. Compare vendors on the gross installed contract before subtracting assistance.

Search results often mix a loose panel subtotal with a complete rooftop quotation. The table separates the five different numbers.

| Price layer | What it covers | Sourced figure as of 26 September 2026 | What it does not prove |

| Panel-only | Photovoltaic modules only | Two named 550 W retail pairs totalled ₹20,598 to ₹23,068 for 1.10 kWp | A national market range or installed cost |

## The official installed-system benchmark

The [MNRE residential CFA guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/operational-guidelines-for-implementation-of-the-component-central-financial-assistance-to-residential-consumers-of-pm-surya-ghar-muft-bijli-yojana/) publish both the benchmark cost and the assistance rate.

For general-category states, the relevant 1 kWp calculation is:

| Input | Published amount | Source scope |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Benchmark cost for the first 2 kW | ₹50,000 per kW | PM Surya Ghar residential CFA benchmark, effective 13 February 2024 |
| CFA for the first 2 kWp | ₹30,000 per kWp | General-category residential rate |
| 1 kWp benchmark remainder | ₹20,000 | ₹50,000 minus ₹30,000 |

The ₹20,000 result is useful for understanding the policy. It is not a promise that your bank balance needs only ₹20,000.

Your gross contract may include a more expensive structure, long cable runs, roof-access work or fees outside the vendor's base scope. You may also need to fund the gross amount before assistance is processed. Ask the vendor to show the payment stages rather than presenting the post-CFA number as a shop discount.

Special-category states and UTs have a different CFA schedule. State support and DISCOM charges can also vary. This article does not combine those changing local amounts into a national post-subsidy range.

## What must be inside an installed quote

The word "installed" is meaningless unless the quotation defines it. Ask for these lines separately:

1. exact panel manufacturer, model, wattage, quantity and DCR status;
2. exact inverter manufacturer, model and rated AC output;
3. mounting-structure material, coating, height and roof-fixing method;
4. DC and AC cable make, size and route length;
5. isolators, surge-protection devices, breakers and junction boxes;
6. earthing electrodes, conductor size and testing;
7. transport, lifting, installation and commissioning;
8. portal application, inspection coordination, meter and DISCOM charges;
9. tax by line, with a clear inclusive or exclusive total; and
10. written warranty terms for panels, inverter and workmanship as three separate commitments.

The equipment-only subtotal ends before site labour and commissioning. The installed pre-subsidy figure is the gross contract value after all stated inclusions. The post-subsidy estimate comes last.

If a vendor starts with "after subsidy" and never reveals the gross contract, you cannot compare the quote.

## What PM Surya Ghar changes at 1 kW

Under the MNRE guidelines dated 7 June 2024, standard-category CFA is ₹30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp. The scheme calculates capacity from the rated DC module capacity, irrespective of inverter rating.

The same guidelines require domestically manufactured modules made from domestically manufactured cells when CFA is claimed. A cheap non-DCR panel listing therefore cannot be dropped into a subsidy calculation.

The process also matters:

- the consumer chooses a registered vendor and agrees the system and financial terms;
- the installation must meet the scheme and local DISCOM requirements;
- the DISCOM physically inspects the installation before CFA processing; and
- assistance depends on eligibility and completed documentation.

For the application sequence, use the [PM Surya Ghar guide](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-yojana-complete-guide/). Before paying an advance, [verify the vendor route](/blog/empanelled-vendor-pm-surya-ghar/) on the current national portal and with your DISCOM.

No inverter manufacturer or installer can guarantee the government's approval or payment outcome.

## Why a 1 kW system can cost more per kW

Several project costs do not shrink in direct proportion to module capacity. A site survey, labour mobilisation, distribution board work, earthing, application handling and meter coordination still exist on a small system.

MNRE's benchmark reflects some scale effect. It sets ₹50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹45,000 for the additional kilowatt up to 3 kW. That does not prove a market discount, but it explains why a homeowner should compare 1 kW, 2 kW and 3 kW gross quotations before choosing the lowest ticket size.

Price the [2 kW system](/blog/2kw-solar-system-price-india/) and [3 kW system](/blog/3kw-solar-system-price-india/) with identical brands, structure and service scope. Then compare total cost, cost per kWp and fit for your electricity use.

## A generation scenario, not an output promise

Capacity should follow the site and consumption, not the subsidy slab. A transparent 1 kWp planning scenario can use:

- rated capacity: 1 kWp;
- assumed peak sun hours: 4.5 per day;
- assumed performance ratio: 0.75; and
- operating days: 365.

The arithmetic is 1 × 4.5 × 0.75 × 365 = **1,232 kWh a year**, or about 103 kWh a month.

Those inputs are assumptions, not Qbits or installer performance claims. Shade, orientation, module temperature, soiling, downtime and local solar resource change the result. Ask every bidder to show its irradiation source and loss assumptions.

Do not convert the 1,232 kWh scenario into a savings or payback promise without your current DISCOM tariff and settlement rules. Import units avoided at your retail tariff and surplus units settled under a different rule may not have the same value.

## On-grid and backup quotes are different purchases

A standard on-grid inverter stops exporting and shuts down during a grid outage. Anti-islanding protection prevents the solar system from energising a utility line while workers may be repairing it.

A backup system needs a hybrid architecture, compatible battery storage, a defined backup-load circuit and additional protection. Its price cannot be compared with an on-grid quote as if both provide the same outcome.

Qbits sells on-grid and hybrid inverters, not panels, batteries or installation. It does not publish inverter prices. If a Qbits inverter is proposed, ask for the exact model, current written warranty terms and a line-item quote. Do not accept a whole-system price presented as a Qbits product price.

## Compare two 1 kW quotes in this order

Use the same sequence for every vendor:

1. Confirm rated DC capacity from panel model, wattage and quantity.
2. Check whether each figure is panel-only, equipment-only or fully installed.
3. Compare the gross pre-CFA total before looking at the subsidy estimate.
4. Match the exact panel and inverter models, not just their brand names.
5. Mark every excluded charge, especially delivery, roof work, meter and DISCOM fees.
6. Confirm DCR and current scheme requirements from primary documents.
7. Ask who carries application, inspection and commissioning responsibility.
8. Treat CFA as conditional until the competent authority processes it.

This prevents a ₹20,000 panel subtotal from appearing cheaper than a ₹50,000 installed benchmark when the two numbers describe different purchases.

## The decision

For planning, use **₹50,000 before CFA** as the official 1 kW benchmark and **₹20,000 after standard-category CFA** only as benchmark arithmetic. Neither number replaces a dated, itemised local quotation.

Compare gross scope first. Check eligibility second. Subtract assistance last.
