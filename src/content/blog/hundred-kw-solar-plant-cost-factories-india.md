There is no reliable public market range for the 100 kW solar plant cost of a factory in India, and this post does not make one up. We searched on 30 October 2026 for awarded tender rates, state agency rate contracts, regulator orders, government reports and company disclosures.

The newest dated figure we could open is ₹40,000 per kW. It is the cost norm that the Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) set for rooftop plants of 1 kW to 2,000 kW outside its domestic 1 to 10 kW class, in an order of 1 June 2023. The benchmark of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) for a 10 kW to 100 kW plant was ₹48,000 per kW in July 2019. Both are regulatory yardsticks. Neither is a price anyone paid, and neither is from 2026.

So the useful question changes. A factory owner comparing three engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) quotes needs to know three things: what each number includes, which rules moved in 2026, and what the plant earns on their own tariff. This post gives the dated evidence with its basis and the rules that change a quote. It adds a Gujarat payback grid with every input labelled illustrative, and a sheet that puts three quotes on one footing.

> **Short version**
> - No dated 2026 awarded rate, rate-contract result or company disclosure for a 100 kW rooftop plant was found. A range built from installer blogs would be a guess.
> - Dated, openable benchmarks exist: MNRE ₹55,000 per kW (2018 statement), MNRE ₹48,000 per kW (July 2019 order), KERC ₹40,000 per kW (June 2023 order). They fall by about a quarter, and none is a market price.
> - "100 kW" can mean 100 kWp of panels or 100 kW of inverter. The two readings differ by the DC:AC ratio, often a fifth.
> - A factory plant gets no central subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar guidelines. Accelerated depreciation for solar is 40% of written-down value on the Income Tax Department's schedule, not the 80% some guides still print.
> - Qbits sells inverters, quotes per model and quantity, and gives no price here.

## 100 kW solar plant cost benchmarks: five dated figures, read carefully

Five dated benchmarks for rooftop plant cost are openable, and none is from 2026. The basis is printed next to each. We re-opened the PIB release, the MNRE memorandum and the KERC order on 31 October 2026, and their figures are unchanged. Read the last column before you quote any number from this table to a bank or a board.

| Date | Source | Size band | Per kW | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 Aug 2018 | MNRE, via a PIB release of a Lok Sabha reply | 10 to 100 kWp | ₹55,000 | A benchmark MNRE "worked out" for rooftop plants. The release does not state what it covers |
| 16 Jul 2019 | MNRE OM 318/33/2019-Grid Connected Rooftop, for 2019-20 | Above 10 kW to 100 kW | ₹48,000 (₹48 per Wp) | A subsidy benchmark. Inclusive of "total system cost and its installation, commissioning, transportation, insurance, five year AMC/CMC, and applicable fees and taxes" |
| 16 Jul 2019 | Same memorandum | Above 100 kW to 500 kW | ₹45,000 | Same basis |
| 2 Aug 2018 | Same PIB release | 100 to 500 kWp | ₹53,000 | Same statement as the first row |
| 1 Jun 2023 | KERC order on solar tariff and norms, FY24 | 1 to 2,000 kW, outside the domestic 1 to 10 kW class | ₹40,000 | A cost norm for setting a tariff. KERC derived it pro rata from its MW-scale build-up, in which GST is added and customs duty and GST to be reimbursed are then deducted. It is not a tax-inclusive price |

Four readings follow, and none is a 2026 number.

1. **The direction is down.** From ₹55,000 (2018) to ₹40,000 (2023) is about 27% lower for the same size band. Module prices explain much of it. Neither the benchmarks nor the regulator's order tells you where a 2026 quote should land.
2. **The bases differ.** The 2019 figure includes five years of maintenance, insurance and taxes. The 2023 norm is a tariff input and carries none of that. Do not line the two up as if they measure the same thing.
3. **Tender-discovered rates can sit above a benchmark.** The KERC order records a stakeholder (KREA) table of rates it says BESCOM discovered in a rooftop scheme: ₹48,900 per kW for 10 to 100 kW and ₹60,312 per kW for 100 to 500 kW. The order does not date the tender, and we did not open BESCOM's document. We cite it only to show that a discovered rate and a benchmark are different things.
4. **MNRE has no reason to publish a factory benchmark any more.** The PM Surya Ghar guidelines state that "no CFA will be provided to non-residential segments of consumers (including government segment, commercial and industrial segment etc.)". The benchmark exists to size subsidy. We found no MNRE benchmark for 10 kW to 100 kW after FY2021-22.

What none of this tells you: today's price, any other state's price, any developer's price, or what your roof costs. Use the table only as a plausibility check. A quote far above the 2019 benchmark, or far below the 2023 norm, deserves a line-by-line question. Neither case proves the quote wrong.

## What "100 kW" means in a quote

An inverter is rated in kW AC. Panels are rated in kWp DC. A plant sold as "100 kW" can be 100 kWp of panels on one 100 kW inverter, or 120 kWp of panels on the same inverter. Many designs run a DC:AC ratio above 1.0 because it lifts output in weak light, at the cost of some clipping at noon.

The label changes the arithmetic. Here are two quotes that both say "100 kW". Every figure is invented for the exercise.

| | Quote A | Quote B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Panels (kWp DC) | 100 | 120 |
| Inverter (kW AC) | 100 | 100 |
| Total, ex-GST (illustrative) | ₹40,00,000 | ₹45,00,000 |
| Per kWp DC | ₹40,000 | ₹37,500 |
| Per kW AC | ₹40,000 | ₹45,000 |
| Units a year at 1,450 per kWp, before clipping | 1,45,000 | 1,74,000 |

Quote B looks dearer per kW AC and cheaper per kWp. It also generates more, though clipping above 100 kW AC will take some of that back. Neither column is wrong. A fair comparison needs both denominators written on the quote. Net-metering caps can count either one, as the section on limits below shows. The [inverter count guide](/blog/how-many-inverters-for-hundred-kw-to-one-mw-rooftop/) shows how a 100 kW plant splits into one unit or two.

## What a 100 kW quote has to contain

We found no sourced split of a 100 kW plant's cost by component, so we show none. A regulator's utility-scale table in the 2023 KERC order puts modules including duty at about 60% of the pre-GST capital cost of a ground-mounted plant. A rooftop adds roof work, longer cable runs and approvals, and the order does not carry that split across. What we can give is the list of lines and where each one gets padded or left out.

| Line | What sets it | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modules | Wp per panel, technology, ALMM List-I model, source of cells | Model, Wp, List-I entry and revision date, cell source |
| Inverters | AC rating, number of maximum power point trackers (MPPT), one unit or two, IP rating | Exact model and quantity, tracker count, datasheet |
| Mounting structure | RCC slab, metal sheet roof or ground; wind loading; coating | Roof type, material, coating, wind design basis |
| DC and AC cables, earthing, lightning protection | Distance from array to inverter to panel; protection class | Cable lengths and sizes, AC cable to the main panel |
| LT panel, metering, transformer work | Contract demand, voltage, feeder headroom | Whether any panel or transformer change is in or out |
| Monitoring and data logging | Communication option, SCADA ask | Which hardware, and whether it is a separate line |
| Civil and roof work | Slab condition, waterproofing, access, walkways, fall protection | Who certifies the roof load, and who pays for repairs |
| Net-metering, DISCOM fees, safety inspection | State rules, feasibility report, charges | Which fees are in the price and which are yours |
| Freight, insurance, site storage | Distance, delivery basis | Delivered to your gate or ex-works |
| Warranty, maintenance, cleaning | Contract term, scope, spares | Written documents and the cleaning plan |
| GST | HSN (product classification) code, goods or composite works supply | The entry and rate on each line, in writing |

Two lines cause most of the surprises. The first is interconnection. A 100 kW plant on a factory with a small contract demand may meet a transformer or feeder limit, and the cost of the fix usually sits outside the EPC's price. The second is roof work. A slab that needs waterproofing or strengthening before panels go up can add cost that the first quote left blank. Shading is a separate risk: neighbouring buildings and rooftop tanks decide how many modules actually yield, so ask for a shadow study. What the maintenance and warranty lines should say is covered in the [O&M contract scope guide](/blog/ci-solar-om-contract-inverter-scope/).

## The 2026 rules that change a quote

### Modules: ALMM List-I and List-II

MNRE's Office Memorandum 283/53/2026-GRID SOLAR, dated 18 July 2026, states that net-metering and open-access projects commissioned on or after 1 June 2026 "shall mandatorily source their solar PV modules from ALMM List-I and solar PV cells from ALMM List-II". The same memorandum gives a limited window. Net-metering projects can commission with an exemption from the cell requirement until 31 December 2026. Those commissioned after 31 December 2026 must comply with List-II. We read the memorandum as a scanned page on 30 October 2026, and again from MNRE's own file on 31 October 2026.

For a buyer this has two effects on a quote. A project that will commission after 31 December 2026 needs modules whose cells are enlisted, so ask about availability and lead time. A project that commissions before that date should record the date and the module model against the exemption. Ask every bidder which List-I model and which cell source they price, and which revision of the list they checked. MNRE's list pages carry numbered revisions. We opened List-II's 13th Revision dated 28 October 2026 and a List-I update dated 6 July 2026, and a later List-I update of 3 August 2026 is listed.

ALMM names modules and cells, and the [ALMM scope guide](/blog/almm-list-phase-iii-guide/) explains both lists. We found no ALMM list for inverters, so a bidder who says an inverter is "on ALMM" is using the word loosely. Ask for the inverter's test reports and the DISCOM's acceptance instead. Whether your own plant falls inside these rules depends on the arrangement and date, so confirm it in writing.

### GST

We read Notification 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate) (G.S.R. 641(E), 17 September 2025, in force from 22 September 2025), and the matching Integrated Tax (Rate) text. Schedule I (2.5% central tax, 5% integrated tax) entry 437 covers "solar power-based devices", "solar power generator" and photovoltaic cells, whether or not assembled into modules, with their parts. Schedule II (9% central tax, 18% integrated tax) entry 477 covers heading 8504, "electrical transformers, static converters (for example, rectifiers) and inductors", other than chargers or charging stations for electric vehicles. We re-read both texts on 31 October 2026. The central tax copy we read is hosted on a non-government site, and the integrated tax copy is an ICAI text hosted on CBIC's site. 

Entry 437 carries an explanation. Where such goods are supplied together with other goods and services, one being the construction or installation service listed at serial 38 of Notification 11/2017, the goods are deemed to be 70% of the gross consideration and the rest 30% is the service.

That is why we state no single GST rate for a whole plant. The modules, the inverter, the structure, the cables and the installation can each land in a different place. The notification does not name inverters in either entry, and in a search on 31 October 2026 we found no CBIC circular or FAQ that settles the point. The [GST guide](/blog/gst-on-solar-inverters-2026/) works through the entries. The practical step is to compare quotes ex-GST and ask each bidder to state the HSN and rate for every line. We did not read Notification 11/2017's rate for the installation service, and we did not verify any customs duty rate.

### Depreciation

The Income Tax Department's depreciation page states that it carries the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, as amended by the Finance Act, 2026. In its Part A table, under machinery and plant, "renewable energy devices" includes "solar power generating systems". Its three rate columns read 80, 80 and 40: 80% of written-down value for assessment years 2003-04 to 2005-06, 80% for 2006-07 to 2017-18, and 40% from assessment year 2018-19 onwards.

We re-opened the page on 31 October 2026, and the three columns still read 80, 80 and 40. So 40% of written-down value is the rate on that schedule today. Guides that still print 80% describe an older rule. The Department's site now also lists the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Their pages returned access errors to us on 31 October 2026, so we did not open their depreciation schedule. Confirm the rate for the tax year your plant is put to use. Whether any first-year rate applies in full depends on the put-to-use date and your tax regime, and that is a question for your chartered accountant.

## What does a 100 kW plant earn? A Gujarat payback worked through

Cost is half of a payback. The other half is what a unit of solar is worth to you, and that is decided by your state's rules and your tariff. Our Gujarat posts read the primary text, so we reuse their readings here and link to the fullest one, [solar for diamond polishing units in Surat](/blog/solar-for-diamond-polishing-units-surat/).

The inputs, all read for those posts on 30 October 2026:

- Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (GERC) net-metering regulations as amended in 2022 (clause 9.2): Gujarat allows net metering from 1 kW to 1,000 kW for a captive plant. For a demand-based low-tension (LT) consumer the set-off window is 07:00 to 18:00 in the same billing cycle. For a high-tension (HT) consumer it is the same window within the same day. Surplus is bought at ₹2.25 a unit for the first five years for the regulation's micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) category, then at 75% of a GUVNL bid average.
- Banking charge (clause 9.2.11): ₹1.50 per unit of solar consumed for demand-based consumers, ₹1.10 for MSME units and for consumers that are not demand-based.
- GERC tariff schedule effective 1 April 2026, Rate LTMD: 460 paise per unit. It adds 45 paise from 07:00 to 11:00 and 18:00 to 22:00, and subtracts 60 paise from 11:00 to 17:00 with a time-of-day meter. Most solar output falls in the discounted window, so an avoided unit is worth nearer ₹4.26 than ₹4.60. That figure assumes 75% of units at 400 paise and 25% at 505 paise.

We did not read other states' tariff schedules for this post, so the worked grid below is Gujarat's only.

**This is an illustrative case built for this post. No quote, no Qbits figure and no site result sits behind it.** The plant is 100 kWp. The yield is 1,450 kWh per kWp a year. This is the Surat figure from those posts: PVGIS modelled output of about 1,550 less roughly 6% for dust and downtime. Running cost is 1% of the installed cost a year. Banking is ₹1.10 a unit. The "other charges avoided" column stands in for FPPAS and similar per-unit lines on your bill, which we did not verify. Read yours from your bills.

Payback is installed cost divided by (units generated times net saving per unit, less running cost). Net saving is ₹4.26 less banking plus the other charges avoided. Annual units are 1,45,000.

| Installed cost per kWp (illustrative) | Other charges avoided: ₹0 per unit | ₹1.50 per unit | ₹3.00 per unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹35,000 (₹35 lakh for 100 kWp) | 8.3 years | 5.5 years | 4.1 years |
| ₹45,000 (₹45 lakh) | 10.9 years | 7.1 years | 5.3 years |
| ₹55,000 (₹55 lakh) | 13.6 years | 8.9 years | 6.6 years |

The three cost rows span the benchmark ladder above, from below the 2023 KERC norm to the 2018 MNRE level. They are round numbers, not market quotes. At the ₹1.50 banking charge, the ₹45,000 row moves to 12.7, 7.9 and 5.7 years.

On the tariff text alone, a Gujarat LT unit does not get a short payback. Everything turns on the lines your bill adds to the energy rate, on the banking charge your DISCOM applies, and on what you pay per kWp. The grid excludes demand-charge savings, finance cost, degradation, inverter replacement and tax. For an HT unit, the energy charge and time bands differ and the same-day set-off matters more.

Tax moves the answer less than older guides suggest. Take the ₹45 lakh row. A first-year deduction at 40% is ₹18 lakh. At an assumed 25% tax rate that is ₹4.5 lakh of tax saved in year one. At 80% it would be ₹9 lakh, which is double. Treat the ₹4.5 lakh as an upper bound for a company that can use the full deduction, and leave it out of the grid until your accountant confirms it.

## Net-metering limits and contract demand decide the size you can build

A 100 kW plant clears every state ceiling in our [state-wise limits guide](/blog/net-metering-capacity-limits-commercial-by-state/), which runs from 500 kW to 5 MW for commercial consumers. The cap that can bite is the link to sanctioned load or contract demand, and the unit it counts. Taking a plant of 120 kWp with a 100 kW inverter, the guide's readings give this, checked on 30 October 2026:

| State | What the rule counts | Smallest contract demand that fits this plant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gujarat | Not stated for commercial consumers in the amendments read. No percentage cap, up to sanctioned load or contract demand | 100 to 120 |
| Maharashtra | AC output, against contract demand | 100 kVA |
| Andhra Pradesh | DC, against contracted demand | 120 |
| Telangana | 80% of sanctioned load or contracted demand, unit not stated | 120 / 0.8 = 150 on a DC reading |
| Punjab | AC, against 70% of contract demand in kVA times 0.9 | 100 / (0.7 x 0.9) = 159 kVA |

A factory with 100 kVA of contract demand therefore builds the full plant in Maharashtra and cannot in Punjab. That changes the plant you can price, which changes the cost you should be comparing. The same guide covers transformer caps and the questions to put to the DISCOM in writing.

## How to compare three 100 kW solar quotes on one sheet

Ask each bidder to fill the same sheet. Compare the "ex-GST total" row only after every line above it is filled or marked "excluded".

| Line | What to write | Quote A | Quote B | Quote C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | kWp DC, kW AC, DC:AC ratio | | | |
| Modules | Make, model, Wp, List-I entry, cell source, commissioning date against 31 December 2026 | | | |
| Inverter | Model, quantity, AC rating, MPPT count, documents | | | |
| Structure | Roof type, material, coating, wind design basis | | | |
| Cables and protection | DC and AC lengths, earthing, lightning protection | | | |
| Interconnection | Panel, metering, any transformer work: in or out | | | |
| Approvals | Net-metering application, DISCOM fees, safety inspection: in or out | | | |
| Civil and roof work | Waterproofing, strengthening, access: in or out | | | |
| Monitoring | Hardware, communication, SCADA | | | |
| Delivery and insurance | Delivered or ex-works, cover during transit and erection | | | |
| Warranty and maintenance | Written documents, term, scope, cleaning plan | | | |
| GST | HSN and rate for each line | | | |
| Payment terms and validity | Stage payments, retention, date the quote lapses | | | |
| Generation commitment | Annual kWh stated, basis and who bears a shortfall | | | |

Then do three things. Convert each total to per kWp and per kW AC. Add the excluded lines at your own assumed cost, and mark them as assumptions.
