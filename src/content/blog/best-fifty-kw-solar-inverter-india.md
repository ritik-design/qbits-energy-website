**The best 50 kW solar inverter for a rooftop is the one with enough trackers for your roof planes and enough current on each tracker for your module's strings.** At 50 kW the rated power is the same on every datasheet, and the maximum AC current agrees to within about 10%. The sheets differ on tracker count (four to six), the current each tracker accepts (20 A to 45 A), the weight (33 kg to 82 kg) and how much of the safety and monitoring package comes as standard.

A 50 kW unit usually serves several roof planes, and one tracker can hold only one orientation. A 600 Wp class module draws about 14.5 A per string and a 700 Wp class module about 17.4 A. Whether a tracker takes one string or two decides if your layout fits, and the sheets disagree on exactly that.

## What should an EPC check before specifying a 50 kW inverter?

Ten checks apply, and each should be run for every shortlisted model before price is compared.

1. **Tracker count against roof planes.** A tracker sets one operating voltage for all its strings, so each orientation and each shaded zone wants its own tracker. At 50 kW the printed count runs from four to six.
2. **Tracker current against module current.** Multiply strings per tracker by the module's operating current (Imp) and compare the result with the tracker's maximum input current. Do the same with short-circuit current (Isc) against the printed short-circuit rating. Printed tracker currents at 50 kW run from 20 A to 45 A, and modules of 600 Wp and above run 14 A to 18 A per string.
3. **Strings per tracker.** Some sheets print a cap (two strings on most, four on one Qbits family) and some also print a per-string or per-input limit. Read both.
4. **DC to AC ratio and clipping.** Look for a printed maximum DC power or a recommended array size, then model the ratio for your site. The [DC/AC ratio guide](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/) covers the method. At 50 kW the printed ceilings run from 65 kW to 75 kW.
5. **DC window against string length.** On the coldest morning the open-circuit voltage must stay under the maximum. On a hot afternoon the operating voltage must stay above the floor of the MPPT window. Use the [string sizing worksheet](/blog/solar-string-sizing-ocp-india/) with your module and your site's lowest temperature.
6. **AC output, 415 V cable and breaker.** Size from the sheet's printed maximum output current, not from kW divided by voltage. Check that the printed grid voltage range covers your LT supply.
7. **Anti-islanding and grid-code settings.** Look for the anti-islanding row and for the grid standards the sheet lists. The [anti-islanding guide](/blog/anti-islanding-protection-solar-inverters/) explains what Indian trip limits apply. Which settings your DISCOM requires on the exact model is a question for the DISCOM, not for a datasheet.
8. **Communication and monitoring for C&I.** See what is standard, what costs extra, and whether the sheet names a protocol. Owners and O&M contractors both want inverter data without buying a separate gateway.
9. **Enclosure, cooling and weight for a roof.** Compare IP rating, cooling type, operating range and the weight someone has to carry up and fix to a wall or frame.
10. **Service route.** Find out who attends a fault, who pays freight and labour, and how long a replacement takes. The datasheets are silent on this, so get the answer in writing for every brand.

## Worked example: a 200 kW AC rooftop as four 50 kW units

This is illustrative, not a real plant. Assumptions:

- A C&I rooftop of 200 kW AC, built as four 50 kW units. The comparison of unit counts with 100 kW units is in [the rooftop inverter-count post](/blog/how-many-inverters-for-hundred-kw-to-one-mw-rooftop/) and is not repeated here.
- Each unit serves three roof planes: a south plane, an east plane and a west plane. Each plane gets two strings, six strings per unit.
- An illustrative 600 Wp module with Voc 49.5 V, Vmp 41.5 V, Isc 15.4 A and Imp 14.5 A. Both voltages move by -0.26% per C. The 700 Wp case uses an illustrative module of Imp 17.4 A and Isc 18.3 A with the same voltages and 16-module strings of 11.2 kWp, six per unit. Substitute your module's values.
- Design temperatures are 5 C at the coldest and 65 C cell temperature at the hottest.

**String length.** At 5 C a single module gives 52.1 V open circuit. At 65 C its operating voltage falls to 37.2 V. An 18-module string reads 937 V at the cold Voc and 669 V at the hot Vmp. That sits inside every window above, including GoodWe's 140 V floor and Solis's 180 V floor. The longest string the 1100 V maximum allows is 21 modules, at 1,094 V. A 22-module string reads 1,146 V and is over the limit. The QB-50KTLC start-up voltage of 300 V is also met by an 18-module string, which reads 669 V hot.

**DC to AC ratio.** Six strings of 18 modules is 108 modules or 64.8 kWp per unit, a ratio of 1.30 on 50 kW. Across four units that is 259.2 kWp against 200 kW AC. The energy lost to clipping at a 1.30 ratio depends on irradiance and temperature at your site, so model it with the [DC/AC ratio guide](/blog/dc-oversizing-in-solar/). The 64.8 kWp figure sits under every printed or implied ceiling in the input table, the lowest being the 65 kW on the QB-50KTLC sheet.

**Tracker current.** Under the strict rule from check 2, strings per tracker is the printed tracker current divided by the module's Imp, rounded down. A pair of 600 Wp strings draws 29.0 A at Imp and 30.8 A at Isc. A pair of 700 Wp strings draws 34.8 A and 36.6 A.

| Inverter | Strings the trackers can carry at 600 Wp | At 700 Wp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growatt MAX50KTL3LV (6 x 26 A) | 1 per tracker, 6 in total | 1 per tracker, 6 in total |
| GoodWe GW50K-SDT-C30 (4 x 40 A) | 2 per tracker, 8 in total | 2 per tracker, 8 in total |
| Huawei SUN2000-50KTL-M3 (4 x 30 A) | 2 per tracker, 8 in total | 1 per tracker, 4 in total |
| Qbits QB-50KTLC-Plus (40, 40, 20, 20 A) | 2, 2, 1, 1; 6 in total | 2, 2, 1, 1; 6 in total |
| Qbits QB-50KTLC (3 x 45 A) | 3 per tracker by current; 4 per tracker on the sheet; 9 in total | 2 per tracker, 6 in total |
| Solis S5-GC50K (5 x 32 A) | 2 per tracker, 10 in total | 1 per tracker, 5 in total |
| Sungrow SG50CX-P2 (4 x 30 A) | 2 per tracker, 8 in total | 1 per tracker, 4 in total |

The QB-50KTLC count is by current only. Its printed 65 kW DC ceiling stops at six strings of 10.8 kWp, which is 64.8 kWp. At 600 Wp, all seven fit the six-string layout on current alone. The Qbits sheet has no Isc rating, so its Isc check stays open.

At 700 Wp, the two Qbits models, the Growatt and the GoodWe can carry six strings. On the Solis, Huawei and Sungrow, every tracker takes one 700 Wp string, so those units hold 5, 4 and 4 strings.

The GoodWe's 40 A trackers take two 700 Wp strings each (34.8 A at Imp, 36.6 A at Isc under its printed 52 A), so it holds eight and keeps one tracker free in the three-plane layout. Five strings of 11.2 kWp is 56 kWp, a ratio of 1.12, and four is 44.8 kWp, a ratio of 0.90, against 1.34 for six strings. Either way, the third roof plane cannot get its second string on that unit.

Two further notes apply. First, Solis and Sungrow print per-string figures of 16 A and 15 A, and a 700 Wp class string at 17.4 A is above both, so confirm before you design that module on either. The GoodWe India sheet prints no per-string figure at all, so ask for it before you design a 700 Wp string on a GoodWe input. Second, no sheet says how the inverter behaves when operating current exceeds the rating, so put that question to each brand before you load a two-string tracker beyond it.

**Allocation on one Qbits Plus unit.** With six 600 Wp strings, the Plus carries all three planes without mixing orientations on a tracker.

| Tracker | Rated current | Allocation | Operating current at 600 Wp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 40 A | South, 2 strings | 29.0 A |
| 2 | 40 A | East, 2 strings | 29.0 A |
| 3 | 20 A | West, string 1 | 14.5 A |
| 4 | 20 A | West, string 2 | 14.5 A |

The west plane's two strings sit on two separate trackers because a 20 A tracker takes one 600 Wp string. That is fine electrically, and both trackers see the same orientation. The layout leaves no spare tracker, and a seventh string would not fit under the 600 Wp current rule. The 700 Wp case uses the same four-tracker pattern, with 34.8 A on each 40 A tracker and 17.4 A on each 20 A tracker. At 67.2 kWp per unit it sits under the Plus 75 kW ceiling and would be over the 65 kW printed on the QB-50KTLC sheet.

**The AC side.** The six sheets print a maximum output current of 79.8 A to 83.6 A at 400 V. At a 415 V supply and the printed 55 kVA, the current works out to about 76.5 A, which is why you size from the printed maximum and your installation derating, not from kW alone. Four units give four feeders of about 80 A, or about 320 A at the combined ACDB for all four at their maximum, plus four breakers and four terminations. If one unit trips, 25% of the plant is offline.

## Which 50 kW unit suits which roof?

These picks are conditional readings of the datasheets. They are not quality or reliability rankings.

**Growatt MAX50KTL3LV** suits a roof with many small orientation groups, since six trackers lets you keep up to six planes apart. It has the highest maximum efficiency here (98.8%), and it carries six strings of 700 Wp class modules at one string per tracker. It trades that for 82 kg, IP65, a 250 V start-up voltage and a 26 A tracker that takes one 600 Wp string.

**GoodWe GW50K-SDT-C30** suits a layout with up to four separate groups of one or two strings, even at 700 Wp, where C&I monitoring matters (it names Modbus-RTU and Modbus-TCP and prints integrated string monitoring and shadow scanning) and the site is cold enough to use a -30 C rating. It is the lightest unit here at 33 kg in IP66. It trades that for four trackers, no printed per-string limit, no maximum DC power row and no standards list.

**Huawei SUN2000-50KTL-M3** suits a site that wants AFCI, integrated PID recovery and an IP66 unit at 49 kg, and where eight inputs on four trackers is enough. Its limits on the sheet are four trackers, one 700 Wp string per tracker, and no grid voltage range printed. Check availability and service in India before you shortlist it.

**Qbits QB-50KTLC-Plus** suits an EPC that wants a light unit (37 kg, second only to the GoodWe's 33 kg) in IP66, four trackers, a 200 to 1000 V window and a 75 kW printed DC ceiling, on a roof where one or two planes carry most strings and the rest carry one. Its limits on the published sheet are a 20 A pair of trackers, no short-circuit rating, no AFCI row, no string-monitoring row, a surge-protection type that is not stated, and RS485 as an option rather than standard. Nothing on the sheet settles warranty terms or the service route, so ask Qbits for both in writing, as with any brand. The datasheet is linked from the [QB 40/45/50KTLC-Plus product page](/our-products/QB-40-50KTLC-Plus/).

**Qbits QB-50KTLC** suits a roof with at most three uniform plane groups of long strings, where 45 A trackers carry two 700 Wp class strings each. It trades that for a 300 V start-up voltage, a 65 kW DC ceiling, 47 kg and dimensions we could not compare.

**Solis S5-GC50K** suits an installer who wants string monitoring and I-V curve scan in an IP66 unit with five 32 A trackers and a 70 mm2 AC terminal. Its AFCI is optional, its sheet prints no maximum DC power row and no grid voltage range, and it is 53.7 kg.

**Sungrow SG50CX-P2** suits a coastal or industrially corrosive site, because it prints a C5 corrosion class, IP66, AFCI as standard and Type I+II DC surge protection, at 41 kg. It trades that for four trackers and a per-string current that needs checking against large modules.

## What do the 50 kW datasheets leave out?

The six sheets are brand documents, and some are older than the unit you will receive. GoodWe's India sheet carries file token 20250814, Sungrow's is version 4 from 2023, and Growatt's file name carries 202305. The Huawei, Solis and Qbits sheets print no date. Ask each supplier to confirm that its sheet covers the exact model and region quoted to you.

Price, stock, warranty length or remedy, certificate validity, DISCOM acceptance, firmware and field failure rates are outside this comparison, and each affects the purchase. Read [how to read solar inverter datasheets](/blog/how-to-read-solar-inverter-datasheets/) before you treat any tabled figure as final, and ask for [DISCOM acceptance](/blog/discom-approval-solar-inverter-net-metering/) for the exact model in writing, because there is no single national list.

| Request | What it settles |
| --- | --- |
| Datasheet for the exact model code and region | The limits for the unit in the quote |
| Short-circuit current per MPPT in writing | Whether your module passes the Isc check |
| Per-string current limit in writing | Whether a 600 Wp or larger module fits one input |
| What happens above the rated input current | Whether a two-string tracker is safe or just clipped |
| Derating curve against ambient temperature | Output on a hot roof, such as a steel-sheet warehouse roof |
| Current certificates for the model | Evidence your DISCOM may ask for |
| Grid-code settings your DISCOM requires | That the unit can be set to them |
| RS485 register map | Whether your O&M contractor can read it |
| Written warranty and service terms | Term, remedy, freight, labour, who attends |
