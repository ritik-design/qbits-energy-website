## What is WACC

WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) is the blended cost of financing for a project, calculated as the weighted average of the cost of debt and the cost of equity, with weights based on each source's share of total project financing. It is the discount rate used in NPV and LCOE calculations to translate future cash flows to present value.

For Indian solar projects, WACC typically ranges from 8 to 12 percent post-tax depending on project type and risk profile:
- Utility-scale solar with strong PPA: 8-10 percent
- C&I solar with corporate offtaker: 9-11 percent
- Residential rooftop: 10-12 percent

The WACC formula:
WACC = (D/V × Rd × (1-T)) + (E/V × Re)

Where:
- D, E, V: debt, equity, total project cost
- Rd: pre-tax cost of debt
- T: corporate tax rate
- Re: cost of equity

## Why WACC matters

NPV calculation. Project economics depends on discount rate.

LCOE. Higher WACC means higher LCOE.

PPA tariff bidding. WACC determines minimum acceptable tariff.

Project selection. Projects with IRR below WACC destroy value.

Lender vs equity. Tension between debt cost (lower) and equity return (higher).

## Technical deep dive

### WACC calculation example

For a 100 MW Indian utility solar project:
- Capex: ₹400 crore
- Debt: ₹280 crore (70%) at 8.5% pre-tax, 6.6% after-tax (T=22.4%)
- Equity: ₹120 crore (30%) at 15%
- WACC = (0.7 × 6.6%) + (0.3 × 15%) = 4.62% + 4.50% = 9.12%

### Indian solar WACC by financing mix

| Leverage (D/E) | Equity cost | Debt after-tax | WACC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60/40 | 15% | 7% | 10.2% |
| 65/35 | 15% | 7% | 9.8% |
| 70/30 | 15% | 7% | 9.4% |
| 75/25 | 16% | 7% | 9.3% |
| 80/20 | 17% | 7% | 8.8% |

Higher leverage reduces WACC but raises equity cost (financial risk premium).

### Cost of debt drivers

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| RBI policy rate | Direct impact |
| Credit spread for renewable | -50 to +50 bps |
| Tenor (longer = lower) | Long tenor preferred |
| Currency risk | INR vs USD |
| DISCOM credit | Stronger = cheaper |

### Cost of equity drivers

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Risk-free rate (10-year G-Sec) | 6-7% in 2024 |
| Market risk premium | 6-8% |
| Project beta | 0.8-1.2 for utility solar |
| Construction risk | +1-2% during construction |

### Tax shield effect

For 22 percent corporate tax:
After-tax debt cost = Pre-tax × (1, 0.22) = Pre-tax × 0.78

This reduces effective cost of debt by ~22 percent of the pre-tax rate.

## Sensitivity to WACC

For a 100 MW solar plant:

| WACC | LCOE (₹/kWh) |
|---|---|
| 6% | 2.45 |
| 8% | 2.75 |
| 10% | 3.05 |
| 12% | 3.40 |

Each 200 bps WACC change moves LCOE by ~₹0.30/kWh.

## Best practices

Use long-term average WACC for stable projects.

Account for currency risk for imported equipment.

Update WACC assumptions during project review.

Document WACC components transparently.

Stress-test sensitivity to WACC changes.
